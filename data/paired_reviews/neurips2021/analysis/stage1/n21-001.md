**Verdict.** Both committees averaged 5.0 and weighted the same facts differently. Reviewers on both sides saw a clear, thorough, but incremental empirical study. Each side also noted the missing comparison with other efficiency methods. A's acceptance departs from its scores: three of four A reviewers rated below threshold. A's area chair (AC) wrote that the rebuttal addressed the concerns, but no A reviewer replied or edited after it. B's rejection follows its scores (3, 5, 7) but not its detractors' reasons. B's AC dismissed both reasons, then rejected on the missing comparison with reduced precision, distillation and pruning. B's champion, Reviewer_2TRF, had raised that point and called it "not a big concern". The concern is accurate. The paper names pruning, quantization and lightweight design, yet compares only adder and convolutional versions of the same detectors. Its energy is an operation count priced at 0.9 and 3.7 pJ, with no memory term. The authors' INT8 table, sent only to A, shows that quantization alone cuts FCOS energy about 20-fold (987.6 to 49.4 mJ). The adder backbone and neck cut it 1.4-fold.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 5, 5, 4, 6. Reviewer_ZgMW (8 hours) and Reviewer_uAeA led their criticism with limited novelty. Reviewer_h7uh called each tweak small and the energy saving "underwhelming", about 25% for 1 to 3 AP. Table 5 bears this out (987.7 to 702.7 mJ). Reviewer_uAeA asked for a pruning comparison. The only vote above threshold, Reviewer_NNwT's 6 at confidence 5, is a short review of mostly presentation requests. The rebuttal gave new numbers: R-PAFPN without shortcut, a convolutional FCOS with R-PAFPN, a Joint-DetNAS pruning comparison, and INT8 energy. The AC's two-sentence meta-review praises presentation and evaluation and says the rebuttal resolved the rest. It does not mention novelty. That judgment is the AC's own.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 3, 5, 7. Reviewer_cnqS (3, 1.5 hours) misread the method as unfreezing BN statistics at inference. Lines 128-129 and the Figure 2 caption say training. After the correction, cnqS kept 3 and cited venue fit. Without that score, B's mean is 6.0. Reviewer_3Btm kept 5, partly because "the neck and detection head are still based on convolution". Table 5 contradicts this for the neck and includes one full-adder RetinaNet. Reviewer_2TRF (7, confidence 5) wrote a careful review and kept its score. The AC called cnqS's venue concern "not valid" and the changes not trivial. The AC then rejected on a limitation that 2TRF itself had listed. One pruning comparison did not satisfy the AC.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A probed the neck ablation. Reviewer_ZgMW read Table 4 correctly: shortcuts give +1.2 over PAFPN, the reversed path +0.4. Only A named memory traffic, which the energy model omits (Reviewer_NNwT, Reviewer_ZgMW). Only A flagged distillation for the adder backbone alone; Table 2 puts it at 0.4 mAP. Two A claims are wrong. ZgMW said the gap grows with longer training; for R-50 FCOS it shrinks (1.9 to 1.2). uAeA called the shortcuts costly; they are element-wise additions, about 11M against 316.5G. Only B raised venue fit, distance from the COCO state of the art, and domain shift. Only B noted that the convolutional baselines lack the learning-rate tricks. The rebuttal confirms it: tuned FCOS reaches 39.0, which widens the 12-epoch gap from 1.9 to 2.5. Table 1 credits these tricks with +1.6 mAP; the text says 0.6. Reviewer_2TRF missed Table 5's full-adder row and asked why such results were "left out".
