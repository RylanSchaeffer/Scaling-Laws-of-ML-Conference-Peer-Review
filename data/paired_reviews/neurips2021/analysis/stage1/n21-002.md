**Verdict.** Both committees accepted the paper and split only on poster versus oral. They saw the same strengths and raised the same main weakness: no comparison to ReActNet. Both received the same core rebuttal, +0.5 top-1 on the ReActNet recipe. No reviewer on either side rated above 8. B's oral comes from its area chair (AC). The AC read the paper, prized "a simple idea that works", and predicted impact beyond BNNs; no reviewer made that claim. B's higher mean (7.75 vs 7.0) helped. Two B reviewers raised scores after a response that left A's scores unchanged. The committees weighted the same facts differently, and the paper supports A's caution more than B's enthusiasm. The PDF is the camera-ready, so Table 5 already holds the rebuttal's ReActNet and RBNN rows.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 8, 7, 6; reviewers spent 6.0 hours on average. Reviewer_oCAy (8) asked for ReActNet and for the noise module's role in Figs. 3-4. Reviewer_KUr5 (7, confidence 5) asked how Fourier series beats tanh or SignSwish when all converge to sign. After the response, KUr5 kept 7. Reviewer_FCVA's 6 does not match its wholly positive text, and its 2021-09-10 edit has no note. The AC restated the consensus and recommended acceptance. The AC required the ReActNet and Lin2020 comparisons and "further justification" of Fourier series over other surrogates. Nobody argued for a higher tier.

**Committee B (oral).** Ratings 8, 8, 8, 7; reviewers spent 3.6 hours on average. The reviews mostly restate the paper's own claims. Reviewer_Xxt6 gave 8 after 2 hours and listed no weakness in the main review. Reviewer_f6JJ raised to 7 after the response. Reviewer_oG66 raised to 8 "after reading comments from other reviewers". Those comments and the AC's "separate post" are not in the public record. oG66 also accepted an O(n^-2) rate for Theorem 1, taken from a result on smooth functions; the square wave's mean-square error falls as O(1/n). The AC called the rebuttal "particularly good" and wrote "I am sure that this paper will be impactful". That sentence, not the reviews, carries the oral.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A questioned the core motivation, and the paper supports that doubt. The energy argument holds for sign, whose coefficients fall as 1/i (Eq. 5). It fails for the gradient used in training: every term of Eq. 8 has amplitude 4w/pi. With the rebuttal's w = 0.1 and n = 18, the surrogate gradient (Eq. 10) is negative for 0.83 < |t| <= 1. The paper never measures gradient direction. Theorem 1 covers the function, not the gradient, so KUr5's "strong theoretical guarantee" and f6JJ's "theorem ... to show the rationality" overstate it. Only A asked for RBNN; the camera-ready margin is 0.3 points (60.2 vs 59.9). Only B probed the noise-module ablations. Table 4 puts the module without its shortcut at 85.27, below sine alone at 85.83 (Table 2). No shortcut-only row exists, so the learned layers, which B's AC was "mainly interested in", show no benefit of their own. All results are single runs without error bars. Neither committee caught that Eq. 7 should read |t| < T/2, a harmless slip.
