**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reason. PlayVirtual adds a backward dynamics model and a cycle-consistency loss on random-action virtual trajectories to SPR. This raises the headline medians on both 100k benchmarks: Atari median HNS 47.2% against SPR's published 41.5%, and DMControl-100k 800.5 against DrQ's 685.5. Table 3 credits the virtual trajectories, not the backward model (Baseline+BDM 38.4%). No final rating is above 7 or below 6, so neither committee had a champion or a detractor. The committees differed in scrutiny, not in outcome. A's reviews total about 1,000 words and call the idea novel. B's total about 2,850 words, and two B reviewers began on the reject side: an incremental add-on to SPR, with no insight into why it works. Rebuttal experiments moved both to 6. So B weighed the same facts more harshly on novelty. This explains its lower mean (6.25 against 6.67), but it did not cross the poster line. Neither committee weighed a weakness that B's record exposes. PlayVirtual is below SPR's published scores in 13 of 26 Atari games, and its mean HNS (63.7% against 70.4%) and superhuman count (4 against 7) trail SPR. The Atari claim rests on the median alone.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 7, 6). Reviewer_kMqF wrote a positive review and asked which loss updates which module. The authors answered with a component summary and two new experiments (frozen-encoder transfer, dynamics-model error), and kMqF kept 7. Reviewer_wH3w asked for an ablation over the number of virtual trajectories, but l.359-360 already points to it in Appendix B. wH3w also said that broader impact was not discussed, but the checklist points to Appendix D. The reply gave that pointer, relative gains and a restatement of the method, and wH3w rose to 7. Reviewer_j7qw wrote a brief review (2 hours) with one real question: can the virtual trajectories be degenerate? The authors answered with argument only, and j7qw did not reply. In B, the same question got experiments. The meta-review copies two phrases from kMqF's summary and adds one question about model-based control.

Committee B (6, 6, 7, 6). Reviewer_1uRz called the work "quite incremental" and asked four specific questions. After the action-sampling and representation experiments, 1uRz rose to 6. 1uRz kept the objection that the takeaway is still "cycle consistency is good". Reviewer_aagy asked for mean HNS, said that RAD matches PlayVirtual on DMControl, and pressed on the opaque latent. aagy rose to 6 after a k-NN retrieval test on one task, "to encourage future work". Reviewer_ddpW (7, confidence 5) wrote the most detailed review and questioned the k-NN setup twice. Reviewer_Lrfb correctly found the per-game losses to SPR. The reply defended the median metric and did not address them. The AC cites "All three reviewers", but B had four. The AC quotes a reservation that is in no public review and uses it to set the tier at poster.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A asked which loss updates which module (kMqF), for the ablation over the number of virtual trajectories (wH3w), and for a safety discussion (wH3w). Only B raised these issues:

- the per-game Atari losses and the missing per-task SPR results on DMControl (Lrfb);
- the unreported mean HNS (aagy);
- the omitted RAD baseline behind the abstract's "by a large margin" (aagy);
- stochastic, partially observable and many-to-one dynamics (1uRz, aagy, Lrfb);
- the action-sampling strategy (all four) and the misleading name (ddpW).

The paper confirms the first two. RAD is cited at l.98 but is absent from Table 2; the authors conceded. None of these issues changed a decision.
