**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected on the same facts. Both saw a CoCLR-style contrastive method, accuracy level with CoCLR (87.4 vs 87.3 in Table 1), and a large saving from codec motion vectors in place of TV-L1 optical flow. Both called the novelty limited. A's majority put efficiency and parity above novelty; B's four reviewers did the reverse. Two factors pushed A up. Its champions, Reviewer_M3h9 and Reviewer_Rmsi, credited compressed-video self-supervised learning as new, but the paper itself cites IMRNet as prior work on it (L95-97). A's one detractor, Reviewer_rcKJ, never answered the rebuttal, so the AC accepted the authors' distinction from CoCLR. B's Reviewer_3btZ correctly called that distinction unfair. B's specific objections are mostly correct, but none is fatal. The mean ratings (5.75 vs 4.5) fall on either side of the threshold.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 4, 6, 7. Reviewer_M3h9 gave the deciding argument: limited novelty, but "both more effective and more efficient than CoCLR", so "a good 'poster' paper". Reviewer_Rmsi (7, 1.5 hours) rated originality "medium-high" and never mentioned IMRNet. Reviewer_ZVsq gave 6 at confidence 5 with a one-sentence summary and three short bullets, then closed with boilerplate. Reviewer_rcKJ (4) wrote a specific, correct critique. It called the method incremental over CoCLR. It also found that the L258 robustness claim has no support, because Table 2 uses MV_CoViAR only at initialization. The rebuttal gave rcKJ a new ablation (ensemble 67.0, with cross guidance 71.3, with hard positives 73.1) and gave M3h9 a Kinetics-400 linear result (51.3). No score moved. The AC set rcKJ aside because it "failed to provide post-rebuttal comments", and followed the majority.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 4, 5, 5. All four reviewers engaged after the rebuttal, and none moved. Reviewer_3btZ (confidence 5) named the two real differences from CoCLR: motion vectors, and one shared embedding space. It called the paper's claim that CoCLR "fails to learn the correspondence between two views" (L183-185) unfair. That is right: CoCLR mines each view's positives from the other view, and MVCGC also initializes its two encoders independently (L206-207). Reviewer_UKWL named IMRNet as the closest prior work and called the re-encoding and residual arguments system-level choices. Reviewer_iwkT and Reviewer_ZRPJ made similar points. The rebuttal restated a contribution that ZRPJ called "too blurry" and added no experiment. ZRPJ (confidence 3) then joined the novelty objection of 3btZ and UKWL. The AC summarized that consensus.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B treated the efficiency gain as engineering; in A, only Reviewer_ZVsq asked whether parallel re-encoding would close IMRNet's gap. The paper supports part of B's doubt. The gain over optical-flow methods is real: TV-L1 pre-processing costs 12.7-13.2 s per video against 0.008 s. The gain over IMRNet (0.355 vs 0.078 s) comes more from inference time (227.2 vs 70.3 ms) than from re-encoding (0.128 vs 0.008 s), and the paper ties inference time to the backbone (L240; R3D-50 vs S3D). Only B found the uncited claim that residuals give "only a marginal performance gain" (L103). Only B saw that Table 4 lists IMRNet's test views as RGB+MV+Res, while Fig. 2 says IMRNet discards RGB. Only B asked why half-resolution flow scores far below motion vectors in Table 2 (51.3 vs 63.4). Only A raised the gap to CVRL and Feichtenhofer et al., and asked for Kinetics-400 linear evaluation, the cross-guidance ablation and per-setting compression results.
