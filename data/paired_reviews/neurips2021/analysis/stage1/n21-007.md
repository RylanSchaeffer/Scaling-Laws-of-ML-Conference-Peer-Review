**Verdict.** The committees saw the same paper and mostly the same weaknesses. They split on the score draw and on how each area chair (AC) used it. A drew three positive but thin reviews. Reviewer_YhTZ's 7 is an "educated guess" (confidence 1). Reviewer_oxRc's 7 lists only two clarification questions as weaknesses. A's AC followed "the major vote" and set their one-line novelty claims against the one detailed review, Reviewer_NVLj's 3. Every checkable point in that review holds against the submission. B drew no champion: 6, 6, 5, 4. No B reviewer posted after the rebuttal, and B's AC built the reject from the critics' own words. No reviewer on either side found an error in a result; the dispute is how much is new. The best-argued review in either record argued for rejection. A's accept rests on its score draw, not on stronger evidence.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 7, 3. NVLj (confidence 5) made specific points, and the PDF confirms each. Fig. 1 draws pre-LayerNorm blocks, but Eq. 6-7 are post-LayerNorm. Section 3.2 presents EasyQuant's alternating search and DFQ's bias correction as the paper's own steps, without citation. Section 3.3 takes HAWQ-v2's Pareto-frontier procedure, swaps the Hessian for the nuclear norm, and never compares the two. The ranking loss adds 0.27-0.68 points in Table 3, from single runs. No hyperparameter is analysed. After the rebuttal, NVLj accepted the Pearson answer, kept the rest, and stayed at 3. Reviewer_bDSX kept 6 after clarifications. YhTZ and oxRc never posted. The AC called NVLj's concerns "highly constructive" but deferred to the count. It wrote that all three others "viewed the proposed approach as being novel". Two said so in one line each; oxRc never used the word.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 6, 4. Reviewer_3X6o spent 15 hours, asked six specific questions, and offered to change the score. The authors answered all six, four with new tables. 3X6o never replied and stayed at 5. Reviewer_gcUY (6, confidence 5) wrote three generic lines and asked for Swin results. The authors gave them, within 0.7 points of full precision at 8 bits; gcUY did not reply. Reviewer_H7ei (6) wrote mostly criticism: unclear method, weak motivation for the ranking loss. H7ei also faulted a "Figure 2" that does not exist. Reviewer_Zj6U (4, 2 hours) misread the method. Zj6U called the ranking loss "not tested", but Table 3 ablates it. The meta-review copies sentences from H7ei and Zj6U. It calls novelty a shared concern, but gcUY called the method "novel" and 3X6o did not question it. It ignores the rebuttal.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the LayerNorm mismatch, the uncited reuse of prior components and the HAWQ-v2 critique. These are the strongest points in either record, and A's AC set them aside. Only B asked for computational cost, calibration-set size, a search ablation and Swin backbones; the rebuttal answered all four. Only B asked whether hand-set hyperparameters favoured the method over re-implemented baselines; the paper does not say how they were chosen. Both raised the Pearson choice, the thin motivation for the ranking loss, the hand-set hyperparameters and the opaque Eq. 18. Nobody in A noticed that the rebuttal's γ and θ sweeps peak at 75.81%. That is Table 3's accuracy without the ranking loss; Table 3 gives 76.49% with it. The camera-ready adds a Hessian comparison: 77.01 versus 76.98 top-1, at 755 versus 53 seconds. The nuclear norm is cheaper, not better.
