**Verdict.** Committee B rejected because of one reviewer and an area chair (AC) who adopted his frame. Reviewer_wmCS ran the supplementary code for 10 hours, reported three bugs and said he could not replicate even the MADDPG baseline. B's AC made reproducibility the deciding issue. On the other facts the two ACs agreed. A's AC called the idea "rather natural" but the results "strong". B's AC called the paper a combination of "two well-known baselines", so a report of experiments that must be reproducible. On the record the code report is weak. The authors replied that the bugs need batch_size_run>1 or max_seq_length=1, settings their configs never use. wmCS gave no numbers or commands and did not reply. B's other two reviewers were stronger than A's champions and ended at 7. The AC overrode them. With three reviewers, one extreme score weighed more than A's lone 4 among five. The program chairs accepted the paper, so they did not see a fatal flaw.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 7, 4, 7; nobody edited a review. The two 7s are weak evidence. Reviewer_JS3e (confidence 1) is "not an expert in this area". Reviewer_q18r takes the results as given ("validates the advantages claimed") and asks two generic questions. Reviewer_X7Th found a real gap: DOP reaches about 20% on MMM2 in Figure 6, against about 50% in the DOP paper. The authors cited a newer SC2 version and different statistics; X7Th did not reply. Reviewer_ZyWj (4) asked for proofs. One request rests on a misconception: FACMAC never acts greedily on its critic, so QMIX's consistency condition does not apply. Only Reviewer_oCU2 replied, and accepted Figure 2(b) as the case for the centralised gradient. That figure is unsound. It shows zero per-agent gradients but a non-zero joint gradient at the same point, which no differentiable critic can give. Nobody in A ran the code or compared FACMAC with its strongest baselines.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 7, 1, 7. Reviewer_Byep found the closest prior work, VDAC, which the submission does not cite. Byep raised the score after the authors' comparison. Byep's note, written after wmCS's report, says he looked at the code and asks only for run commands and configs. Reviewer_Ve1n wrote a careful review and correctly noted that MADDPG and DOP converge as fast as FACMAC on bane_vs_bane. wmCS (1, confidence 5, 25 hours) worked hard but read carelessly. He took the legend's seed counts for citations. He blamed the authors for DOP's "first actor-critic" claim. He asked for super-hard maps that Figure 6 already has. He said Eq. 7 needs shared parameters; lines 185-186 say it does not. Two of his points hold. COMIX matches or beats FACMAC on every MAMuJoCo task, as line 287 admits. With separate parameters, Eq. 7 is Eq. 6 taken at the other agents' current actions, not a new search space. The AC used neither point. The meta-review echoes wmCS's words and ignores the authors' reply and the two 7s.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B examined the code, and that decided the outcome. Only B named VDAC; the camera-ready adds VDAC-mix. Only B challenged FACMAC's lead over the baselines, and the paper confirms part of it: COMIX ties FACMAC on MAMuJoCo, and three of six SMAC maps saturate. Only A raised the DOP mismatch, scale against graph-based critics and the small number of MAMuJoCo tasks. Both raised the modest novelty, the thin case for the centralised gradient and the missing centralised baseline.
