**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same paper: an incremental mix of known parts with state-of-the-art numbers on LLP, the only benchmark for the task. Both area chairs (ACs) followed their reviewers' scores. A's AC called the work "somewhat incremental" but publishable. B's AC called it "very close to the border" and took the mean, 4.75. The split comes from the reviewers and the rebuttal. Two of A's confidence-5 reviewers, Reviewer_2QRh and Reviewer_jrWL, asked for experimental controls. The authors answered with new numbers, and both raised to 7. B's reviewers framed the same weakness as novelty and motivation. New numbers cannot answer that, and no B reviewer moved up. The paper supports both readings. The full method beats MA by 0.2 to 3.5 points, and a parameter-matched control on HAN rules out extra capacity as the source of the CM-Co gain. But the part the authors call their main novelty, the cross-video contrastive loss (CM-S), only matches MA's own contrastive loss. Reviewer_4KfH (B) saw this in Tables 1 and 2. 2QRh (A) raised the same gap and accepted a rebuttal table with gains near 1 point. No result has error bars. The committees weighted the same facts differently.

**Committee A (poster, mean 6.25).** Ratings 6, 7, 5, 7; confidence 4, 5, 5, 5; 5.0 hours on average. jrWL asked whether the CM-Co gains come from extra parameters. The authors showed that HAN with 2 or 3 layers (2.9M, 3.5M parameters) does not beat HAN + CM-Co (2.8M). That is a fair control, and jrWL raised to 7. 2QRh raised to 7 after the MA + CM-S table and the authors' admission that Eq. 8 adds label noise. Reviewer_nrEQ held 6 and called the work "incremental". Its summary and main review largely restate the paper's own sentences and the review-form prompts; its concrete points sit in the limitations list. Reviewer_ffnZ (5, confidence 5) gave the fullest critique and never replied after the rebuttal. The AC copied ffnZ's and jrWL's summaries into the meta-review and accepted.

**Committee B (reject, mean 4.75).** Ratings 4, 4, 5, 6; confidence 4, 4, 3, 4; 2.9 hours on average. Reviewer_6GpX (4) found CM-Co close to Tirupattur et al.; the paper itself says "Similar to [46]". After the rebuttal, 6GpX still leaned to reject. Reviewer_4KfH (6), the only positive reviewer, then agreed that the techniques lack novelty and "would not be upset" by rejection. Reviewer_2ihx (4) found a real dimension error in Eq. 7 and missing dimensions in Eq. 6. It also misread Eq. 8: positives in the denominator are the standard InfoNCE form, not an error. 2ihx never replied after the rebuttal. Reviewer_aCaK (5) spent 1.5 hours at confidence 3 and wrote a generic "not inspiring" review. The AC counted all four scores equally.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for a parameter-matched control, and the answer helped the paper. Only A noted that HAN + Ours loses audio accuracy (segment F-score 60.1 to 59.2). Only A raised label noise in the positive pairs of Eq. 8; the authors agreed. Only A objected to the single dataset; the objection is weak, because LLP is the only benchmark for the task. Only B found the dimension problems in Eq. 6 and Eq. 7; the authors promised to fix Eq. 7. Only B asked why cross-video and cross-class modelling should help. Both committees raised limited novelty, CM-S against MA's contrastive loss, parameters that grow with the number of classes, and a trivial Figure 2. The paper confirms the last point: every ground-truth event in Figure 2 spans the full 10 seconds.
