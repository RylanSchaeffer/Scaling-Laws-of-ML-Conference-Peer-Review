## Verdict

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. In each, three of four reviewers scored 6 or more, and no reviewer found an error. Each committee judged its criticisms fixable in the camera-ready. A's mean (6.5) is above B's (6.0). A had a confident champion, Reviewer_Xk7N, who praised the speed-up. B had a confident detractor, Reviewer_hdeg, who questioned novelty over Ho, Petrik and Wiesemann (2018). Neither area chair (AC) departed from the mean. No reviewer noticed that the paper's own results fall short of its headline speed-up for the S-rectangular case in the title. In Table 2, Algorithm 2 is only 3 to 4 times faster than Gurobi on MR and RS, and 41 times on IM. Fig. 5 shows about 50 to 100 times at 100 to 400 states. The abstract claims "several orders of magnitude"; the introduction claims "over 1,000 times".

## Committees

Committee A (8, 5, 7, 6). Reviewer_Xk7N was the only reviewer in A to test a claim against the results. They asked why Fig. 5 grows with the number of states if the method is quasi-linear. The authors replied that the O(SA log S) bound is per state; the abstract does not say this. Xk7N accepted the reply, then lowered a higher score to 8 "to be more in line with the other ratings". That is conformity, not new evidence. Reviewer_7bXv (7) did not read the proofs in detail and asked for results inside a full solver. Reviewer_Hc9Q (6) wrote three generic sentences and some notation points. Reviewer_7V4J (5, confidence 1) could not follow the paper. They asked for results beyond 30 states, but Fig. 5 already goes to 400. The AC's two-sentence meta-review weighted by confidence: "the most confident reviewers having the most favorable opinions".

Committee B (6, 6, 7, 5). Reviewer_hdeg (5, confidence 4) made the only substantive objection: what is new beyond replacing L1 with L∞ in Ho et al. (2018)? Part of it is wrong. Norms that are equivalent up to a constant only nest the balls, so a rescaled L1 algorithm solves a different problem. The paper also states a concrete difference: under L∞, all components of p can move, not two (lines 228-235). hdeg did not answer the rebuttal. Reviewer_cHzA (6) correctly found no data for the claim that runtime is insensitive to κ (lines 280-281); the rebuttal gave none. Reviewer_n2xU (7), the top score, built the review from the paper's own sentences, including "our method is over 1, 000 times faster". Reviewer_So7E (6, confidence 2) asked for larger domains. The AC accepted and moved hdeg's novelty point and the missing details to the camera-ready.

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A: the quasi-linear claim holds per state, not per sweep (Xk7N); no results inside value or policy iteration (7bXv); hard for non-experts, with Example 3.1 and Fig. 2 unclear (7V4J, 7bXv); notation gaps and the distinct-z assumption (Hc9Q).

Only B: novelty over Ho et al. (2018) and no direct comparison with it (hdeg); the case for L∞ over L1 sets (hdeg); the unsupported κ claim (cHzA); non-rectangular sets (n2xU), which lines 79-82 already set aside as NP-hard.

Both committees asked for larger benchmarks (7V4J, So7E). B's issues concern the contribution; A's concern framing and presentation. Neither committee treated any issue as disqualifying. hdeg's request for a direct comparison with Ho et al. had the most force. With S-rectangular sets, the speed-up over Gurobi is 3, 4 and 41 times for L∞ against 79, 158 and 452 times for L1. Line 289's "similar dramatic speedups" does not hold there.
