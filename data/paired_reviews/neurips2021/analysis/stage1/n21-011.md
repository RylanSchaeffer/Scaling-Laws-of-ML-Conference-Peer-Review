**Verdict.** The committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. All eight reviewers found the method simple and built from known parts. Message passing on the dual hypergraph uses the line-graph neighbourhood: two edges are neighbours when they share a node. HyperCluster and HyperDrop run GMPool and SAGPool on the dual. Most reviewers on both sides also credited the problem and the breadth of the experiments. A's reviewers turned this into 6, 5, 6 and 7; B's reviewers gave four 4s. The paper supports the shared doubt. The submission never mentions line graphs or Monti et al. (2018), and the camera-ready adds both. The authors conceded to Reviewer_QU8H that "the adjacency between the edges" is the same as in a line graph, and defended only the lower cost. No rating moved visibly after the rebuttal, and both ACs followed their mean. The split is calibration, plus one uncritical 7 in A.

**Committee A (poster, mean 6.0).** Reviewer_5K8S (6) cited Monti et al. and called the dual "not novel per se", but valued HyperDrop as learnable rewiring. 5K8S never replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_EuYn (5, confidence 5) argued over three rounds that the dual, message passing on it and edge pooling are all known. EuYn raised its confidence, not its rating, and ended with "accepting it would not be that bad". Reviewer_xkvv (6) showed that a star graph makes Eq. 4 quadratic. That is right for Eq. 4 as written; the authors' O(m) needs a two-stage aggregation that the main text does not describe. xkvv accepted the answer and edited its review on the last day; any score change is not visible. Reviewer_JU4W (7) restates the paper, names no weakness, and says the related work "covers most important related works". That is wrong for line graphs. The AC's short meta-review repeats the positive framing and skips the novelty dispute.

**Committee B (reject, mean 4.0).** All four reviewers gave 4 and led with novelty. QU8H made the point checkable through Eq. 4 and got the concession above. QU8H's Figure 1 objection was wrong. With hydrogens suppressed, as in the paper's ZINC graphs, Tylenol and NAPQI share 11 heavy atoms and one skeleton; QU8H accepted this. Reviewer_dR4y wrongly said the pooling "cannot be trained in an end-to-end way". dR4y was right that classification gains are small: HyperDrop averages 73.20 against GMT's 73.07 and trails GMT on three OGB datasets. Reviewer_HuNj (confidence 5) cited HGP-SL accuracies from a different protocol; in the authors' rerun, HGP-SL scores below HyperDrop. Reviewer_KF8o praised the empirical study but found the model not conceptually stronger than existing GNNs. KF8o, HuNj and dR4y never replied after the rebuttal. The AC summarised the shared novelty concern.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for an MLP baseline, statistical tests on Table 2, a quantitative two-moon result and hypergraph-attention baselines. The authors supplied the first three; EuYn still held 5. Table 2 does show larger spreads for EHGNN in six of eight cells, and the ChEMBL success-rate gain is smaller than either standard deviation. Only B said EHGNN needs a parallel node GNN (KF8o); the paper confirms this. Only B raised dataset scale, memory, a flawed Figure 1 and end-to-end training; the last two claims were wrong. Both committees raised novelty, the line-graph equivalence, pooling reuse, the complexity claims, and undefined tasks and compression ratio.
