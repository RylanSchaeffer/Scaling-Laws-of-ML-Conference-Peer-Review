**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same borderline paper and weighted the same two facts in opposite ways. The problem, topology imbalance, is new and interesting; both ACs say so. The fix, rank re-weighting by PPR conflict, is a simple heuristic without theory that adds about one F1 point; both ACs say that too. Committee A's AC read it as a problem paper. That AC reused the authors' last reply almost word for word: "the task of solving topology imbalance is non-trivial due to the asymmetry and complexity of the graph topology". Committee B's AC read it as a methods paper and rejected on three points. One point, the missing formal definition, appears in no B review. The authors made the same "first study, lite solution" plea to B (reply to WAqa, 2021-08-20), and it moved nobody. The reviewer pools tilted the same way. A's only sub-threshold reviewer misread the experiments and still raised his score. B's two 5s came from its two most specific reviews, and their main checkable claims hold. Neither committee found an error.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 5 (mean 6.0). Reviewer_t36c (4 h, 6) wrote a balanced review: a novel problem, a method built from "existing techniques", no theory, and labels that may themselves be biased. After the rebuttal t36c kept 6. Reviewer_9Atj's 7 rests on a short review with no stated weakness, confidence 3, and a one-line reply. Reviewer_1Cph spent 1 hour at confidence 5. 1Cph called the Table 1 models baselines "not designed for imbalanced classification" and doubted the results. But Table 1 is a with/without-ReNode ablation on six GNNs, and Table 3 compares six imbalance methods. 1Cph mentions neither. 1Cph then raised the score "slightly" to 5 and kept "limited novelty" and "lack of theoretical insights". The AC accepted on problem novelty and called the simple method fitting for a first study.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 5 (mean 5.33); no score moved. Reviewer_84DB (3 h, confidence 5) gave the most specific critique, and most of it holds. Table 1 gains have a median of 0.8 Weighted-F1 points (range −0.2 to 2.7); 27 of 30 are below the baseline's standard deviation. All datasets are homophilous, and Section 4.2 admits the limit. No alternative to PPR is tested; the rebuttal answered with weight schedules. One 84DB claim is wrong: Table 1 shows ReNode on five GNNs besides GCN. 84DB never replied after the rebuttal. Reviewer_WAqa (3.5 h) found real presentation faults, such as the unexplained Figure 2(a) legend and "GTA" on line 198. After the rebuttal WAqa argued that boundary nodes need better graph filters, not lower weights. The paper's own Figure 5 (GAT least sensitive) supports that premise, but Table 1 shows ReNode still helps GAT. Reviewer_PVVk's 6 came from 36 minutes and "significance: Ok". The AC's third point says boundary nodes are "discarded"; Eq. (4) only lowers them to w_min.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B raised the homophily scope, the size of the gains, the missing alternatives to PPR, and the case for using boundary nodes. These points are correct but not fatal, and A's reviewers never weighed them. Only B's AC asked for a formal definition of topology imbalance. The paper has none, but no reviewer asked for one. Only A raised prior label-propagation work on graph imbalance (a KDD 2018 paper that the submission does not cite), biased labels feeding Totoro, and the undefined P_v. None of these drove A's decision. The shared concern, a heuristic method without theory, was outweighed in A and decisive in B.
