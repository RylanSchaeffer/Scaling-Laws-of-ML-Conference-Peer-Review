**Verdict.** Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. Most reviewers on each side found the question new and important. Average calibration does not guarantee accurate decision-loss estimates, and threshold calibration is the exact condition that does. Each committee had one 4 whose specific complaints the submission confirms. Both chairs overruled that 4. On both forums the authors conceded the same notation "typo" in Definitions 3 to 6 and admitted there is no finite-sample guarantee. The camera-ready fixes most of the flaws that the two 4s named. The notation flaw drew an 8 from Reviewer_H5Nm in A ("slightly off") and a 4 from Reviewer_vtF8 in B ("fundamentally flawed"). The reviewers weighted that fact differently; the two committees did not. Neither committee went above a poster. No reviewer in A argued for an award, and B's top score is a 9 at confidence 2.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 8, 5, 8, 4; confidences 4, 3, 4, 4. Reviewer_H5Nm wrote the most careful positive review and raised to 8 after the rebuttal. Reviewer_YN6e's 8 lists only minor comments. One asks whether the reliability gap can be negative, but Definition 2 is an absolute value. Reviewer_Y7ES's 5 (2 hours) is three questions and no reason for the score. The paper answers two of them (line 100, Section 3.1). Reviewer_CBqV's 4 (2 hours) is specific and correct. Theorem 1 has no sketch (lines 162-166). The converses of Proposition 1 have no examples (lines 182-184). The experiments use one loss family and only post-hoc recalibration baselines. The rebuttal answered the theory points but not the experiments point. CBqV did not reply. The chair reduced CBqV's review to that one point and called it "relatively weak". The chair set Y7ES aside and followed the two 8s.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 4, 9; confidences 3, 4, 4, 2. Reviewer_vtF8 (6 hours) wrote the most rigorous review in either committee. Definition 3 conditions on h[x](y0) ≤ α for a fixed x, which is not a random event (line 147 against lines 74-77). The gloss "α-quantile equal to y0" (line 148) misstates it. Algorithm 1 needs the true TCE, and Theorem 2 says nothing about generalization, so line 201 overstates. The authors conceded the notation and the empirical-only guarantee. vtF8 stayed at 4 but wrote, "I am excited about reading the paper once the notation is updated." Reviewer_eKGZ's 9 praises "intuition and examples for each theoretical result" and clear notation. In the submission, only Theorem 2 has an intuition. Reviewer_APiD's 7 misreads the aim as cost reduction. It asks the authors to cite Song et al. 2019, already reference [30]. Reviewer_VsY9's careful 7 held after the rebuttal. The chair cited "general agreement ... on the novelty and significance". The chair reduced vtF8's case to "polishing of the notation" and did not mention Theorem 2.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised these:

- no sketch of Theorem 1, and no counterexamples for Proposition 1 (CBqV, YN6e);
- one loss family, no simulations, no baseline that optimizes the decision loss (CBqV);
- why regression and not classification (Y7ES).

Only B raised these:

- a hidden TCE oracle, and a Theorem 2 that holds only on the recalibration data (vtF8);
- running time and scalability (eKGZ, VsY9);
- fairness, and no societal-impact discussion in Section 7 (eKGZ, VsY9);
- related work on survival analysis and cost-sensitive regression (APiD).

Both committees doubted the distribution-calibration result. H5Nm suspected a small recalibration set; VsY9 noted a binning variant, not the method of [30]. The camera-ready abstract still says the algorithm "provably outputs a threshold-calibrated forecaster".
