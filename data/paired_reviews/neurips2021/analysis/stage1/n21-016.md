**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. Reviewer depth set the weights. The facts: one large gain (CIFAR-10, 40 labels: 6.54% against USADTM's 9.54% error), parity elsewhere, a notation-heavy method with many parts, and no clear source of the gain. A's four 6s came at confidence 2 to 3, mostly as clarification questions. B's reviewers, at confidence 3 to 5, checked the ablations and the per-dataset margins, and the paper confirms them. A's area chair (AC) named B's central doubt, "not very clear where exactly the gains come from", yet accepted on the uniform scores. B's AC rejected on incrementality, weak results elsewhere and clarity. It overstated the results point: they are on par, not "relatively poor". Neither committee found an error. The paper sits at the threshold, and A's less critical pool placed it above.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 6. The uniformity is weak evidence. Reviewer_YU4M (confidence 2, 2 hours) says it is not an SSL expert and gives generic praise. Reviewer_8Nkc asked for a UDA comparison that Table 1 already contains, and never replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_cBn7 rated 4 for clarity. The authors asked it to re-evaluate on 2021-08-30; the next day it raised to 6, saying the rebuttal "addressed most" of its concerns. Reviewer_AJRp saw that the no-MCL ensemble (Exp1) already beats FixMatch. It accepted the reply that the label model carries the gain and kept 6. It called the gains on SVHN and STL-10 "significant". That holds against FixMatch (RA), the authors' chosen baseline, but not against USADTM. The AC left clarity and ablation fixes to the camera-ready.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 4, 4. Reviewer_gFDp gave the most specific critique, and Table 4 bears it out. MCL, the headline component, adds 1.36 and 0.31 points of annotation accuracy. The regularizer adds 0.63 and 0.39. The no-MCL variant (92.46%) already beats FixMatch's proxy labels (88.51%, line 279). The authors conceded that the label model, not MCL, carries most of the gain. One gFDp claim is wrong: Table 3 compares the label model with majority vote. Reviewer_ux7Q's point also holds. At 40 labels, DP-SSL leads USADTM by 0.03 points on SVHN and 0.31 on STL-10, inside one standard deviation. Reviewer_xTCd (confidence 5) caught a label-count typo and the missing training cost. The explanations that moved cBn7 in A moved nobody in B; gFDp asked for "a major revision". The meta-review's three reasons follow ux7Q's W1, W2 and W4 in order. Reviewer_ECpd wrote that "most reviewers" cited limited novelty. Only two of four did, and gFDp called the approach original.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B called the method incremental (ECpd, ux7Q). Only B quantified the small effects of MCL and the regularizer; in A, AJRp only asked where the gain comes from. Only B asked for soft-versus-hard label, abstention and ε ablations (gFDp, ux7Q). Only B compared margins with USADTM instead of FixMatch (RA), and asked for training cost (xTCd). Only A asked for a self-training comparison such as SimCLRv2 (AJRp). Only A raised label bias with very few labels (cBn7). Only A asked about augmentation fairness, which Section 4.1 already states (8Nkc). Both committees flagged unclear notation, the unexplained four-part potential in Eq. 7, the lack of ImageNet results, and multi-stage training cost.
