**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently. Both saw a narrow but new result: a classical numerical-analysis theorem turns a gradient-flow (GF) analysis into an almost-sure gradient-descent (GD) guarantee for fixed-size deep linear networks from random near-zero initialization. Both raised the same weaknesses: a dense main text with related work and conclusion in the appendix, a guarantee only for linear networks, and a step size exponentially small in depth. Neither found a technical error. Before the rebuttal the gap was small: A's mean stayed at 5.0, and B's was at most 6.0. The authors answered both committees with promises to restructure. A's two detractors did not credit the promises; four of B's five reviewers raised their scores. B's spotlight rests on one careful champion and two low-confidence 7s. The dataset PDF is the camera-ready; I checked claims against it.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 4, 3, 7, 6, all at confidence 3. Reviewer_x5V9 (4) found the positioning unclear, between a generic GF-to-GD tool and a deep-linear guarantee, and noted that the main text gives no idea how Proposition 5 is proved. That holds: even the camera-ready gives one sentence ("cf. [4]") and "See Subappendix J.8". Reviewer_jeQC (3) called the paper "extremely dense" and Theorem 4 weaker than Arora et al. (2019). The core of that comparison is right: eq. (19) shrinks the step size with the target accuracy; [5] does not. But jeQC wrongly said the paper does not discuss [5] or Zou et al.; Appendix A did. After the rebuttal both detractors kept their scores; x5V9 saw "little guarantee that the weaknesses will be fixed". Reviewer_4GXj (7) praised the writing, asked two questions and never replied. Reviewer_nsKx (6) valued the tool but agreed with x5V9 on presentation. The area chair (AC) settled the 2-2 split with x5V9's positioning critique, cited by name.

**Committee B (spotlight).** Ratings 7, 6, 7, 7, 8; confidences 3, 4, 1, 2, 3. Reviewer_4TKP (8, 12 hours) wrote the most careful review on either side. It raised A's density and scope problems, then went from 6 to 8 because "the proposed changes completely address my concerns". Reviewer_kYKg went from 6 to 7 after an answer about future work on large step sizes. Reviewer_r4iB (confidence 4) claimed Theorem 4 follows trivially from classical Euler error analysis. That is wrong. A uniform Hessian bound gives a step size exponentially small in the convergence time (Corollary 1), and that time grows as 1/‖W_{n:1,0}‖ (eq. 17). The paper avoids this because the integral of −λmin stays logarithmic for small ϵ (eq. 18). r4iB accepted the correction and raised to 6. Reviewer_hENG (7, confidence 1) is "not a theorist" and did not read the proofs. Reviewer_DACB raised to 7 but "cannot intuitively understand or vouch for the new results". The AC's three sentences say all reviewers saw "a strong contribution" and give no reason for a spotlight.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A compared Theorem 4 with Arora et al. Theorem 4 needs about 1/ε̃ iterations (eqs. 19-20); [5] needs log(1/ε) but starts near the optimum. Only A asked whether trajectory comparison is less efficient than direct discrete proofs; nsKx agreed the paper leaves this open. Only A flagged the claim that the λmin bound "becomes non-negative"; the authors conceded and fixed it. Only B asked for experiments at the theoretical step size (below 10^-20 at depth 3); the camera-ready main text still uses only η0 = 0.001 and its fractions. Only B called the scope too large for a conference, yet B gave a spotlight. The camera-ready kept the layout promises.
