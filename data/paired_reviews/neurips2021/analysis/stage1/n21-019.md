**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently; A also probed deeper. Reviewers on both sides found the variance claim for Eq. 4 unsupported and the gains small outside COCO. The rebuttal gave both nearly the same new numbers. A doubted them; B took them as answers. A's doubts have a basis in the paper. Lines 103-108 say Eq. 4 trains slowly because its gradient resembles the high-variance REINFORCE estimator. But Eq. 4 is an exact sum over all grid positions, so its gradient has no sampling variance, and the paper gives no number. The rebuttal's closed-form expected error, also free of variance, reached 31.2 mAP, no better than Eq. 4 (30.9). So variance does not explain the failure, and the reason sampling helps is unexplained. Against the best regularized variant, the gain is 0.4 to 2.3% relative on four of six tasks, with no error bars (checklist 3c). Neither committee found a fatal flaw. The COCO gain (+5.3 mAP) and the MTFL gain (about 7%) stand on the paper's numbers.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 4, 5, 7, 5, all at confidence 4. Reviewer_mj3d asked why sampling is needed, proposed the closed-form loss as a baseline, and found Soft w/ V.R within about 1% on several tasks. After the rebuttal, mj3d accepted the closed-form result but called the gap "very surprising". mj3d ended at 5, citing the KDE framing and the lack of significance tests. Reviewer_CcdV suspected the Eq. 4 run "was simply not performed properly" and kept 5. Half of CcdV's main request was moot: a basis mixture under the soft-argmax loss is soft-argmax, which CcdV conceded. Reviewer_N85X gave 4 for a framing objection (why not maximum likelihood). N85X asked for a rebuttal discussion and then never replied. The lone 7, Reviewer_K5Z7 (1.5 hours), found the contribution "a little light" and did not reply after the rebuttal. The AC followed the three-to-one split. Its meta-review says the reviewers "remained unconvinced" by the new baselines, but mj3d wrote "I am now convinced" about the closed-form comparison.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 9, 7, 7, 7. Reviewer_1xmt's 9 (1 to 2 hours) is exuberant. It calls the sampling "well-justified with the comparison of Eq. 5 to the REINFORCE convergence problems". The paper has no such comparison. It also calls the related work complete, but the soft-argmax origins (Goroshin et al., Finn et al.) are not cited. Reviewer_Sf1a asked for evidence of the variance claim, received the 30.9 mAP number, and did not reply. Reviewer_WkN8 called the gains on MTFL, KeypointNet, OCT and Human3.6M "less than 1%". The rebuttal answered with gains over plain soft-argmax: 76.1% on OCT, where both regularizers come within 1.5%. It also gave HRNet at 75.4 mAP with no soft-argmax HRNet baseline. WkN8 raised to 7. Reviewer_LEuR raised to 7 after a clarification on heatmaps. The AC wrote three sentences. They give the baseline as "the usual Gumbel softmax". The paper compares with soft-argmax and uses Gumbel-softmax as one step (lines 158-160).

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for the closed-form loss, error bars, and a justification against maximum-likelihood training. Only A flagged two anomalous baselines: Table 5 soft-argmax at 3.08 MAD against 0.735 to 0.746 for all other methods, and Table 7 Soft w/ D.R. at 50.6 against 59.1 for Soft. Only A noted missing soft-argmax citations and the cost of more samples. Only B asked for a fully continuous reparameterized distribution (LEuR), plots of the predicted distributions, and results on newer pose methods (WkN8). Both sides wanted state-of-the-art comparisons and clearer table captions.
