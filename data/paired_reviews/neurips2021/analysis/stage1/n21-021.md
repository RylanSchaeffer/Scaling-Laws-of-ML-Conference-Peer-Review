**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, and they weighed the same facts the same way. In each, three reviewers accepted on the same strengths: convincing audio demos and a broad evaluation in which VoiceMixer leads on naturalness, subjective similarity and FDSD (Tables 1-2). In each, one reviewer fell below the bar for the same reason: eleven generator losses stacked on AutoVC, with ablations that do not isolate the new bottleneck. Both ACs accepted the complexity: A's called the combination "non-trivial and novel", and B's said the results "justify this additional complexity". Nobody made a case above poster: A's two 8s say "clear accept", and B has no rating above 7. The committees differed sharply only on ethics. B's two ethics reviewers recommended rejection; A's judged the risks answerable. B's AC overrode them with a conditional accept. Word-for-word identical conditions (broader impact, restricted release, mitigation) then appeared in both meta-reviews. So the ethics outcome was not independent between the committees.

**Committees**

Committee A (4, 7, 8, 8; mean 6.75). Reviewer_fx4p's 4 is the better-founded dissent in the pair. Its final doubt, that the similarity bottleneck fails without the other components, matches the paper. Without the auxiliary losses, the model can only "reconstruct source speech" (l.283-285). Without Lpos and Lneg, Similarity falls to 42.3% (Table 3). Its other points also hold. The closest prior work on contrastive segmentation (Kreuk et al., 2020) is uncited, even in the camera-ready. The phoneme-alignment claim rests on one example in Figure 4, with about 66 segments against about 52 phonemes. Reviewer_NNWU wrote the most careful review and found that Eq. 7 runs past the end of the sequence; the authors admitted it. Reviewer_H8yZ's 8 mostly restates the method. Reviewer_Yn2G rose from 6 to 7 after the reply on tuning. The AC answered the novelty dissent in one sentence ("non-trivial and novel") and asked for ablations in the camera-ready. The camera-ready Table 3 is unchanged, and Eq. 7 is still wrong.

Committee B (7, 5, 7, 7; mean 6.5). Reviewer_SxHX wrote the strongest review (12 hours) and found real selective reporting. Section 4.4 cites only favourable metrics, while VoiceMixer trails AUTOVC on MCD and RMSEf0 (6.77 against 6.44; 42.76 against 39.93; Table 1). SxHX kept the 7. Reviewer_yW2U's 5 (2 hours) rests partly on misreadings. Eq. 18 compares per-block discriminator features, not final outputs (l.149-156), and l.79-82 define the notation. yW2U ignored the rebuttal, and the AC ignored the 5. Reviewer_T6dF framed the outcome. The AC's sentence on complexity justified by results, and its view that the paper should not be rejected "simply because" of its area, paraphrase T6dF.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the uncited prior work, instance normalization as a second bottleneck, the errors in Eq. 3 and Eq. 7, the inverted "too narrow/too wide" wording, the missing tuning procedure for eight loss weights, and the weak phoneme-alignment evidence. Only B raised the selective reporting of metrics, the outdated StarGAN-VC baseline, unnormalised loudness in the listening tests, and the lack of prosody conversion. Only B's ethics reviewers judged the risks to outweigh the benefits; Ethics_Reviewer_8woF added that better disentanglement may defeat spoofing detection. None of these issues changed a tier. A's issues were checkable and mostly correct, but one 4 against three accepts could not move the committee. B's ethics objections lost to the AC and to the joint conditions.
