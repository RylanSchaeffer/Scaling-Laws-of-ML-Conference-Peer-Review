**Verdict.** Both committees ended at a mean rating of 6.0 and saw largely the same weaknesses. The area chairs made the split. A's AC counted "3 reviewers hold positive opinions and 1 reviewer is fine with either". That AC added that the idea "may open the door to a new world" and named no technical issue. B's AC rejected over ratings of 6, 6 and 8. It restated the three objections of the lone detractor, Reviewer_DKEh (4, confidence 5): generic theory, capacity versus search, and PGD-only training. The submission confirms the theory objection. Theorems 1 and 3 hold "for any mapping hb, hd", but Eq. 2 sums backbone and dilation features at every block. Theorem 1's extra term equals 1 when the dilation adds nothing, so the bound is then vacuous. The submission also confirms Reviewer_WsAL: TRADES-6 is more robust than NADAR-B (PGD-20 56.43 vs 53.43). The committees weighted the same facts differently, and B's weighting rests on better-checked points.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 5, 6. Reviewer_dFot (6) and Reviewer_Tmzn (7) wrote short, generic positive reviews and posted nothing in the discussion. Reviewer_NJWB (5, confidence 5) made the most specific objection. Natural accuracy falls 8-9 points below standard training on CIFAR-10 and 16-17 points on CIFAR-100 (Tables 1 and 2). PGD-7, FAT and TRADES-1 all keep more (Table 1). Reviewer_dUSe raised the score to 6, citing "fair comparison and wrong accuracy". The rebuttal told dUSe that NADAR can "reach both better standard and adversarial accuracy" than PGD-7. Table 1 and l.303-304 contradict this: 86.23 vs 87.25 natural accuracy. A never saw the later TRADES retraining table. The AC called the rebuttal "particularly good" without naming a point.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 4, 6, 8. Reviewer_kvfV's 8 took one hour, names no weakness and asks three design questions; it is weak evidence. DKEh's review is specific, but one claim is wrong: the NAS baselines are adversarially trained (l.292-294). On 1 September DKEh said no answer addressed larger networks. The authors had cited a random-dilation control in supplement Figure 3. Table 6 also shows a dilated network without separate objectives at 45.97% PGD-20, against 53.18% for NADAR. Reviewer_WsAL raised the score to 6 after the AutoAttack and timing answers. The AC's third concern omits the authors' 25 August table, which retrains with FAT and TRADES. That table supports the concern: under TRADES-6 the gain falls to +1.00 PGD-20. Its "backbone" rows copy the cited Table 1 baselines, so they are not reruns. Its TRADES-6 AutoAttack value, 53.08, conflicts with Table 4, where APGD-T alone gives 43.10.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B challenged the theory, and the paper confirms the challenge. Only B compared NADAR with TRADES-6, and Table 1 confirms the comparison. Only B asked for a best-random-architecture control and for non-ResNet backbones. Only A pressed the natural-accuracy drop, which Tables 1 and 2 confirm. Only A said absolute robustness trails well-tuned adversarial training; the paper cannot settle that. Both committees raised the capacity confound and the old PGD-7 recipe. A's reviewer accepted the answers; B's detractor and AC did not. Separately, A's dFot and B's babp share whole sentences that are not in the paper. Both give 6, confidence 4, time "3", and an edit on 2021-07-19. The committees were not independent on this seat, but this does not explain the split.
