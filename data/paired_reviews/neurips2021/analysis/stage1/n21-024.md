**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same paper: a moderate extension of APN with strong benchmark numbers and a poorly explained training procedure. No reviewer in either committee found an error in the method or the results. A's four reviewers gave 6, and its AC followed them. B split 5, 5, 6, 6. Reviewer_8tZA's 5 does not follow from its own text. Reviewer_hqqu kept 5 after the rebuttal on two follow-up requests that the authors never answered. B's AC adopted both requests and added a guess of its own: ImageNet pretraining may overlap the benchmarks and explain the aPY gain. No reviewer raised this guess, and Table 1 refutes it. Base-V2S uses the same backbone and reaches H 35.5 on aPY, against 48.4 for DPPN. The discrepancy comes from reviewer draw and from how each AC weighted a borderline split, not from a flaw that only one committee found.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 6. Reviewer_UvLd reports that "the ratings diverged" before the discussion. Reviewer_ZcLN edited its review on the day it wrote that the authors "addressed most of my concerns", so ZcLN most likely rose to 6. ZcLN asked the hardest questions: AUSUC instead of H, a max-pooling aggregation baseline, and 224-pixel, non-fine-tuned runs. DPPN kept its lead in most of these tests; it trailed only on SUN AUSUC and on AWA2 at 224 pixels. Reviewer_LSav wrote a three-sentence main review at confidence 5 but caught two real errors. C = 512 at l.215 hides an undocumented reduction layer, and Eq. 9 is cross-entropy, not a contrastive loss. Reviewer_UvLd claimed that seen accuracy carries H. Table 1 contradicts this for CUB: unseen accuracy rises from 50.5 to 70.2 while seen accuracy falls from 84.4 to 77.1. The AC's four sentences name no specific point.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 6, 6. Reviewer_fpHH wrote the most exact review; it found that Table 3's TF-VAEGAN SUN row is transductive, and the camera-ready corrects it. Reviewer_hqqu (9 h) found λ = 0.1 at l.216 against λ = 1.0 at l.282. After the rebuttal, hqqu kept 5 for two reasons. It wanted backbones newer than the added ResNet-50 runs, though the main baselines also use ResNet-101. It also asked how batch size changes with K. That question treats the K iterations as backbone passes, but they run in the head: Table 1 gives 62.842 against 62.900 GFLOPs for K = 1 and K = 3. Reviewer_8tZA's 5 follows a review that praises "significant novelty" and a "sufficient" ablation study. That review asks for hyperparameter values that Sec. 4.1 gives and changes "complementary" to "complimentary". After the rebuttal, only hqqu added visible text. The AC still cited a "very thorough discussion", copied hqqu's two points almost word for word, and added the ImageNet guess.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A tested whether DPPN's lead survives other metrics and settings: AUSUC, input size, fine-tuning and aggregation. The rebuttal passed these tests, so A's acceptance rests on more evidence than B's rejection. Only A flagged C = 512 and the misnamed loss. Only B raised the λ inconsistency, the transductive TF-VAEGAN row, the role of g(·), newer backbones, batch size under K and ImageNet overlap. B's decision rests on the last three, and none of them bears on the reported comparison. Both committees saw the same core facts: an incremental step from APN, an unclear training description, weak SUN results and no conventional ZSL results. A treated these as text fixes. B's AC treated them as reasons to reject.
