**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. All eight reviewers called the trajectory memory, memory-based planning and dynamic consolidation novel, and the experiments broad. Most also found the method complicated and hard to follow. Both committees started at a mean of 5.5 (A: 5, 5, 6, 6, per its AC; B: 6, 6, 4, 6, from the edits). B ended higher, 6.5 against 5.75, because B saw evidence that A never saw. Three B reviewers pressed for model-based baselines, and Reviewer_G6K7 supplied code links. On 2021-08-23 the authors posted Dreamer-v2 and SimPLe comparisons on six Atari games to B's forum only, while A's discussion was still open. Reviewer_trSB and Reviewer_BrPf rose to 7 and cited these results. In A, Reviewer_m2e7 asked for the same baselines and got only a scope argument. m2e7 stayed at 6, and A's AC set the concern aside as "not shared by the other reviewers". The new numbers show parity, not superiority: Dreamer-v2's better run beats MBEC++ on three of six games, and MBEC++ wins the other three. No reviewer and neither AC argued for more than a poster.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 6, 5, 6). Reviewer_R2hp held at 5 for clarity. In 1.5 hours R2hp also misread three checkable points. The noisy transition hides the next state; it does not drop the action (L236-237). The paper states a limitation and the societal impact (L312-315). It points to the navigation results in the appendix (L302-303). Reviewer_bLjg wrote the most careful review and rose from 5 to 6 on promised clarity fixes. bLjg withheld a 7 because the hyperparameter heuristics lack support. Reviewer_m2e7 wrote a thin review at confidence 5, but its one concrete point holds: Table 1 compares only with DQN and episodic-control methods. Reviewer_Crem asked sound questions about stale memory keys and did not join the discussion. The AC summarised each reviewer and accepted on the "aggregate lean towards acceptance".

Committee B (7, 6, 6, 7). Reviewer_grEv found the most consequential flaw. Most Atari games ran once, and the per-game scores come from the best checkpoint; the authors confirmed this on 2021-08-18. grEv still rose from 4 to 6 on a promise to disclose it. grEv's pretrained-network worry was partly right. L199-200 uses a pretrained ResNet in the noisy maze, though the authors told grEv they used none. All maze baselines get the same vector, so that comparison stays fair. Reviewer_BrPf correctly noted that Section 2.2 omits MFEC and that MBEC alone runs only in the maze. Reviewer_G6K7 stayed at 6 and wanted model-based baselines on all games. The meta-review copies phrases from trSB and BrPf and names only complexity as a concern.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the number of hyperparameters (bLjg), a stronger model-free backbone such as Rainbow (m2e7), and stale keys over long Atari episodes (Crem). bLjg also asked whether "10M frames" means 40M emulator frames. The authors said each timestep counts as one frame but did not say whether they skip frames, as the DQN setting they adopt does (L278). If they do, MBEC++ trained on 40M emulator frames, the budget Table 1 gives only to EMDQN. Nobody followed up. Only B raised single-seed, best-checkpoint Atari results (grEv), the pretrained feature network (grEv), whether the TR loss addresses stochastic rewards (grEv), MBEC alone outside the maze (BrPf), and balanced baseline tuning (G6K7). No B reviewer asked how the MBEC++ row of the new Dreamer-v2 table was produced. None of these issues changed either decision.
