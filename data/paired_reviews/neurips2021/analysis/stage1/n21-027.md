**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently, and the split sits with Committee B's area chair. Mean ratings were 6.5 in A and 6.25 in B; seven of eight reviewers rated 6 or above. Both committees named the same weaknesses: an incremental extension of Wan et al. (2021), no stated reason for the average-reward formulation, one small tabular domain, and no discounted comparison. The submission confirms them. Its introduction gives no motivation (the camera-ready adds a paragraph), and all experiments use one Four-Room grid. The authors sent near-identical rebuttals that promised text, not experiments. No rating moved in either committee. A's chair followed four positive scores. B's chair called the paper "strictly borderline" and rejected it for the reasons its one confidence-5 reviewer gave. No reviewer found a technical error beyond typos, so the outcome turned only on whether a theory-first tabular extension clears the bar.

**Committee A (accept).** Ratings 6, 8, 6, 6; confidences 4, 4, 3, 4; no review edited. Reviewer_TT1t gave the 8 with eleven specific points. Three hold against the submission: the Eq. 17 summation, the undefined v_µ in Theorem 6, and a Fig. 2 caption that overclaims robustness (reward rate about 0.035 to 0.056 across α). Reviewer_Dp3F said the theory "directly and trivially" extends Wan et al. Reviewer_Pn3J called the experiments "rather weak", and Reviewer_ufJp asked why average reward is needed at all. All three stayed at 6. Pn3J's request for a primitive-action Differential Q-learning baseline was wrong: the O = A curve in Fig. 2 is that algorithm, though the paper does not say so. The four-sentence meta-review cites the unanimous scores and calls the work "novel", against Dp3F.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 7, 7; confidences 2, 5, 3, 4. Reviewer_EQtC held its 5 in an edit of 2021-09-16. It saw no clear novelty beyond Theorem 1 and called the refusal of new experiments "quite negative". That judgement holds, but three of its specific claims are false: Gosavi is cited at lines 33-35, other β values are referred to Appendix B.1, and Section 7 discusses limitations. The positive side was weak. Reviewer_cm1m rated 6 at confidence 2 and misread a loosely worded remark at line 138. Reviewer_WKn4's 7 is mostly generic praise at confidence 3. Reviewer_mBza's 7 rests on a five-sentence, mostly descriptive review and two critical limitations ("borrowed heavily", "very natural extension"); mBza never replied. Only here did the authors concede that the intra-option, planning and interruption parts are "more derivative in nature". The meta-review restates EQtC: weak motivation, incremental novelty, and no experiment that shows what average reward gives over discounted.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised the overclaimed Fig. 2 caption, the missing Gosavi learning curve, the undefined v_µ and the Eq. 17 error (Reviewer_TT1t, Reviewer_Pn3J), multi-time models and an Average Cost TD baseline (Pn3J), and whether options help only in exploration (TT1t). Only B raised:

- the special recurrent state as an artifact of Gosavi's proof, not of his algorithm (Reviewer_mBza);
- updates to all options versus consistent ones (EQtC), which the importance-sampling ratio in Eq. 13 already handles;
- the relative weight of the four contributions, which drew the authors' concession (EQtC);
- why options rather than primitive actions in real problems (Reviewer_cm1m);
- the expected-length remark (cm1m) and the line 290 index error (Reviewer_WKn4).

Both raised the incremental extension, the missing motivation, the single domain, the discounted comparison and the tabular restriction. A's reviewers accepted these as limits of a theory paper. B's chair treated them as grounds to reject.
