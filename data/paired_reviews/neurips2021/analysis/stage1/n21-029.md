**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster because they read the paper the same way. Before the rebuttal, their ratings were the same set: 5, 6, 6, 7, mean 6.0. Both credited the LP-duality link between δ* and the lower bound, and the optimal rates on complete bipartite and F_2^k graphs. Both discounted the log-factor gains, the tightness on special families only, the standard OSMD algorithm and the absence of experiments. The rebuttal is the only difference. The authors sent both committees nearly the same text. In A, two reviewers engaged and each rose one point (mean 6.5). In B, nobody moved: three reviewers never answered, and pb7N got the key argument late. Both chairs accepted a borderline-positive slate. The rebuttal changed A's numbers, not either outcome.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 7, 6, 7). Reviewer_3V7a wrote the most critical review. It said the algorithm is Exp3.G with a new exploration distribution and that BESTARMID (l.351) is undefined; both are true. It called the step from δ to δ* "a little bit incremental". The authors argued that δ* is a reasonable candidate for the right parameter. 3V7a agreed that finding the right parameter matters even when the gain is small. 3V7a also noted that one can take the better of the old and new lower bounds, and rose from 5 to 6. Reviewer_9yjH rose from 6 to 7 for the ad and video examples and because "the authors promise to add experiments". The camera-ready main text has no experiments and no application text. Reviewer_LkFC (7) read the supplement closely. Reviewer_DW8T (6) called the techniques standard and their combination new. The meta-review cites the score raises and names no weakness.

Committee B (7, 6, 6, 5). Reviewer_G8Mu's 7 is thin: "I like this paper", no proof checked, typo notes only, no reply. Reviewer_xh89 (confidence 2) asked for readability, experiments and applications. Reviewer_pb7N kept 6: the gains are logarithmic, and the general-graph gap "may be of order δ*/n", worse than before. The expression is inverted. Corollary 10 alone leaves a gap of (n log n/δ*)^(1/3), which can exceed the old O(log n). But Theorem 8 recovers the bound of [1], so the paper's best bounds are never worse. The authors' first reply skipped this concern. On 2021-08-22 they sent the argument that had moved 3V7a, after pb7N had kept the score. Reviewer_hwCL (5, one hour) found the significance low: the old gap was ln n, the new worst case at least ln n/ln ln n. The authors answered only with the ad and video examples that moved 9yjH; hwCL did not reply. The chair named the limits and accepted, calling the LP integrality-gap link "particularly compelling".

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the missing Exp3.G comparison, the undefined BESTARMID, the withdrawn claim that [35] is optimal, extension to adaptive settings (DW8T), and a disjointed proof at supplement l.741-750 (LkFC). Only B raised readability (results before the model, no figures), the general-graph gap of Corollary 10, the regime where Theorem 8 beats [1], and the restriction to weakly observable graphs. Line 281 gives only the part from log² n up; pb7N gave only the part below. Both raised the log-factor gains, the incomparable lower bounds, the LP's cost and the missing experiments. Neither noticed that Table 1 overstates the gain at δ = log² n. Theorem 8 gives Ω((log log n)^(1/3) T^(2/3)) there, not Ω(log log n · T^(2/3)). The remaining gap is thus log n/(log log n)^(1/3), not log n/log log n. Reviewer_9yjH and Reviewer_hwCL quoted the table's figure, and the camera-ready keeps it.
