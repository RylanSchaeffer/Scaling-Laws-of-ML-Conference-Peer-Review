**Verdict.** Both committees ended at a mean rating of 6.0. The split comes from who argued in the discussion and how each chair treated a paper that reviewers wanted rewritten. Committee A drew two engaged critics at 5. Reviewer_SfKb found the best results under-developed: the linear bound on consistency (Figs. 3-4) and the train-versus-validation forms of difficulty (Fig. 7). Reviewer_Cip6 agreed in substance. Both said they could not raise their scores without reviewing a revision, which NeurIPS 2021 did not allow. A's chair followed them. Committee B had one critic, Reviewer_UY6d, who still called the paper "important". B's two positive reviews are weak, and B's chair misdescribed the critic. The same borderline profile met a "re-review the rewrite" bar in A and a "fix it in the camera-ready" bar in B.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 8, 6. Cip6's original review discusses no result. Its novelty case quotes phenomena that the paper itself credits to prior work (L274-278). The rebuttal moved Cip6 to 5. SfKb did not move, and SfKb's points hold against the submission. It has no actionable experiment. The Sec. 3.3 intervention changes the loss, the batch size and the learning rate at once (L221-226), and the paper concedes "many unknowns". Reviewer_GF8b (6) also doubted Sec. 3.3 but found the paper "worth being published". The champion, Reviewer_nMGW (8), wrote a specific review but posted nothing after the rebuttal. The meta-review calls the paper "borderline" and asks for "an additional round of reviews". It closely tracks SfKb's comment of 2021-08-12.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 5, 7. UY6d spent 15 hours on the only critical review: overfitting, robustness of the k-NN support set, and dataset size. The rebuttal moved UY6d from 4 to 5. One argument misreads Fig. 5, whose test-accuracy drop comes from a run with 40% random labels. Reviewer_YcZq's 7 rests on a three-sentence main review, posted a month after the others. Its summary says low-depth examples have lower margins; the paper says larger (L43-44, Fig. 6). Reviewer_LUjh (6, confidence 3) claims that training-split depth is never analyzed; Sec. 4 and Fig. 7 analyze it. Neither positive reviewer joined the discussion. The meta-review says all reviewers found the paper "clear" and "none argue for rejection". In fact LUjh and UY6d found it hard to read, and UY6d asked for more experiments before publication.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- novelty and information gain of the correlational results (SfKb, Cip6);
- no actionable insight and no causal account of the unifying picture (SfKb);
- the confounded Sec. 3.3 intervention (SfKb, nMGW, GF8b);
- why k-NN rather than other probes (GF8b);
- an altered template and unreported correlation coefficients (nMGW).

Only B raised overfitting and memorization in the k-NN support set, the robustness of the depth definition, thin evidence for the visual sanity check, and small datasets (all UY6d). Both raised the dense presentation, the unexplained 2D histograms, and the dependence of depth on the training data. The camera-ready adds the rebuttal's probe result (25% to 98% on 100 selected examples), which no committee reviewed.
