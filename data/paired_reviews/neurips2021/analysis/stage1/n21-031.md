**Verdict.** Both committees recommended a poster. All eight final ratings are 6 to 8. No reviewer found an error in a theorem or an experiment. Both slates read the contribution the same way. Adaptive discretization by change-point detection removes the single-bandwidth limit of kernel methods. The toy example motivates it; simulations and the calibrated Warfarin study favour it over three kernel baselines. Both slates found the same weaknesses: an opaque dynamic program, an unclear cost of fitting many networks, and no discussion of local-bandwidth alternatives. The rebuttal answered these with promised edits. It raised one score in A (Reviewer_Ru8S) and three in B (Reviewer_fMSm, Reviewer_srXj, Reviewer_Dcr5). B's pre-rebuttal mean was at most 5.75, so B relied more on the rebuttal. A's top score, Reviewer_cawi's 8, rests on three sentences. Without it, A's mean is 6.33, below B's 6.5. Both meta-reviews restate the consensus and request the promised revisions. The committees saw the same facts and weighed them the same way.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 8, 6; mean 6.75. Reviewer_Ru8S (15 hours) wrote the most detailed review, asking how data splitting, the change-point list and the propensity model work. It correctly noted that the partition can only merge the m initial intervals (lines 162-164). After detailed answers, Ru8S raised to 7 but kept this "lingering concern". Reviewer_54Kf correctly noted that the simulations use only n = 50 to 300 (line 308) and that two related NeurIPS papers are not cited. The authors answered with runtimes up to n = 10,000 (2.86 hours). Reviewer_P63y (2 hours) is brief but apt: it asks whether computation is the price and suggests adaptive kernels or tree-based splits. Reviewer_cawi's 8, at confidence 4, says only that the results are "exciting" and equations 8 and 9 are hard to read. It skipped the discussion; the 8 is a vote, not evidence. The area chair singled out Ru8S and 54Kf.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 7, 6; mean 6.5. Reviewer_Dcr5 read Algorithm 1 in the supplement as needing O(m²) network fits. The authors conceded that the pseudocode was wrong and claimed an expected O(m) count from PELT pruning. Dcr5 ran the code and raised to 7; fMSm raised to 7 on the same answer. But the cited linear-cost result of Killick et al. assumes that the number of change points grows with the data. Theorem 1 fixes that number (line 247), so the conceded O(m²) worst case stays relevant. No reviewer noticed this. Reviewer_fMSm wrongly said J(D) is undefined; lines 145-146 define it. Reviewer_srXj raised to 6, but three of its criticisms do not hold. The method does not need piecewise-constant Q: Theorem 2 and Scenarios 3 and 4 cover continuous Q, at a slower rate. Lines 335-338 state a limitation. SLOPE's Mountain Car and GridWorld are sequential tasks. Its point that γn replaces the bandwidth as a tuning parameter is right (line 179). Reviewer_8Ltc kept 6 and correctly noted that Theorem 2 has no lower bound.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked about large samples, the two uncited papers, sequential settings, one-way merging and propensity estimation. Only B found the pseudocode error and probed the theory: mismatched bias and variance rates, the missing lower bound, the Hölder class, and novelty over PELT and Farrell et al. Only B (srXj) doubted that discretization plus a standard doubly robust estimator is a new estimator. Both raised the opaque dynamic program, computation, local-bandwidth alternatives and the missing "+" in Scenario S2 (line 301). None of these issues lowered a score.
