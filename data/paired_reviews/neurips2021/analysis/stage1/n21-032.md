**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster from the same facts, weighed the same way. Six of seven reviewers gave 6, and no reviewer argued for rejection. Each committee had a reviewer who called the headline speed-up unfair: Reviewer_WTSt and Reviewer_9hgj in A, Reviewer_3zgJ in B. The submission claims 65x on AIDS (Table 3: E-SimGNN 9.672 s against Student-F 0.148 s). But only the student reads embeddings stored offline. The authors posted the same fix to both forums. Against offline-feature baselines, the factor is 17.5x on AIDS and 10.6x to 41.8x per pair on four datasets. In both forums, they also admitted that Table 3 set per-pair GENN-A* and Hungarian times beside whole-test-set times. No reviewer lowered a score, and the camera-ready abstract now says "more than 10x". Both ACs gave the same reason: distillation is new for graph similarity, and the experiments support it. Neither committee tested the accuracy claim. On AIDS, the teacher's mse (1.601) is worse than that of SimGNN (1.189), GraphSim (0.787) and GENN-A* (0.635). On LINUX, GraphSim's mse (0.058) beats the teacher's (0.163). Reviewers in both committees saw only the GENN-A* gap.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 6, 6, 6). Reviewer_WTSt derived the 65x from Table 3 and doubted the copied GENN-A* and Hungarian times. Its pointer was wrong, per the authors, but the doubt was right: the copied times were per pair. That error made GENN-A* look about as fast as SimGNN, so it worked against the authors. Reviewer_9hgj found two real equation errors: l.174, and Eq. 7, which gives the Huber loss three arguments. The camera-ready fixes both. Reviewer_Srnx asked for larger graphs and MCS, and guessed "a few hundreds of nodes" per graph; IMDB averages 13.66. Reviewer_zANd wrote a two-hour review, noted the GENN-A* gap, and never replied. Nobody changed a score: WTSt and 9hgj accepted the timing answer and kept 6. The AC opened with WTSt's summary sentence, set novelty aside, and called the results "significant".

Committee B (7, 6, 6). Reviewer_quwN's 7 restates the paper: "65x faster", "cutting-edge accuracy on most datasets", and better than GraphSim "with comparable computation time". Table 1 contradicts the GraphSim point on AIDS and LINUX mse, and the paper reports no GraphSim time. quwN raised only presentation points and skipped the discussion, so its 7 is weak evidence. Reviewer_wszK spent one hour but asked correct questions and found the blank checklist pointers. Reviewer_3zgJ's main objection was that the student sometimes beats the teacher, so the teacher may be unnecessary. Table 1 has one such cell in 20. Table 2 already shows that the student is worse without distillation (AIDS mse 1.77 against 1.546). 3zgJ edited its review on the day it wrote "leaning towards an acceptance". A rise from 5 to 6 fits, but the record does not state it. The AC's verdict echoes quwN's "solid contribution to this field".

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the equation errors and the cost of embedding new query graphs (9hgj), the doubtful GENN-A* times (WTSt), and the absence of MCS (Srnx). Only B raised the student-teacher question, the copied baselines and misuse for adversarial search (3zgJ), the incomplete checklist (wszK), and figure clarity and code release (quwN). Of these, only the student-teacher answer may have moved a score, 3zgJ's. The GENN-A* timing error reached both forums anyway, because the authors posted the same corrected table to each. Neither committee noticed that on AIDS the teacher leads only in tau and p@20. Those are the two columns where GENN-A*, GMN and GraphSim report nothing.
