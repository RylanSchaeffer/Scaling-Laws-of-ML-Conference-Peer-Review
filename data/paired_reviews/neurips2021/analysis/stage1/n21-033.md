**Verdict.** B's reviewers knew the single-agent offline RL literature and checked the baselines; A's reviewers did not. Three of B's four reviewers saw that the submission's D4RL CQL scores fell far below CQL's published ones. The authors conceded and swapped in copied numbers. B also tied the implicit constraint to EMaQ, BRAC and KL-control. A's reviewers, all at confidence 2 or 3, asked about derivations and notation and read the single-agent results as a strength (Reviewer_559w: "already impressive"). All eight reviewers raised their scores, so the rebuttal did not close the gap: four 7s against 6, 6, 5, 5. The ACs then weighted the same rebuttal facts differently. A's AC called the new D4RL table "substantial additional empirical evidence" and made the consensus a spotlight. B's AC followed Reviewer_5dG2, who read that table as mixed, and wanted another review round. On the single-agent evidence the record supports B. Neither committee refuted the StarCraft II gains, the core result. The PDF is the camera-ready (built 2021-11-01); the submitted version is missing.

**Committee A (spotlight).** Ratings 7, 7, 7, 7, all raised after the rebuttal (Reviewer_uost: "from 6 to 7"). The reviews are careful but narrow: the batch-softmax partition function, the KL derivation, the ε_b term, notation. Reviewer_uost was the sharpest. It caught that the rebuttal's "we have fine-tuned" contradicted the paper, and got a sweep: the default baseline settings were the best of five values. Reviewer_P8yc's 7, at confidence 2, states no reason beyond "seem to gain improved performance". The authors told Reviewer_icWA that ICQ "maybe has a limited performance with randomly sampled data"; icWA filed it as a limitation. The AC praised "strong theoretical insight", but every reviewer's confidence label says the math was not carefully checked. The table A credited gives higher ICQ means than the table posted to B in 7 of the 8 rows where they differ, with identical standard deviations. The camera-ready gives a third version (walker2d-medium ±0.7 became ±10.7). Neither committee could see this.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 5, 6, all raised after the rebuttal. Reviewer_xr9t's CQL point was right, but its 6 is thin: 2 hours at confidence 5, then a one-line raise after the authors copied CQL's numbers instead of re-running it as asked. Reviewer_5dG2 is the strongest evidence. In the rebuttal's own table ICQ trails CQL on all six MuJoCo random and medium-replay tasks (halfcheetah-random 2.2 vs 35.4). The camera-ready Table 1 drops those six rows. 5dG2's "only does well" with a good behaviour policy overstates it: ICQ leads on AntMaze medium and large. Reviewer_Rr7B, who knew the ICML reviews, argued the method is single-agent at heart. The authors then offered to retitle and move the multi-agent part to the appendix. The AC judged the promised edits large enough to need another review.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B caught the under-run CQL baseline, which undercuts the single-agent results A praised. Only B counted closeness to EMaQ, BRAC and KL-control against novelty; A's icWA saw the AWR resemblance but still called the critic update novel. The camera-ready does not cite EMaQ, despite a promise to B. Only B asked for a DOP baseline and flagged importance-sampling fragility. Only A probed the batch-softmax approximation of Z, the dropped entropy term and StarCraft's determinism; the authors answered all three. Both saw behaviour cloning beat every other baseline in StarCraft II. P8yc asked whether that makes BC the default; Rr7B read it as a sign of weak reproductions. Both heard about the poor-data weakness; only B counted it against the paper.
