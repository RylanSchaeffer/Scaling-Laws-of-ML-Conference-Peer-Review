## Verdict

The two committees saw almost the same ratings; the area chairs made the difference. Committee A ended at 5/7/6 (mean 6.0) after each reviewer added one point in the rebuttal. Committee B stayed at 6/7/6 (mean 6.33); no B reviewer replied to the rebuttal or edited a review after it. No reviewer on either side rated above 7 or asked for a highlight. A's area chair carried Reviewer_7cFv's two unresolved points into the meta-review: the link to the original network "remains speculative", and "the experimental results could be stronger". That gave a poster. B's area chair paraphrased the abstract, gave topical interest as the only reason ("a hot research topic", "will interest many readers") and dropped every weakness B's reviewers had listed. That gave a spotlight. Mostly, the same facts were weighted differently. A also tested the results harder, and its main objection holds.

## Committees

Committee A was critical and engaged. Reviewer_7cFv (confidence 5) made specific objections that the paper confirms. In the robustness table (Table 3 of the camera-ready PDF on file, Table 1 in the submission), the clustering loss raises clean accuracy by 1.23 and 1.26 points but AutoAttack accuracy by only 0.63 and 1.31. So the robust gain is no larger than the clean gain. Algorithm 2 needs a fully trained robust model before it retrains from scratch, yet Section 4.2.1 calls the time cost "negligible" from per-epoch figures. Reviewer_cKPT found the AlexNet R+C anomaly in Table 2 (90.68 against 85.50 coarse accuracy, fine accuracy flat) and the fine-tuning ceiling (ResNet-18: 94.25, 94.40, 94.75). Reviewer_92hs gave the 7, but its pros copy the paper's contribution bullets almost word for word, and it says limitations are "discussed well" where the other two say they are absent. All three raised their scores after new TRADES and FAT results and clarifications. 7cFv kept two concerns, and the meta-review repeats them.

Committee B's reviews were thin. Reviewer_HkhD gave the 7 after two hours, with generic praise, three short questions and the remark that the gains are "moderate". Reviewer_aQh1 (three hours, confidence 5) asked one good question, whether the hierarchy or any clustering regularizer drives the gain, but wrongly said the observations rest on CIFAR-10 alone. Reviewer_psEt was the most careful: it caught a label/figure mismatch and noted the undiscussed linear approximation and the missing TRADES baseline. The authors answered aQh1 with a ClusTR comparison and a wrong-hierarchy ablation (PGD-20 45.36 against 47.78). Nobody replied. The meta-review says the authors "illustrated how to further boost adversarial robustness", while aQh1 had written "The robustness improvement is limited".

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A raised the clean-accuracy confound, the hidden cost of pre-training plus retraining, the lack of state-of-the-art comparisons and seed variance, the two-way animal/non-animal setting, the domain-adaptation design (source accuracy reported, no fine-level accuracy, near-ceiling fine-tuning, the AlexNet anomaly), and weak writing, which B's psEt called "easy to follow". Only B raised whether the class hierarchy or the regularizer drives the gain, whether a binary C_op scales to complex hierarchies such as ImageNet, the line-158 label mismatch, and the direction of cause in the attack confusion matrix. Both saw the linearization gap, the small gains, the missing TRADES baseline, retraining from scratch, and unclear Figures 5 and 6. A's extra issues are mostly correct and weaken the evidence. B's extra issues are clarifications that the authors answered. None of B's points supports a spotlight.
