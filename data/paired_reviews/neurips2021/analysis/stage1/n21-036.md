**Verdict.** The two committees weighted the same facts differently. Both saw a method built from ITLM and FairBatch, robustness tested only against label flipping, logistic regression on tabular data, and a 6 to 10 point accuracy gap to FR-Train on COMPAS. A's most detailed reviews asked about the fairness half: Eq. 2's group caps, the fairness baselines, fairness under noise. A's reviewers called the combination clever and the problem new, and all four gave 6. Three of B's reviewers probed the robustness half, the weaker one, and B called the method incremental and narrow. Reviewer_Dqdb, B's only reviewer above threshold before the discussion, dropped to 5 after reading the others. No A reviewer posted a comment. B's AC says its private discussion followed Reviewer_1QFb. No reviewer found an error that sinks the paper. The means, 6.0 and 4.5, straddle the threshold, so the split reflects which four reviewers each committee drew.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 6: no champion and no detractor. Reviewer_pcux (7 hours) asked why Eq. 2 caps group sizes instead of setting floors. It also saw that the baselines' trade-off parameters are missing; the main text gives only the step size. Reviewer_5Ear listed five uncited papers on fairness under noisy labels or attributes, and none is in the references. Reviewer_GddM saw that FR-Train with clean validation data is more accurate (on COMPAS, .620 against .521) but kept a 6. Reviewer_75jQ called the problem "pretty narrow" and said the paper has one real dataset; Section 5 also uses AdultCensus, with results in the supplement. Its only edit says the rebuttal convinced it the problem is of wider interest. The AC restated the "unanimous" appreciation and did not mention the narrow scope or the COMPAS gap.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 4, 4, 5. Reviewer_Dqdb read closely: it caught that Eq. 6 takes the sign of an absolute value, so the update can only lower lambda. Its later drop cites "others' comments", not new evidence. Reviewer_ifBb (confidence 5) correctly noted label-flip-only robustness, no convergence guarantee and two uncited fair-and-robust papers. Both papers address distribution shift, so they are context, not missing baselines. Reviewer_1QFb drove the discussion. Its main question was why not filter noise first and then train fairly. The paper already tests ITLM->FB and ITLM->Penalty, which are usually worse in Tables 1 and 2, and 1QFb did not mention them. Its claim that the method beats all baselines in accuracy is wrong for COMPAS. Its follow-up, asking for stronger filters such as L2R and CRUST, is fair and got only predictions. Reviewer_ERAJ (1.5 hours) found the FR-Train results mixed. The authors' trade-off table used only synthetic data, where they already won, and ERAJ did not reply. The AC's scope and novelty reasons echo 1QFb, ifBb and Dqdb.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B weighed the robustness scope and called the method a straightforward application of ITLM. Only B asked for convergence theory, deep or multi-class models, and cascades with stronger noise filters. Only A asked about the upper-bound constraints, the baselines' hyperparameters and the runtime against FR-Train. A's missing references are on fairness under noise; B's are on distribution shift. Neither committee noticed that on COMPAS the method's accuracy (.521 to .545, Table 2) sits at the roughly .53 majority-class rate implied by Figure 1a. A constant predictor would match it with zero disparity. So the real-data case for robustness is weaker than either committee said. That fact supports B's doubt about robustness better than B's own arguments do.
