**Verdict.** Committee A rejected on an issue that nobody in Committee B raised: treatment advice drawn from confounded observational data. Reviewer_fnTo (confidence 5) objected that ICU treatments depend on patient state, but the model treats them as exogenous and states no causal assumptions. The objection overreaches on the experiments, which test only factual predictions under the treatments the patients received. It holds for the framing. Section 3.5 (lines 185-186) promises help to "design the best treatment plan", and Figure 1C draws no arrow into the treatment node. The Patient A case argues against starting dexamethasone from an unvalidated latent curve. A's area chair (AC) quoted Section 3.5 and asked for "significant reframing". In B, Reviewer_Cu63 called the same section "well formed". The reviewer draw also differed. A's only champion, Reviewer_DE5k (8), gave no technical assessment. Two confidence-5 detractors and a 5 put A's median at 4.5. B had three reviewers at 6 to 7, each with specific technical questions. The authors also gave B more evidence: a misspecified-ODE control for Reviewer_62Te, which lost about 0.2 RMSE to LHM. A's Reviewer_V3vo asked the same question and got only an argument. No score moved in either committee, and each AC followed its majority. This is not the same facts weighted differently: the decisive issue appeared only in A.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 8, 5, 4, 4 (mean 5.25). Reviewer_DE5k's 8 (2 hours, confidence 3) praises the work in general terms and asks only for a new section order and a limitations paragraph. Reviewer_TPwF (2 hours, confidence 5) misread Eq. 6, which conditions on z(0) (line 111). Its other points hold: one disease, one drug and one expert model (lines 226, 350), and Time LSTM is the only non-ODE baseline. Reviewer_V3vo found the work "promising but incomplete". It wrongly said that the real data had single doses; Figure 4 shows repeated doses. Reviewer_fnTo wrote the most specific review. The authors replied that LHM is not a causal-inference method and that the term does not appear in the main text. Line 214 uses it. Only V3vo answered, with one line. The AC took fnTo's objection and checked it against Section 3.5.

**Committee B (accept).** Ratings 4, 6, 7, 6 (mean 5.75). Reviewer_yPRn (4, 6 hours) made a principled case. Nothing in the ELBO (Eq. 8) stops the decoder from ignoring the expert variables. Section 3.3 says that independent priors "guarantee" no duplication, but that constrains only the prior. yPRn also argued that APHYNITY and the physics-integrated VAE of Takeishi and Kalousis subsume LHM. yPRn did not answer the authors' second reply, yet the AC called the concern addressed. Reviewer_Cu63 (7, 10 hours) agreed that novelty is narrow but kept 7. Reviewer_62Te (6) pressed twice on the source of the gain. The new experiment "helps", but 62Te wanted it on real data before raising the score. Reviewer_59iy (6) asked clarifying questions and did not reply.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised confounding by indication, informative measurement times and unverifiable treatment claims (fnTo). Only A objected that a COVID-only study cannot support a general disease-progression claim (TPwF). Only B asked whether the model uses its expert variables at all and whether the simulation recovers θe (yPRn). Only B asked for an M=0 condition, absent from Figure 3, and for comparison with Hussain et al., Linial et al. and Miller et al. (62Te, Cu63). Both committees noted the single dataset and modest novelty. Both asked whether the expert ODE drives the gain, flagged APHYNITY as missing and questioned the inferred expert variables.
