**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently, and B's AC broke a borderline tie toward reject. Without the thin and the non-expert reviews, both committees sat at 6 to 7: A's YPjs and wuG4 at 7 and 6, B's Hm8b and PEdv at 6. A's other two 7s rest on a one-hour review with no weakness (7UgD) and a review with one concrete point (bP1K). B's two 5s come from reviewers who said they do not know BO. Both committees saw the same weaknesses. The privacy proof "follows directly" from Abadi et al. (lines 241-242). Theorem 1 charges a worst-case cost for the other agents, so it shows no gain over local BO (lines 259-264). A's reviewers accepted early-stage acceleration as the contribution. B's AC called the novelty limited and the significance unclear. The same day, the authors showed A a heterogeneity test and told B it was future work. No reviewer found an error that survived the rebuttal.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 7, 6. Reviewer_YPjs (5 hours) wrote the most careful review. It asked about the threat model, the weighted average, and local BO when objectives differ. The authors added a test: FTS-DE beats local TS at α = 0.7, loses at α = 1, and nearly ties when 1−p_t decays as 1/t⁴. YPjs raised to 7. Reviewer_wuG4 (confidence 4) correctly noted that Theorem 1 needs 1−p_t = O(1/t²), but the experiments use 1/√t and 1/t (footnote 11). The authors gave a 1/t² run (error 0.068 against 0.085 for TS) and a 200-iteration run. wuG4 called the latter "very impressive" and raised to 6. Reviewer_7UgD (one hour) wrote "No major weaknesses. This is a strong submission." Reviewer_bP1K raised the privacy cost of tuning extra hyperparameters and never replied. The AC's two sentences cite the scores and repeat 7UgD's "strong submission".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 6, 5, 5. Reviewer_Tavh ("I do not know much about Bayesian Optimization") claimed that shared random features break privacy. The claim is wrong: the features come from RFF [30] and use no client data. Tavh conceded, rose to 5, and kept the novelty objection. Reviewer_PjxP (confidence 2) "totally gave up" on the FBO setting. It rose from 4 to 5, since writing "is not a big problem for the expert". Its valid point is thin related work on FL with DP: one sentence, none of its four papers. Reviewer_Hm8b correctly noted that the bound ignores the federated benefit, yet kept 6: "the results are good". Reviewer_PEdv correctly flagged privacy loss up to 20 (Fig. 2c) and never replied. The AC wrote that "several reviewers" were unsure of Theorem 1. In public only Hm8b was, and PjxP praised the theorems' "clear messages". The AC's own complaint about the introduction has a basis: lines 17-32 state the problem; lines 33-82 describe algorithms.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the 1−p_t gap between theory and experiments, the 60-iteration horizon, and the threat model. The rebuttal answered all three. Its 200-iteration table shows the gain shrinking: 0.032 error for TS, 0.028-0.029 for DP-FTS-DE. Only B raised privacy loss up to 20, related work on FL with DP, and higher-dimensional tasks. Hm8b asked for deep-learning tuning, but Section 5.2 already tunes a CNN on EMNIST. Only B's AC faulted the introduction; A's YPjs raised similar setting gaps (C1, C5, Q4) and still rated 7. B never saw A's heterogeneity test. Its α = 1 result, a loss to local TS, bears on B's doubt about gains over local BO.
