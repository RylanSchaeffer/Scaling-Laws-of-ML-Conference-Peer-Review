**Verdict.** Both committees averaged 5.75, so the split comes from the area chairs (ACs), not the scores. B's reviews leaned more positive (6, 6, 7 against one 4), yet B's AC rejected. The AC made Reviewer_bU1a's practicality point the primary concern. It restated that point as "the method requires experts to express all pairwise relations". The paper contradicts this. Experiments use 6000 random pairs, which lines 225-227 call a small fraction of the O(N²) total. bU1a had conceded as much. The AC also called the data sets "very standard"; that ignores the heart-echo and UTKFace experiments. A's reviews split 2-2, and its AC accepted. A had a careful, engaged champion; B's positive reviews were thin. One real issue sits under B's rejection, and both committees saw it. Every constraint is simulated from class labels (lines 222-223), so no experiment shows pairwise judgments doing what labels cannot. Reviewer_5L8k in A raised this and still rated 7. B's AC made it decisive. The authors answered both with the same crowdsourcing paragraph.
