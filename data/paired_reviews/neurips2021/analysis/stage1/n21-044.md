**Verdict.** B accepted because all four of its reviewers ended at 6 or 7 (mean 6.25), and its area chair (AC) followed them. A rejected because its panel split 2-2 (4, 5, 7, 7; mean 5.75), and its AC took the negative side. Both committees saw the same doubts. Reviewer_p3QD (A, 4) noted that the "class-wise" masks are per-instance outputs. Reviewer_6Ffx (B, 6) called fine-tuning "a stochastic version of edge dropout". The paper supports both readings: Eq. 3-5 compute each mask from the instance, and Section 3.2 says the selection "possibly acts as a dropout function". Both committees also heard that the method is PGExplainer per class plus a contrastive loss. B scored these doubts at 6; p3QD scored them at 4. A's AC granted the formulation "some merits", then rejected on unnamed "concerns in experiments". All four A reviews praise the experiments. The discrepancy is calibration plus an AC call at a split panel, not a flaw that only one committee found. Only the camera-ready PDF (built 2021-10-26) is available; its Table 2 is the reviewers' Table 1.

**Committee A (reject).** Each half holds one specific review and one thin one. Reviewer_p3QD (5 hours) found unclear notation and a "global" phase that still gives instance-specific masks. The authors confirmed that the masks are instance-specific. After the rebuttal, p3QD kept 4: asked for the formula of G_exp^(c), the authors gave only its name. One p3QD point is a misreading: Section 3.1 says the class superscript is omitted for brevity. Reviewer_c92W's 5 rests on two points, similarity to GEM and no time cost, while its text calls the paper correct, clear and effective. The authors answered both points; c92W never replied. Their table puts fine-tuning at 13-27 times PGExplainer's per-instance time, which they call "computationally comparable". Reviewer_edev's 7 is substantive; it found a sigmoid error in Eq. 5 that the authors conceded. Reviewer_LqNe's 7 is praise only and calls the method "well-explained". The meta-review copies p3QD's summary nearly word for word, but its stated reason matches no review.

**Committee B (poster).** Reviewer_AmNU, Reviewer_gLGL and Reviewer_TzAp called the novelty limited, yet ended at 6 or above for strong results on four datasets. TzAp rose from 5 to 6 after a PGExplainer baseline with the same parameter count; ReFine still led on every dataset. TzAp's doubt about fine-tuning rested on a misreading: ReFine-CT has no fine-tuning. The paper's text invites that misreading. Reviewer_6Ffx (8 hours) kept 6 and stayed unconvinced about local explainability. Reviewer_gLGL gave 7 at confidence 3 while calling the method "a combination of existing techniques" without theory, and did not engage. The AC followed the unanimous scores and passed 6Ffx's point to the camera-ready.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised unclear notation, the Eq. 5 error, missing related work (MAME, GEM, graph contrastive learning), time cost, and an ablation that swaps the phase order. Only B raised the parameter count of per-class attributors, the sampler's hyperparameters, node classification, and the lack of theory. Only B asked where the gains come from. 6Ffx is right that fine-tuning helps mainly on MNIST: +0.049 ACC-AUC there, +0.008 to +0.014 elsewhere. Between the two new parts, the contrastive loss adds more on three of four datasets. The per-class attributors give the largest share on Mutagenicity, VG-5 and BA-3motif.
