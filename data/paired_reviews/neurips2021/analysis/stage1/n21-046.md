**Verdict.** Committee A accepted on four 6s; Committee B rejected on 5, 3, 4, 4. Both saw the same main facts. The method's gains over the paper's own baselines are small: 0.4 to 2.5 points where it wins, with no error bars. It never wins at the smallest ε. Table 1 runs CROWN-IBP with β = 1 only. Section 7 shows the standard 1→0 schedule beats the method at CIFAR-10 8/255 (66.94 against 68.44 verified error). Table 2 shows COLT 8.4 points ahead at 2/255. The notation is dense, and the theory rests on an approximation (Eq. 5) and upper bounds. A read the paper as an analysis of an open question and judged these defects fixable. B judged the method against the state of the art and treated the writing as disqualifying. B's AC then took the weak numbers as a sign that the smoothness claim "remains shady"; A did not link the two. Two A reviewers raised their scores to 6 after a long exchange. In B, the two confidence-5 detractors never answered the rebuttal. B found one real theory flaw that A missed, but B's decision did not rest on it. The split is one of weighting, not of facts.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 6; no outlier. Reviewer_ySnr found real gaps in the proof of Theorem 1, and the authors conceded the missing positive-definiteness assumption. ySnr kept 6 for the "convincing claims". Reviewer_FkXz noted that the method trails CROWN-IBP 1→0 at 8/255, which Section 7 confirms. FkXz accepted the reply that 1→0 wins through smoothness, which supports the thesis, and raised to 6. Reviewer_Sz2Y raised to 6 on the smoothness-versus-tightness answer, though the bound's tightness still concerned it. Reviewer_kMq3 (confidence 5) pressed on notation and the small-ε results, and held 6. The AC praised the novel question and left the proof to the camera-ready.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 3, 4, 4; two at confidence 5. Reviewer_iUZ4 wrote the strongest review, and the paper confirms its checkable points. COLT leads by 8.4 points at 2/255, though COLT is certified with an exact MILP verifier (Table 2). Eq. 5 is only an approximation. Assumption 1 requires Lipschitz continuity, which CROWN-IBP's indicator slope (line 140) violates; the authors conceded this. Reviewer_fogS listed seven strengths and based its 4 on the writing. Reviewer_Bv3w gave 5 mainly for "marginal" 1–2 point gains, true per Table 1, and called the paper "well-written". Reviewer_fj3F gave 3 at confidence 5. Its main complaint, that the paper never says whether the landscape is over inputs or weights, is wrong (lines 107, 194, 220). fj3F and iUZ4 never replied to the rebuttal; fogS acknowledged it without change. The authors pasted one paragraph of selected deltas into four answers. The AC led with readability and "marginal or even no improvement".

Only B raised the COLT gap (iUZ4), an undefined "loss landscape" and no ℓ2 results (fj3F), no link from the theorems to certified accuracy (Bv3w), and a terse method description (fogS). Only A asked to separate smoothness from tightness and for a stronger IBP baseline (Sz2Y). The Lipschitz point is the one B-only issue that is both substantive and confirmed. It weakens Theorem 2's explanation, not the experiments in Figures 1 and 2.
