**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. All eight ratings are 6 or 7 (A: 6, 7, 6, 6; B: 7, 6, 6, 7). No reviewer argued for rejection or for a higher tier. Both committees saw the same facts. GradInit costs under 1% of training time and much less memory than MetaInit. It gives large first-epoch gains, but final accuracy rises only 0.1 to 0.8 points over the best CIFAR-10 baseline (Table 3). It also trains the Post-LN Transformer without warmup. The committees probed different weaknesses. In each committee, those weaknesses held ratings at 6 or 7 but did not lower them. Both meta-reviews restate the consensus. Neither committee caught a contradiction on tuning. Lines 236-237 say τ was chosen by best average final test accuracy. The authors told both committees that they chose it by first-epoch test accuracy.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 7, 6, 6) tested the method itself. Reviewer_vKtB wrote the strongest review. It noted that the SGD update at l.131-132 contradicts Eq. 2; the authors replied that the update is simply g, which concedes the point. It noted that Algorithm 1 enforces the constraint only heuristically. It also asked whether rescaling the BN scales explains the gains. The authors' new ablation says yes: with BN scales fixed, GradInit drops to Kaiming level (94.6 against 94.4 on VGG-19; 94.7 against 95.0 on ResNet-110). vKtB said this "reduces the significance" and kept 6. Some vKtB points were wrong: l.117-119 states the BN initialization, and He et al. do treat backward propagation. Reviewer_tN3S proposed two cheaper substitutes. New experiments showed that neither beats Kaiming, and tN3S raised the score to 6. Reviewer_DheR noted that one warmup epoch beats GradInit's first-epoch accuracy in three of five Table 3 models. The rebuttal skipped this point. The meta-review is two sentences of agreement.

Committee B (7, 6, 6, 7) tested breadth and effect size. Reviewer_QztT wrote the most argued 7. It caught that the reimplemented FixUp scores 75.7, below the published 76.0. GradInit's 75.8 beats only the reimplementation. QztT called test-set tuning "a little concerning" and kept 7. Reviewer_Ztx8 caught an overclaim: l.291-293 calls the acceleration "even more significant than FixUp" on gains of 1.2 and 0.1 points. Ztx8 also asked for DenseNet results that Appendix E already holds. Two reviews are thin. Reviewer_Qphs repeats its summary as its main review and gives 7 at confidence 4 after 2.5 hours. It reads Table 2 as showing no real time cost, but the penalty form takes 38 to 64% longer. Reviewer_AaUJ (confidence 2) says only VGG and ResNet were tested, which misses Appendix E. The AC asked the authors to tone down "architecture agnostic". The AC also doubted that warmup costs more than GradInit. The paper (l.318-322) compares only iteration counts, not time.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A asked whether BN rescaling drives the gains; the ablation confirmed it for BN networks. Only A tested cheaper substitutes and found the SGD-formula and constraint problems. Only A raised the warmup-epoch comparison and the mechanism of variance reduction (MSFd, DheR). Only B pressed the "architecture agnostic" claim (QztT, AaUJ, Ztx8, AC). Only B raised the FixUp gap and overclaim (QztT, Ztx8) and the missing BLEU gain over Admin (QztT). Only B's AC questioned the cost of warmup. Both committees got the same answer on test-set tuning: tN3S accepted it, and QztT objected but kept 7. None of these differences changed a tier, because each committee's critiques capped ratings at 6 or 7 instead of lowering them.
