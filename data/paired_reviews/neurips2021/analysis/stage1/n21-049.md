**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reason. In each, three of four reviewers gave 6 or 7, and the AC followed that majority. The committees saw the same facts and weighed them alike. The paper generalises butterfly matrices to non-square, non-power-of-two shapes and is clearly written. Its gains are small: 0.25 to 0.62 points over Butterfly on MNIST, and 0.38 on CIFAR-10 with 0.3M more parameters (Tables 5, 6). Adaptive Fastfood matches DeBut's accuracy with fewer parameters. ResNet-50 on ImageNet loses 1.5 to 1.7 points, against the abstract's "without compromising accuracy" (Table 4). Each committee had one dissenter, and neither dissent changed the outcome. The paths differed. No score in A moved. In B, rebuttal experiments raised two scores, so B ended higher (mean 6.25 against 5.75). Neither AC engaged with the open objections. The agreement rests on two thin majorities, not on a shared argument.

**Committees**

Committee A (4, 6, 6, 7). Reviewer_8jnC gave the only reject, for reasons the paper confirms. Three DeBut layers in LeNet cost 0.65 points from scratch; ResNet-50 loses 1.5. "Renders a new paradigm" and "where other fast linear transform schemes fail" overclaim; no competitor ran on ImageNet. After the rebuttal, 8jnC said Table 6 compares a CPU sklearn Fastfood with GPU butterflies. The authors' reply supports him: sklearn's Hadamard transform took 0.0001 s on an 8192-vector, theirs 13.4 s on GPU. Nobody answered him. Reviewer_Nvoh (6) asked sharp questions and kept the score, citing "the other positive reviews". Reviewer_aaRU (7) and Reviewer_fZAT (6) wrote short reviews and never replied; fZAT asked questions that l.180-191 and l.237-242 answer. The AC accepted in three sentences and asked only to tone down "sensational" language, 8jnC's word.

Committee B (5, 6, 7, 7). Reviewer_jjhd rose from 4 to 6, and Reviewer_X4Ja upgraded to 7. Both cited new experiments: Tucker-2, HRank plus DeBut, a binary ResNet-18. These answered B's requests for more baselines. A's objection, the size of the gain over Butterfly, is one that new baselines cannot answer. jjhd's closing request to "try large-scale datasets like ImageNet" ignores Table 4, which jjhd's own summary cites. Reviewer_tsAg gave 5 at confidence 5 in a generic review. Its claim that no experiment compares inference cost with butterfly methods is wrong; Table 6 does. tsAg never replied. Reviewer_tNGs (7, confidence 5) asked precise questions and called the Fastfood timing (l.261) unfair. The AC judged the work "more of the same" on "easy" datasets, though ImageNet is in the paper. The AC then followed reviews that "lean strongly toward acceptance"; at 5, 6, 7, 7, that overstates them.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised implicit-GEMM convolution (8jnC), mixed-radix FFT, channel counts that must factor to meet p/r = q/s, and the wrong claim at l.104 that DeBut replaces the circulant structure of convolution (Nvoh). Only B raised the undiscussed link to tensor-train compression (X4Ja), the memory of intermediate activations (tNGs), and the misleading name "deformable" (X4Ja, tNGs). Both committees asked for the dense network's runtime, but only A got it: 2.01 s inference against DeBut's 4.01 s. fZAT had suspected this; nobody followed up. The rebuttal's 94.11% for an 8x9-block chain on CONV13 exceeds the 93.91 ± 0.08 that Table 3 reports for the same layer, block and size; nobody asked. The camera-ready still says "without compromising accuracy".
