**Verdict.** The committees saw largely the same weaknesses and weighted them differently. Panel dynamics and the area chairs (ACs) did the rest. B's reviewers (7, 6, 7, all confidence 3) raised the two concerns that sank the paper in A. Reviewer_LCrq noted that the BP updates assume the parameters a and b are known. Reviewer_y2ff noted that the StSBM is a static SBM revealed in random order. Both treated these as minor, and B's AC restated the scores. In A (8, 6, 5, 5; mean 6.0), Reviewer_QSCc alone posted after the rebuttal. In two comments it held that estimation of a and b "needs to be included in the proof". The champion, Reviewer_N5WW (8), and Reviewer_WcZo (6) never replied. A's AC adopted QSCc's points and rejected. QSCc's core fact is right: the paper says its real-data estimates of a and b "cannot be implemented in practice" (lines 337-338). Whether that disqualifies a theory paper is a judgement. The authors note that the offline results they build on also assume known parameters. N5WW called the Appendix A sensitivity study "particularly interesting". No reviewer in either committee found an error in a theorem.

**Committee A (reject).** No review was edited after posting. N5WW wrote the most technically informed review: five hours, confidence 4, and a reading of Appendix G. Its minor points check out. Ê is undefined, and StreamBP's lead over offline BP goes unexplained. WcZo, a repeat reviewer, said the previous round's criticisms were still unaddressed. Its specific points are correct. The paper has no conclusion; it ends at line 347. Lines 69-71 say StreamBP is not expected to beat offline BP, yet it does at small R in every panel of Figure 2. On Polblogs, StreamBP drops below the side-information baseline (Figure 4c). QSCc (confidence 3, three hours) misread several details. The neighbourhoods are asymmetric by design (lines 142-143). Eq. 5 covers k groups, not two. Corollary 2 proves optimality in three regimes. Reviewer_Y4Hw (5) opens by repeating its summary and asks for GNN baselines. It never replied. The AC called the writing "suboptimal", "as is evident from multiple reviews". Yet N5WW, WcZo and Y4Hw all praised the clarity of the main sections.

**Committee B (poster).** LCrq's edit predates the rebuttal. The support is thin. LCrq says it is "not an expert" and cannot vouch for novelty. Reviewer_YnVr (7, three hours) calls B_R^t undefined, but line 114 defines it. It asks about recovery up to permutation without side information. Eq. 2 and Corollary 1 already answer that. YnVr accepted the rebuttal on novelty relative to [MX16]. y2ff (6) misread Eq. 2 but correctly found the arrival order independent of the graph. It did not reply. The AC's meta-review is two sentences, starting "Each reviewer has a positive opinion".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the missing conclusion and StreamBP's unexplained lead over offline BP. Only A raised the temporal-network literature, GNN baselines, missing code and an earlier round's unaddressed criticism. Only B raised novelty relative to [MX16], the non-standard use of "streaming", and a growing number of communities. Both raised known a and b, the arrival model, side information, the two-group theory and SBM fit to real graphs. Both saw StreamBP's erratic behaviour.
