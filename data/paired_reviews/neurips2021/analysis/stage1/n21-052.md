**Verdict.** The reviewers did not cause the split; the area chairs (ACs) did. Both committees scored the paper a borderline accept: A 7, 6, 6, 5 (mean 6.0); B 6, 6, 6, 7 (mean 6.25). Both saw the same weaknesses: typos in the Lemma 1 and 2 proofs, an offline full-coverage setting, and a forward objective that keeps task-irrelevant information. In each committee, a reviewer who doubted the proofs accepted the rebuttal. A's AC then added a critique that no A reviewer made: 5 seeds, default hyperparameters, unclear bottleneck use. Three A reviewers had praised the experiments. The AC also wrote that Proposition 1 "seems to assume that the identity transformation is analyzed". That reading is wrong. The paper calls an objective sufficient only if every maximizer is sufficient (lines 146-148), and maximizers can discard noise (lines 188-189). The AC still wrote that the paper "could be accepted" if there was room, and A rejected. B's AC accepted in two sentences, on the scores. The committees weighed the same facts differently.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_jykz (7, ten hours) wrote the most careful review, but its main objection fails. It said the aliased map in Proposition 2 cannot maximize J_state because the maximum is ln 3. By the data-processing inequality, no representation exceeds I(S';S) = ln 2 in the Figure 2 MDP. The identity and the aliased map both reach ln 2 (Figure 2, right). The authors showed this; jykz did not reply. Reviewer_mtq5 (6) objected to the decoupled setting and to the word "sufficient". Reviewer_UFXP (6) found typos in Lemma 2 and in the Lemma 1 Markov chain, which the authors conceded. UFXP had promised to raise its score. It wrote "The proofs now make sense to me" and edited the review that day. Reviewer_Vgsv (5, 3.5 hours) praised quality and clarity, asked for partial observability and online data, and never replied. No flaw supports its 5. The AC set aside the reviewers' main concern, the offline setting ("I do not share this concern"), and decided on its own concerns. The seed and hyperparameter points rest on Appendix A.2, which neither PDF contains. The main text never mentions the bottleneck, so "insufficiently explicit" holds.

**Committee B (poster).** Reviewer_nwZo (6, confidence 5) called Proposition 1 false, using contextual bandits with action-dependent reward. The paper excludes that case. Line 95 defines r: S → R, and footnote 1 restricts the analysis to state-only reward. nwZo was right that lines 100-102 write r(s_t, a_t) and that a footnote hides the assumption. nwZo then raised to "weak accept". Reviewer_Ncmr (6) asked whether the reversed arrow in Lemma 1 breaks the data-processing step. It does not, because the chain has no collider. Ncmr also called the experiments "rather artificial". Section 6.2 confirms this: each game is chosen to make one objective fail. Reviewer_qUv5 (6) accepted the rebuttal. Reviewer_w9XU (7) wrote four sentences and did not join the discussion. On 2021-07-31, before any author response, it called the Lemma 2 proof "now corrected". The AC wrote that "All the reviewers found the paper worthy of acceptance". A's AC could not write that, because of Vgsv's 5.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised experimental rigour (seeds, hyperparameters, bottleneck), and only its AC raised it. Only A raised partial observability, the "sufficient" versus "necessary" wording, and MI terms as auxiliary losses. Only B raised the hidden state-only reward assumption and the bias of InfoNCE estimates. Only B raised novelty against state-abstraction work (Li et al. 2006), relevance on standard benchmarks, and the failure of end-to-end RL with distractors.
