**Verdict.** The committees disagreed on novelty, not on facts. Before the rebuttal they differed by one reviewer: A stood at 5, 5, 7 and B at 5, 5, 5. Reviewer_2HKg (A, 7) called the method "a brand-new idea". No A reviewer doubted novelty. In B, Reviewer_Hqei and Reviewer_jtrd judged it a known reweighting scheme with a different divergence. The rebuttal added many experiments, and both committees accepted them. Experiments cannot answer a novelty objection. So Reviewer_KSRV in A, whose concerns were experimental, rose from 5 to 6. No B score moved. Reviewer_Xqv1 in B had called the instance-level weights "interesting and new". Its own concerns were experimental, and the rebuttal met them. It still kept 5, citing the other reviewers' novelty objection. The paper's related work favours B's reading. IWAN [46] and ETN [5] already pair reweighting with adversarial discriminators. Huang et al. [16] and Balaji et al. [2] already learn weights to align distributions (lines 55-69). Whether a Wasserstein critic for the weights is enough is judgment. The ACs also read the rebuttal oppositely. A's AC found the new experiments sufficient. B's AC took their volume as a sign that the paper needed major revision.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 5, 7. Reviewer_2HKg spent eight hours and replied after the rebuttal. Its reasons are specific: AR beats its own baseline on all five data sets (Tables 2-4). But 2HKg also wrote that AR is "not state-of-the-art", and "brand-new" ignores [46]. Reviewer_KSRV wrote a thin review; three of five bullets concern captions and typos. Its one substantive point was right: the paper never tests AR's own robustness to noisy weights. Reviewer_3wdy (two hours, confidence 5) wrote the sharpest review. The noise simulation does give all source-only classes one weight (lines 138-139). The Wasserstein choice is not ablated. The authors answered with four new tables, but 3wdy never replied. The AC judged the answers sufficient on its own reading.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 5. Hqei's checkable points hold. The paper calls the weights of [5, 46] heuristic, although those methods learn them; the authors conceded. The classifier is a spherical layer (lines 264-265). RTNet and DPDAN lack results on three data sets. Reviewer_jtrd (confidence 3) grounded its novelty doubt in a feeling: "making me feel like a combination". It was right that AR adds only 1.41 points over the baseline on DomainNet. Xqv1 was right that the paper compares with BAA, a weak variant of BA3US. Its claim that the hyperparameters look sensitive is wrong: Fig. 3(d) moves less than two points. The meta-review tracks Hqei's review closely. It restates fairness and margin concerns that no reviewer pressed after the rebuttal. It says AR does not "outperform existing methods by a large margin". With a linear classifier, AR still beats BA3US by 11.4 points on VisDA-2017 and 5.1 on DomainNet. The AC did not discuss those results.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the uniform noise simulation, the untested noise on the classification loss and the closeness assumption behind the weights (3wdy). Only A asked for a robustness test of AR, convergence evidence and SHOT. Only B called novelty a weakness. Only B raised the spherical classifier, the strong baseline and the unexplained "challenging" data sets. Only B raised the "heuristic" misstatement and the missing RTNet and DPDAN results. Both committees asked for full BA3US and a JS-divergence ablation. Per-sample weights cut both ways: 3wdy saw extra parameters, Xqv1 saw the novelty.
