**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reason. RLQP gives real speedups over OSQP's hand-tuned ρ rule on the benchmark classes, and it transfers in part to unseen problem sets. Both committees judged the novelty over Wei et al. 2020 [44] modest. Neither found an error. The agreement is solid in B and fragile in A. B ended at 6, 7, 7, and two reviewers raised their scores after the rebuttal. A ended at 7, 7, 1, 5 (mean 5.0), and no reviewer replied to the rebuttal. A's AC accepted on their own reading, as an RL application paper with "reasonable evidence" of generalisation, and set aside Reviewer_BuNG's 1 as "excessively harsh". That call is sound, because BuNG names no error. But A's two 7s are thin, so A's acceptance rests on the AC, not on the reviews. The ACs weighed the same facts the same way. Only reviewers differed sharply: the overlap with [44] drew a 1 from BuNG and a 6 from Reviewer_cZUC.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 7, 1, 5). Reviewer_Ktc7 wrote a short pro/con list. Reviewer_Kvzc spent 1 hour at confidence 2 and wrote "I am not an expert". BuNG gave "trivial or wrong" at confidence 5 and claimed 24 hours, but discusses no result. Its one checkable point, a difference from [44] "marginal, if any at all", is overstated. The submission cites [44] once, as RL tuning of an inverse-imaging ADMM solver (l.76-77). RLQP's per-constraint policy for general constrained QPs transfers across sizes and classes; [44] targets inverse imaging only. Reviewer_riVf (5) made two specific points. The QPLIB point is correct: in Table 1, vector RLQP loses to OSQP on 3 of 12 instances, 2 by timeout, and scalar RLQP times out on 7. The Sec. 5.3 point is a misreading: only the benchmark-trained policy loses there, and the SQP-trained vector policy wins (OSQP's shifted geometric mean 1.37). riVf did not reply. The AC's "not reasonable to expect ... a silver bullet" answers riVf only indirectly.

Committee B (6, 7, 7). Reviewer_THP1, an optimisation reader, wrote the most specific review in either committee (8 hours). THP1 rose from 5 to 7 after the replies, the policy visualisation and the missing script. cZUC challenged the novelty over [44] and the multi-agent framing. After the exchanges, cZUC saw that the vector policy is one R^6→R^1 network applied per constraint. cZUC kept the framing objection and rose to 6. Reviewer_nymS wrote a three-sentence main review plus useful clarity points. The meta-review copies THP1's summary: "clear gains within specific problem classes and some evidence of generalization".

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the QPLIB timeouts, the unreported variance over training seeds (both riVf), and the objection that RL moves tuning into a harder non-convex problem (BuNG). The seed point holds: the submission shows one policy per setting, and the rebuttal's pointer, Fig. 2 right, is a box plot over warm-start problems. Only B raised these issues:

- unstated tolerances and a Gurobi comparison that ignores solution precision (THP1);
- a multi-agent framing that adds nothing to a shared per-constraint network (cZUC);
- the missing training script, and an ablation in which the residual features add little (THP1);
- the runtime cost of the policy and of refactorisation, and embedded deployment (THP1, cZUC).

The submission states no tolerance. Its Fig. 2 shows Gurobi faster than vector RLQP on Huber, SVM and Portfolio, so the rebuttal's "strongly outperforms ... Gurobi" is too strong. The camera-ready gives 10^-5 for OSQP and RLQP but runs Gurobi "using default settings". None of these issues changed a decision.
