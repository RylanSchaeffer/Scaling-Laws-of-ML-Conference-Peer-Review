## Verdict

Committee B's spotlight came from its area chair, not its reviewers. B's ratings were 7, 6, 7, 7, 7, so no reviewer went above "Good paper, accept". The meta-review's "very strong contribution" and "the broader community" are the AC's own judgement. Committee A rated the paper 6, 6, 7, 7, and one 6 was a 5 before the rebuttal. A's reviewers found real faults in the paper's positioning, and the A AC called them "major presentation concerns". Neither committee found a fault in the theorems. The gap is AC calibration on near-equal, accept-level reviews, widened by positioning faults that only A's reviewers found.

## The two committees

Committee A (poster) had four reviewers who averaged 6 hours each. Reviewer_MXxs (6) said fairness is never defined and the related work is "not complete and not accurate". Both points hold in the camera-ready PDF. The objective is pure utility (eqs. 1 and 3), and Section 1 still groups a delayed-feedback paper with recharging bandits as "delayed action impact [56, 38]". Reviewer_oRvo went from 5 to 6. It then showed that Appendix B fixes the Lagrange multipliers; the authors conceded and called fairness constraints out of scope. Reviewer_i7rY (7, raised after the rebuttal) said Lemma 3.1 and the importance weighting are known and uncited. The authors conceded, and the camera-ready adds the citation and a sentence on the similarity. Reviewer_PRpe (7) showed that the negative result depends on a mixed optimum; the authors agreed. The AC's two sentences follow these reviews and give no reason to go above poster.

Committee B (spotlight) had five reviewers who averaged 4.4 hours each. They praised novelty and clarity. They asked how to set ε, s_a and ρ, why the benchmark is a fixed policy, and how general the impact function is. The authors answered or deferred each point, and no score moved. No B reviewer questioned the fairness framing or the related work. Reviewer_dTZk and Reviewer_8ugy named the policing example as a strength. Reviewer_8ugy's 7 is thin: generic praise ("very novel"), three questions, posted two weeks after the others, and no reply to the rebuttal. The AC promoted the paper on its own reading. It rated the missing lower-bound intuition and the probability-driven impact as "minor weaknesses".

## Issues one committee raised and the other did not

Only A raised these: the undefined fairness link and the loose Lagrangian extension; missing and mis-grouped related work; standard tools without attribution; experiments reported without explanation. Each is correct, by author concession or by the camera-ready text. None touches the results.

Only B raised these: how to set ε, s_a and ρ (ε hides the Lipschitz constant); the fixed rather than dynamic benchmark; importance-weight variance; the ε floor on every arm; the cost of the argmax over the ε-cover. The author reply on cost discussed the choice of ε, not the argmax. No score fell on any of these points.

Both committees raised the thin lower-bound intuition, the probability-driven impact, the restrictive impact function and the δ/γ typo. Both also saw that Lemma 3.1 and the importance weighting are standard, but weighted this in opposite directions. Reviewer_dTZk lists both as pros, and the B AC praises the negative result. Reviewer_i7rY lists both as known results without attribution.

The PDF in the dataset is the camera-ready version: it has the NeurIPS footer, author names and thanks to the reviewers. Claims about the submitted text, such as the δ/γ typo and the lower-bound paragraph, cannot be checked against it.
