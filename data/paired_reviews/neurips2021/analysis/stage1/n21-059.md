**Verdict.** Committee B rated the paper slightly higher than Committee A (mean 5.67 against 5.5) but rejected it. The area chairs (ACs) made the difference, not the scores. The gains are large: one image per class gives 63.4% on CIFAR-10, against 40.5% for KIP FC (Table 1). The algorithm (KIP and LS) comes from Nguyen et al. 2021; the kernel and the engineering are new. The submission has no method section. Section 2, "Setup", is background, and the distributed framework (contribution 2) is only in appendix §B.2. The firm detractor in each committee made this same point. Reviewer_HQy7 in A called the main contribution "an engineering novelty that is not given the center stage". Reviewer_AopF in B said the engineering "should be in the main text". AC A overrode HQy7. It called the gain significant, asked for the algorithm in the main text, and left ImageNet scale to future work. AC B adopted AopF's complaint almost word for word, cited missing large-scale results, and did not mention the gains. AopF kept its 5 after new continual-learning and intrinsic-dimension results, because "reviewers cannot see an updated manuscript". The camera-ready added a short §2.1 on the distributed workflow. The committees weighted the same facts differently. One AC saw a presentation flaw to fix at camera-ready; the other saw grounds to resubmit.

**Committee A (accept).** Ratings 5, 6, 5, 6, all at confidence 3. Reviewer_xee8 rose from 3 to 5 because it had replied late; it stayed "not convinced about Table 2 results". Its points hold. In Table 2, transfer on CIFAR-100 at ten images per class gives 28.3%, against 32.3% for DSA. But xee8 left novelty to the AC: "if the ACs ... do not think this is a concern, I would be okay with accepting". Reviewer_HQy7 (6-7 hours) said transfer misses prior art in half the settings; Table 2 confirms 7 of 14. The two 6s are weak support. Reviewer_Vxyf wrote four short points in 2 hours and did not reply. Reviewer_ET9m offered a higher score if the authors explained transfer, then did not reply. AC A relied on its own reading, with loose figures. Its "64.3% ... on CIFAR" is the SVHN entry (CIFAR-10 is 63.4%), and its 29% baseline ignores KIP FC's 40.5%.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 6, 5, all at confidence 3. Reviewer_AopF (7 hours) is the only reviewer below threshold, and its main claim is correct. Section 2 has two sub-heads that begin "Background on". Section 3 is results, and Related Work runs 13 lines. Its minor claim that initialization goes unreported is wrong: lines 106-107 state it. AopF also wanted continual-learning benchmarks and analyses tied to the method; the rebuttal's new tables did not move it. Reviewer_UNoV rose from 5 to 6 once the authors gave compute details. Reviewer_zgVX praised the writing but never joined the discussion. Neither 6 defended the paper in discussion, and the AC followed AopF.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A questioned the venue: xee8 suggested MLSys. Only A raised the transfer losses (7 of 14 settings, and CIFAR-100), NAS usefulness, the choice of λ and reproducibility under heavy compute. Only B faulted the structure beyond the missing method: Section 2 as background, a short related-work section, unexplained intrinsic dimension and Figure 4 checkpoints. Only B asked about continual learning, initialization, adversarial robustness and the compute details that checklist item 3(d) deferred to the appendix. Both committees noted the borrowed algorithm, the appendix-only framework, the small datasets, the missing CIFAR-100 50-image setting and transfer to other architectures.
