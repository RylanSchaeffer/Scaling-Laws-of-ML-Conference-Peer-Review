**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. All nine reviewers rated the paper accept: A gave 8, 7, 7, 7, 7 and B gave 7, 7, 7, 7. Each called the results significant or the technique new. With shared randomness, correlated sampling makes most users output one hypothesis. Then O(log(1/δ)/ε) users suffice for any Littlestone class, with near-matching lower bounds (Table 1). No reviewer found an error in a result. Every error found is a slip in a statement, and the submission confirms each one. Only B raised substantive limits of the setting, and its reviewers called them open problems and still gave 7. No score moved after the rebuttal. Nothing argued for a spotlight. The only rating above 7 is a three-sentence 8 at confidence 2, and both meta-reviews restate the consensus. The committees differ in how well their 7s are supported, not in outcome. Two B reviewers read the proofs closely; no A review shows such a reading.

**Committees**

Committee A (mean 7.2). Reviewer_kC68's 8 at confidence 2 rests on three sentences of praise, with no question or weakness. It says the authors "adequately discussed ... the societal impact", but the checklist marks that N/A. Without kC68, A's mean is 7.0, as in B. Reviewer_R7Bc (confidence 2) says nothing specific to this paper and concedes it could not check the proofs. Reviewer_R9c6, "outside of the NeurIPS community", wrote A's most useful review. It found that Theorem 3 names the local model where the central model is meant (l.74-77). It found that Section 5 states no limitation, though the checklist points there. Both claims are correct. Its claim that "SQ is not spelled out" is wrong: l.64 expands it. Reviewer_4gym (confidence 4) and Reviewer_xkiJ asked conceptual questions; xkiJ also caught the typo at l.35. The AC listed R9c6's and R7Bc's points as minor revisions.

Committee B (mean 7.0). Reviewer_wvJN wrote the most technical review in either committee. Its three corrections are all right: Lemma 13's brackets, "with probability β²/60" at l.283, and Theorem 25's stability parameter, which the proof needs as 1 − β/3. The camera-ready adopts all three. Reviewer_Wgf7 (confidence 4) made the strongest critique. The regime has few users with poly(d) samples each. That is enough for each user to learn alone: in Algorithm 2, each user runs an accurate learner on its own data. Federated learning usually has the reverse, with heterogeneous users. Wgf7 still gave 7; its only edit (2021-08-01) predates the rebuttal. Reviewer_5d6f asked for results at fixed m. The authors called both points open problems, and neither reviewer replied. Reviewer_ZiPC gave 7 at confidence 1 while "not really qualified to review this paper". It still caught the Theorem 3 slip and the undefined α at l.53. The AC restated the consensus.
