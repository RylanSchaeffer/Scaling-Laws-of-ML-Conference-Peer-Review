**Verdict.** The split comes from reviewer draw and from how each committee weighted the same facts. Both committees saw the same weakness: the method is a simple cut in input resolution, with a cycle schedule taken from multigrid training [51]. Committee A drew a high-confidence champion, Reviewer_krQv (8, confidence 5). Its review recast the simplicity as a fix for a limitation "simply accepted in hundreds (?) of TAL papers", and A's chair adopted that framing. After the rebuttal, A's two novelty critics rose to 6, though their texts still call the novelty limited. B's best rating was a 6 from a reviewer who then went silent. B's critics kept 5 after a rebuttal of the same substance. B's extra points (one TAL head, a sub-point gain over ACP+) are accurate, but they rest on tables that A also read. Both chairs followed their majorities: mean 6.75 in A, 5.33 in B.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 8, 7, 6. krQv wrote a specific review, and its points check out. The paper gives no reason why low-resolution training should transfer to full resolution (Sec. 3.2), and L179 has the typo "multri-grid". Reviewer_BMhR's 7 (confidence 2, 2 hours) lists no weakness and asks nothing; it adds a vote, not evidence. Reviewer_mQTb calls the originality "below the threshold" but rose to 6 after the rebuttal answered its questions on time and memory. Reviewer_6HSH rose to 6 "after reading the rebuttal and the other reviews", without the full-fidelity comparison it asked for. The paper itself answers 6HSH's worry about extra parameters and target data: ACP+ also fine-tunes the encoder on target data (L241-243), and LoFi beats it (Table 1). The rebuttal told 6HSH that LoFi is "5 times faster to converge". That is misleading: LoFi starts from the ACP model (L139-142), so its 15 hours add to ACP's 74.9. The meta-review restates krQv: the method challenges "a standard assumption".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 5, all at confidence 4. Reviewer_kXbC's only criticism is novelty ("seems trivial"). Its reply repeats this and engages none of the authors' arguments. Reviewer_g6Bi made checkable points, and most hold. The main paper tests only the G-TAD head; a BMN result sits in the supplement (L187-189), which g6Bi did not mention. The gain over ACP+ is 0.65 AmAP on ActivityNet, though 1.31 on HACS. The submission claims a "superior efficiency/accuracy trade-off" (L75-76) but reports no training time or per-video memory. g6Bi kept 5 after the rebuttal added a BSN result (+0.54 AUC) and time tables. Reviewer_tLdR (6) argued for acceptance with the same point as krQv: frozen encoders are "taken as granted". It never answered the rebuttal. The meta-review says consensus "slightly converged to the reject opinion", but no rating moved. Its two reasons map to g6Bi (more baselines) and kXbC (significance).

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- no argument for why low-resolution training transfers (krQv);
- scalability to large encoders and long videos (krQv, mQTb);
- whether the gain comes from the TAL loss or from target data and trained weights (6HSH);
- more configurations and more GPUs (mQTb).

Only B raised the number of TAL heads and the small gain over ACP+ (g6Bi). Only B raised the unequal backbone and modality against prior methods (tLdR). That point favours LoFi: with ResNet18 and no optical flow it still beats BC-GNN by 0.23 AmAP (Table 3). Both committees raised limited novelty, the debt to [51], the missing full-fidelity comparison, and the missing time and memory figures. A weighted the challenge to an accepted assumption; B weighted thin experiments and a sub-point gain.
