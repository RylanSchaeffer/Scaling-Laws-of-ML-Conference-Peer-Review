**Verdict.** Committee A recommended a spotlight; Committee B rejected. They did not disagree about the facts. Both found the theory correct. Both saw that the verbal claims reach beyond Theorem 4, that the lossless notion of optimality is narrow, and that the work leans on Dai and Wipf (2019). A treated these points as camera-ready fixes. B's area chair (AC) treated them as "a major revision". Yet B's final ratings were 6, 6, 6, 5, and Reviewer_ATkA called the remaining work "polishing" and would "not oppose accepting". So the split comes mainly from how two ACs priced the same overclaiming. Reviewer background amplified it: B's two most critical reviewers read the paper from variational-inference and information-bottleneck positions. Line numbers below refer to the submitted PDF.

**Committee A (spotlight).** Ratings 7, 7, 8, all with confidence 4; mean time 4.0 hours. The reviews praise the writing and find the work well distinguished from Dai and Wipf (2019). Reviewer_e4Tf found the real flaw: Theorem 4 reads as holding for every decoder class, but the proof gives only an existence result. The authors agreed, and e4Tf raised to 8. Reviewer_9TAc questioned Definition 1 against information-theoretic views and asked about non-Gaussian likelihoods, then accepted short answers. The AC asked why Eq. (6) uses one h for both terms. The authors said Theorem 4 extends to any reconstruction penalty g, and the AC wanted that in the camera-ready. The meta-review asks for "slightly more careful quantification to avoid overclaiming". It gives no reason for a spotlight beyond "a strong paper".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 6, 5, 6; mean time 2.9 hours. Reviewer_69AE gave a confidence-5 "firm no" after reading "until page 5". Two of its concrete claims are wrong. Definition 1 covers deterministic AEs such as PCA (lines 99-115), and the paper ties the divergence to manifold data, not to finite-sample overfitting. Its main objection is that zero reconstruction error forces a Dirac decoder and a diverging KL term. That is true, and the paper says so (lines 126-133); whether it matters for VAEs is a judgment. Reviewer_ATkA asked that the claims be limited to lossless, noise-free settings, and raised from 5 to 6 after two rounds. Reviewer_f5mJ raised to 6 without joining the discussion; 69AE ended at 5. The AC never posted. Its meta-review says "they were not convinced" about VAE applicability, but only 69AE held that view; ATkA wrote "In contrast to reviewer 69AE". The AC's request for a prior-work section is fair. Dai and Wipf (2019) is cited nine times and supplies key steps of Section 2, but no section compares the results.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B questioned exact reconstruction for a variational model. Only B noted that the smoothing claim rests on one unrepeated CelebA run, with no other method and no count of active dimensions; the checklist marks error bars "N/A". Only B raised aggregate posteriors as another route to sparsity, the initial value of γ, and the narrow VAE links: Lemma 2 needs an affine decoder, and Lemma 3 reaches Eq. (6) only after an added log transform. Only A found the quantifier gap in Theorem 4 and the 1/√γ growth of decoder gradients. Only A's AC questioned the word "sparse" and the long introduction. The camera-ready took fixes from both sides: explicit quantifiers in Theorem 4 from A; "lossless", a deterministic Definition 1 and a remark on lossy alternatives from B; a separate reconstruction penalty g from both. The rewrite was large, and the conclusion still says "there is no obvious alternative".
