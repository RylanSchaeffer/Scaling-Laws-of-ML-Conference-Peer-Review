**Verdict.** The two committees weighted the same facts differently. Both saw a sound but incremental theory paper. Both named the same weaknesses. The gain was expected from constant-step results for Q-learning. The paper has no experiments. Its rate is worse than vanilla Q-learning's by a factor of (1−γ)^-2. No reviewer on either side found an error in a theorem. Committee A turned these facts into four ratings of 6 and accepted. Committee B drew a split panel (4, 5, 7, 6; mean 5.5). Its area chair (AC) then made the missing experiments decisive. The authors promised the same experiments to both committees. That promise satisfied A's Reviewer_NuYj ("addressed all my concerns") but not B's AC.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 6; confidences 2, 5, 2, 3. Reviewer_cHVh (confidence 5, 10 hours) wrote the sharpest critique. It called the result "not too surprising" after Li et al. (2020). It said the analysis is adapted from Q-learning proofs. It noted the (1−γ)^-2 gap and the smaller step size. Theorem 2 confirms both: step size of order (1−γ)^6 ε^2, time of order (1−γ)^-7. Lines 341-343 concede the gap. After the rebuttal, cHVh was "not super convinced" and kept 6. Reviewer_fiWX and Reviewer_NuYj rated at confidence 2. fiWX wrote that it is "not an expert in the theory area". Reviewer_vEGk asked for a definition of F that Eq. (7) gives. So three of the four 6s carry little weight. The AC listed the concerns, then accepted because "most reviewers lean towards acceptance". It did not mention experiments, which cHVh and NuYj had requested. It credited better γ scaling. Lines 226-227 show that this gain is only a (ln(1/(1−γ)))^7 factor.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 5, 7, 6; confidences 5, 5, 4, 4. Each review took 2 to 3 hours. Reviewer_YcUb (4) made three correct but generic points: no new algorithm, tabular only, no experiments. The first two would rule out any tabular analysis of an existing algorithm, Xiong et al. (2020) included. Reviewer_Z175 (5) found the paper "very similar" to Xiong et al. Its review stops mid-sentence. It asks about "the SA in (9)", but Eq. (9) is an inequality in a proof. Z175 made one correct catch. Section 3.1 calls the noise i.i.d., but that section also covers Markovian sampling. The authors called this a typo. Z175 then "updated the score" but stayed "not convinced"; the wording implies a rise to 5. Reviewer_k2nn's 7 is two paragraphs with no weakness, so it is not evidence of quality. Reviewer_p1oe kept 6. The AC rejected a two-two split. It cited "lack of clarity", but three B reviewers called the paper clear or well written. Its main argument is a personal wish to see whether practical rates match the theory.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked what the analysis says about overestimation (fiWX, cHVh). The paper concedes that its analysis does not separate deterministic from random rewards (lines 343-344). Only A asked for a lower bound or a tightness argument. Only A found the main text not self-contained: Proposition 1, H_t and μ_t appear only in the appendix. Only B counted the tabular scope against the paper; A's vEGk raised function approximation only as future work. Only B noted missing related work; the submission does not cite Carvalho et al. (2020). Only B caught the i.i.d. misstatement, and B's AC did not cite it. None of the B-only issues is a flaw in a result.
