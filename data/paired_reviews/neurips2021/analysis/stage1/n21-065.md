**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently; one correct point, the missing i.i.d.-sample comparison, reached only B. Both found the main result new: one Lloyd step from a well-separated start gives W2² ≲ N^(-1/d) despite non-convexity. No reviewer found an error in a result. A had two invested champions. Reviewer_u2eX (9, 7 h) and Reviewer_nnEq (8, 10 h) checked the proofs, and u2eX caught real slips in them. A read the paper as an explanation of why a non-convex method works. B read it as an approximation method and asked what it buys. Reviewer_Xcrv showed that an N-sample from ρ already has a better expected rate. The paper never makes this comparison, and the authors conceded the point. B also noted that the paper never says how the power-cell barycenters are computed. Both committees saw the gap to the optimal N^(-2/d), the weak Theorem 7, the 2D-only experiments and the need for the density. A accepted these as future work. B's AC called them "important aspects that are not treated" and rejected split ratings (6, 7, 5, 5). No B reviewer rated above 7 or spent more than 4 h.

**Committee A (spotlight).** Ratings 9, 8, 6, 7; mean 7.5. u2eX's 9 rests on a close reading. It found that the Theorem 3 proof transports cells to y_i instead of b_i(Y), and that σm_σ does not tend to √(2π) in Appendix E. u2eX also doubted Theorem 7, whose multi-step rate is worse than the one-step rate. The authors kept Theorem 7 for its proof strategy, and u2eX accepted that promise. nnEq asked how the method works when only samples of ρ exist. The authors had "not studied" this, and nnEq stayed at 8. Reviewer_T7tc claimed confidence 5 but misread eq. (15): its ε^(1-d)/N term grows as ε shrinks, so the suspected typo does not exist. T7tc kept 6. Reviewer_gM3z (2 h) placed the bound on min F_N in the wrong remark and raised to 7 after the tightness reply. The AC wrote that all reviewers praised "strong theoretical and numerical contributions". T7tc had called the experiments "quite poor", and gM3z asked for d ≥ 3.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 7, 5, 5; mean 5.75. Reviewer_Xcrv (5) asked how the method compares with an i.i.d. N-sample, citing Fournier and Guillin. The submission never mentions this baseline. The authors replied that a random sample "achieves a better convergence rate (although only on average)". Xcrv then showed that lines 150-152 invoke experiments on the gradient flow that the paper neither contains nor cites. The authors did not answer. Reviewer_t3bd (6) asked how the barycenters are computed and at what cost; the paper is silent. t3bd kept 6, citing other reviewers' issues and "limited practical experiments". Reviewer_fudr's 7 is a light, positive review, and fudr never posted again. Reviewer_AWCx (5, confidence 2) restated the abstract. AWCx asked whether the algorithm is a contribution, but the paper credits [11] and [14] and claims only the analysis. The AC's three concerns are Xcrv's and t3bd's.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B compared the result with i.i.d. sampling. The point is correct, and the camera-ready still lacks the comparison. Only B asked about computational cost, constants exponential in d, and the uncited gradient-flow claim. Only A found the slips in the Theorem 3 proof and Appendix E. Only A asked about classical quantization, asymptotic optimality and k-means references. B's points question practical significance, not the theorems. A's points fix proofs without changing any result.
