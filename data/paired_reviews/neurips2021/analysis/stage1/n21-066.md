**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, and they weighed the same facts the same way. Six of seven reviewers credited a new problem: change point detection under a dynamic Huber contamination model. Reviewers in both committees also noted that the algorithm joins known parts (RUME and a scan). It covers univariate mean changes only. No reviewer found an error in a theorem. The confirmed errors are in presentation. Line 142 calls condition (5) the complement of Lemma 1's regime, and it is not. Assumption 1 allows ε up to 1/2, but Theorem 1 needs ε below 1/4, and the paper never says so. The means are close: A 6.67, B 6.5. Each committee had one thinly argued 8, so nobody made a case for a higher tier. B's only reject vote rested mainly on a misreading, and its author ignored the rebuttal. The committees differed in emphasis only. A's careful reviewers stressed known techniques and thin experiments and gave 6; no A reviewer mentioned the phase transition. B's careful reviewers credited the phase transition and gave 7.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 6, 8). Reviewer_pALD and Reviewer_6R4u wrote the detailed reviews, both at confidence 4. pALD found no new technique and asked for the breakdown point. Theorem 1 condition (ii) confirms that the guarantee stops below ε = 1/4. pALD also said that Lemma 2 contradicts the "same order" claim at l.358. That point is wrong in rate terms: for ε away from 1/2, ε changes only constants (l.145-152). 6R4u asked for timing results and an h-sensitivity study. Both are missing: every simulation fixes h = 170 (l.284). Reviewer_J2f5 gave 8 after 2 hours at confidence 3. J2f5 asked for a complexity analysis that l.212-215 already give. No reviewer answered the rebuttal. 6R4u edited the review on 2021-08-14 without comment. The meta-review has two generic sentences and addresses no concern.

Committee B (8, 4, 7, 7). Reviewer_aT7R gave 8 with praise only: no weakness, no question, math not checked. Reviewer_k3Ng gave 4. Its central objection is that a detector should signal the change points that the contamination creates. Assumption 1 defines the targets as changes in the inlier means, so this misreads the problem. k3Ng also asked for a source for log(n) that l.114-116 already cite. The rest is scope and style. Reviewer_x4pj made the sharpest points. The lemmas assume ε_i = ε, and (5) is not the complement of Lemma 1's regime. The hiding attack also uses the true change points. The authors conceded the first two points. They added two attacks that use no change-point knowledge. ARC was not the best method in either, but x4pj raised the score to 7. Reviewer_9vuk kept 7 but praised ARC for not needing ε; l.201-202 say it does. The AC called the phase transition "a sufficiently solid and complete contribution on its own". It deferred multivariate data and other contamination models.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the breakdown point, timing, sensitivity to h, ARC's weaker localisation, the missing supplementary cases, and quantitative real-data results. Only B raised whether an attacker can know the change points, the ε_i = ε restriction, the true σ and ε given to the algorithms in simulations, the (5) mismatch, and an empirical test of the phase transition. Both raised the univariate scope, other adversary models, median-of-means, and the benchmark of van den Burg and Williams [44]. The authors gave both committees nearly the same reply on the adversary model. None of these issues changed a tier. Neither committee noticed that the code is "proprietary" and unreleased (checklist 3a).
