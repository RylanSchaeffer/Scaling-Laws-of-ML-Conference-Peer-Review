**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, by different routes. In Committee A, all four reviewers ended at 7. Two reached 7 only after the rebuttal: Reviewer_9MQr was "satisfied with the response", and Reviewer_GxXa raised the score "to simplify the decision". The AC followed the scores. In Committee B, the reviewers split 6, 6, 5 and 4 (mean 5.25). The accept came from the AC's own reading, which judged the identifiability results a genuine contribution and the baselines suitable. The paper supports that call, because most specific objections in B's two reject reviews are wrong. Fig. 5 benchmarks five methods on four fMRI datasets, the noise is Gaussian (l.73-75), and l.394-399 state the train/test split. The discussion phase widened the gap: two scores rose in A, and one fell in B. The valid criticism that both committees raised, no IVA benchmark, changed neither decision. B's agreement rests on its AC, not on its scores.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 7, 7, 7) wrote specific, expert reviews. Reviewer_qGdn (confidence 5) questioned the noise-diversity assumption and called the fMRI gains marginal. The authors' own table confirms the second point: at 20 components, the ShICA-ML and MVICA intervals overlap on all four datasets. Reviewer_GxXa argued that a shared response is unrealistic in fMRI and that the paper dismisses IVA unfairly. The authors conceded both points and promised changes. GxXa also said that MultiViewICA [41] is not compared. It is compared, under the undefined acronym "MVICA" in Figs. 3-5. The meta-review restates the consensus and lists the promised changes.

Committee B (6, 5, 4, 6) wrote weaker reviews. Reviewer_v4RY wrote B's only substantive positive review and kept 6 after the rebuttal. Reviewer_6yar spent 30 minutes and called the paper "very excellent". Then 6yar lowered the score "after reading some other reviewer comments" and named no concern. Reviewer_vUQJ (4, confidence 5) wrote four short points, and two are wrong. Real-data experiments exist, and Theorem 5 shows that Multiset CCA fits the model from exact covariances. Reviewer_Mkd2 reported 40 hours but read the noise as non-Gaussian. Mkd2 also missed the stated split and the time-segment-matching accuracy in Appendix G.2. Neither reject reviewer answered the rebuttal. The AC set both reviews aside.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the noise-diversity assumption, the shared-response assumption in fMRI, the neuroimaging framing, the fixed and uncited super-Gaussian density, and the small, overlapping fMRI gains. B's AC instead cited the left-out-subject reconstruction as evidence of usefulness. Only B raised these issues:

- the relation to Bayesian CCA (v4RY), which the paper does not cite;
- metrics not comparable with Chen et al. 2015 (Mkd2);
- no per-method tuning of the number of components (Mkd2); Fig. 5 sweeps 5 to 50, but R2 still rises at 50;
- untested SRM extensions MN-SRM and RSRM (Mkd2);
- simulations with only 4 components and 5 subjects (vUQJ), which l.315-316 confirm.

Both committees asked for an IVA comparison. No figure includes IVA, yet l.57-58 claim that the experiments show ShICA "outperforms existing GroupICA and IVA methods". The camera-ready keeps the claim, and neither committee named it.
