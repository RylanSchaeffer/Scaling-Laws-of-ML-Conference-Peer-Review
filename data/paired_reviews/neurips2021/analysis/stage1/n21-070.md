**Verdict.** Both committees named the same gaps. BCD is tested only on point cloud completion. It is not shown as a training loss. Its evaluation is mixed with the authors' own completion network. Committee A rated 5, 5, 5, 5 and rejected; Committee B rated 6, 6, 5 and accepted. The facts were the same; the weighting and the rebuttals were not. In B, Reviewer_gwGK (6, confidence 5) called the loss gap the "main drawback" and still leaned to accept. In A, Reviewer_UpWV (5, confidence 5) made the same gap the reason to reject. In B the authors cited a user study in the supplement and argued that registration is the wrong test for a density-aware metric. Reviewer_p1L4 then raised to 6. In A the authors never cited the study. They conceded the scope point ("We will try it on more tasks") and offered a training-loss table whose gains they called "marginal". A's chair cited that table against the paper, though the submission never claims BCD as a loss.

**Committee A (reject).** No rating moved after the rebuttal. Reviewer_ziDa, a self-declared non-expert, wanted other applications and kept 5 because "the authors mostly agree with my assessments". Part of UpWV's case fails against the paper. UpWV said the base network was unknown, so the balanced design could not be judged. Sec. 5.3 (L287-288) names VRC, and the base row of Table 3 is VRC's row in Table 2. UpWV also said BCD "can not be used for training"; the rebuttal trained with it. UpWV's final comment ignored that table and called the change "trivial". UpWV's sampling point holds: in Table 3 the BCD gain comes from up-sampling; the query-frequency filter barely moves BCD and mainly lowers CD. Reviewer_b3Fh made the sharpest point: "balanced" assumes a uniform ground truth (L45-46). The authors conceded, and the camera-ready renames the metric "density-aware". Reviewer_1bxc's short review calls the work "technically sound" and "well-written" and gives no specific reason for its 5. The chair followed the four 5s.

**Committee B (poster).** Reviewer_gwGK read the supplement, asked exact questions on Eq. 5 and Eq. 7, and leaned to accept from the start. gwGK did not reply to the rebuttal. Reviewer_p1L4's posted review carried the summary of another paper. It also missed the user study and the limitations in the supplement, though the main text never mentions the study. After the rebuttal, p1L4 accepted the study, excused the missing comparison to Nguyen et al. as a pre-print, and trusted a promised rewrite of Sec. 5.1. p1L4 raised to 6 and cited gwGK. p1L4 still wanted "a thorough evaluation ... across different datasets and tasks", which is A's reason to reject. Reviewer_9BXU (5) misread how n_x and n_y are set: L142 defines them by nearest-neighbour queries, with no radius. 9BXU did not reply. The three-sentence meta-review follows the scores and turns the scope concern into advice.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- the uniform ground-truth assumption behind "balanced" (b3Fh);
- limited novelty, and method gains that come from sampling (1bxc, UpWV);
- the unsupported word "robust" (ziDa);
- the claim that EMD needs equal point counts (A's chair; true only for the bijection in Eq. 2).

Only B raised the missing human study, the missing sliced-Wasserstein comparison and other completion datasets (p1L4), and sensitivity to α and the discriminator's inference cost (9BXU). Both raised the single-task scope, the missing training-loss result, and the mix of metric and method. B also received the training-loss table, in the reply to gwGK; nobody in B commented on it.
