**Verdict.** Both committees saw largely the same weaknesses. They differed in who reviewed the paper and in how each area chair (AC) used the reviews. Committee A's AC asked more than ten people, found two reviewers, and needed the senior AC for a third. Their confidence was 3, 2 and 2. All three gave 4, and none moved after the rebuttal. A's only claim of a technical error is wrong. A's AC doubted its reviewers' grasp of the paper but still followed their scores. Committee B had an expert champion, Reviewer_iTr9 (8, confidence 4). B's two low-confidence reviewers rose to 6 on the linear-versus-cubic argument that did not move A. B's AC judged the topic of interest to NeurIPS; A's AC judged it far from mainstream ML. This is a different weighting of the same facts, plus one factual error in A.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_odbV (2 hours) called the practical parts "boilerplate" and a "mash-up", with no specifics. Its one technical point says Eq. (2) "is not the integrated Wiener process" and shows "a fundamental misunderstanding" of [6]. The paper (lines 48–54) uses the standard SDE form of that process, with A and B from [6, Equation 2]. odbV ignored the rebuttal, which gave the explicit matrices. The AC's reviewer who "knows the subject better" fits only odbV, the sole confidence 3 and the only one to call the results "not significant". Reviewer_JUUq asked what separates the paper from GOODE [12]. Lines 36–43 answer this: [10–12] cost cubic time, and the Markov prior gives linear time. The authors repeated it, and JUUq kept the 4 "after reading other reviews". Reviewer_Cw4U wrote A's most careful review; its points check out. The main text does not give the drift and diffusion and defers equations to other papers. The boundary conditions enter as a likelihood, and the "bridge prior" matters only for initialisation (lines 129–132). Chkrebtii et al. and Cockayne et al. are not cited. These are presentation faults, not errors. The authors never replied to Cw4U. The AC's "we can do nothing but reject" followed the scores and added a venue-fit argument.

**Committee B (poster).** Reviewer_iTr9 praised the initialisation, mesh refinement and calibration, which are "often neglected" in this literature. iTr9 valued exactly what odbV dismissed. The champion has one lapse. It says "benchmarking is not provided", but Figures 6 and 7 compare wall-clock time with SciPy. On substance, B was the more critical committee. Reviewer_9rs3 and Reviewer_zNRW noted that the solver is about 100 times slower than SciPy (Fig. 6 confirms). zNRW rightly noted the lack of an end-to-end ablation and of any probabilistic baseline. zNRW and Reviewer_j7RB (confidence 2 each) raised their scores to 6 on 2021-08-16. zNRW accepted that "GOODE is clearly not as scalable". j7RB cited "the discussions with other reviewers" and a promise to cite Chkrebtii et al. The meta-review names no reviewer. It adopts the paper's "first practical method" claim, although no experiment compares against [10–12].

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A disputed the prior (odbV wrongly; Cw4U rightly on its missing specification) and the paper's fit to an ML venue. Only B raised the 100-fold runtime gap, the missing ablation and baselines, the choice of ν, and ill-posed problems. iTr9 also found calibration under-confident and worse at larger grids; Fig. 6 (right) confirms this. Both committees raised dense notation, the relation to GOODE, and the missing Chkrebtii et al. citation. In both, low-confidence reviewers moved toward their committee's majority: JUUq stayed with the other 4s, and j7RB followed B's discussion up to 6.
