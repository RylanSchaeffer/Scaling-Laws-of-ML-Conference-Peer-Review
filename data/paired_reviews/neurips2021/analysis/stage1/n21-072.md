**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently. Both saw a clear first study of how humans rate a rule-based and a learned Hanabi teammate. Both saw narrow evidence: one agent of each kind, one game, 29 participants. No reviewer found an error that overturns the main result, a strong preference for SmartBot at equal mean scores (14.07 against 14.39). A's reviewers rated the study just below the bar, with three 5s. A's area chair (AC) then ruled that "one learning agent and one bot in one environment" limits the paper's interest. B's reviewers rated it just above the bar, with two 6s and two 7s. B's AC listed the same narrowness as a revision. Matched reviewers show the split. Reviewer_Tigt (A, 5) and Reviewer_tDFg (B, 6) made one agent per class their main objection. Reviewer_mR2Y (A, 5) and Reviewer_PtgZ (B, 7) both asked for qualitative analysis of the free responses. Neither AC departed from its reviewers.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 5, 5. Reviewer_aZxX (7 hours) wrote the most demanding methods review in either committee, and most of its points check out. Section 3.1 never says how participants were recruited. Lines 250-253 make uncited claims about human play. The conclusion says participants "cited" reasons, but the study measured those constructs only with Likert items. One point is wrong: the "second-order" models (line 182) include interactions. The rebuttal disclosed word-of-mouth recruitment and live video-call sessions run by experimenters who knew the hypotheses. These answers gave aZxX new objections, snowball sampling and experimenter demand, and it kept its 5. mR2Y correctly saw a score-experience correlation for Other-Play too: r = 0.188 against 0.247 for SmartBot. The paper still reads only the SmartBot correlation as real (lines 212-213). Tigt praised the analysis but found the takeaway unclear. Neither mR2Y nor Tigt replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_zPDw's 6 is two hours and five sentences at confidence 5. The AC called the protocol answer "reassuring" and rejected on scope.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 6, 7. Reviewer_WHgS (confidence 5) was the one sceptic. It called the results "perhaps too preliminary for publication" and asked for a larger between-subjects study. Its facts check out: a $50 prize for the top scorer, no SmartBot score of 15, no demographics. The rebuttal gave a power analysis, the 15/14 order split, and three players who scored 24 with Other-Play yet strongly preferred SmartBot. WHgS raised to 6, but its text still asks for a new study. The same power analysis conceded that, per participant, only a 5-point score difference was detectable. Reviewer_8ptp (7) named the same weaknesses as A and called them "modest". tDFg found the result unsurprising, since SmartBot encodes human knowledge and Other-Play was not built for human conventions. PtgZ (7, 1.5 hours) said of the survey design and analysis, "I do not know much about these topics". The AC followed the scores and trusted the "comprehensive response".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised snowball sampling, experimenter demand, single Likert items instead of multi-item scales, the "near-significant" p = 0.061, overstated or uncited claims, and the unclear takeaway. Only A questioned the experience correlation. Only B raised statistical power, order effects, the incentive design, missing demographics, and the Figure 1 note on perfect scores (8ptp). The camera-ready did not fix A's wording concerns. It keeps "humans have a clear preference", "largely fail to convince humans" and "near-significant". Its main text still omits the recruitment method.
