**Verdict.** Committee B rejected because one reviewer's argument turned two positive reviewers negative in the discussion. Committee A held no discussion and accepted on three thin 6s. B began more positive than A. Reviewer_tNwH fell from "a clear accept" to 5 and Reviewer_oVRK lowered its score to 5, so B's starting mean was at least 6.25, against A's final 5.75. Reviewer_j4Fr argued from Foster & Stine that martingality matters only as a route to accuracy. The paper should therefore report MSE and beat a baseline made martingale by post-processing. B's chair made this "the core concern". The paper confirms the factual part. Its three metrics are mean calibration, expected volatility and interval coverage (L259-273). GLIM meets the first two by construction (Prop. 1, Y_T in {0,1}). No accuracy score and no post-processed baseline appear. On interval coverage, the only open test, GLIM's edge over MMFE is at most 0.06 except at weather day 6, and MMFE wins some cells (Table 1). But B's literal remedy misfits the task. Every sensible model has mean path y0 (L85-91), so point MSE cannot separate martingale models; a proper score of the path distribution could. Both committees saw the same strengths and the same thin case for martingality. B's discussion made it fatal; A never discussed it.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 5, 6, 6. No review was edited and no reviewer posted after the rebuttal. Reviewer_KTCw (1 hour) gave generic praise bullets. Reviewer_1PPk wrote four generic sentences ("not the best paper I reviewed"). Reviewer_BQ9v summarized well but asked only for notation and HMC details. Reviewer_mTu3 (5) wrote the one substantive review. It said the paper "does not do a good job of defending" the need for a martingale model. It asked why GLIM beats MMFE by orders of magnitude on metrics that both models should meet. The authors replied that they truncated MMFE paths to [0,1], which breaks its martingale property. The main text never says so. mTu3 did not reply. The chair wrote "no major critiques" and followed the scores, though "unsure about the eventual scope of application".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 5, 7. j4Fr (5, 2 hours) also noted that L108-111 describe Foster & Stine as diagnostics only. Their abstract proposes a post-processing "martingale filter" that guarantees smaller MSE. j4Fr questioned whether MQLSTM is "Bayesian", as L251-252 say. The authors first described a dropout posterior, then retracted it as an earlier baseline. They answered only j4Fr, and conceded that MSE matters without reporting it. The updates of oVRK and tNwH restate j4Fr's argument. tNwH also cited the authors' silence toward other reviewers. Reviewer_DdpC's 7 is unedited and DdpC posted nothing. It praises "strong empirical results" yet finds the experiments "harder to follow". The chair claimed "a consensus" that DdpC's score does not show. The meta-review repeats tNwH's words: martingality "in and of itself" is not the goal.
