**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. No reviewer disputed the theory: a convergence and asymptotic-optimality proof for a Frank-Wolfe-type sampling rule in generic structured pure exploration. The parts are known, and the paper says so: Best Challenger (l.67-70), LMA "close to ours" (l.100), non-smooth FW "similar but simplified" (l.119-124). The submission reports no running times, although the title promises speed. No rating is above 8 or below 6, so nobody argued for a higher tier. B's lower mean (6.5 against 7.33) is the same facts weighted differently. Reviewer_2Czm and Reviewer_8vBR counted the closeness to prior work against significance; A's reviewers did not. The agreement is partly not independent. Reviewer_83MA (A) and Reviewer_ABte (B) have matching summaries, the same two main questions, and six of ABte's seven technical comments at the same appendix lines. ABte posted on 16 July after 10 hours; 83MA posted on 18 July after 1 hour. Neither committee noticed that the empirical lead rests on unequal stopping rules. The submitted Table 1 runs LT with its own rule and BC with a looser heuristic threshold (l.343-345). That gives the "rather spectacular" linear-bandit gain (l.351-352). In the camera-ready, with a shared rule, LT needs fewer samples than FWS at every δ (919 against 1,030 at δ = 0.1). The abstract changes "outperforms" to "competitive".

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 8, 7). Reviewer_AySN (8, 3 hours) asked about the choice of r_t, which the main text omits, and took the empirical lead at face value. Reviewer_uPhV asked for intuition on Assumption 3 and for run times. Direct answers lifted uPhV to 7. Reviewer_83MA wrote the most detailed proof check in either committee. 83MA found that the loop condition evaluates F outside Λ, and suggested the rename that became FWS. 83MA did not reply to the rebuttal. The AC capped the tier: "clear accept but not a top paper", because the work is "relatively technical relative to antecedent work" and its computational limits stay open.

Committee B (6, 6, 7, 7). Reviewer_2Czm (confidence 3) argued that every ingredient exists in prior work. That is a fair reading of the paper's own related-work section. The rebuttal said that the cited non-smooth FW needs gradients in a neighbourhood. Lines 230-235 already say this, and 2Czm accepted it. Reviewer_8vBR asked what separates the paper from LMA beyond empirics, and when problem (1) is hard. The authors cited LMA's µ-dependent learning rate and the first Lipschitz-bandit results, but gave no hard example. 8vBR rose to 6. Reviewer_5onQ called the experiments "biased" by missing baselines and was "mostly satisfied" with the justification. The AC restated the consensus and added nothing.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the misleading algorithm name, the loop condition outside Λ, and the choice and sensitivity of r_t. The camera-ready fixes the first two. Only B raised these points:

- significance relative to LMA, Best Challenger and non-smooth FW (2Czm, 8vBR);
- missing baselines: RAGE, LinGapE, ALBA, LUCB and top-two algorithms (ABte, 5onQ);
- how restrictive Assumption 2 is, and whether problem (1) is ever hard (8vBR);
- the undefined LT-H and the loose definition of Λ (ABte, 5onQ).

The submission confirms the missing linear baselines, the undefined LT-H and the absent hard example. Both committees asked for run times. The authors' replies disagree: BC is faster than TaS (to uPhV and 83MA), similar (to ABte), and "close to and sometimes better" (to 5onQ). None of these issues changed a decision.
