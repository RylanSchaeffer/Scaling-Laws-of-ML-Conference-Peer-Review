**Verdict.** Committee B found real errors in the derivation. Committee A's reviewers caught only one of them. This is the main cause of the split. Reviewer_oVep (6 hours) and Reviewer_H49w (5 hours) checked Section 3.1, and the submitted PDF confirms their points. Line 137 calls the noise normal, but Eq. (4) is a Laplace likelihood. Eq. (7) sets p(x|y,θ)p(θ) equal to p(x|y). The improper Jeffreys prior is written as a density. The heteroscedastic noise model is called "epistemic". The authors conceded all of these. On the shared points, the committees weighed the same evidence differently. Identical rebuttal tables on edge-weighted baselines moved Reviewer_g7Ce in A from 4 to 6. B's AC called them "convincing", but no B reviewer moved to accept. B wanted another review cycle for the fixes. A left them to the camera-ready, where the authors fixed most of them.

**Committee A (poster).** The ratings were 6, 7 and 7. Reviewer_g7Ce (confidence 5) first gave 4, for a correct reason: Tables 3–4 compare only Original, GRAM and the proposed loss, with no gradient-weighted control. But it called the theory "thorough and insightful", and Section 3.1 does not support this. g7Ce claimed to check the math carefully, yet it raised no point on Section 3.1. The authors answered most math points with one-line promises: "we will correct those mathematics issues". H49w thanked them for the baselines but kept 4, because "the revisions to be made to the manuscript are too many at this point". Reviewer_WXXg reported 0.5 hours and did not check the math. It lists zyeM among the math critics, but zyeM only asked for more explanation of the prior.
