**Verdict.** The committees split because of who reviewed the paper, not because they saw different evidence. A's lowest rating (6) equals B's highest. Both saw a T2T-ViT variant with parallel convolutions and a multi-dilation stem. It gains ImageNet accuracy at small size, has no detection or segmentation results, and overlaps with concurrent hybrids such as Conformer and LocalViT. A's reviewers treated the overlap as a framing problem and the missing dense tasks as fixable. B's two confidence-5 reviewers called the design incremental. In both committees, every reviewer who answered the rebuttal was satisfied. B's two detractors never answered. B also had the most exacting reviewer, Reviewer_dhAk. The paper confirms its main baseline point and refutes its DeiT point. A's AC wrote a generic accept. B's AC rejected on an unnamed "comparison to prior models". Neither committee saw that Table 2 overstates the compute of most baselines about twofold.

**Committee A (poster).** Reviewer_Zi4o gave 8 with a three-line summary. The submission already answers three of its five questions. Table 3 separates the two inductive biases, and lines 291–293 explain the training budget behind Fig. 1. Its reply to the rebuttal is one line. Reviewer_eo3B (7, confidence 5) knew the concurrent work but judged that it does "not nullify the contributions". eo3B and Reviewer_dFPw (8) raised their scores after the rebuttal's new experiments, including COCO and ADE20K results. Nobody checked those replies. One quoted eo3B a Table 3 accuracy, 71.1, that the table does not contain. Reviewer_D6zB (6) asked for wall-clock speed and never replied. The AC wrote four generic sentences. They claim "All of the reviewers were impressed" and credit a good compute–accuracy trade-off. Table 2 does not support that trade-off. It lists DeiT-S at 9.8 GMACs and Swin-T at 9.0. The T2T-ViT and Swin papers give 4.6 and 4.5. ViTAE's own figures are on the smaller scale, so ViTAE-S (5.6) costs more than both.

**Committee B (reject).** Reviewer_kqV3 (5, confidence 5) found the design "not very interesting". It called the accuracy "not competitive" but named no model. In Table 2, ViTAE-S beats every listed transformer of similar size. Reviewer_dhAk (5, confidence 5) wrote the most checkable review in either committee. It is right that Table 2 omits T2T-ViT-14↑384 (83.3%). That model uses fewer parameters and MACs than ViTAE-S↑384 (83.0%). It is right that the evidence for multiple dilations is thin. Table 3 has no single-dilation control, and its multi-dilation rows score 68.9–69.9 against 68.7, without error bars. Its charge that the DeiT comparison is unfair is wrong. In Table 2, the T2T-ViT-7 base (71.7) is below even the undistilled DeiT-T (72.2). Its novelty charge rests on two concurrent preprints that the paper cites and beats. The authors answered every point, several with new runs. Neither detractor replied. Reviewer_seSX accepted the dense-prediction results and rose to 6. The AC conceded that the rebuttal addressed "a number of concerns" but still followed the scores.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B challenged the baselines: the missing T2T-ViT-14↑384 row, the unreported T2T variants and the DeiT claim. Only B asked whether multiple dilations help, why batch norm changes the fusion result, and how the model trains from scratch beyond ImageNet. Only A asked about ResNet-RS, "real Top-1", the Fig. 1 budget and the cost of attention at high resolution. Both committees asked for dense-prediction results, for speed or memory figures, and for an ablation that Table 3 already contains. Both rebuttals revealed that the reduction cells use Performer, which neither version of the paper states.
