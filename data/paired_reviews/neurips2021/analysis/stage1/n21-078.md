**Verdict.** Committee B recommended more because the rebuttal moved its panel and its area chair (AC) followed the panel. Committee A's AC broke a split panel with its own reading of the theory. Before the rebuttal, B's mean was at most 5.0, against 5.25 for A. After it, B stood at 4, 6, 7, 7 and A at 4, 5, 6, 7. Both committees saw the same paper. Its DomainBed gains are small: the camera-ready average is 67.9, against 67.5 for CORAL. Its information theory is only loosely tied to the algorithm. A's AC gave two reasons, and the paper (camera-ready; no submitted PDF) confirms both. Theorem 1 compares label information inside source domain S1 and has no target-domain term. The paper itself concedes "no guarantee" that source domain-specific information is relevant to the target. Lemma 1 and Definitions 3–4 are credited to two multi-view learning papers [14, 16]. In B, Reviewer_SAJX raised the first point. B's AC was "convinced" by a reply that restated Theorem 1 and said that zero covariance means independence, which is false. The two ACs weighted the same weakness differently.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_d6mf wrote the strongest review. It argued that domain-specific (DS) features hurt when a background predicts a different class in the target. It also found that the paper left out the original Colored-MNIST and gave its own dataset the same name. The authors conceded both, and admitted a 40% drop on one class in a background-swap test. d6mf rose from 5 to 6 on a promised limitations paragraph. Reviewer_88Lv kept its 7; two of its three requests were met in full. Reviewer_4Vtm kept its 4 on novelty, a concern "shared by Reviewer YJwo". Reviewer_YJwo (5, 2 hours, posted two weeks after the others) is weak evidence. It said no supplementary material existed, but d6mf cites "Table 3 of Appendix B.3". It asked for a benchmark ablation that the appendix has on PACS. It never answered the rebuttal. The AC called the paper "borderline" and rejected on its own theory points, not on the reviewers' novelty complaints.

**Committee B (poster).** Reviewer_FWcu (7) valued the formalism; FWcu and Reviewer_xiMp (7) valued the DomainBed results. Reviewer_sfZU began at 3 or lower, citing the theory–algorithm gap, novelty, the small gain over CORAL, and run time. The first-round rebuttal, which reused much of the text that A received, moved sfZU to 4. A second exchange moved it to 6. In A, 4Vtm replied once, a month later, unmoved. The authors' new training times (8 hours for mDSDI, 14 for CORAL) went unchecked. B never saw the 30 GB memory figure that A received. xiMp rose to 7, but its first weakness, no comparison with DMG [7] or DSON [43], got only a promise. The camera-ready Table 1 still lacks both. Reviewer_SAJX (4) wrote a thin review that repeats its summary and never replied, but its central point is right. The AC followed the 3–1 majority and named SAJX only to overrule it.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the flipped-correlation failure, the missing Colored-MNIST and Terra Incognita results, the two-backbone model against one-backbone baselines, and the multi-view origin of the theory. Only B raised the small gain over the simpler CORAL, the missing DMG, DSON and Fourier baselines, the marginal-only definitions, and meta-learning on DS features alone. Both raised the theory–implementation gap, the limited novelty of the parts, and whether DS features transfer to an unseen domain. A's AC made the last point decisive. B's AC accepted the authors' answer.
