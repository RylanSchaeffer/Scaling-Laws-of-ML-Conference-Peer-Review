**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same paper: Gaptron (van der Hoeven, 2020) extended to feedback graphs, with high-probability bounds, a √T lower bound and synthetic experiments. Seven of eight reviewers gave 6 or 7. The difference is where the novelty concern appeared. In A, two reviewers raised it, the authors answered it, and both reviewers raised their scores. The area chair (AC) then followed four accept-side ratings. In B, no reviewer gave novelty as a reason to reject, so the authors never had to defend it. B's AC raised it at decision time, credited it to "the reviewers", and rejected against three accept-side scores. Set aside the uninformative reviews (TAuC in A; CwtQ and fiNV in B). Then B's ratings (7, 6) are no lower than A's (6, 7, 6). The committees weighted the same facts differently, and B's AC made the decisive call.

**Committee A (poster).** Reviewer_p3fb called the reliance on van der Hoeven (2020) the "most major issue". Reviewer_Qt6c found the results "not so much surprising". The rebuttal listed what is new. A general gap map for regular losses (Lemma 1) gives the high-probability bounds. Any OCO algorithm works, with up to a factor K gain when separable. With the √T lower bound, the bandit graph appears to be the hardest case. Qt6c rose to 7 in a "high level response" while travelling. p3fb rose to 6 after the authors were "clarifying the generalizations". A's specific claims all check out. Reviewer_u4LL saw that the hinge loss is not a regular loss, so the Banditron comparison (lines 84–98) misleads. Qt6c saw that Gaptron is missing from the bandit experiments. The authors conceded both. Reviewer_TAuC's 7 at confidence 1 ("I lack the domain knowledge") carries no information. The meta-review summarises the consensus loosely. It calls ρ the "independence number", but ρ is the domination number. It credits "improved experimental results against prior art", although Gaptron was never tested.

**Committee B (reject).** Reviewer_SLin, B's only confidence-4 reviewer, checked the proof of Theorem 1. SLin noted that the work "mainly relies on tools developed in (van der Hoeven 2020)" and still gave 7. Reviewer_V4xK (6) leaned to accept and only acknowledged the rebuttal. Reviewer_CwtQ gave 6 after one hour and three sentences. CwtQ asked what the gap map is for; Section 2 and Lemma 1 answer this. Reviewer_fiNV gave B's only score below 6. Its main objection is that the upper bounds hold only with full information. That is wrong: Theorems 1 and 2 (lines 222, 253) cover any feedback graph, and only Theorem 3 is full information. fiNV ignored the correction. No B reviewer posted after the rebuttal. The meta-review grants "a fairly complete picture of achievable regret bounds" but finds the paper "not sufficiently innovative". It calls incrementality "the main concern raised by the reviewers". The visible B reviews mention van der Hoeven (2020) only as context, never as a reason to reject.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the hinge-loss gap in the Banditron comparison, the missing Gaptron baseline and the length of the high-probability proof. Only A noted the log factor from a greedy dominating set (lines 148–151 agree) and the unexplored T^1/2 versus T^2/3 contrast. Only B criticised synthetic-only data and theory-based tuning (CwtQ, V4xK, fiNV). Only B misread the scope of the bounds (fiNV). Both committees noted the reliance on van der Hoeven (2020) and a lower bound that ignores ρ. A's reviewers treated the reliance as a weakness that the rebuttal answered. B's reviewers treated it as context, and B's AC treated it as decisive.
