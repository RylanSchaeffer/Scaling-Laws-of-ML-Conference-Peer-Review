**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster because every reviewer settled at a borderline accept. All seven reviewers found FDS clear and useful. Forward-mode differentiation keeps memory constant as the horizon grows. Sharing a hyperparameter over a window of steps lowers hypergradient error (Theorem 4.1, Figure 2). No reviewer found an error in the method or the theorem. Final ratings are 6, 6, 6, 6 in A and 7, 6, 6 in B. In both committees, the rebuttal lifted the reviewers who began below threshold. Both committees named the same limits and treated them as scope, not flaws. FDS covers only SGD hyperparameters on three small datasets, and its forward mode is derived by hand although JAX provides one. The committees differed in scrutiny, not in outcome. A's four main reviews total about 2,500 words; B's three total about 540. Only A examined the search range behind the headline ×20 speedup over black-box methods. Reviewer_MsYr pointed out that the baselines search learning rates in [−1, 1] on a linear scale (l.331), while FDS starts at 0. If the seven learning rates are drawn uniformly, the chance that all seven are non-negative is 1 in 128. The authors conceded that FDS and BOHB "become equally good around the [0, 0.1] range". MsYr kept the objection after the rebuttal, yet the final rating is 6, and A's AC called the concerns "addressed". B never examined the search range. B's higher mean (6.33 against 6.0) comes from a 7 whose main review is 103 words. It reflects less scrutiny, not a stronger case.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 6, 6, 6). Reviewer_E74o rose from "just below the acceptance threshold" on promises of Adam and CIFAR-100 results in an appendix. The authors had said that FDS is "less consistent across seeds for CIFAR-100". Reviewer_3Csr asked whether sharing fixes ill-conditioning or only variance, and the authors conceded that it targets variance. After a second prompt from the authors, 3Csr wrote "You have addressed my main concerns" and edited the review. Reviewer_MsYr wrote the most thorough review in either committee (10 hours). Its post-rebuttal summary calls the paper "a slight modification of RTRL". Reviewer_7zoB (confidence 2) wrote generic praise and misread the mechanism. 7zoB credits forward mode with fixing gradient degradation in greedy methods. The paper says greediness avoids degradation and sharing mitigates it (l.128, l.148). The meta-review restates the unanimous scores.

Committee B (7, 6, 6). Reviewer_CQeN was B's only substantive reviewer. CQeN called the sign-based outer optimizer "rather hidden", and the main text gives it one sentence (l.332-334). The authors said that without it FDS needed 25 outer steps instead of 10. Two new greedy-with-sharing ablations and memory figures moved CQeN to 6, without a revised paper to check. Reviewer_3NBa gave 7 while calling novelty "limited", and asked one question. Reviewer_FmV3 asked for more architectures and for joint use with architecture search. l.308-310 puts the latter out of scope. Neither 3NBa nor FmV3 replied to the rebuttal. The AC called it "a straightforward decision".

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A questioned the black-box search range and asked for RTHO and MARTHE baselines (MsYr). Only A asked how to choose the window size and whether sharing needs smooth optimal schedules (3Csr). Only B asked for an ablation of the sign-based optimizer, for sharing applied to greedy methods, and for memory costs (CQeN). Only B asked about validation-set size (3NBa). The paper confirms both the search range and the hidden optimizer. Neither issue changed a decision.
