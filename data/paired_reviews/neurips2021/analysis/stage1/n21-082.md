**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reason: a broad, careful 3D robustness study with no new method. It covers three backbones, four datasets, three threats and three SSL tasks. It applies the 2D methods of Hendrycks et al. [24] and Chen et al. [25] to point clouds. The committees differed in score (mean 7.0 against 6.0), not in decision. Their reviewers weighed the same novelty gap differently. In A, the paper was on the accept side from the start: Reviewer_Vmom and Reviewer_tgUE gave 7 and never changed it. Reviewer_wEE7 and tgUE called the novelty limited and still gave 7. In B, no rating was above 6 before the rebuttal, and three of four were below the threshold. Three B reviewers named weak technical novelty as a main weakness. The rebuttal turned B. An ε sweep, C&W, PointContrast and augmentation results lifted Reviewer_ygsD to 7, and Reviewer_Dx1d and Reviewer_euZV to 6. Reviewer_CMgR stayed at 5 and did not reply. B's AC set "thin on novelty" against a "solid experimental study" and accepted. A's AC deferred to four 7s. Nothing in either record argued for more than a poster. Gains over the AT baseline range from +1.1 to +14.2 RA, and from +0.4 to +0.8 mIoU in segmentation. No ablation isolates the local-feature explanation.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 7, 7, 7). Vmom wrote the most detailed review. Two of Vmom's puzzles are real: "Adversarial x" rows often have lower RA than "x" rows (Table 2), and ST baselines keep 38-61% RA under PD and PA (Tables 4-5). Neither is an error, and the replies explained both. Vmom also accepted an argument-only answer on EdgeConv. Reviewer_6Xbb correctly found that the SSL loss in Eq. 5 is clean, and that "F = M ◦ H" has the wrong order. New tables moved 6Xbb from 6 to 7. wEE7 rose to 7 after C&W and LG-GAN results, but still found the novelty limited. The meta-review has two sentences and relies on unanimity.

Committee B (5, 6, 7, 6). ygsD called the setup flawed because of one ε "(0.005)". The paper uses ε = 0.05 (l.169); 0.005 is the test step size (l.173). After this correction and an ε sweep, ygsD rose to 7 but kept the novelty concern. CMgR's 5 mixes wrong and right points. The PA points are PGD-optimized (Eq. 3), not random, and Chen et al. (CVPR 2020) is cited as [25]. But CMgR is right that PA and PD are weak; the paper says so (l.315-316). Dx1d misread Table 2: every row uses adversarial fine-tuning. Dx1d then accepted a jigsaw-augmentation baseline at chance level (CA 2.4% on 40 classes) without comment. euZV rose to 6 after an ε = 0.06 table and autoencoder-AJT results.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these issues:
- the clean SSL loss in "adversarial" joint training (6Xbb; Eq. 5 confirms);
- cross-architecture ensembles and max-ensemble gradients (6Xbb, Vmom);
- non-AT defenses such as feature denoising and Defense-GAN (tgUE);
- generative attacks such as LG-GAN (wEE7);
- undefined Π and saliency() (6Xbb).

Only B raised these issues:
- one ε and the significance of small gains (ygsD); only Figure 3 has error bars;
- prior evidence that DGCNN is more robust (ygsD, citing AdvPC [73]);
- a modified PCT instead of other point transformers (CMgR);
- augmentation baselines and point-level contrastive tasks (Dx1d);
- the conflict with SSL for domain adaptation (euZV).

These B issues lowered B's starting scores. The rebuttal answered most of them, and none changed a decision.
