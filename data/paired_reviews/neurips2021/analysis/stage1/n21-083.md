**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a spotlight for the same reasons and weighed the same facts the same way. Every reviewer accepted a checkable headline result. With classifier guidance, the model beats BigGAN-deep on ImageNet FID at 128, 256 and 512 pixels (2.97, 4.59, 7.72 against 6.02, 6.95, 8.43; Table 4). Without guidance, it beats StyleGAN and StyleGAN2 on three LSUN classes. Six of eight reviewers called the parts mostly prior work; both committees ranked that below the result. The authors gave both committees nearly the same reply: no earlier paper guided an already class-conditional model or scaled the classifier gradient. Table 3 supports this (FID 10.94 to 4.59 at 256 pixels). Both means are 7.5, and each committee has one 9. Neither meta-review argues for the tier; each restates the consensus. The support for the tier differs in quality. In A, three reviewers gave 7, and the only top-tier vote is a thin 9. In B, the 9 and the 8 come with argued reviews.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 7, 7, 9). Reviewer_H51k wrote the most critical review: no methodological novelty, and few insights beyond Nichol and Dhariwal [46]. Table 4 confirms that [46] already beat BigGAN-deep at 64 pixels (2.92 against 4.06). H51k rose from 6 to 7 after the novelty reply and the promise to release the trained models. Reviewer_rkcu and Reviewer_NiCt wrote short, accurate 7s and kept them. Reviewer_Fjfm gave 9 at confidence 5 ("checked the math/other details carefully"). The review holds generic praise and one question, concedes that most ideas "are not brand new", and ends "I think it is a good paper". It names no detail it checked and ignores the rebuttal. Without it, A's mean is 7.0. The meta-review borrows Fjfm's "non-trivial insights" and rkcu's sentence on sFID and precision-recall.

Committee B (6, 7, 8, 9). Reviewer_i9rh called the scaled guidance "very novel", compared the paper to StyleGAN and BigGAN, and pressed five specific points. One was right: only Table 4's 25-step rows support the conclusion's claim that guidance narrows the sampling-time gap. One was wrong: l.126 and l.144 state how class labels enter the model. Reviewer_jJSL gave 8 ("possible contender for a highlight") and spent most of the review on the title, which it said did not affect the score. Reviewer_NtJ2 gave a solid 7. Reviewer_yLx5 gave 6 at confidence 3 and said most of the gain comes from manual architecture design. That holds for LSUN, but not for the ImageNet headline: unguided ADM loses to BigGAN-deep at 256 and 512 pixels (10.94 and 23.24). yLx5 did not answer the rebuttal. The AC added two revision requests: a new title, and a discussion of how the ImageNet classifier might interact with FID.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised memorization as a function of guidance scale (NiCt), FID code and model release (H51k), the weak DDIM latent space for editing (H51k), and missing related work such as VQGAN (H51k). Only B raised the boastful title (jJSL, i9rh, AC), measured latency against GANs (yLx5, i9rh), FFHQ at 1024 pixels (NtJ2), inverse problems and the cost of a separate classifier (i9rh), and the FID-guidance interaction (AC). A's record holds the nearest evidence for it: the authors told NiCt that high scales give "strangely washed-out images" while Inception Score keeps rising, and no A reviewer followed up. None of these issues changed a tier. Neither committee saw that Algorithm 2 never uses the gradient scale s; a 2023 public comment found it, and the camera-ready keeps the error.
