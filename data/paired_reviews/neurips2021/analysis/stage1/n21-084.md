**Verdict.** Committee B recommended acceptance because of who reviewed and how its area chair (AC) counted, not because it saw a different paper. Both saw the same borderline method, nearly the same rebuttal tables, and the same two facts. The loss is a known large-margin push-pull term, and the gain over recent methods (FEAT, FRN, IEPT, distribution calibration) is small. In B, the three reviewers who spent the most time (6.5, 8 and 10 hours) pressed for ablations, got them, and ended at 6, 7 and 6. Two said novelty was sufficient. B's AC counted only these three. In A, reviewers spent 3 to 5 hours, and Reviewer_FGzB (4) and Reviewer_5hh7 (5) held on novelty and parity with SOTA. A's AC repeated 5hh7's three reasons. One, "reliance on extra data", is wrong for the in-domain results, whose negatives come from the base training set or are random vectors (line 175, Table 1). B's reviewers had the authors' answer on this from 2021-08-10; A's came after 5hh7's last edit. That error did not decide A alone, because FGzB and Reviewer_FMo7 stayed below threshold on novelty.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 4, 5, 6, 5 (mean 5.0). FGzB's specific points check out. Figure 2(a) shows a MobileNet gain of about 0.8, against 3.5 for SSL and 2.6 for KD, despite the caption's "consistently". In Table 5, β = 0.75 beats β = 1 in 5 of 8 cells, unexplained. On tiered-ImageNet, Table 3 puts POODLE below Distill and TIM-GD. FGzB's post-rebuttal note is three generic sentences. FMo7 rightly noted that the comparisons use only the Rot+KD backbone and omit DSN, LEO, AWGIM and ECKPN. Its last objection, softmax overconfidence on outliers, treats POODLE as an outlier detector. Reviewer_C2g5 (6, confidence 3) engaged most and rose. 5hh7's first review was careful. After the rebuttal it claimed the method needs "additional examples from related domains", unlike distribution calibration. The paper contradicts this in-domain, the only setting where distribution calibration reports results. The meta-review copies 5hh7's three phrases. It credits the method with "feature learning", but the extractor is frozen (line 107).

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 4, 6, 7, 6 (mean 5.75). Reviewer_kzjj read most closely. It found that Table 2 swaps the push and pull labels and that line 220 overclaims "1-3%" gains; 13 of 18 gains in Table 1 are below 1 point. It asked for distractor-class, entropy and label-smoothing ablations, got all three, and raised its score. Reviewer_e7JL (confidence 5) first called the transductive pull loss "really bad practice", then rose to 7 after a clarification. Reviewer_kwqH rose to 6 on the new results. Reviewer_6ffC (4, 2 hours) is a weak detractor. It said the paper compares only with "very basic methods", but Table 3 lists 14 others, including TIM-GD and LaplacianShot. It called the optional broader-impact section a guideline violation. The AC's time-weighted count fairly sets 6ffC aside, but the meta-review is silent on novelty and the small SOTA margin.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the choice of α and β, the β pattern in Table 5, the stop-gradient, the uncited Liu et al. (2018), and comparisons with classifier-refinement methods such as distribution calibration. Only B found the Table 2 label swap, the line-220 overclaim and the raw-input distance in Eq. 2, and asked for analysis beyond accuracy and the three new ablations. Both raised novelty, incomplete SOTA comparisons, missing confidence intervals, why random negatives work, the source and number of negatives, and Figure 2(a). A treated novelty and parity with SOTA as disqualifying; B, as acceptable or as wording fixes.
