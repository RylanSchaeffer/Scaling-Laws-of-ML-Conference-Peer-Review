**Verdict.** Both committees recommended a poster for the same reason. Strong sample-efficiency results on NAS-Bench-101/201 and a state-of-the-art ImageNet number outweighed thin novelty over Bayesian-optimization (BO) style predictor NAS. In both, rebuttal tables raised several scores. The committees differed in route, not outcome. A's five reviewers ended at 6 to 8 (mean 6.8), and its area chair (AC) deferred to that unanimity. Yet it called the paper "too informal" and "half-baked". B's four regular reviewers ended at 6 to 7. B's AC called this borderline and added an emergency reviewer, Reviewer_93Rt, whose 4 (confidence 5) arrived a day before the discussion deadline. That score never moved and alone lowers B's mean from 6.25 to 5.8. The authors then ran 93Rt's ablations: a plot against BRP-NAS and a deep-ensemble EI variant of their predictor. The AC and 93Rt were "very surprised" by the results. The AC judged novelty "low" but accepted on the condition of a full code release. So B weighed novelty more harshly, but both let the results decide. Much of the decisive new evidence sat behind anonymous links, and no one in the record reports checking it.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 8, 7, 6, 7, 6. Reviewer_vUV5's 8 mostly restates the paper's own framing. Its one query, a Semi-NAS result, sits in Table 2 under an unclear label. Reviewer_NXMZ checked the most. NXMZ showed that predictor training cost grows quadratically with iterations, and that footnote 1 scores only 1,000 random candidates per iteration in a space of about 10^18. The authors described a fixed 10K evaluation pool that the submission does not mention. NXMZ saw no link between position and rank in the authors' t-SNE plot. The authors re-ran t-SNE at lower perplexity and drew good points on top; NXMZ then rose to 7. Reviewer_tHc2 questioned novelty and theory; the authors replied sharply ("your assumption appears to be impractical and wrong"). tHc2 denied a conflict of interest, apologised, rewrote the review and raised the score. Reviewer_Lp96 rose to 6 but kept the con that the paper could mislead on its relation to BO.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 7, 6, 4. Reviewer_onF1 (7, confidence 5) wrote sweeping praise ("a crucial step to rescue the NAS community"). onF1 called the paper an analysis of "the current NAS's ineffectiveness", which it is not. Reviewer_nK9v rose from 4 to 6 after two new ablations but kept reproducibility as the main concern. Reviewer_eYgg rose after a zero-cost-proxy comparison. 93Rt made a principled novelty case: the method is a simplified BO, and its sampling is close to BRP-NAS. The authors confirmed that Table 1's "number of predictors" is just the iteration count. Two 93Rt claims were wrong. BONAS does not use weight sharing on NAS-Bench, as 93Rt conceded. Tables 3 and 4 do not conflict. Table 3 reports the final predictor's top pick (l.128), and Table 4 counts samples until the optimum is first queried. The paper never says so.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised wall-clock cost, the 1,000-candidate footnote, the ImageNet retraining recipe (Reviewer_chmj) and parity with local search. Table 2 shows parity (94.24 against 94.25). But that row is WeakNAS with local-search sampling; standalone local search reached 94.15 in the rebuttal. Only B asked for zero-cost proxies, a biased-sampling ablation, NAS-Bench-301 and a BO variant of the method. Only B weighed gains against their SDs, rightly: at 200 queries WeakNAS beats BONAS by 0.09 with an SD of 0.14 (Table 3; nK9v, Reviewer_YANc). Two YANc claims are wrong: Tables 1 to 3 report SDs, and the set difference in Eq. 3 is correct.
