**Verdict.** A rejected and B accepted the same borderline paper on largely the same facts. Both committees saw a direct extension of Chow et al.'s CVaR game to Bayes-adaptive MDPs; the paper itself says Prop. 2 "directly extends Prop. 1 from [9]" (line 226). Both saw two toy domains and no experiment that separates epistemic from aleatoric risk. B drew four reviewers at 6 or 7 (mean 6.5). A drew five from 4 to 7 (mean 5.6), two of them below the threshold. A's reviewers also showed, correctly, that the paper overclaims novelty. A's area chair (AC) called the contribution "quite incremental" and rejected, although three of five reviewers rated 6 or 7. B's AC named the same scalability and simple-domain limits and accepted on "reviewer consensus". No reviewer found an error in a result. The discrepancy is reviewer draw plus two ACs that weighed the same limits differently.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_hcVR (4, confidence 3, math not checked) correctly found the min/max typo on line 121. It also correctly noted that no epistemic-only or robust baseline exists. Its "not well written" verdict conflicts with three other A reviews. Reviewer_aH4a (5) claims confidence 5, yet asks whether sampling the test MDP from the prior means access to the true MDP. It does not: the planner sees only the prior. Its real objections are scalability and the missing decomposition experiment. Reviewer_4TDb (6) gave the sharpest critique: uncited prior work on both uncertainty types, and one shared budget that gives no separate control. Depeweg et al. 2018 does treat both types in risk-sensitive model-based RL. 4TDb also wrongly called G_{π,σ} undefined; line 139 defines it. Reviewer_Kn8T (7, 8 hours) wrote the most thorough review and correctly flagged the "first MCTS for CVaR" claim against RAMCP [33]. Reviewer_JA6Z (6, 3 hours) is thin and asks for CARLA/Torcs and VariBAD, which do not fit a tabular planner. No A reviewer replied to the rebuttal. 4TDb's later edit says its "main points remain unchanged". The AC claims a reviewer "consensus" that the public record does not show, and asks for an "incorrect prior" analysis that no review requested.

**Committee B (poster).** Reviewer_ZrWX (7) and Reviewer_XkNN (7) wrote specific, positive reviews. ZrWX found the same line-121 typo. XkNN asked for the same decomposition experiment as hcVR and aH4a, but called it possibly "out of scope" and kept 7. Reviewer_aq52 (6) called the algorithm "a combination of well-known techniques" but judged Props. 1-2 novel "as far as I know". Reviewer_dKwV (6) is thin: a five-sentence review and generic questions. All four acknowledged the rebuttal; XkNN and ZrWX restated support. The meta-review copies sentences from dKwV, XkNN and ZrWX. It says the draft "needs significant improvement", then accepts.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised uncited work on both uncertainty types and risk-averse tree search (4TDb), and no separate control of epistemic and aleatoric aversion (4TDb). Only A raised the MCTS overclaim and the missing RAMCP baseline (Kn8T), robust baselines and non-conjugate posteriors (hcVR), and a convergence bound (aH4a). Only B raised the unmotivated static-risk choice (XkNN; lines 69-70 state it without a reason) and minimax stability (XkNN). Only B raised the vanishing prior perturbation δ, a root-sampling variant (ZrWX), and model-free variants (dKwV). The A-only issues bear on novelty and are mostly correct. The B-only issues are requests for clarification. Neither committee tested "significantly outperforms" against Table 1. CVaR BAMDP PG beats RA-BAMCP at α = 0.03 in navigation (23.92 vs 23.51), and exact value iteration matches it in betting. Its time column compares per-episode planning with one-off training.
