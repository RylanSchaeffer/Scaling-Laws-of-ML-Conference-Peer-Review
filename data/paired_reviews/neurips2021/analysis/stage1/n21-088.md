**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected because only B had a reviewer who checked the theory, and B's chair took his side against a positive majority. B's Reviewer_7HJc found a real error. The submitted Theorem 4 claims an Ω(√(c(d−m)T ln T)) lower bound, and its proof borrows an assumption that only gap-dependent bounds allow. The authors conceded and re-proved Ω(√(cdT)), which the camera-ready states. 7HJc also argued, and the paper's text confirms, that the bounds are optimal only in T and that the full-bandit result is an explore-then-exploit T^(2/3) bound with no lower bound. B's other reviewers (7, 7, 6) did not answer him and did not move. B's chair rejected because the "technical contribution of this paper is too weak", and added a personal bar: "It would not cross the threshold if one just has some sublinear rate." No reviewer in A checked a proof, two A scores rose on the rebuttal, and A's chair followed the scores. Both committees saw the open full-bandit lower bound: A called it a limitation, B the main weakness. The proof error and the expert critique never reached A.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 7, up from 5, 7, 6. Reviewer_DREH and Reviewer_nzbx declare "Math/other details were not carefully checked". nzbx spent 2 hours and gave three sentences of assessment. DREH asked what the three feedback settings mean, though L134-138 and Sections 4-6 define them; the authors' re-explanation moved DREH to 6. nzbx moved to 7 on a rebuttal table of mean regrets on five ETF funds. Reviewer_S7fm (confidence 4) praised the paper without comment on any theorem. S7fm did not question the reply that each step is "a convex program"; for MC-UCB that is false, because its domain Δ^c_d is not convex. The chair called the panel "three expert reviewers" and repeated "matching upper and lower regret bounds". The bounds match only in T, and the submitted semi-bandit lower-bound proof was wrong.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 7, 7, 4, 6; no score moved. Reviewer_7HJc (confidence 4) is a principled outlier: each point I could check holds against the submission (Theorem 4 at L264-267; "with respect to T" at L235, L259, L269; "The lower bound for CMCB-FB remains open" at L312). He accepted the corrected proof but kept 4 on significance. Reviewer_u6Hw correctly flagged the d^(3/2) gap between the semi-bandit bounds and an undefined Δmax. Reviewer_1ysV wrote a short positive review and later "I retain my score", with no word on the error. Reviewer_kCrL asked clarity questions and kept 6. The chair's "suboptimal rate" goes beyond 7HJc's "potentially loose", but it is right in substance. Two pulls of one action give an unbiased estimate of f(w), and f(w) is linear in (w, ww^T), so a linear-bandit method reaches order √T. The paper's claim (L305-308) that statistics has no covariance estimator for such data also misses random-coefficient regression.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only B raised the flawed Theorem 4 proof, the standard full-information and semi-bandit techniques, an over-long Theorem 2 proof, tightness only in T, an unfair comparison with Degenne and Perchet (2016), the undefined Δmax, and sub-exponential noise. Only A raised the lack of real data, the motivation for the covariance risk and for each algorithm, and computational cost. Both noted the missing full-bandit lower bound.
