**Verdict.** Committee B accepted and Committee A rejected because the committees weighted the same weaknesses differently, and only A's reviewers tested the novelty claim against DisCor. Both found that Theorem 1 holds only for an unstated relaxation, that approximations separate theory from algorithm, that PER is missing, and that the gains have high variance. In all five Atari games of Table 1, the ±1 SD ranges of ReMERT and the authors' DQN baseline overlap. In A, Reviewer_MNmG and Reviewer_wsEk showed, correctly, that ReMERN's weight is DisCor's weight times an LFIW density ratio (Eq. 7; L287-288 call DisCor "a composing part of ReMERN"). A's chair also found, correctly, that Theorem 2's bound is near-vacuous. B's reviewers praised the regret framing as new and never compared it with DisCor. But the authors' answer to B's chair shows that the first relaxation turns regret into expected |Q_k − Q*|, the quantity DisCor minimizes. Each committee had one 9 that its own text does not support. B also had an 8 where A had a 5, and B's chair called the weak theory "not a fatal issue".

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 9, 4, 5, 6, all at confidence 4. Reviewer_UE11's 9 conflicts with its own text, which calls the ReMERT results "misleading to the reader" and says "not much of the theory propagates to the actual algorithm". UE11 answered the rebuttal with one line. MNmG's DisCor critique holds: Eq. (3) keeps DisCor's two terms and adds only the density ratio, and the authors concede that DisCor relaxes d^π to uniform "while we keep" it. MNmG and wsEk never answered the rebuttal or its new tables. Reviewer_4bps wrote "I may have misunderstood things", ended at 5, and kept two concerns: weak theory and weak experiments. The chair's Theorem 2 point is right. Eq. (8) starts with γR_max/(1−γ), close to the largest possible Q value, and the camera-ready drops that term. The chair rejected on these points and the head count.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 3, 9, 8. Reviewer_Giki gave 9 at confidence 5 after 1.2 hours. Most of its text is criticism: Figure 1 "not convincing", Eq. 1 "flawed", the DQN baseline "weak". It says ReMERT beats DQN "by a large margin", but against the authors' own baseline the gains are 5 to 22 percent with overlapping error bars. Reviewer_8h92 (8, eight hours) asked careful implementation questions but assessed neither novelty nor significance, and never replied. Reviewer_okGN (6) wrote that the proof needs work "before this result is ready for publication", and did not reply to the new proof. Reviewer_hPUt's 3 is principled: Figure 4 is unstable in push and sweep, and limitations are absent although the checklist says Yes. Its "regret" objection is weak, because L73-76 already state the equivalence. The chair agreed that Theorem 1 is "vacuous if read as a theorem", did not rebut hPUt, and accepted.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- novelty over DisCor, in the derivation and in ReMERN (MNmG, wsEk, chair);
- Theorem 2's near-vacuous bound (chair);
- an ablation that separates TCE from the importance weights (wsEk);
- the proposed method on the Figure 1 example (MNmG, wsEk);
- missing code and a missing related-work citation (MNmG, 4bps).

Only B raised the "regret" terminology and the two definitions of η(π) (hPUt), the weak DQN baseline (Giki), computation time (okGN), and whether the fast buffer approximates on-policy data (8h92). Both raised the loose theory, Assumption 1, high variance, the missing PER baseline, ReMERT's need for terminal states, and the weak Figure 1 example.
