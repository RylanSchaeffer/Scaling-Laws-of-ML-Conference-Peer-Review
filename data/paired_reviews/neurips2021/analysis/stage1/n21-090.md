**Verdict.** Committee B's oral does not follow from its own record. B's ratings (4, 7, 7, 8; mean 6.5) are lower than A's (6, 7, 7; mean 6.67). B's reviewers also found more faults, and more specific ones. No B reviewer argued for an oral. The only score above 7 is Reviewer_1UMR's 8, at confidence 2, on a two-sentence main review that calls the result "perhaps not surprising". B's meta-review calls the novelty "absolutely above the bar", but no public review or comment contains that phrase. The same meta-review calls the writing "almost ... unpublishable". So the oral came from B's AC or SAC, who prized the first coreset for decision trees in a discussion that is not public. A's AC followed moderate scores to a poster. Both committees saw the same facts: a novel first result, weak exposition and thin related work. The two ACs weighted the novelty differently.

**Committee A (poster).** All three reviewers praised the first decision-tree coreset. Reviewer_eRj1 called the signal assumption "too strong", then accepted the rebuttal's defence of it. Reviewer_tPz3 asked for intuition. Reviewer_QFcb noted that there is no related-work section; the camera-ready still has none. Two reviewers misstated the main bound as roughly linear in k. Theorem 8 gives (k log N)^O(1)/eps^4, and the paper admits this exceeds N at its own data scale. Nobody argued for more than a poster, and the AC gave one.

**Committee B (oral).** Reviewer_nDd9 (4) rejected the paper for its writing and for missing database-histogram work. nDd9 also said the theory covers single trees, not the forests and boosting in the experiments. The camera-ready still cites no histogram work, and the authors conceded the gap for dependent trees. One nDd9 point is wrong: the experiments are 2-D, not 15- or 18-dimensional. Reviewer_ybbQ (7, six hours) wrote the most detailed review in either committee. It found a real flaw in Algorithm 2: the last slice is computed and then dropped. As printed in the camera-ready, the loop also never ends; a simulation of the printed pseudo-code confirms this. The rebuttal skipped the point. Reviewer_BfeE (7) asked for a formal d > 2 result and a comparison with Feldman-Langberg; the camera-ready has neither. No score moved after the rebuttal.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked why the coordinate swap in Algorithm 3 keeps the guarantee. Only A asked whether real data satisfy the signal assumption. Only A noted that the coreset beats uniform sampling mainly at small sizes. Only B raised the gap between single-tree theory and forest experiments, the uncited histogram and range-search work, the missing d > 2 theorem and the Algorithm 2 flaw. These B findings hold up, but none breaks the main theorem. So B's reviewers had more grounds for a lower tier than A's, and B still went higher.

Neither committee examined the experiments closely. Section 5 fits forests to (row, column) -> value on two UCI tables. That is matrix completion, not learning a label from features. The x10 speed-up holds for sklearn random forests, at a test-loss increase of about 0.03. For LightGBM the saving is about 1.5x where the loss nears the full-data baseline.
