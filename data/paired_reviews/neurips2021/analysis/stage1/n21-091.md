**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently, and the gap opened in the discussion. Both saw strong-looking samples, a hard Section 3 derivation and no baseline. The only PSNR figures compare SNIPS samples with their own mean. At posting the committees were close: A had 8, 7, 7 and a reject score (mean at most 6.5); B had 7, 7, 6, 4 (mean 6.0). In A, the lone detractor accepted the promised PnP/RED comparisons and rose to 5. In B, the lone detractor rejected the same promise, and the other three reviewers moved to that position. A's AC gave a spotlight because the figures "speak for themselves". B's AC credited the same figures to the pretrained NCSNv2 prior. Only samplers compared on the same prior could settle this; the camera-ready adds just a RED comparison. All checks use the camera-ready paper.pdf; no submitted PDF was available.

**Committee A (spotlight).** Reviewer_nVFq (5, over 12 hours) wrote the most specific critique. The method "seems to boil down to applying [19] in the spectral space". It has no comparison and no step-size ablation. The authors' promises confirm the last two points. Reviewer_KMnW (8, confidence 5) praised the formulation in one sentence. KMnW then listed faults: misdescribed literature, which the authors conceded, and a needlessly intricate derivation. KMnW did not ask for baselines. Reviewer_y4bh's 7 rests on "original and technically sound" and also asks for baselines. Reviewer_YnSg gave 7 at confidence 5 after 2 hours, while writing "I am unable to judge whether the proposed method is an improvement". YnSg also said the compressive-sensing tests use pixel-domain undersampling, but Section 5 uses random projections. YnSg was right that Eq. 3 is Stein's identity. The meta-review concedes that "the innovation over [19] isn't strong enough". It omits the missing baselines that three reviewers raised, and it gives no case for a spotlight.

**Committee B (reject).** Reviewer_GwuL (4) asked for baselines from the start. After the rebuttal, GwuL said the promised PnP/RED comparisons "should have been included in the main submission". GwuL asked for a test of "whether the SVD techniques help in any way". The paper's own Section 2 names candidates: [15, 23] already sample such problems with non-annealed Langevin. So the rebuttal's "there is no alternative in the literature" contradicts the paper. GwuL also cited a post-deadline preprint and wrongly said NCSNv2 is not a denoiser. Reviewers d1T5, kSnH and VyNV first rated 7, 7 and 6, and none of them raised baselines. Between 6 and 14 September each dropped two points, citing baselines and Section 3 clarity. kSnH's two-paragraph review never raised the derivation issues that its edit cites. d1T5 worried about instability near σ_i s_j = σ_0. That worry does not hold: the Eq. 12 step sizes cancel the singular factor, so the drift stays finite. The meta-review opens with VyNV's misreading of SNIPS as "an approximate MMSE estimator".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A questioned the novelty over [19], the missing step-size ablation, and whether posterior samples suit medical imaging. Only A caught three minor, conceded faults: the uncredited Stein-Tweedie identity, the misdescribed PnP/RED literature and the missing Bigdeli et al. reference. Only B treated the admitted approximation in the derivation as a flaw; nVFq saw it and thought it held. Only B asked whether prior samplers on the same prior do as well, and tied the visual quality to NCSNv2. B's points test the claim that the SVD construction is needed. Most of A's points concern framing and citations. Neither committee noted that Table 1, the only PSNR table, covers 8 CelebA images.
