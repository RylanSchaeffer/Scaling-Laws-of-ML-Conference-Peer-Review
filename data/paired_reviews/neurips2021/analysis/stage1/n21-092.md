**Verdict.** A recommended a spotlight and B a poster because A's reviewers scrutinised less and scored higher, not because the committees disputed any fact. Both accepted the same result: vanilla alternating SGD reaches O(ε^-2) samples because y*(x) is smooth (Lemma 2). Both area chairs followed their mean rating, 7.75 in A and 6.25 in B. A's three 8s rest on short reviews that took the breadth claim at face value. That claim is that the analysis "improves or matches" the best rate in every special case. B's reviewers tested the special cases and found real, fixable limits, and the authors conceded them. Neither committee found a fatal flaw. The spotlight rests on A's less-checked scores.

**Committee A.** Ratings 8, 8, 8, 7, from 2 to 5 hours per review. Reviewer_PMym gave an 8 with a two-sentence review at confidence 3 ("Math/other details were not carefully checked"). Its only request was experiments. Reviewer_vxWe gave an 8 at the same confidence, called the proof technique "fairly standard", and named no weakness. Reviewer_WgWk (8, confidence 4) gave the most specific praise: Lemma 2 is easy to prove, but its use in the proof is not. Reviewer_TdaR (7) called the novelty "merely" Lemma 2 and traced the algorithm and estimator to Ghadimi and Wang. The paper confirms this (Sections 2.1 and 2.2). After the rebuttal, the only reviewer post was PMym's one-line thanks for the experiment. The AC wrote three sentences that cite "consensus" and call the analysis "tight". The paper claims only a "tighter" analysis and proves no lower bound.

**Committee B.** Ratings 7, 6, 6, 6. Reviewer_tqUa (7, 12 hours) checked the bilevel proofs. It found a constant error in eq. (48) and a hidden log factor. It also found a min-max assumption that no function meets: f Lipschitz and strongly concave in y over all of R^d'. Reviewer_b2gP (6, 20 hours) noted that the min-max result needs an extra Lipschitz assumption. The authors conceded that the result "complements, rather than improves" Lin et al. Reviewer_r5Xc and Reviewer_wdT9 pressed on κ. The κ-optimal rate needs T = Θ(κ^4) inner steps, and the rebuttal compared with STABLE in ε only. Two objections were wrong, and the rebuttal corrected them. b2gP read the compositional result as quadratic-only; wdT9 thought two loops were necessary. b2gP then updated its score; the text implies a rise to the final 6. No reviewer went above 7. The AC wrote "All reviewers give positive evaluation on this paper. So do I." It also required a κ comparison in the bilevel table.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A questioned novelty (TdaR, vxWe) and the missing conclusion (TdaR). Here the weighting is reversed. A saw thin novelty and still scored higher. B called the technique "new" (b2gP, wdT9) and scored lower. Only B raised these issues:

- the impossible min-max assumption and the "complements" concession;
- the κ-dependence and the inner loop against STABLE;
- the hidden log factor and the eq. (48) constant;
- restrictive unconstrained assumptions and step sizes fixed by K;
- the smoothness assumption on y*(θ) in the actor-critic result.
