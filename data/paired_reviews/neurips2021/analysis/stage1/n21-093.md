**Verdict.** Committee A rated the paper higher (6, 4, 7; mean 5.67) but rejected it. Committee B rated it lower (5, 7, 6, 3; mean 5.25) but accepted it. The cause is how each area chair (AC) handled one negative reviewer who did not answer the rebuttal. A's AC let Reviewer_egt5's 4 outweigh a 6 and a 7, and gave no technical reason. B's AC set aside Reviewer_mowT's 3. B also had a champion, Reviewer_1KQg, who argued in the discussion. Nobody in A posted after the rebuttal. Both committees saw the same paper: a light network that leads on small benchmarks, with unproven convergence and a loose link between the fixed-point motivation and the architecture. They weighted these facts differently.

**Committee A (reject).** egt5 (confidence 5, 12 hours) asked whether the kernel mixture can represent in-focus regions. The paper answers this: the kernel set contains the Dirac delta (line 72; lines 150–151). egt5 asked for a two-stage, blur-map-first baseline. Table 1 already compares three two-stage methods (JNB, EBDB, DMENet), though not the exact map-as-input variant. egt5's valid points are two missing citations (Aljadaany et al., CVPR 2019; Liu et al., TCSVT, March 2021) and no limitations paragraph in the main text. None of these is an error in the paper, and the camera-ready fixes all three. The rebuttal answered every point and added two experiments: a map-guided DPDNet (24.54 against 25.47 dB) and MPRNet (25.02 dB). egt5 posted no reply. Reviewer_e2uj (7, confidence 3) wrote a thin but correct review: Eq. (5) uses n where Eq. (1) uses ε, and convergence is not justified. Reviewer_uKyg (6, confidence 5) asked for stronger baselines and got them. Neither defended the paper afterwards. The meta-review says only that the paper "has both positive and negative sides" and that "Reviews on the negative side remained". A fourth reviewer never responded.

**Committee B (poster).** mowT (3, confidence 5) gave no decisive reason. Its points are design suggestions (unequal loss weights; a transformer instead of the Conv-LSTM), a GPU time the paper already calls "comparable" to SRN's, a non sequitur (Gaussian kernels denoise, so robustness tests are "unnecessary"), and a generic novelty charge. The AC rightly ignored the score. Reviewer_SBT5 (6) wrote five generic sentences while reporting 11 hours. The real debate was between Reviewer_SpFs (5) and 1KQg (7). SpFs asked why a network without an artifact-removal step works, and wanted HQS-style unrolling compared. In a second round the authors showed that HQS+CG takes 0.31 to 0.82 s against 0.04 s, and that an artifact-removal block adds 0.02 dB. SpFs then became "OK with both rejection / acceptance". 1KQg argued that the fixed-point iteration only motivates an efficient end-to-end architecture. The AC conceded that nobody knows why the method works, offered implicit regularisation from non-converged iterations as a guess, and accepted.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked about in-focus regions, generalised-Gaussian kernels, map-guided baselines and dual-pixel PSFs. Only B raised the missing artifact-removal step, the confusing "GSM" name (the final title adopts 1KQg's "Gaussian Kernel Mixture"), the scales-versus-iterations ablation and mowT's design points. Both raised convergence, Figure 3, limitations and other unrolling schemes. B's questions went deeper, yet B accepted. Its reviewers replied, so the authors could answer in two rounds. A's reviewers never replied.
