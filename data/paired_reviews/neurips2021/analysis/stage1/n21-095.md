**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected mainly because of which reviewers each drew and how hard they checked. Both saw the same paper: MGDA on the upper level of a MAML- or DARTS-style bilevel loop, convergence theorems under strong assumptions, and modest gains. A drew two champions, Reviewer_jJ4x (7) and Reviewer_e5P4 (8). Both took the theory and the results at face value, and neither posted in the discussion. A's two 5s were soft, and A's chair restated the positive reviews. B's four reviewers (5, 4, 4, 3) tested the claims. Reviewer_CNYc showed that Theorem 1 was false as stated, and the authors conceded a missing assumption. B also showed that MOML trades clean accuracy for PGD accuracy and wins mainly on a self-defined B-score. The rebuttal added experiments, but no B score moved, and B's chair followed the scores. Mostly the committees weighted the same facts differently; B also checked more. Only the camera-ready paper.pdf, without its appendix, was available for checking.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 5, 8, 5. e5P4's 8 is the highest of all eight ratings and rests on five bullets and two questions. jJ4x (2 hours, math "not carefully checked") called the paper the meta-learning analogue of Sener and Koltun. Reviewer_nv9K (confidence 5) described the method as a weighted sum with learnable weights and put the MOPSolver at the lower level. Algorithm 1 does neither: MGDA recomputes the weights at each upper-level step. He conceded that the weighted sum was the SOML baseline and moved up to weak reject, keeping "a recombination of all existing techniques". His Table 2 point holds: MOML beats SOML by 0.17 and 0.63 average points, with no error bars. Reviewer_MQP9 liked the paper and asked for error bounds and a rate. The meta-review paraphrases jJ4x and adds one line on related methods.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 4, 4, 3. CNYc gave a counterexample to Theorem 1, and the camera-ready now assumes f jointly continuous. CNYc also noted that Theorem 2 holds only as K → ∞ and under P-convexity, which the experiments do not meet. The abstract still claims convergence. His Table 1 reading is right: MOML dominates SOML only for BOIL, in 2 of 6 settings. Reviewer_7cN3 asked for a Pareto front, a PGD-trained MAML and the SOML weight search. The authors supplied them, and 7cN3 wrote that they "do provide more convincing evidence", but he kept 4 "after reading the other reviews". Reviewer_3cBH kept 5. Reviewer_kG42 (0.5 hours, confidence 1) is weak and ignores the SOML baseline. Still, his "big drop" is right: clean accuracy falls 5.8 to 9.6 points below the original methods, which the paper calls "slightly drops". Without kG42, B is 5, 4, 4. The meta-review cites only the scores.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only B raised:

- the false Theorem 1 and the gap between Theorem 2's assumptions and practice (CNYc, 7cN3);
- the self-defined B-score and the clean-accuracy cost (CNYc, 7cN3, kG42);
- no Pareto front, no hypervolume, and no other loss-weighting baselines in Sections 5.1-5.2 (3cBH, 7cN3);
- missing error bars and baselines not trained on adversarial data (3cBH, 7cN3, kG42).

Only A raised runtime (jJ4x), equivalence to DARTS (nv9K), approximation-error bounds and conditional meta-learning (MQP9), and preferences over objectives (e5P4). Both raised limited novelty, small gains, dense writing, a single Pareto solution, and the choice of SOML weights. A's jJ4x counted the same combination of known parts as a strength.
