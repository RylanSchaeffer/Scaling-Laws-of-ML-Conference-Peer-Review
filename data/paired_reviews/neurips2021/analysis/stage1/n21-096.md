**Verdict.** Committee A rejected and Committee B gave a spotlight, but the two committees mostly saw the same facts and weighted them differently. Both saw the privileged training: the executor learns from programs synthesized on the full map, and the hallucinator learns from true states. Both saw a user-supplied state abstraction and small static grids, and both liked the core idea. A's careful critics, Reviewer_kENU and Reviewer_EBwR, held these limits against the paper. Three B reviewers rated 7 anyway. A also drew two thin 4s at confidence 3. A's AC then made novelty the main reason, from two papers that no reviewer had cited. In B, one rating rose to 7 with no written reason, and the AC chose a spotlight on enthusiasm. Reviewer draw and the two ACs, not different evidence, separate the outcomes.

**Committee A (7, 4, 4, 4, 5; reject).** Reviewer_aK3Q (7, confidence 5, 11 h) ran the code but never joined the privileged-training debate. Reviewer_Wt3P (4, 3.5 h) asked four questions, named no flaw and never replied. Reviewer_1PbL (4) found the paper hard to follow. B's Reviewer_SkvM made two of the same points (options used before definition, Section 2 before the formalism) and called them minor. 1PbL also wrote that the paper compares "only to ablations", which Table 1 contradicts. Reviewer_kENU's factual points hold: full-map training, small MLP baselines, Relational below End-to-end, and no TAMP citation. kENU conceded the Table 2 ablations and accepted the baselines after new runs. It kept 4 for clarity, privileged training and grounding in planning work. Reviewer_EBwR read the code and lowered 6 to 5. It wanted a neural controller over the same options, which is a fair request. But it overlooked Table 2, which holds the options fixed (optimistic 0.42, random 0.48, MPPS 0.70). The AC cited TD-VAE and POMCP after the discussion. Its novelty argument targets the generative model, which the paper credits to world models. kENU had called the approach to partial observability "a worthwhile contribution on its own".

**Committee B (7, 4, 7, 7; spotlight).** Reviewer_F1k5 (7) engaged most. It got a new non-deterministic test (MPPS 0.47, oracle 0.50, baselines 0.20-0.22) and a fix to a related-work overclaim. Reviewer_SkvM (7) found the paper "clear and easy to follow" and did not upgrade. Reviewer_DgEc's text says the work is not "enough to warranty an acceptance". Yet its rating, edited on 2021-08-31, is 7, and DgEc posted no comment. Reviewer_456H (4, 2 h) misread two things. Stone and water zones defeat its 25-step sweep of the map. The oracle shares the MPPS executor, so executor training cannot explain the small gap to the oracle. 456H never replied. No reviewer rated above 7. The AC wrote that the method does not depend on "ground truth programs"; that holds at test time only.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A probed the baselines: network size, Relational tuning, recurrence and a neural option controller. Only A questioned the framing as program synthesis rather than task planning. Only A's AC raised POMCP and TD-VAE; the camera-ready cites neither. Only B argued that partial observability is mild in small static maps. Both noted the deterministic setting, but only B asked for a test. The A-only findings are real but not fatal. Both committees noticed that more hallucinator samples barely change mean reward (Figure 6: 0.930-0.940). So the robustness claim rests on lower variance alone.
