**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Seven of eight reviewers rated the paper 6 or higher, all eight found it clear, and none found an error in a result. The flaws were presentational: a reversed condition in Eq. 4, Table 1 notation, and limitations and training details in the appendix. Both committees saw the main weakness and accepted anyway. On direct regression, SAPE + FF beats SIREN by about 1 dB (28.09 against 27.03 on natural images). The large gains are on indirect-supervision tasks: silhouette IoU 0.928 against 0.779, mesh-transfer Chamfer 0.39 against 0.74. The committees weighed these facts the same way. The gap in mean rating (6.75 against 7.5) comes from one reviewer at each end. In A, Reviewer_SbWq gave 5 because the submission had no quantitative ablation of spatial against global progression. That was correct. The rebuttal ablation, now Table 1's Progressive FF row, supports the spatial component on four of five columns, but not on Thingi10K (0.981 against 0.98). SbWq never replied. In B, Reviewer_69T8 gave 9 after 2 hours, named no weakness of substance, and added one sentence after the rebuttal. That 9 is not evidence of a stronger paper, and B's AC did not act on it. Neither AC discussed a tier above poster. paper_submitted.pdf is missing; I checked claims against the camera-ready paper.pdf.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 8, 7, 5). Reviewer_bcJK (7 hours) wrote the most careful review and found the Eq. 4 error and an unbolded tie at 0.928 in Table 1. Reviewer_FBfi gave 8 and floated a spotlight after 2 hours, as a self-declared non-expert who called most gains "incremental". That is weak evidence of quality. Reviewer_cagt questioned the linear unmasking policy, the missing local-bandwidth baseline and the cost. cagt rose to 7 on storage figures, a tiny-NeRF result and a motivation reference. The tiny-NeRF gain is 0.32 dB, below the reported spread of about 0.9 dB, and the camera-ready main text has neither result. The AC set novelty against coarse-to-fine NeRF schemes and credited the spatial adaptivity, which only SbWq's requested ablation isolates across tasks.

Committee B (8, 9, 6, 7). Reviewer_SEHH (8, 6 hours) said that SAPE always gets a larger σ than the baselines, and that appendix Section 5.3 breaks the paper's "double the σ" rule (20 against 12). The reply did not explain 20 against 12, and I could not check the appendix. Reviewer_nnVB found the same Eq. 4 error and the wrong τ, and said SIREN is about as good on images and occupancy. Table 1 shows margins there of about 1 dB and 0.02 to 0.04 IoU. nnVB kept 6. Reviewer_6j88, a self-declared non-expert at confidence 2, cited the same SIREN margin. 6j88 rose to 7 on promises to expand the motivation and move the limitations into the main text. The AC wrote two generic sentences.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these:

- no quantitative spatial-against-global ablation (SbWq);
- a hand-set linear unmasking policy instead of a learned one (SbWq, cagt);
- no baseline that estimates local bandwidth directly by DCT, FFT or mesh Laplacian (cagt);
- no NeRF test, no codec comparison, and the storage cost (cagt, SbWq);
- training time and parameter counts only in the appendix (SbWq, cagt).

The paper confirms the gaps in the first, third and fifth. Only B raised the σ given to SAPE (SEHH), the τ error and Φ notation (nnVB), held-out data for early stopping (69T8), and hand-picked images and soft against hard masking (6j88). None of these issues changed a decision.
