**Verdict.** The two committees weighed the same fact in opposite ways. SSMF is SMF (Hooi et al., 2019) plus a memory of several seasonal regimes that MDL selects. The submission's Table 1 gives SMF every listed property except regime shifts. In A, Reviewer_ppBZ called this an extension of low novelty, and the chair agreed. In B, Reviewer_Fqt5 named the same difference and called the paper "a valuable contribution". The submission and the SMF paper confirm ppBZ's specifics. Sections 4.1-4.2 restate SMF's update and forecast rules with a regime index and do not cite SMF. Two of the three datasets come from SMF's sources (NYC taxi trips, weekly US disease counts). Figure 4 repeats the layout of SMF's Figure 7 and its "bars and restaurants" reading. SMF's first author, Bryan Hooi, is an SSMF author, so this is self-extension that no reviewer could see. Two process gaps widened the split. The authors answered all four B reviewers and no A reviewer. B's two 7s are thin. A also found checkable experimental gaps that B missed. Neither committee found a technical flaw. The paper was accepted because one committee recommended it.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 4, 5. Reviewer_ppBZ (4) made the decisive case: the SMF overlap, gains that may come from the single COVID shift, no naive baseline that copies the previous week, and no error bars, which SMF reported. The submission and SMF confirm the facts behind each point. Reviewer_fpwd's review still praises a "principled" method with "compelling" results. On 2021-09-02 it lowered its score and cited ppBZ's point and "the authors not responding". Reviewer_Mjyk called the regime idea "very novel" but saw no advantage over SMF without it. Mjyk also asked for the data sparsity, which Table 3 gives, at confidence 5. The three-sentence meta-review says "the reviewers identified novelty as a main concern". Only ppBZ did.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 5, 7, 6. Reviewer_ZNwm (7, 2 hours) wrote one line of praise and three comments. Reviewer_Fqt5 (7, 10 hours claimed) wrote four sentences of praise and named no weakness. Neither replied to the authors. Reviewer_CeEZ (6) wrote the one careful review. It questioned the fixed k = 15, which SMF also used, and a Gaussian error beside a nonzero-count model cost. It praised the positioning against prior work. Reviewer_gthx (5) asked for the tensor methods cited in the related work as baselines and stayed unconvinced after the reply. The chair waived this ("I understand the choice to focus on matrix factorisation methods") and accepted on the majority. No B review looks at SMF beyond the paper's own description of it.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- the depth of the overlap with SMF (ppBZ);
- gains that may come from one COVID shift, with regime analysis for one dataset only (ppBZ);
- no error bars; the camera-ready adds them, and SSMF's and SMF's error bars overlap on NYC-YT and DISEASE (ppBZ, fpwd);
- no naive or classical forecasting baseline (ppBZ);
- unbounded growth of the regime memory (Mjyk).

Only B raised:

- the fixed k = 15 across methods (CeEZ);
- Gaussian errors beside a nonzero-count model cost (CeEZ);
- cited tensor methods left uncompared (gthx);
- unclear case-study maps (gthx);
- the use of known regime-change times (ZNwm).

Both asked for deep-learning or broader baselines and for tests at other sparsity levels. Both noted the missing limitations section.
