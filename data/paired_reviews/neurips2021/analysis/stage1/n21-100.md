**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. Every reviewer valued the new problem, online label shift, and its simple reduction to online learning: a confusion-matrix estimator gives unbiased losses and gradients without labels (Eq. 3, Theorem 1). No reviewer found an error in this core. The paper confirms the weaknesses that reviewers raised. Convexity holds only approximately (l.161), the hold-out estimate of the loss is a step function (l.169-172), estimation error is set to zero (l.123-124), and the regret compares only to one fixed re-weighting (Eq. 6). Both committees treated these as limits of scope, not as errors in a key result. Ratings sat between 5 and 7 (means 6.0 and 6.25). Seven of eight reviewers chose a confidence level that says the math was not carefully checked, and no score moved after the rebuttal. The committees differ in depth, not in direction.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 6, 6, 5; confidences 2, 3, 3, 2). The 5 is the most careful review. Reviewer_e9d1 says that Assumption 1 does not hold, that confusion-matrix noise is ignored, and that FTL's sublinear regret comes from Assumption 2 with δ=0. The paper bears out all three; the authors answered the last with "You are correct". The 7 is the weakest evidence. Reviewer_yNzP (confidence 2, 20 hours claimed) wrote a short review that expects the Optimal Fixed Classifier to lower-bound every error. The paper defines it as the best fixed re-weighting and says that OGD beats it (l.277-280). Reviewer_4NjZ's points (approximate convexity, δ>0 only in the appendix, three seeds) are all correct. Only 4NjZ replied to the rebuttal, to keep the 6. The AC accepted on novelty, then added its own critique. It noted that Theorem 1 uses a Jacobian of the confusion matrix with no differentiability assumption, which a finite sample never meets. It also judged that the appendix plots do not show that the assumptions "hold in practice". The camera-ready adds the differentiability and finite-gradient assumptions but still calls the assumptions "mild and empirically verifiable".

Committee B (7, 6, 6, 6; confidences 3, 3, 3, 4). Reviewer_EUMs gave the 7. It says the theorems and proofs "appear rigorous" but "I have not checked the proofs". Its main concern, a fixed p(x|y) in medicine or credit scoring, is fair but general. The three 6s each named a weakness and judged the merits larger. Reviewer_qmA9 objected that the regret compares only to a fixed classifier while q_t changes; Eq. 6 confirms this. It held that view through two exchanges. Reviewer_BpAt spotted that the arXiv validation set is a later time slice, against Q0 = Q_train (l.80, l.286-291). The authors conceded and reran: errors rose 0.2 to 0.6 points, in the same order. BpAt also saw that the known-label OGD oracle beats label-free OGD in all seven columns, by 0.03 to 0.14 points. Reviewer_r9Tm rightly calls the arXiv gain small (27.21% to 25.52%), but its naming complaint misreads l.62, where the paper uses both terms as synonyms. Five new rebuttal results moved no score. The AC's "uniformly positive reviews" and "comprehensive experimental study" restate the scores and omit qmA9's open objection.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A questioned the theory itself: approximate convexity (4NjZ, e9d1), the strength of symmetric optimality (e9d1, AC), zero estimation error (e9d1) and implicit differentiability (AC). Only A asked for more seeds and a shift-magnitude ablation (4NjZ, zQxb). Only B questioned the label-shift assumption on real data (EUMs, BpAt) and the arXiv split (BpAt). Only B asked for a labelled-oracle baseline (BpAt) and for large label spaces (r9Tm). None of these changed a decision.
