**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Nearly every reviewer saw SS-Conv as built from the steerable kernels of [24] and the submanifold sparse convolution of [9]. Every reviewer also credited its practical value. Table 1 shows about twice the speed of ST-Conv (293 against 148 FPS). The pipeline reaches 99.2 ADD(S) on LineMOD and 41.7 against 28.2 at 5°5cm on REAL275. Committee B was uniformly lukewarm (6, 6, 6, 7), and its AC followed that consensus. Committee A was split (5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9), and its AC followed the majority. A's top scores are weak evidence: the 8 is generic praise, and the 9 overstates Table 3. A's two 5s argue novelty for NeurIPS and find no error in the paper. No reviewer in either committee found a checkable error that could sink the paper. The same facts got the same weight and the same decision.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 5, 6, 5, 8, 9). Reviewer_aue9 (9, confidence 5) read closely, but the score rests on enthusiasm ("tremendous practical impact"). Its claim that the method "outperforms all methods on pose and size" is wrong: FS-Net leads IoU50, 92.2 against 79.1. Reviewer_hnKB (8) wrote six sentences of generic praise and never joined the discussion. The authors sent one novelty defense to four reviewers: a new strided SS-Conv with a Gaussian-blurred kernel, plus the Feature-Steering refinement stage. Reviewer_hf1K rose from 6 to 7, and Reviewer_BHAN softened. Reviewer_viJM held at 5: the strided convolution is an unablated detail, and the pipeline suits a vision venue. The paper supports viJM. The submission never mentions a strided SS-Conv, and Fig. 2 downsamples by pooling. Footnote 1 of the camera-ready says the network uses only stride-1 convolutions with average pooling, following Weiler et al. Reviewer_zbbB ended at 5, "more and more convinced" that the work misses the NeurIPS bar. The same comment says "I have upgraded my recommendation", so the direction of the edit is unclear. The meta-review says the rebuttal resolved "most of reviewer's concerns" and cites "highest rate was 9!". In fact, both reviewers below 6 kept their objection.

Committee B (6, 6, 6, 7). The rebuttal lifted Reviewer_BhGd and Reviewer_zYbA to 6. It used clarifications and new experiments, for example ModelNet40 under arbitrary rotation (80.8 against 21.9 for SP-Conv). Both kept their novelty doubts; BhGd says they are why "it is not a 7 or higher". Two review points are wrong. zYbA "did not find comparisons" to [24], but Table 1 is that comparison. Reviewer_gTpb (7, a self-declared non-expert) found no Dense-Conv details, but l.258-264 give them. Reviewer_dFJh kept 6 and called the new place-recognition baseline weak. The meta-review copies gTpb's summary nearly word for word. Its one new sentence says that all reviewers agree on soundness and efficiency. It does not mention novelty.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A argued venue fit: "NeurIPS is not a vision conference" (viJM, with zbbB). Only A noted that without the second stage the method trails G2L, 97.2 against 98.7, and asked its cost (hf1K). Only A raised the IoU50 gap to FS-Net (BHAN) and one-stage real-time tracking (zbbB). Only B raised these issues:

- unclear exposition of Sec. 3, and m undefined at l.137 (zYbA);
- no stated rotation parameterization, and no comparison with Minkowski Engine (dFJh);
- no named segmentation network, and "marginally" used for large gaps at l.138 and l.267 (BhGd);
- memory use and data sparsity not quantified (gTpb).

None of these changed a decision.
