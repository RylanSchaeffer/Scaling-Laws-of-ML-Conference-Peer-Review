**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts in opposite ways. Both saw strong, fine-grained edits made by adding a new editing protocol to DatasetGAN/SemanticGAN, StyleGAN2 and a pSp encoder. The paper states this dependence itself (lines 102-109, 124-133). Committee A's reviewers called the result an incremental composition. Committee B's reviewers knew the same dependence but judged the masked optimization and transferable editing vectors new and useful. The kind of objection then shaped the rebuttal. B's objections were empirical, new experiments answered them, and two B ratings rose. A's objection was a judgment of novelty, which no experiment answers. Only one A reviewer replied, and it did not move. Neither area chair (AC) added a reading of its own. A's AC rejected because "none of the reviewers advocated"; B's accepted because "Scores have been increased".

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 4, 3, 5, 5, mean 4.25. Reviewer_7mk5 made the sharpest case, and the paper confirms it: the joint model and the inversion are prior work, and the advantage of 16 to 30 labels comes from DatasetGAN. Reviewer_zqNn (3) added Paint by Word, which the paper does not cite. Reviewer_M7u3 wrote the most constructive review. It asked for a baseline that edits pixels directly, and it found real artifacts: a deleted cat ear in Figure 4 leaves faint outlines, and the shrunk wheels in Figure 5 leave grey smudges. Reviewer_9mLQ's text is positive throughout, yet it rated 5. It asked whether the edit strength can be scaled; s_edit and Figures 9 and 10 already show this. The rebuttal could only restate the protocol in words. Only zqNn answered: "the limited novelty is obvious". The AC's "four reviewers unanimously" overstates 9mLQ.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 8, 6, 8, mean 7.33. Reviewer_Wd7V (4 before the rebuttal) wrote the most checkable review, and most of it is right. FID uses 400 images (line 258). Footnote 2 links an unofficial LocalEditing repository. Table 1 compares edits at unit scale, and Figure 10 puts InterFaceGAN at about (0.90, 0.70), level with plain EditGAN; the paper itself says "on-par" (line 288). The claim at lines 239-240 has no baseline on fine edits. Its concern about a user-drawn mask r is wrong: Eq. (1) builds r automatically. The authors answered with FID on 4000 images, KID, e4e and a StyleGAN2 Distillation baseline. Wd7V rose to 6 but still wanted Table 1 "removed or ... rescaled". Reviewer_nroC rose to 8, won over by the transfer of edit vectors. Reviewer_6weF's 8 is weak evidence: 2.5 hours, three sentences of assessment, no engagement. It calls the evaluation "extensive", but baselines appear only on the face smile benchmark. No one noted that the Distillation baseline beat EditGAN on accuracy (98.3 vs 91.5) and identity (0.78 vs 0.70).

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A treated novelty as decisive. Only A named Paint by Word and SEAN, asked for the direct-edit baseline, and pointed to visible artifacts in Figures 4 and 5. Only B probed the evaluation: the FID sample size, unmatched scales, the LocalEditing code and the missing baselines. Only B named Linear Semantics in GANs. Both sides noted the DatasetGAN dependence, the restriction to shape edits with no colour or texture changes, and the question of editing one wheel or one eye alone. So the split is not about facts. A asked whether the method is new; B asked whether it works and is useful.
