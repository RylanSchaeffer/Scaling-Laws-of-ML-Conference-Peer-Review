## Verdict

Both committees accepted the paper unanimously; they differ by one tier. The cause is reviewer draw and AC framing, not information. Committee A drew Reviewer_e5Ff, whose 9 rests on enthusiasm for the direction, and its AC paraphrased that review. Committee B drew two reviewers who reached only 6 after the rebuttal, and its AC followed the 6.75 mean to a poster. The information ran against the outcome. Only Committee A heard the authors concede that a tuned Transformer beats the model by 3 to 4 BLEU on MT; the camera-ready now calls its MT results "largely negative". A's AC never mentioned this and called the results "strong". B's rebuttal experiments mostly favoured the method, yet B stayed at poster. Both committees saw the same core facts: a novel latent grammar, respectable small-scale results and a very expensive dynamic program. A weighted them with more enthusiasm. The PDF in this folder is the camera-ready version (author named, Transformer row added), so I checked claims about missing content against the rebuttal and the camera-ready changes.

## Committees

Committee A (Spotlight; ratings 7, 7, 7, 9; mean 7.5). Reviewer_e5Ff spent 2 hours, lists five general strengths, asks no question, and restates the paper's own limitation as its only weakness. It says the model "outperforms traditional seq2seq models substantially" on MT and shows "better sample efficiency". The MT table contradicts the first point: the base Neural QCFG scores 23.5 BLEU against the LSTM's 25.1. No experiment varies training-set size, so the second point is unsupported. The three 7s are specific. Reviewer_FyUp and Reviewer_fSHR rightly flag the missing Transformer baseline. Reviewer_fSHR then raised the rating to 7 after the reply that the Transformer wins. The meta-review follows Reviewer_e5Ff's structure and wording, and it chooses to "downplay" scale because sample efficiency is a "stated goal".

Committee B (Poster; ratings 6, 8, 7, 6; mean 6.75). Reviewer_3r7i (8) wrote the most useful review in either committee. It asks for a pipelined baseline and asks how the per-task nonterminal counts were chosen. The rebuttal's pipelined numbers (SCAN jump 60.4 vs 96.8, length 38.3 vs 95.7) satisfied it, but its question about selection on dev versus test stayed open. Reviewer_9RED (from 5) and Reviewer_sHgm moved to 6. Their reservations are positioning against NQG and per-dataset tuning, not errors. Reviewer_sHgm partly misreads the MT test set as template-generated; only the 8 daxy sentences are, and the QCFG scores 100% on them. Reviewer_LKem (confidence 2) says "I am not an expert" and misreads the authors' answer about the autoregressive LM; its 7 carries little weight. The meta-review rests on "All reviewers agree the acceptance" and adds Reviewer_sHgm's GeoQuery request.

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A: the missing Transformer baseline on MT and the 3 to 4 BLEU deficit it revealed; the tiny, formulaic MT data; COGS, CFQ and CLOSURE; pseudocode and a shorter introduction. The Transformer result is the one issue that should have lowered a score. It lowered none.

Only B: no pipelined or non-neural QCFG baseline; whether NQG-T5 really uses SCAN-specific knowledge (the authors called this "a fair point"); per-dataset hyperparameters and rule constraints, with SCAN falling below 1% on two splits without the constraints; the sample-and-rescore decoder; the untested StylePTB easy and medium tasks. These issues held two reviewers at 6 and the AC at poster, although the rebuttal answered most of them in the method's favour.
