**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same weaknesses differently, and each took a different result as the contribution. No reviewer found an error in a result. Both committees found the lower bound weak: Theorem 1 holds only for CTS with Gaussian priors, on one K = 2 instance, under a clipped greedy regret. Both also saw that the 1/Δ² comes from the greedy-regret benchmark, not from Thompson sampling. In A, Reviewer_ah1S noted that every action in the instance beats 0.75·OPT. Table 1 confirms this, so the usual approximation regret there is zero. In B, Reviewer_1vtP noted that swapping the means of u1 and u4 removes the 1/Δ² term. Recomputing Table 1 confirms this: {u3,u4} moves from 0.008 + 0.52Δ below the greedy solution to 0.008 − 0.51Δ above it, and OPT stays 0.97. The benchmark cuts both ways. It makes the upper bound stronger than the usual approximation guarantee, and it makes the lower bound weak evidence of hardness. A valued the upper bound, the first for CTS with a common approximation oracle. B judged the paper as its title presents it: a hardness analysis.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 7, 6. Reviewer_ah1S (10 hours) wrote the most thorough review in either committee. It proposed that the real story is "a positive result on the greedy regret", a story that "requires drastic changes". The rebuttal adopted it: CTS with Greedy "breaks the misconception" that TS fails with approximation oracles. The camera-ready abstract now ends on that phrase. Reviewer_wYkT then raised to 6, finding the result "more significant than I thought". In light of the response, ah1S closed "inclined towards accepting". Reviewer_nW4R (7) found that Section 6.2 measures regret against the lowest-reward greedy solution, the most lenient benchmark, yet calls it the worst case. Reviewer_RTgq noted the Beta and Gaussian prior mismatch. A treated all of these as limits, not flaws. The chair's two sentences follow the scores: the lower bound is "a bit weak" but "a useful complement".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 7, 4, 5, 4. Reviewer_Lbtx (4, confidence 5, 3 hours) read closely. Theorem 1 covers one instance, Δ has no stated link to Δ_min, and "almost matching" overclaims. The paper confirms all three. Reviewer_1vtP (4, 2 hours) made the swap argument. The authors replied with a two-armed bandit analogy and did not dispute the numbers. The rebuttal also conceded that "the proof technique is indeed not very difficult" and that CUCB has a smaller bound on bar-graph PMC. After private discussion, Reviewer_kJUS dropped from 6 to 5. Reviewer_x5US's 7 restates the abstract, praises clarity and asks only for experiments. It is no evidence of quality. The chair listed Lbtx's edge-versus-node feedback objection first. That objection applies to the whole CMAB line the paper follows, and the chair did not engage with the authors' reply.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- the lenient benchmark in Section 6.2 (nW4R);
- the ε⁻⁶ constant with ε below Δ (ah1S);
- the earlier Ω(log T/Δ²) greedy-regret lower bound of Lin et al., which the camera-ready now cites (ah1S);
- monotone rewards forfeiting TS's advantage, a conflation the authors corrected (ah1S);
- whether a per-round greedy oracle is standard (wYkT).

Only B raised edge-level feedback, the crude proof technique, the link between Δ and Δ_min, and the absence of experiments. Both raised the CTS-only, single-instance lower bound, the benchmark-driven 1/Δ², the clipped regret, the prior mismatch, the greedy-only scope and the missing problem definitions. A called these limits of a first positive result. B called them reasons to reject a hardness paper.
