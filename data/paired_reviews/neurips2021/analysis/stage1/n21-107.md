**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected because the two committees weighted the same gaps differently, and the difference follows reviewer expertise. Both committees saw three gaps. Section 6 compares MMU with no earlier PSD-factorization algorithm (Reviewer_8tm7 in A; Reviewer_Ux46 and the chair in B). The paper gives no convergence rate and no per-iteration cost (8tm7, Reviewer_k2ys; Ux46, Reviewer_DJs1). The algorithm starts only on page 5 (k2ys; DJs1). The submission confirms all three. A's reviewers judged the paper as mathematics: a non-obvious matrix extension of Lee-Seung, with a monotonicity proof through Lieb's concavity theorem. Two of them proposed shorter proofs of Lemma 3, so they read the appendix. They treated the gaps as revisions and gave four 7s. B's reviewers had less confidence (2, 3, 3) and judged the paper as an algorithm that must beat baselines. Reviewer_LdXA and DJs1 misread it. B's chair called the paper "mainly empirical", but its experiments fill about 1.5 of 9 pages. In B the rebuttal skipped Ux46's main request, and no score moved.

**Committee A (poster).** Reviewer_k2ys (confidence 5) read the proofs and the code, and noted that the code runs a damped update that the theorems do not cover. Reviewer_QbiV raised the same damping point and the unstated stopping rule. QbiV also noted that the analysis needs positive definite factors, which slack matrices of polytopes never have. 8tm7 asked for the comparison with projected gradient and coordinate descent. The authors promised it, with a comparison table and a per-update cost. No reviewer had to move from 7. The camera-ready adds a damping paragraph and a citation for the Cardano step, but no comparison and no cost analysis. Reviewer_wGDo (confidence 2) wrote two sentences of praise and no criticism; that 7 adds no evidence. The chair's three-sentence meta-review repeats the praise and names no weakness.

**Committee B (reject).** Ux46 wrote the strongest review in B, and its main points are correct. It has one error: it says Lee-Seung NMF minimizes KL divergence, but Eq. 25 is the Euclidean update. LdXA says the paper proves convergence to critical points and that MMU must handle "many SDP constraints". Both are wrong. L212-214 say MM gives no convergence guarantee, Theorem 2 covers fixed points only, and L149-151 say conjugation keeps the iterates PSD. LdXA also called the method "incremental" without naming what it combines. DJs1 spent one hour, is "not familiar" with the problem, and asked for a convergence-rate advantage over Lee-Seung. MMU reduces to Lee-Seung on diagonal inputs, so that request has no clear meaning. The authors told LdXA "This is again not true" and told DJs1 that the comparison "is not a meaningful task". They did not answer Ux46's request for experiments. No reviewer replied. The chair agreed with the reviewers.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- damping outside the analysis, and the stopping rule (k2ys, QbiV);
- positive definite factors against singular slack matrices (QbiV);
- shorter proofs through Cauchy-Schwarz and superadditivity (8tm7, QbiV);
- an earlier source for the Cardano step (k2ys);
- the meaning of "infinite-dimensional" building blocks (8tm7).

Only B raised novelty ("incremental"), the cost of SDP constraints, the KL objective, the size of slack matrices, a 7-dimensional NMF baseline, missing face data for the code, and experiments on quantum information. Both raised the missing comparisons, the convergence rate, the per-iteration cost, the long introduction, and narrow interest.
