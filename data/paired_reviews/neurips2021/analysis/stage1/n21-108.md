**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected because the committees weighed the same facts differently. The threshold fell between borderline 5s and 6s. Both committees saw a simple method built from known parts: SimNet [47], graph regularization [51] and an adversarial loss [8]. Both saw a new setting, gains of about 3 points over fused baselines (Table 7), and many results left to the Appendix. Both called the novelty limited. In A, the rebuttal answered specific questions, and two doubtful reviewers rose to 6. In B, the objections were novelty and presentation, which no rebuttal can fix, and no reviewer rose above 5. The one real disagreement is clarity. All three A reviewers called the paper clearly written. All four B reviewers raised presentation problems. Both chairs counted scores.

**Committee A (poster).** Reviewer_QXjD (2 hours) wrote "Novelty is low" and "the outcomes are obvious". The authors added a table of accuracy for rare, moderate and popular Car classes, and QXjD moved from 5 to 6. The table groups classes by test-set image counts, which is a weak proxy for web popularity. Reviewer_4wp8 (confidence 5) doubted the practicality of the setting and the novelty. Its final text still ends "so I vote negative", but the rating is 6. The edit date (2021-08-30) and the chair's note that 4wp8's motivation concern was "adequately addressed" point to a post-rebuttal raise. Reviewer_31ZF gave the only 7, at confidence 3, with generic praise. Its one concrete weakness, missing related work on similarity methods, is wrong: L54-58 and L104-110 cover them. 31ZF did not join the discussion. The meta-review follows the scores and does not mention novelty.

**Committee B (reject).** Reviewer_hoaz (10 hours) wrote the most careful review in either committee. It found the paper "not easy to follow" and asked whether transfer holds across meta-categories. That concern is correct: every experiment in the main text splits one dataset (L231-234). The rebuttal offered a conjecture and an ImageNet result, and hoaz kept 5: "it is fine to reject it as well". Reviewer_wyya kept 4 on novelty. Its MADA analogy is loose, but it grants that the core idea is new. Reviewer_K9m3 (5, 8 hours) asked about very dirty web data and the generalized setting. The authors answered both, one with a new table, but K9m3 never replied. Reviewer_bmx4 (3, confidence 5, one hour) reversed the direction of transfer and said the experiments use no real web data. L232-234 say the novel training set is web images. bmx4 conceded this only "to some extent" and raised its score "a bit". Its clarity points are valid: Section 3 has eight lines, and five of the ten Section 5 subsections only point to the Appendix. Without bmx4, B's mean is 4.67, so the outcome does not depend on it.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- cheap crowdsourced cleaning as an alternative to the setting (4wp8);
- ImageNet pre-training, which L235-237 already handle (4wp8);
- accuracy on rare against popular classes (QXjD);
- how the novel classes were chosen (QXjD);
- the absence of a formal model of label noise (31ZF).

Only B raised the hard-to-follow presentation, transfer across meta-categories, very dirty web data, the drop in the generalized setting, the overlap with DANN and MADA, and the release of scraped images. Both committees raised limited novelty and results left to the Appendix. A treated them as minor. B treated them as reasons to reject.
