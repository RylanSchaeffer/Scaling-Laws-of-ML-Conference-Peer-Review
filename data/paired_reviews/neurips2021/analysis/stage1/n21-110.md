**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. The submission was pure theory. It characterised equivariant functions for O(d), Lorentz and related groups through scalar products, using classical invariant theory. It ran no experiments (checklist: "We didn't run experiments"), and seven of nine reviewers named this as a main weakness. Both committees got the same anonymised GitHub repository in the rebuttal. It reran toy tasks from Finzi et al.'s EMLP paper and beat EMLP. That one rebuttal moved six reviewers up: Reviewer_D3qF and Reviewer_Rogp in A, and all four B reviewers. Both committees also made the authors drop "gauge" from the title. Both ACs judged the idea known outside ML but useful within it. Only the path differed. B began further below the line (3, 4, 6, 4) and ended higher (7, 6, 7, 5; mean 6.25 against 5.8), because each B reviewer's main objection was the one the rebuttal fixed. In both committees the decisive evidence lay outside the reviewed PDF and got a light check. Both missed a flaw: Propositions 8 and 9 claim the Lorentz characterisation for every function. It fails for discontinuous ones, because a nonzero null vector and the zero vector have the same inner products. The camera-ready adds "continuous". Three reviewers had vouched for the proofs.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 6, 7, 6, 4). Reviewer_Rogp (5 to 6) wrote the strongest review: classical results, uncited prior work (Gripaios et al., Haddadin), experiments "pressing and essential". Rogp also found that Eq. 17 counts each gravitational pair twice; the camera-ready adds the factor 1/2. Reviewer_D3qF (up to 6) called the method feature engineering and rejected "gauge". The experiments moved it, though it had "not thoroughly studied the code". Reviewer_K6Cj (6, confidence 2) correctly found no proof of Proposition 9. Reviewer_9FH3's 7 comes from a self-declared beginner (confidence 2) who names no weakness and never notes the missing experiments. Reviewer_WMkN's 4 rests on one uncited paper (Ling et al. 2016) and a generic point about unknown symmetries. WMkN posted two weeks late and never replied. The AC restated D3qF's gauge point and Rogp's view of the experiments, and did not mention the 4.

Committee B (7, 6, 7, 5). All four objected mainly to the missing experiments. Reviewer_f6cj called the paper complete as theory but gave 3, then rose to 7. Reviewer_jRKc (4 to 6) correctly found a missing conclusion and broken sentences. It then praised experiments "on synthetic and real data", but the record shows only toy tasks. Reviewer_xhVY (6 to 7) said the paper does not discuss O(3) graph models, but Section 2 covers irreps-based models and points to Appendix A. Reviewer_StjN (4 to 5) correctly said model building gets half a page. It called acceptance before a "(non-minor) rewrite" premature. The authors told StjN they reproduced its reference [c] (LieConv), but the repository uses EMLP, a different paper. StjN did not notice. The AC recounted the score rises, called the theory "a direct application" but valuable, and overrode StjN.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the double-counted energy (Rogp), the missing proof of Proposition 9 (K6Cj), feature engineering without a locality prior (D3qF) and Ling et al. (WMkN). The paper confirms the first two, and the camera-ready fixes both. Only B raised these issues:

- the missing conclusion and incomplete sentences (jRKc, confirmed);
- the purpose of the polynomial results (jRKc);
- no support for fields or vector-bundle sections (jRKc);
- which results are new (xhVY);
- too little on model building, and the need for a rewrite (StjN).

None of these issues changed a decision.
