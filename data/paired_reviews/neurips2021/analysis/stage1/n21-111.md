**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. All seven final ratings are 6 or 7: A gave 6, 7, 6 and B gave 7, 7, 6, 7. Both sides valued the same main contribution, a locally private SGD estimator whose regret matches the non-private rate in T for stochastic contexts. The adversarial LDP algorithms of [36] get T^{3/4}. No reviewer found an error in a regret theorem. Each committee found different weaknesses and treated them as revisions. In A, Reviewer_iyDf asked for lower bounds under the margin condition and flagged careless notation and a linear real-data regret curve. The submission confirms all three. Explanations and promises, not new results, moved iyDf from below threshold to 6. In B, Reviewer_ydgj found the only real error. Lemma 2.1 states the Gaussian mechanism without the ε < 1 condition of the cited theorem, yet Section 5 runs ε = 5. It touches only LDP-OLS's privacy claim and those runs; LDP-SGD's l2-ball mechanism is valid for any ε. The authors conceded and reran at ε = 0.5. No reviewer argued above 7, and both meta-reviews restate the score consensus.

**Committees**

Committee A (mean 6.33). Reviewer_iyDf (2 hours, "not an expert in ... differential privacy") wrote A's only critical review, and its claims hold. Theorems 3.3 and 4.1 have no matching lower bound. Assumption 5 uses h_sub but says "for some h > 0". LDP-SGD's real-data curve in Figure 2 is a straight line. iyDf then wrote "happy to up my score to 5", but the final rating is 6. Reviewer_tbHD (confidence 2) found the synthetic runs small (d = 2, K = 3 or 10), as Figure 2 shows. The authors sent runs with K and d up to 20; tbHD did not reply. Its claim that the paper "newly introduces" SGD to bandits is wrong (l.74-75 cite [12]). Reviewer_PwWa gave the top rating, 7 at confidence 4, in three sentences with no weakness. The AC praised novelty and timeliness in three sentences.

Committee B (mean 6.75). Reviewer_SZ1X (confidence 2) wrote the most careful review in either committee. Most of its notation points are right. Assumption 3 uses an undefined v, Theorem 3.3 names C1 and C2 but uses C, and the noise is sub-Gaussian at l.115 but bounded at l.126-127. Two points are wrong: a* is used (l.168), and e_i ⊗ θ* is a Kronecker product, so a vector. SZ1X also saw that the OLS mechanism perturbs sufficient statistics as in [29] and [36]. The authors conceded this, and a d versus √d gap. Reviewer_ydgj (2 hours, "no expert in contextual bandit") made the ε catch but wrongly wanted M_t inverted in Eq. (3). Reviewer_9jC7's summary copies the abstract, and its main review answers the reviewer-guideline questions in their own words. Reviewer_5SWK named no weakness and wrote "I cannot find limitations", though Section 6 lists open problems. No B reviewer replied to the rebuttal. The AC wrote one sentence: "the reviewers were all positive".
