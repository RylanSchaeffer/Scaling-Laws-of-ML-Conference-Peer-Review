**Verdict.** Committee B recommended more than Committee A because B gave little weight to a flaw both committees saw, and because its accept votes came from short, uncritical reviews. The flaw is in the user study, the main evidence. Participants predict which of two occluded images the classifier scores higher (L261-269). SAG displays exactly such scores. Only SAG users can click an option to highlight its patches in the graph, with labels such as "65% drop" (L285-287, Fig. 4c). A SAG group without the click aid scores 6.4 of 10 before and 6.8 after the explanation; the group with it scores 5.7 before and 8.6 after (Fig. 6a). Grad-CAM and I-GOS users fall from 6.3 to 5.3 and 5.4 (Fig. 5a). In A, Reviewer_2sbT and Reviewer_uoik made this point, and the chair rejected on it. In B, Reviewer_RJDW made it, accepted the reply that the extra information is the contribution, and rose from 5 to 6. B's chair named a "representation bias" and accepted anyway. The facts were the same. The weighting differed, and the paper supports A.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 4, 5, 5, 4, all at confidence 4, after 4 to 20 hours per review. Reviewer_2sbT gave the sharpest checkable argument: the click aid is SAG-only, "also supported by your figure 6a". The figure confirms it. Reviewer_uoik wrote that the test was "designed specifically to highlight the strengths of SAG" and that the baselines were shown as colour overlays. The authors confirmed using the original visualizations. uoik overstated one detail: the test options are not SAG nodes, so the display is a strong cue, not the literal answer. Reviewer_sSPC wanted localization and MS COCO experiments; its Eq. (1) objection was wrong, and it accepted the correction. Reviewer_ay96 is the weakest vote. Its main concern, that the graph adds nothing over the root nodes, is answered by the SAG/G ablation (5.8 against 8.6). Its final 5 rests on "as a non-expert" and "I share the same feeling as the others reviewers". No score moved. The chair extended uoik's point: users "aren't actually interested in predicting model confidence under occlusion".

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 4, 6, 7, 6. Each accept vote took 2 to 2.5 hours. Reviewer_cwRt (7) lists no weakness except terminology, says "the authors sufficiently provide evidence" without discussing the study design, and never joined the discussion. Reviewer_kZGR (6) says the steps "do not include much novelty", found the rebuttal "not really insightful", and kept 6. RJDW raised the fairness problem and the time cost, then rose to 6, partly because "I refer to the opinions of other reviewers". The only long review, Reviewer_gy9K (8 hours, rating 4), asked why predicting the network's output on sub-images defines a good explanation. That is the objection of A's chair. gy9K offered to raise the score if answered, and did not. B's chair wrote that "the most negative reviewer agrees", dropping gy9K's condition "if the motivation of the work can be well-explained".

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- the SAG-only click aid and the overlay display of the baselines (2sbT, uoik);
- only two baselines and no automatic metric (uoik);
- that most images have one MSE without overlap (2sbT; Table 2 gives 79-87%, which contradicts Table 1's mode of 2);
- t-tests on bounded scores, and missing code (2sbT, uoik);
- localization and MS COCO experiments (sSPC).

Only B raised thresholding versus maximal confidence in the MSE definition, faithfulness versus user predictions, and confusion with attention-based networks. Both raised runtime, ad hoc hyperparameters, missed patch interactions, modest novelty and the extra user time.
