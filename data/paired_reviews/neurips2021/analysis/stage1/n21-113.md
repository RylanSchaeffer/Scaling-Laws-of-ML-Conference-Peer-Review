**Verdict.** Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. RIFT adds a conditional mutual-information term to adversarial fine-tuning. It has the best mean in all eight settings, but by small margins: 1.0 to 2.9 points over the runner-up on IMDB and 0.5 to 1.2 on SNLI (Tables 1-2). No reviewer rated above 7 or found an error. Each committee had one reviewer who doubted the baselines: Reviewer_kRk6 in A and Reviewer_gPjn in B. Both got the same answer: every compared method already uses early stopping. kRk6 engaged and rose to 6. gPjn did not reply and stayed at 5. B's AC did not mention it. The authors reused the same rebuttal text in both forums, so both committees saw the same evidence. The committees weighed the same facts the same way. Neither pressed two weak points. In five of eight settings, the ± ranges of RIFT and the runner-up overlap, and the rebuttal reports one significance test (SNLI, BERT; p = 0.0425). The forgetting evidence is parameter-space L2 distance, which the paper's introduction calls an imperfect proxy, and RIFT is absent from Figure 2. I checked claims against the camera-ready paper.pdf (no appendix), because the submitted PDF is missing.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 7, all at confidence 3. Reviewer_jFCo (5 hours) found the gains "relatively marginal". jFCo also asked how RIFT reduces forgetting against the baselines. The reply pointed to the theory and the α ablation and asked what analysis would help. jFCo did not answer, and the paper still has no direct measure. kRk6 doubted that L2 distance shows forgetting and that the baseline is strong. kRk6 missed that the baselines already use the TRADES loss and early stopping. After a second reply (same β for all methods, Figure 4 trade-off curves), kRk6 wrote "The authors address most of my concerns" and raised the rating. Reviewer_NEaE (1.5 hours) gave 7 and deferred on baselines: robustness "is not my main area". The meta-review praises the paper in generic terms. Two of its three revision requests quote kRk6.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 5, 6, 7. Reviewer_8Qx5 wrote the longest review. It called the gains "non-negligible but limited" and flagged the cost in vanilla accuracy. The reply gave standard fine-tuning accuracy for BERT: 93.1% on IMDB and 89.2% on SNLI. Table 4 puts RIFT at 78.3% and 80.4%, so the cost is about 15 and 9 points. RIFT pays slightly less than the adversarial baseline. Reviewer_CwDX asked for a significance test on SNLI and accepted the Mann–Whitney result. gPjn's 5 rests on one point: no early-stopping baseline. The camera-ready says every method uses early stopping; the authors say the submitted appendix did too. Reviewer_aWT9 restates the abstract and says "all claims are well supported", but it doubts the L2 evidence. The meta-review repeats 8Qx5's reservations and says the rebuttal answered all questions.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A asked whether RIFT itself forgets less (jFCo) and whether the clean/robust trade-off was tuned fairly (kRk6). Only A asked about information-bottleneck defences that fail in vision (kRk6, AC), gradient obfuscation (kRk6), and the variational bound (NEaE; it is valid). Only B raised:

- the cost in vanilla accuracy against standard fine-tuning (8Qx5, AC);
- small batches for the contrastive loss (8Qx5);
- embedding-level training against word-level test attacks (CwDX);
- learning curves of clean and robust accuracy (gPjn);
- continual-learning and token-level tasks (8Qx5, CwDX).

Both raised the small SNLI gains, the L2 proxy, baseline strength, GLUE, the score function f_y and the NLP-only scope. None of these issues changed a decision.
