**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected on largely the same facts. Both rated the paper borderline: A 6, 6, 6, 5 and B 5, 6, 5, 5. Six of eight reviewers called the novelty limited: DIB-R plus standard graphics parts. In each committee, one reviewer found that the main text does not say what forces the disentanglement (FzMA in A, ShTU in B). Both rebuttals named a multi-view consistency loss that renders one view's predictions in another view. The submission omits it: Section 4 and Eq. 7 show only a loss between the input image and its re-rendering. The committees mapped these facts to different scores. A's AV84 and LsqZ called the novelty limited but rated 6, because unsupervised single-image recovery of shape, material and light is hard. B's ShTU, 8HSK and SWGM judged the renderer as the contribution, found every part pre-existing, and rated 5. The discussions also differed. In A, the authors sent LSUN real-image results and turntable videos through an anonymous link, and LsqZ rose from "leaning towards negative" to 6. That gave A's chair the third of "three of the four reviewers". In B, three reviewers never replied. B's chair conceded that the rebuttal "addressed many of the concerns and misunderstandings", but rejected because the fixes "would require a second round of reviews". In A, 2BAr made that argument, and the chair overrode it.

**Committee A (poster).** All four reviewers engaged. FzMA asked how albedo and light separate. The answer, the multi-view loss, was "missing in the original manuscript", and FzMA lowered the score to 6: "I was overly impressed". LsqZ's Table 1 objection was half right: albedo improves only with the matched shader (MC on metallic 0.145 against SH 0.201; SG on glossy 0.123 against 0.136). LsqZ had written that "we cannot submit new images in the rebuttal"; the link supplied them and won the 6. AV84 kept 6 in a brief note. 2BAr kept 5 because the promised ablations and comparisons were not shown. The chair counted votes.

**Committee B (reject).** ShTU (10 hours) wrote B's best review. It correctly says "there isn't really anything in the formulation to encourage disentanglement", the omission FzMA found. FLZ6 gave the only 6, asked for geometry metrics and DIB-R edits, and never replied. SWGM posted late and described a two-stage pipeline: DIB-R infers the mesh, then a network infers albedo and light. L196-200 show one U-Net that predicts shape, material and light together. SWGM also called the paper hard to follow, against six reviewers who called it clear, and never answered the correction. 8HSK (2 hours, confidence 5) voted reject on novelty and asked for supervised baselines and ground-truth supervision. After the rebuttal, 8HSK accepted the unsupervised framing and rose to 5, but stayed "concerned about novelty". The chair's clarity concern rests on ShTU's valid point and on SWGM's misreading.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- "SV-BRDF" overstated, since only diffuse albedo varies spatially (FzMA; L144-145);
- the BRDF definition, a wrong supplementary equation, the title, and multiple importance sampling (LsqZ);
- loss-term ablations and heuristic weights (AV84, 2BAr);
- background modelling and fixed mesh topology (AV84).

Only B asked for experiments against supervised single-image methods (Li et al. 2018 and 2020, Deep Parametric Indoor Lighting Estimation). Only B also raised nvdiffrast, training time, global illumination, PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, and proof that the cleaner decomposition helps editing. Both raised novelty, the hidden multi-view training, a pipeline figure, no Mitsuba 2 timing behind "highly performant", no real photographs, no 3D shape metric, and missing ground-truth views.
