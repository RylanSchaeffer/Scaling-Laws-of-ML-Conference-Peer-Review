**Verdict.** Committee B accepted and Committee A rejected because B drew a confident champion and a lenient weak accept, and A drew neither. Both committees saw the same Table 1. GraphRetro trails DualTB in all eight columns and DualTF in five. It leads the earlier semi-template methods mainly at top-1 and top-3. All three A reviewers and the A chair treated the gap to the dual models as decisive. In B, the two reviewers who engaged in discussion also rated below threshold: Reviewer_aDNh (4) and Reviewer_okuQ (5), both at confidence 5. Reviewer_Az8s gave 8 at confidence 5 after three hours and never mentions the dual models. Reviewer_bGnx (6, confidence 3, two hours) called the DualTF gap "not a critical weakness". B's chair accepted on a 5.75 mean. Without Az8s, that mean is 5.0. The committees weighted the same facts differently, and the reviewer draw decided the outcome.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_Ep3z (4, six hours) wrote the most detailed review, and its checkable points hold. L260 says GraphRetro "outperforms all the template-free methods in top-n accuracy"; DualTF is better at n=1 and n=3 with class known. The abstract's claim to beat template-free methods rests on 53.7 against DualTF's 53.6 and omits DualTB's 55.2. Table 1 has no error bars. Reviewer_1dhj (5) held that all method classes can be compared on one benchmark. After the rebuttal 1dhj kept the 5 and proposed "a different, more specialized, venue". Reviewer_7Zmw (4, two hours, confidence 5) alleged test leakage from L163's "50,000 examples". L42 rules this out: the vocabulary covers 99.7% of the test set, not 100%. 7Zmw never answered the rebuttal, and no score moved. The chair adopted 1dhj's view. The chair also wrote that the method underperforms "even other semi-template based work". Table 1 contradicts this: GraphRetro beats G2Gs and RetroXpert at top-1 and top-3 in both settings.

**Committee B (poster).** Az8s praises elegance and "strong results" and finds related work "well acknowledged". Each other B reviewer found missing work: RetroPrime, AiZynthFinder, D-MPNN, line graphs. Az8s raised only a per-class breakdown and stereochemistry, and posted nothing after the rebuttal. This review is not evidence of quality. bGnx argued that duality enforcement "is quite general and applicable to the proposed model". The authors said it would leave the graph paradigm; 1dhj read that answer as a reason to reject. aDNh found RetroPrime missing, and the authors conceded it is better at class-known top-1 (64.8 against 63.9). aDNh also asked for USPTO-full results, questioned hand-coded attachment rules in the code, kept 4 and suggested resubmission. okuQ moved from 4 to 5 ("weak reject (+1)"). It accepted the synthon-completion gain but still found end-to-end results against G2Gs "mixed". The chair cites only that the method "is simpler and achieves some better top-k accuracy for k<5". It lists the detractors' points as revisions and never mentions DualTB or DualTF.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- overclaims in the abstract, at L260 and in the conclusion (Ep3z);
- no error bars or significance tests (Ep3z);
- unsupported interpretability and manual-correction claims (Ep3z, chair);
- missing Tetko et al. and Seidl et al. baselines (Ep3z);
- test leakage into the vocabulary, refuted by L42 (7Zmw).

Only B raised the missing RetroPrime baseline, the absent USPTO-full experiment, hand-coded attachment rules, prior art for the bond line graph, missing background for non-chemists, and per-class results. Both saw that the two-stage workflow follows G2Gs and RetroXpert, and both saw the gap to DualTF. A's main issue is a performance ceiling that no revision removes. B's chair recast its detractors' points as revisions.
