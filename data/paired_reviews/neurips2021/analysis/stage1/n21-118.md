**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently. Both saw a simple, general method with a small in-distribution gain. Its large out-of-distribution gains are measured against a pre-trained network that never saw that noise level or image type. Committee A judged GainTuning against that network and valued its simplicity and breadth. Committee B judged it against the strongest alternatives and asked why tuning gains should work. B's objections are correct readings of the paper. Figure 3's average in-distribution gains are 0.06 to 0.12 dB. In Figure 4, a network trained on σ∈[0,100] and the bias-free CNN tie GainTuning at σ=70. At σ=80 they beat it by 0.22 to 0.37 dB. Section 5.3 reports that Self2Self beats GainTuning on all three new-content experiments. A rebuttal cannot change these facts, and no B score moved. A's objections were mostly questions and one misreading, which the rebuttal answered. The same new experiments (Poisson noise, tuning only the last layers) went to both committees. In A, no reviewer contested them; in B, a reviewer's note quoted by the area chair dismissed them. Neither committee found an error in the paper.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 5, all at confidence 4. Three reviews were edited after the rebuttal and end with positive notes. Reviewer_YFNi's central objection was that GainTuning "impairs" a bias-free network. That is a misreading. Lines 100-101 and 115-117 define the tuned networks with biases, and Figure 4 lists the bias-free model as a separate column. After the clarification, YFNi wrote "I therefore recommend acceptance". Reviewer_uD7E asked clarifying questions and was satisfied by an added overfitting figure. Reviewer_sYYW asked for related work, colour images and non-Gaussian noise; the rebuttal answered "most" of these. Reviewer_aT3f's review repeats its summary and raises two speculative concerns. aT3f then argued over three rounds. aT3f stayed at 5 and called the per-channel choice "a bit adhoc". The meta-review says the experiments show "that it outperforms existing methods". That holds in-distribution, but not against the bias-free CNN at σ=80 or Self2Self on new content.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 4, 4, 5 at confidence 5, 5, 5, 3. Reviewer_2yuX and Reviewer_YCi9 called the in-distribution gain marginal. They noted that a network trained on a wider noise range does as well or better. Both points hold. YCi9's number is too low: about 21 of 67 plotted BSD68 images gain less than 0.04 dB, not a majority. Reviewer_bRMP asked why tuning gains works and noted that Self2Self is better. Section 5.3 confirms the second point. The rebuttal said Self2Self wins only on images with repeating structure. Lines 240-244 contradict this for piecewise-constant to natural images. bRMP did not reply. Reviewer_9H2a wrote four one-line requests, cited a "table3 and table 4" that the main paper does not have, and did not engage. Only YCi9 replied, to "stand by my ranking". The meta-review cites four sub-threshold scores "with a high indicator of confidence". It quotes a reviewer note that dismisses the new experiments.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B weighed the size of the in-distribution gain and the σ∈[0,100] alternative. Only B noted Self2Self's wins on new content and asked for more single-image baselines. Only B noted that the TEM result is one visual example without a metric. Only B raised test-time cost. Only A raised feature-modulation related work, colour images, a noise-conditioned baseline and locally varying gains. Only A found the contradiction between line 277 and the Limitations, and asked about network size. Both raised Gaussian-only experiments, the unexplained choice of gains, the bias-free baseline, prior test-time adaptation work, and missing details.
