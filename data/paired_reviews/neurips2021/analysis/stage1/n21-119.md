**Verdict.** Both committees saw a novel, clearly written method with broad experiments, clear gains on speech and small gains on text translation. The split comes from B's area chair (AC), not from a flaw that B's reviewers found. A's reviewers all rated above threshold (6, 6, 7, 7), and A's AC accepted. B's reviewers rated 6, 7, 5, 6, after two of them raised their scores, and B's AC rejected. B's meta-review names two weaknesses. It says the authors clarified the first, the conditional VAE formulation. The second, weak positioning against methods that learn to share components, is Reviewer_zLUP's point, and zLUP raised the score to 6 despite it. The meta-review also counts adapters as undiscussed, yet the paper discusses them (lines 68-70) and uses them as a baseline in every table. The strongest objection in the record arose only in A. The baselines use 4 heads, and the method draws on 8 (lines 205-216). The authors answered with an 8-head Transformer. It matches the method on text translation (M2O 23.7 vs 23.5 BLEU) but not on speech (ASR 46.0 vs 40.0 WER). B never asked. Reviewer_zLUP's summary even says the method wins "without increasing the number of parameters".

**Committee A (poster).** Reviewer_QWw7 (1.5 hours) wrote the most exact review. Each specific point checks out: 4-head baselines, one adapter variant, efficiency claims without timing, typos. Reviewer_8mmz raised the same capacity question and asked for a static allocation baseline. The authors delivered both on 08-28, and 8mmz moved to 7. QWw7 kept 6 on 08-31 and still asked for the 8-head run, which the authors had posted three days earlier in 8mmz's thread. The static baseline scores below separately trained models in multi-domain speech translation (11.5 vs 13.2 BLEU), so it is a weak test of learned selection. Reviewer_gJez (7) and Reviewer_8Ajg (6) wrote short reviews and never replied. gJez called the related work "comprehensive", but it omits Zhang et al. (2021) and Switch Transformer. The AC followed the scores and treated the baseline questions as settled, though QWw7 did not.

**Committee B (reject).** Reviewer_HGp8 (5, confidence 5) called Section 3.2 "not very rigorous, even wrong". The wording is loose: lines 120-122 call p(z|Θ) a posterior. But the claim that the bound fails unless q conditions on y is wrong. Jensen's inequality bounds log p(y|x) for any q. After four rounds, HGp8 accepted the authors' derivation on 09-07, then raised only to 5. The new reason was a limited contribution next to dynamic networks, which the review had raised only as missing citations. HGp8's correct point is the 0.1 and 0.2 BLEU margin over adapters in Table 1, which the paper concedes (line 223). Reviewer_WC28 correctly noted that no result has seed error bars, although the checklist answers [Yes]. The authors promised seed runs but posted none. Reviewer_NgLe (7) gave a one-line summary and stayed positive. The AC praised novelty, clarity and "consistent gains", then rejected.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the capacity confound (Reviewer_QWw7, Reviewer_8mmz, Reviewer_gJez), learned versus static allocation, state-of-the-art reference points, and Arabic WER above 100 (Table 2). Only A received new experiments. Only B raised the VAE formulation, seed variance, context-independent selection and weaker low-resource gains. Both sides saw the related-work gap but weighted it differently. In A, Reviewer_8mmz cited Zhang et al. (2021) as context for the paper's strengths. In B, Reviewer_zLUP cited it as a weakness, and the AC rejected on it. Both committees asked for parameter counts, per-language BLEU, measured efficiency and a clearer Section 3.
