**Verdict.** The two committees saw largely the same facts and weighted them differently at the threshold. Both found that Section 3 extends Gunasekar et al. (2018), as the paper itself says (lines 109-111), and that the design results of Section 4 are new. Committee A rated 6, 6, 6 at confidence 4, but two of its reviews called the contribution incremental or of unclear impact. A's area chair (AC) followed that text, not the scores, and rejected for "limited significance". Committee B rated 7, 7, 6, 8. Its best reviewer, Reviewer_o8fi, made the same novelty point as A's Reviewer_Hyxs but rated 7, for completeness. B's two highest scores rest on thin reviews. B's AC accepted in three sentences that name no weakness. One fact reached only A: the authors conceded a close overlap with Jagadeesan et al. (2021).

**Committee A (reject).** Hyxs called Sections 3.1-3.2 "mild generalizations" of Gunasekar et al. and found the motivation of Section 4 weak, because one can regularize directly. Hyxs also said that Theorem 10 is positive only for l1 and l2. That is wrong: the condition is that 2/p is an integer, which includes quasi-norms. The paper's loose use of "norm" invited the error. Reviewer_w51Y named Jagadeesan et al. and the Pilanci-Ergen convex-duality papers. The submission cites Jagadeesan et al. only inside a ten-item list (line 22) and does not cite the others. The authors replied that its results "largely overlap" Section 3.2. The camera-ready says the same and moves finite-width CNNs to the supplement. w51Y's claim that B is undefined is wrong (lines 86-87). Reviewer_tBBM asked how representation cost affects learning efficiency and why homogeneity matters; the submission barely addresses either. After the rebuttal, Hyxs and w51Y kept 6, and w51Y still called the work "incremental". The AC's "consensus" on limited significance fits Hyxs and w51Y, not tBBM.

**Committee B (poster).** o8fi (5 hours) wrote the most careful review. It called Section 3 "slight generalizations/improvements of known results", found the finite-width CNN section without insight, and read the residual networks as parallel networks. The definition is unclear, as the authors conceded, but the weights are shared. o8fi still rated 7 for "a good overview". Reviewer_ZwDT (7, confidence 3) spent one hour on three sentences that name no weakness. Reviewer_GxMP (8) listed the theorems as "very solid and intense" and made no novelty judgment. Its one question assumes that Section 3.1 covers general homogeneous networks. It covers only fully connected, diagonal and convolutional ones; Section 3.4 treats any depth-two architecture. GxMP also asked for a summary table, but Table 1 on page 2 already gives one. Reviewer_n4py (6) asked fair questions and later said it "would be happy to see this paper accepted". No score moved. The meta-review echoes GxMP's "solid theoretical contribution".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A learned of the Jagadeesan et al. overlap and the convex-duality work. Only A counted the linear-only scope, the missing link to statistical efficiency, the lack of experiments and the thin limitations section against significance. Only B raised the residual-network definition, the relation of Sections 3.1 and 3.4, and the need for more examples of predictors that architectures rank differently. Neither committee found a technical error. The overlap alone does not explain the split, because o8fi had already discounted Section 3 and still rated 7. The split comes from how each side weighed completeness against significance, and from A's AC, who treated unanimous 6s with significance doubts as a reject.
