**Verdict.** The two committees saw mostly the same faults and weighted them in opposite directions. Both found Section 3.1 hard to follow. Both noted that the main text lacks the training loss and a proof of Theorem 3.1, and that the novelty over diffeomorphic registration is unclear. Both wanted a test of per-vertex integration against grid-based SVF integration. Committee A (4, 3, 5, 5) made the exposition decisive and judged the promised rewrite a major revision that needs a new review round. Its AC repeated that argument and named no specific flaw. Committee B (7, 7, 7, 6) treated the same faults as camera-ready fixes and weighted the results: a 500-fold speed-up over DeepCSR, genus-zero meshes and strong figures. B's critics raised points that cut deeper than A's, and the paper confirms them. No B score moved after the rebuttal. B's AC went past the reviews to a spotlight that no reviewer asked for.

**Committee A (reject).** ZUMJ (3) said that Section 3.1 never states what the DMT computes, and that hL ≤ 1 is checked after training, not built in. Both points hold (lines 171-208). ZUMJ also wanted a VoxelMorph baseline, but misread Section 2.2, which faults grid interpolation, not the constant field. oT7u raised its score to 5 but still asked for rejection, because the rewrite is "outside the scope of a conference cycle". Its doubt about outlier-sensitive HD was fair: the paper never says that HD is a 90th percentile. FY8L (5) asked for background, the loss and a proof of Theorem 3.1. iY4e (4) found a real domain gap: separate models give inner and outer surfaces that may cross, and the authors conceded this. iY4e also assumed a correspondence-based loss, an error that the missing loss definition invited. Its text praises the experiments, and nothing explains its 4.

**Committee B (spotlight).** xE8n, clearly expert in computational anatomy, wrote the most informed review. It called the paper well written and an "excellent application", and showed that ODE integration on mesh vertices has been standard in that field for a decade. It warned "I would not overstate the significance of this work". EX4h (6) found the method unclear and the gains over DeepCSR small outside Chamfer distance. Table 1 confirms this: DeepCSR matches or leads on normals, Dice, self-intersections and inner-surface HD. The authors themselves called CorticalFlow "as competitive as DeepCSR" in accuracy. Two of the 7s are weak. wgwm made a wrong Lipschitz objection and said that limitations "are addressed", but the paper has no limitations discussion. 4s5o (confidence 2) asked a question that lines 201-208 answer. The AC called prior work "the only controversy" and gave no reason for the spotlight.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the missing EMD metric, outlier-sensitive HD, crossing inner and outer surfaces, template bias and notation. Only B named the specific prior art (LDDMM on meshes), the narrow accuracy gain, FreeSurfer pseudo-ground-truth bias and NeurIPS fit. B's unique points strike harder at the paper's claims than A's, but B scored higher. Reader background explains part of the split: oT7u "had to read into related work" to follow the method, while the expert xE8n found it clear. The camera-ready added the loss, Algorithm 1 and the SVF/LDDMM framing in one extra page. Thus A treated fixable exposition as fatal. B, in turn, promoted a paper whose accuracy claim its own authors softened. Neither version has the grid-integration comparison that both committees wanted.
