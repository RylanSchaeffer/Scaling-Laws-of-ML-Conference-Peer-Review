**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same weaknesses and weighted them differently. Reviewers on each side said that the learnability assumption makes the bounds near-immediate, that the bounds are likely vacuous for deep networks, and that the title or abstract overclaims. Committee A rejected because Reviewer_MoMA pressed these points hardest and the area chair (AC) agreed with them. Committee B accepted on a 6.25 mean. That mean rests on a two-bullet 8 from Reviewer_MbMa, who writes "I do not have much knowledge about the prior work". B's two 6s shared A's doubts but did not penalize a theory they found correct. The rebuttal also moved scores in opposite directions: MoMA fell from 5 to 4, and Reviewer_8ktt rose to 6. A's objections hold against the paper. Lines 174-175 claim "a complete characterization", and the Table 1 gains are 0.6 to 1.7 points with no error bars.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 4, 5; no rating is above 6. MoMA (confidence 4) wrote the decisive review. It called the theory a decent but overclaimed formalization of what was already known. Its main reason was the experiments: three style-shift datasets, and one selection over models pooled from five algorithms (lines 289-294). DomainBed's published validation-selected results match or beat the selected models. CORAL averages 86.2, 68.7 and 78.8 on PACS, OfficeHome and VLCS; the paper's selections get 86.57, 68.09 and 76.32. The authors' first reply deferred more experiments to "future work" and answered the insight critique with "We cannot agree". MoMA called it dismissive and lowered the score. A fuller reply came on 2021-09-07, after the discussion had closed. Reviewer_ZmeN called the gains marginal and the "first" claim an overclaim; both points hold. The AC rejected on MoMA's two theory points, insight and overclaiming. The AC did not cite the experiments.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 8, 5, 6. MbMa's 8 names no weakness, and MbMa posted nothing in the discussion. Without it, B's mean is 5.67. Reviewer_8ktt raised A's concerns: the assumption is too strong, and the bounds are likely vacuous. The authors replied that the reviewers had misread the one-dimensional definitions. 8ktt accepted this and rose to 6. Reviewer_Ja6b checked the main proof line by line. Ja6b then noted that line 177 takes a supremum over all projections, which weakens the "mild" defence. Ja6b kept 6 because "the theory looks correct". Ja6b also read Table 1 as better in every setup; one setup is worse and two tie. The AC wrote that the reviewers found the paper "very strong" and "compelling". Only MbMa's thin review says that, and the AC engaged no specific point.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A checked the experiments against DomainBed and questioned the pooled selection and the choice of datasets. Only A called the gains marginal. Table 1 has 9 gains, 2 ties and 1 loss, yet line 302 says "significantly". Only A asked about structured shifts (ICP, IRM), finite-sample estimation of P(φ|y), and the trivial feature φ(x)=y. Only B found that the bounds hide factors in d; the proof sketch carries O(r1^d r2^d). Only B asked why Algorithm 1 averages per-coordinate variation when the theory uses a supremum. The authors replied that a supremum makes all models look the same. Ja6b also found typos in Eq. 4 and in the appendix proof. The camera-ready keeps "complete characterization" and the title that Ja6b asked the authors to change.
