## Verdict

Both committees accepted the paper for its importance to official statistics; the tier gap comes from weak 5s in A and a thin 8 in B. In A, Reviewer_ecPK cut a 6 to a 5 in a review edit dated 2021-09-06, a month after the author response. The new argument was that privacy amplification by subsampling would let point queries take less noise, so the trade-off holds only for one synthesis technique. That is wrong. Theorem 3 covers every private mechanism that outputs positively weighted data, and each point query has sensitivity 1, like the sum (footnote 2). Nobody answered the objection, and A's meta-review repeated it as an open question. In B, the one strong objection was also wrong, but the authors refuted it and the reviewer raised the score. B's AC then chose a spotlight on scores of 8, 7, 6, 6, with no reason given for the tier. Even without ecPK's late cut, A's mean would be 6.0 against B's 6.75, so the draw of reviewers explains most of the gap.

## Committees

Committee A (5, 7, 6, 5; mean 5.75). Reviewer_ecPK was the most careful reviewer: 9 hours and a check of the pure-DP proof. After the response, ecPK still doubted the group-privacy step, while writing "I am not familiar with this proof technique". Group privacy is a standard, valid step. Reviewer_WJWj spent 2 hours and asked how tight the bounds are; Theorem 4 answers this. WJWj alleged "many undefined terms" without examples and never replied. Reviewer_epzC raised the score after clarifications. Reviewer_dx3d's 7 is generic praise with typo fixes. The AC accepted on "high significance" against a divided panel. The AC also asked for discussion of "the assumptions on the mechanisms", a concern that Theorem 3 does not support.

Committee B (8, 7, 6, 6; mean 6.75). Reviewer_bo89's 8 is mostly a restated summary. It names no weakness, asks one implementation question and fixes a typo. It is weak evidence for a spotlight. Reviewer_4cAR asked whether the proof of Theorem 3 is original. The authors conceded that the novelty is in the interpretation, and 4cAR kept the 7. Reviewer_NcCk (confidence 2) said correctly that the principle comes from the nonnegativity requirement and holds for one error metric. Reviewer_eGa2 (confidence 5) first claimed that Blum, Ligett and Roth subsume Theorem 3. That claim confuses simultaneous error with per-query error, and eGa2 conceded. The AC described the result correctly, as applying to "any private algorithm", and recommended acceptance.

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A raised mechanism specificity, the group-privacy step, the tightness of the bounds, writing quality, missing practical implications, and comparisons with the matrix mechanism and local DP. Only B raised overlap with Dinur-Nissim and Blum-Ligett-Roth, the restriction to one error metric, numerical stability at near-zero noise, and a summary of results on all datasets. Both committees saw that the trade-off comes from nonnegativity and that the theory leans on Balcer and Vadhan [3]. The PDF confirms the second point: Theorem 3's pure and approximate DP cases follow from their Theorem 7.2. WJWj counted this against the paper; 4cAR and eGa2 did not.
