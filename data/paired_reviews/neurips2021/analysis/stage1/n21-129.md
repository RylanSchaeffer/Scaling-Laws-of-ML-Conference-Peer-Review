**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. No reviewer in either committee questioned the theory. Both saw the same contribution: a ski-rental algorithm with the optimal dependence on prediction error, a matching lower bound, and a monotone reduction to multi-state power management. Both saw the same weaknesses: synthetic experiments, a thin link to machine learning, and steps built on known techniques. The committees weighted the fit question differently. A ended at 6, 5, 6, 6 (mean 5.75), and its AC called the paper borderline. The AC settled it with an own view that algorithms with predictions belong at NeurIPS, citing recent NeurIPS and ICML papers. B ended at 6, 6, 7, 7 (mean 6.5), and its AC accepted on "good support from the reviewers". Both committees got the same rebuttal experiment on real smartphone traces with a learned predictor. So A's acceptance rests on its AC's scope judgement; B's rests on its scores.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 5, 6, 6). Reviewer_LdW3 gave the only rating below threshold, for a correct reason. The data-center motivation appears in the introduction and never returns. The experiments draw idle periods uniformly from [0, 4] and add Gaussian noise to make predictions (l.312-315). After the rebuttal, LdW3 moved to 5 but kept the objection to the paper's "positioning". Reviewer_8AFS doubted that the paper "stimulates the related areas of machine learning" and correctly noted a missing disk spin-down reference. Reviewer_JJ9s misread the paper. It says robustness "is skipped over", but l.64-66 and l.127-129 state near-optimal robustness through Theorem 5, and footnote 1 deals with Wei and Zhang's bound. JJ9s never answered the correction. Reviewer_cGaH found each step careful but unsurprising. Three of four reviewers never responded to the rebuttal.

Committee B (6, 6, 7, 7). Reviewer_da7H read the experiments most closely. The question about high noise was right in substance. In Figure 3 (right), the proposed algorithm reaches about 1.6 near σ = 4, slightly above the classical online baseline. The authors traced this to a bug in their Theorem 9 code and posted corrected figures. da7H wrongly said the noise scale was undefined; l.313-315 define σ. da7H then wrote "I'm satisfied" and edited the review the same day, which fits a rise to 7. Reviewer_SQBd spent 20 hours, asked for comparisons with earlier ski-rental algorithms, and caught a typo at l.182. Reviewer_53BA questioned fit for the AI community but leaned to accept and kept 6. Reviewer_Bp21's 7 is two short paragraphs with details not checked; it is weak evidence. B's AC did not mention the bug.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A said the data-center motivation is never revisited (LdW3). Only A asked why robustness is absent and what Theorem 5 adds, which is a misreading (JJ9s). Only A noted the missing Helmbold et al. citation and asked for an algorithm box (8AFS), and asked for a limitations section (cGaH). Only B found the high-noise anomaly that exposed the bug, asked what the noise scale means, and noted that the paper has no conclusion (da7H). Only B asked whether earlier ski-rental algorithms are monotone and proposed relabelling Theorem 3 as a lemma (SQBd). The camera-ready adopts most of these. None changed a decision.
