**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. Each saw a simple, clearly written method with a large measured gain. Table 1 shows 31% to 37% fewer FLOPs and 43% to 54% more throughput on three backbones, for at most 0.5 points of accuracy. No reviewer found an error beyond a typo in Eq. 2. Apart from novelty, every weakness asked for an experiment or an explanation, and the rebuttals supplied them. Both forums got the same loss ablation. A also got DeiT-B results and random and attention baselines; B got conv-downsampling, Performer and Linformer baselines. Both slates sit at 6 with a ceiling of 7, and nobody argued for a higher tier. The committees differed in tone only. A's reviewers knew the CNN work on spatially adaptive computation, and A's AC called the novelty "incremental". B's AC called the method "novel" and "a clear acceptance". Each committee had one reviewer who doubted a pretrained, self-distilled model with 30 extra epochs against baselines trained from scratch: Reviewer_qFNP in A, Reviewer_Gaez in B. The authors gave both the same argument and no experiment. Gaez accepted it. qFNP kept 5, and A's AC overruled that 5.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 5, 7, 6; 4 to 7 hours per review). Reviewer_mx5s and Reviewer_9buE saw the work as known CNN dynamic computation applied to ViTs, and still gave 6. 9buE asked for DeiT-B, 384×384 input, and random and attention baselines. The AC called the rebuttal "convincing" and followed the three-to-one majority.

Committee B (6, 7, 6, 6, 6, 7; 2 to 4 hours per review). Most reviews are short. Reviewer_z3xW's 7 is five sentences ("Maybe it is the first paper in this topic") and skipped the discussion. Reviewer_b7CH's 6 lists one weakness, dense prediction. Reviewer_m39c started below threshold with misreadings: it said the experiments "only" use LV-ViT (Table 1 also has DeiT-S), and took DeiT for parameter pruning. m39c "turns positive" after the rebuttal. **Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A held prior CNN and early-exit work against the paper; B's Reviewer_Gaez noted dynamic CNNs too but called the ViT version new. Only A asked for channel pruning and distillation as baselines, DeiT-B and higher resolution, random and predict-at-start baselines, and the number of stages. Only B raised transfer to detection and segmentation, efficient-attention baselines, how N1 and N2 are set, the checklist missing from the PDF, and training time. None of these moved a decision. The novelty point changed only the wording of A's meta-review. Both committees asked for a loss ablation, a fairer training comparison, an attention-score baseline, a stronger downsampling baseline, and an account of batched throughput.
