**Verdict.** A rejected and B accepted because A's reviewers raised objections that B's reviewers never raised. A's four reviewers, all at confidence 4, cited the emergent-communication literature in depth. Reviewer_fU14 attacked the premise. Listeners already embed one-hot tokens, and the gain needs a noisy channel that the field rarely assumes. fU14 and Reviewer_bPM2 noted that the only discrete baseline is one atomic one-hot token. The paper confirms the baseline point. Section 4.1 lists only one-hot and continuous baselines. The triangle gain comes from three tokens in "distant corners" of a 9-D cube (lines 227-228). Binary messages, which the paper cites (line 57), reach the same corners. Three of B's four reviewers had confidence 3 or less, and none raised these points. A's AC liked the paper and said its problems were not "show-stoppers", but deferred to a 4.75 mean. B's AC restated the praise behind a 7.5 mean.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 4, 6, 4. Reviewer_fU14 first saw no difference from standard practice. After the rebuttal, fU14 accepted that the method differs under channel noise and for zero-shot input, and rose to 4 (edit 2021-08-10). fU14 then asked for the noisy channel up front and for sequence baselines. The authors answered with references, not experiments. Reviewer_bPM2 (4) asked for the same baselines. bPM2 also noted correctly that prototypes do not beat one-hot in the uniform environment (Table 2: -113.6 vs -111.8). Reviewer_BeFo (5) made three correct points. The number of prototypes equals the number of goals in every discrete task. One-hot has the best Self Out score in Table 3 (69%). The human test only separates an animal from a vehicle (Appendix C.2.2). BeFo and bPM2 did not reply to the rebuttal. Reviewer_V8Ya (6), A's only vote above threshold, misread the token table as shared by both agents; lines 54-56 exclude this. V8Ya kept 6 because the paper "doesn't have any agent-agent referential games". The Self columns of Table 3 are such games, and there one-hot beats prototypes (96% and 69% vs 93% and 66%).

**Committee B (accept).** Ratings 7, 8, 7, 8. Reviewer_nATd (8, confidence 2, 2 hours) describes 9 points as "which point of the triangle". nATd praised the "ethical conduct" of studies that paid $1.84 per hour (Appendix F). Reviewer_AvnV (8, confidence 5, 11 hours) gave careful notation notes and found "no unjustified claims". But Section 6.2 calls a p = .073 human result favourable. Appendix F reaches significance only after it drops a failed model. Line 126 calls one-hot "an optimal solution", but Appendix A proves only local optimality. Under Eq. 4, balanced binary codes beat one-hot once z = c >= 4. Reviewer_HBxw (7) misread the supervision as breaking zero-shot; lines 297-298 contradict this. No score moved. Only Reviewer_SUhS replied: "Many thanks for the clarifications".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A said that the listener embeds one-hot tokens anyway (fU14, V8Ya). Only A called the noisy channel non-standard (fU14). Only A asked for sequence or binary-message baselines (bPM2, fU14, A's AC). Only A said that zero-shot understanding borrows Sentence-BERT's space; line 309 sets the tokens to its label embeddings. Only A flagged the Self Out result and the coarse human test (BeFo). Only B raised the undefined term "prototype", broader impact and language drift. Both sides saw simple particle tasks and prototypes no better than continuous messages. A counted them against the paper; B listed them as minor cons. Clarity split the committees on the same text. BeFo and bPM2 found it hard to read; AvnV called it "significantly above average".
