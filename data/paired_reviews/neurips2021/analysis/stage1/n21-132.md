**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same gaps and weighted them differently. The paper attacks only GCN and GIN on the TU datasets, tests no defence or robust model, and combines known parts. In A, Reviewer_FwkT called newer victims and defence experiments "essential" after the rebuttal and kept 5. A's area chair (AC) restated that comment almost word for word as the reason to reject. In B, Reviewer_yB9K made the same victim-model point and kept 7, as future work. Reviewer_qDG8 called the missing defences a "main concern" and kept 6. B's rebuttal also lifted two scores to 6; A's lifted none past the threshold. Nobody in either committee found a technical error.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 2, 5, 6. FwkT (5) wrote the strongest review. Its claim about the victims is correct (lines 223-225, 259, 276). After the rebuttal it listed four graph-classification robustness papers; three are not cited. The paper cites a certified-robustness paper [10] and a backdoor paper [34] but tests no defence, so the authors' argument that defences are scarce was weak. The authors did not answer FwkT. Reviewer_Jxr4 (2, confidence 5, 45 minutes) pasted one dual-submission claim into every field. The NeurIPS FAQ and the non-archival workshop call, both quoted by the authors, contradict it. Jxr4 never replied, and the AC did not cite it. Without Jxr4, A's mean is still 5.33. Reviewer_PcFP gave confidence 5 but wrote "I do not fully understand the motivation and details". It wrongly said [17] is not reinforcement learning; the bad citation in that sentence is [37]. Its point that random search nearly matches GRABNEL on fake news is correct (Fig. 7, lines 295-297). PcFP raised its score to 5 without a reason. Reviewer_8giU (6) asked about surrogate details and cost and did not reply.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 6, 6. yB9K (7, eight hours) asked for hierarchical victims "if possible" and kept 7 after the runtime analysis. Reviewer_FuZ8 found Sequential Random close to GRABNEL. New tables showed GRABNEL ahead under tighter budgets and constraints, but tied on PROTEINS at the default budget. FuZ8 then raised 5 to 6. Reviewer_twfu called the method "incremental". Its claim that the only published baseline is one 2018 method is correct. twfu accepted that its suggested works are not black-box evasion baselines and most likely raised to 6 on 2021-08-26. qDG8's checks are correct: Table 1 says "Validation accuracy", and Algorithm 1 is unclear about the initial queries. Late in the discussion, the authors told yB9K of preliminary Graph U-Net results; A never saw them. The AC cited a "consensus" and named no remaining weakness.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the dual-submission claim, robust GNNs trained on perturbed graphs, an unclear attack flow, and the thin account of the WL extractor and acquisition step. Only B raised Sequential Random, RL-S2V on real data, the Table 1 label, the budget accounting, a ridge-surrogate ablation, and the statistics behind Section 5. Both raised the narrow victims, missing defences, incremental novelty, runtime, and some of the same related papers. The camera-ready adds a Graph U-Net victim (Table 2), so A's main objection was a fixable gap, not a flaw. The split comes from FwkT's bar for missing experiments, which A's AC adopted, against B's readiness to accept promised work.
