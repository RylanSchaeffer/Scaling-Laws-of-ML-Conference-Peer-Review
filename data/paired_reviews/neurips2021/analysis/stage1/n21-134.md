**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, and both means are 7.0. They read the paper the same way. The algorithm is a special case of Kar et al. [20] with a FIFO buffer of size 1, as Remark 4 says. The contribution is the analysis of the resulting biased gradient for convex, nonconvex, smooth and nonsmooth losses, plus a private variant. The experiments are preliminary: Table 1 ties SGDpair [25] within 0.001 AUC on all six datasets. No review checks a step of a proof. The committees differed in scrutiny, not in outcome. In A, three of four reviewers offered little critique, and the one critic rose to 6. In B, an unsupported 9 and a principled 5 cancelled out. Only Reviewer_4Fd5, in either committee, tested the novelty of the theory. By the paper's own results, one-pass Algorithm 2 reaches O(1/√n) in n steps. Algorithm 1 needs n² steps for the same rate on nonsmooth losses. The authors conceded that the growth of the bound with s is a proof artifact. They also conceded that Markov-chain SGD gives the rate of Theorem 6. B's AC accepted "based on my own evaluation" and did not address this objection. So the same facts were weighted the same way. The objection that only B found changed no decision.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 7, 7, 8). Reviewer_7nrA said that the algorithm is [20] with a buffer of one and that the gains are not significant. Table 1 confirms both. The authors re-framed the paper as theory and promised experiments, and 7nrA rose to 6. Reviewer_7Peg gave 8 at confidence 2 and praised "excellent results" against the state of the art. Table 1 and l.338-339 show parity with SGDpair. Reviewer_nSDq (confidence 2) asked for the high-level decoupling idea, which Remark 4 already gives. It called the preliminary experiments "exhaustive". Reviewer_PEqh's 7 rests on a two-hour review that names no weakness. The AC saw "no argument to go against this consensus" and weighed no weakness.

Committee B (9, 5, 7). Reviewer_3zKR gave 9 ("Top 15%") on a 93-word main review of praise. It did not join the discussion. Two of its six line corrections are wrong. Line 153 correctly calls the gradient of [25] unbiased. Line 176 equates optimization error and excess risk on purpose. The camera-ready adopted the l.153 change and now contradicts its next sentence. Reviewer_NJf5 gave 7 at confidence 5 for a surprising result from a restrictive algorithm. Its remarks stop at notation, and it wrongly says that ERM and F are never defined. Reviewer_4Fd5 pressed through three rounds and kept 5: the results are "closed variants of the 'pointwise' framework". The AC asked to fix the title typo ("Gradient Decent"), so it opened the paper. It gave no reasons.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only B raised these issues:

- one-pass Algorithm 2 matches Algorithm 1's rate with n instead of n² gradients (4Fd5);
- the correlated pairs form a fast-mixing Markov chain that existing SGD analyses cover (4Fd5);
- the bound worsens as the buffer grows, l.272 (4Fd5);
- the statistical cost of one pair against n−1 pairs, and examples of the PL condition (4Fd5);
- the undefined w_S in A4 (NJf5) and the title typo (AC).

The paper and the authors' replies confirm the first three and the last. Only A asked whether the method beats [25], for AUC against sample size (7nrA), and for limitations and template-compliant tables (7Peg). Both committees noted the special-case algorithm, the thin experiments and the need for more on applications. None of these issues changed a decision.
