**Verdict.** Both committees found the same main weaknesses, but only Committee A received new evidence on them. STEP builds on SimCLR features, yet the paper compares it with no self-supervised OOD detector such as CSI or SSD. Its only test on unseen OOD data trains on resized images and tests on crops of the same source (Fig. 2). In A, Reviewer_oqBv raised both points, rejected the first reply and asked again. The authors then gave an SSD comparison on five OOD sets, and STEP won on four. oqBv raised the score to 6. In B, Reviewer_TQrA and Reviewer_Jitz raised the same points. The authors declined the baselines there, and no B reviewer replied in the discussion. The reviewer draw widened the gap. A's other ratings were 7, 8 and 9 at confidence 5, but two of those reviews are thin. B's Reviewer_U8Ns made a valid objection that no A reviewer made, and nobody answered it.

**Committee A (poster).** oqBv was the one A reviewer who probed the experiments, and each checkable point holds. Fig. 3b shows about 95% AUROC with 10 labels, so the labels add little and SSD is the natural baseline. Against UOOD†, the Table 1 gains are at most 3 AUROC points, and UOOD† wins two CIFAR-100 pairs. Lines 159-161 put the test OOD images into the unlabeled training set. Reviewer_Wkgz (7) made useful suggestions but called the gains significant "with or without" OOD knowledge, which Table 1 does not support against UOOD†. Reviewer_cW9s reported 8 hours for an 8 with a one-sentence assessment. Reviewer_vv5y gave 9 after two hours and left its own question about unsupervised detectors open. The area chair (AC) repeated the praise and asked for related work on positive-unlabeled learning.

**Committee B (reject).** U8Ns (3) is a principled detractor. Algorithm 1 estimates the covariance on 250 or 400 labeled images, in a feature space that the paper calls "relatively large". That estimate is singular, but Eq. (5) needs the Cholesky factor of its inverse. The main text and the rebuttal do not say how STEP regularizes it, so U8Ns kept 3. Jitz (3) correctly found Eq. (3) ill-posed and the OOD sets narrow. But Jitz read the method as working in image space and asked for N, which Eq. (4) defines. TQrA (5) correctly noted the missing CSI, SSD, MSP and Gram baselines. TQrA wrongly suspected test-set tuning: line 186 fixes K = 12, which is not the best K in Fig. 3a. Reviewer_7FHe (6) called the paper "very well written", but the text has many typos. The authors gave 7FHe a FixMatch+ODIN baseline with an AUROC of 7.35%, far below chance, and nobody queried it. The AC copied 7FHe's summary and TQrA's baseline list. It added a theory objection that no B reviewer raised, and called TinyImageNet missing, although the paper uses it.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B raised the singular covariance, the unclear method, the MSP, Gram and likelihood-ratio baselines, a two-stage SSL baseline, and unknown ID classes in unlabeled data. Only A raised the redundancy of labels, the small margin over UOOD†, and prior work on semi-supervised novelty detection and positive-unlabeled learning. Both raised CSI and SSD, the narrow OOD sets, computational cost and the lack of theory. A treated these as fixable and saw one answered; B treated them as grounds to reject. The split comes mainly from the reviewer draw and from one reviewer's follow-up. The covariance objection is the one material fact that only B saw.
