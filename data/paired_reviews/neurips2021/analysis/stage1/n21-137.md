**Verdict.** The committees differed in who reviewed and in what they weighted, not in any contested fact. Both saw a clear paper. Its core step, Theorem 6, is Markov's inequality applied to (Z − μ)², and it adds a new PAC-Bayes-Bennett inequality. A rewarded clarity and theoretical novelty. B's two PAC-Bayes specialists judged the paper against its direct predecessor, the tandem bound (TND) of Masegosa et al. 2020. They found no empirical gain, and the submission agrees. Section 6 says COTND is "consistently looser than TND" on random forests, and CμTND "often performs similar or slightly worse than TND" in test loss. In B's rebuttal the authors conceded that they "did not achieve empirical improvement over Masegosa et al." No A reviewer raised this. One reviewer per committee (hg22, Mc3o) said correctly that Theorem 12 needs no union bound over the μ grid. The authors denied it in both forums, then removed the grid in the camera-ready. Neither committee saw that the submitted Theorem 12 does not follow for negative μ.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 9, 7, 7, 7, 5. Reviewer_AxQU (9, confidence 5) made careful line edits and found real slips: Theorem 7 omits μ < 0.5, and "range" means the length of the range. But the 9 rests on the writing ("Reading this paper was a pleasure"). The review says the experiments demonstrate "improved tightness", which holds only for CμTND on heterogeneous ensembles. It says "The proofs look correct", but it "Only skimmed the appendices". Reviewer_Am83's 7 at confidence 2 is five generic sentences and is no evidence. Reviewer_WkCd asked whether any method optimises C-bounds. The authors said none is efficient, and nobody challenged this. Reviewer_hg22 opened with a misreading of Theorem 6 and conceded it. The authors called most of his points "factually incorrect". But his factor-2 point for truncated Chebyshev was right, as they later agreed, and so was his μ-grid point. He rose to 5, partly because his criticisms "were not shared by the remaining reviewers". The meta-review says all objections were addressed. The one objection that proved correct got a wrong answer.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 5, 5. Reviewer_Mc3o (confidence 5) called Theorems 9 and 15 "the highlight", but found that the new bounds give no better learning algorithm than TND. Reviewer_Paqn (confidence 4) made the same point from the appendix tables. He asked for experiments with weak, decorrelated voters. The rebuttal conceded the null result and recast it as an insight, and Paqn kept his 5. Reviewer_ZLiP found the paper "mechanical". Later he was "not opposed", but he stayed borderline. Reviewer_R8qn's 5 at confidence 1 is an admitted educated guess. Paqn's doubt about the Bennett-Bernstein comparison was unfounded, because PAC-Bayes-Bernstein requires γ ≤ 1/b. The meta-review follows the unanimous 5s.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only B raised:

- no empirical gain over TND, with the first-order bound often tighter (Paqn, Mc3o; conceded);
- missing C-bound minimisation papers, Bauvin et al. 2020 and Viallard et al. 2021 (Mc3o; the camera-ready cites both);
- no discussion of the margin losses of Laviolette et al. 2017 (Paqn);
- direct kl-bound minimisation instead of PAC-Bayes-λ (Paqn, Mc3o).

Only A raised the range terminology and the missing μ < 0.5 (AxQU), the ln √n cost against a plain union bound (hg22), and which new bound is better when (WkCd, ViFo). Both raised the unnecessary μ grid, the bound names and the simplicity of Theorem 6. A read that simplicity as elegance. B read it as incrementality.
