**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same limits differently, and only A's reviewers engaged with the rebuttal. No reviewer found an error in a result. Both committees saw that the theory holds only in the NTK linearisation and describes the regularised optimum, not training dynamics. Both saw that REx goes undiscussed and that CelebA needs a re-tuned SD variant. A called these limits, and three of its reviewers raised their scores after the rebuttal. B's three lowest reviewers (3, 4, 5) treated the same limits, plus some of their own, as reasons to reject, and no B reviewer replied to the rebuttal. B's lowest score is weak evidence. Reviewer_TXkC spent one hour, and its headline claim, "no such guarantees", overstates: Section 3.4 derives that SD decouples the dual variables "for any initial coupling". B did raise two valid points that A missed. Reviewer_PD11 noted that Eq. 5 is strongly convex in θ, so "gradient starvation" is a property of a unique ridge optimum, not of gradient descent. Reviewer_i1Jd noted that SD is never compared with weight decay, dropout or batch norm on real data. Neither is fatal, and A accepted a milder form of the first as a limit.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 6, 7. All four reviewers replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_U7r7 found the NTK regime "already enough" for a theory that motivates an algorithm, and rose to 7. Reviewer_3tUD rose to 7 but still wanted clearer hyperparameters and future work. Reviewer_3LiL (1.5 hours) misread Eq. 5 as not cross-entropy. It called an experiment that tracks z₂ "crucial", although Fig. 1(d) already plots z₁ and z₂. It rose to 6 "tentatively" and declined to advocate. Reviewer_MbCs kept 6: the theory covers only the converged solution, and the implications need discussion. The chair followed the scores: "lack of enthusiasm", so "borderline acceptance".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 3, 7, 7, 5, 4. TXkC's 3 rests on rigour. Two of its points hold: Assumption 1 is unmotivated, and Lemma 1's "approximated" is undefined. Its Taylor complaint ignores the rationale at lines 145–147. PD11 (2 hours) made specific, correct points: strong convexity, SD as plain L2 on the logits, REx undiscussed. i1Jd made the strongest case: NTK-only theory, a two-feature Theorem 2, an unquantified δ, and no simple baselines. Its edit after the rebuttal kept the 5 because its NTK concerns "remain". The two 7s carried little weight. Reviewer_ZevV's short review rests on Figure 1 having "sparked a lot of ideas" and assesses neither theory nor experiments. Reviewer_7wTW was careful but rated at confidence 2. The authors' follow-ups of 19 August got no reply. The chair cited "the by now well-established discrepancy between NTK and neural network models". It also cited arXiv:1809.06848, where the term was first coined.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only B raised:

- no simple-regulariser baselines on real data (i1Jd, PD11);
- strong convexity of Eq. 5 (PD11);
- Definition 2 is a local sign condition and does not show that a feature goes unlearned (7wTW);
- a two-feature Theorem 2 with unquantified approximations (i1Jd, TXkC);
- overclaims: "guarantees" (TXkC) and discovery of a named phenomenon (7wTW, chair).

Only A raised test-set tuning on Colored MNIST, missing CIFAR code and details, and the paper's unclear importance. Both raised the NTK restriction, solution rather than dynamics, REx, the CelebA variant, notation errors and the lack of a direct measurement of starvation. A called these limits; B called them reasons to reject.
