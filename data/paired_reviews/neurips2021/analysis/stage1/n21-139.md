**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reason. All seven reviewers valued the first statistical analysis of fine-tuning-based meta-learning and its provable separation from frozen-representation methods (FrozenRep). Both committees named one main weakness, dense notation and hard-to-grasp assumptions, and both judged it not fatal. The score profiles match. All four confidence-3 reviewers gave exactly 6. Each committee's 7 came from a confidence-2 reviewer who did not assess the proofs. Without the 7s, A is 6, 5, 6 and B is 6, 6. No review was edited after posting, so no score moved after the rebuttal. The agreement is real but thin. Every reviewer chose a confidence level that says the math was not carefully checked. Both acceptances rest on the novelty of an unverified theory, not on checked proofs.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 6, 5, 6). Reviewer_dKjD gave 7 with an accurate summary, but says "I cannot judge the soundness of the proofs". Reviewer_Jbdo gave the only score below the threshold, on a principled basis. The data model is AdaptRep's own model class, so FrozenRep is misspecified by construction. The authors conceded that the gap is "in a way, obvious". Their own worked example shows that the hard case needs the task-specific residual to carry more signal than the shared representation. Jbdo replied that the intuition was still unclear and kept the 5. Reviewer_h1FC said that the paper has no empirical verification, but l.57 and l.216 point to simulations in Section B. Reviewer_4qNe asked whether the head and the representation are fine-tuned together; Eqs. (1) and (3) already do this. The AC's meta-review paraphrases Jbdo's review and passes on Jbdo's request for diagrams.

Committee B (6, 6, 7). Reviewer_j1xW spent 7 hours and found a real typo: θ at l.186 omits the β factor. The authors conceded it. j1xW also misread k·r_S as an undefined symbol, which supports the density complaint. Reviewer_NrMx wrote a one-sentence main review and put specific cons in the limitations field. Two are correct: Assumption 4.1 excludes Gaussian linear regression, and Theorem 5.1 has no δ on its right-hand side. Reviewer_L4rJ wrote three sentences in one hour, called the bound "solid" without checking the math, and kept the 7. The AC added a reason of its own: the optimization-aware analysis gives an apples-to-apples comparison with FrozenRep.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A questioned the substance of the separation and the realism of the assumptions:

- the gap is built into the assumptions (Jbdo; conceded);
- near-linear fine-tuning fails for large moves (h1FC; Assumption 4.3 and the βR² term in Theorem 4.1 confirm);
- the l.43 novelty claim lacks "theoretical" (h1FC; fixed in the camera-ready);
- whether the general bound advances over Du et al. (4qNe).

Only B found errata: the missing β (j1xW), the missing δ in Theorem 5.1 (NrMx) and the unexplained symmetrization A_B0 = [B0 B0] (j1xW). The submission confirms all three, and the camera-ready addresses them. B also asked about the bounded-loss assumption and PGD iteration counts (NrMx). None of these issues changed a score in either committee.
