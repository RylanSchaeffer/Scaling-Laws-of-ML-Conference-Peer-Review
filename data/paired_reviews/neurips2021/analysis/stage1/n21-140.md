**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reason. The method is a simple, new generalization of sinusoidal positional encoding. Its gains are consistent but small, and an MLP alone gets much of them. Each committee had a single 5 and nothing lower, and neither 5 pressed for rejection after the rebuttal. The gap in mean rating (A 5.75, B 6.83) comes from reviewer draw, not from a different reading of the paper. A's four reviewers all sat at the threshold. Two of them (Reviewer_ZKdD, Reviewer_aPhY, 12 hours each) pressed on the ablations. The rebuttal then showed a DETR gain of 0.1 AP at convergence (40.2 against 40.1). B drew three short positive reviews. Reviewer_9tzP (9, 3 hours) had reviewed the paper at ICML and rewards the authors for fixing that review's objections. 9tzP's main inference rests on the MD-Sine baseline. In A, the authors said that this baseline uses the default Transformer frequencies on both axes, so it encodes only x1+x2. The camera-ready drops it. Reviewer_TqtX gave 8 in 1.5 hours but called the experiments "the biggest issue". Neither AC added a reading of its own. The same facts, weighed with different enthusiasm, gave different score levels and the same tier.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 5, 6, 6). ZKdD (confidence 5) wrote the longest review in either committee (1,610 words). ZKdD correctly found the degenerate MD-Sine baseline and a wrong bold value in Table 1: the best SPICE (18.4) belongs to a fixed-Fourier variant, not to the bolded learnable one (17.9). aPhY raised two correctness points, and both are misreadings. The inner product of the concatenated cos and sin vectors does sum to cos(a−b), and line 187 compares two boxes. New ablations moved aPhY from 5 to 6: MLP depth, Sine-4D with an MLP, and DETR on held-out image sizes. Reviewer_HjX3 (confidence 2) kept 5 because the gains are "still slight", but was "fine with accepting". Reviewer_xZYh posted two weeks late, called the paper "well ablated" and did not join the discussion. The meta-review paraphrases xZYh and calls the gains "somewhat marginal".

Committee B (6, 5, 6, 9, 7, 8). After the rebuttal, reviewers posted two comments of 25 words in total. Reviewer_cSb4 rose to 6 after a ViT-B/16 ImageNet result (74.5% against 73.6%). The authors also corrected cSb4's wrong claim that Eq. 2 splits into per-axis features. Reviewer_gVFH correctly noted that an MLP alone beats DETR's default encoding and that the paper has no classification task. gVFH did not reply. Reviewer_xpKV credited the paper with machine-translation experiments that it does not have. Reviewer_SAzJ (7) wrote the most careful B review but admits that "Judging significance is hard". The meta-review is one sentence that cites "the majority".

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A tested the logic of the ablations:

- the MD-Sine baseline, and fixed against learned frequencies (ZKdD);
- MLP capacity: deeper MLPs, and Sine-4D with an MLP (aPhY);
- unseen positions: only widget captioning tests them, and 2685 of its 2692 unseen positions lie inside the training hull (ZKdD, aPhY, HjX3).

Only A saw the converged and held-out-size DETR results. Only B asked for image classification, relative positional encoding, runtime (1.22 against 1.86 steps/s on Reformer), more related work (LeViT, CPVT, Wang et al.) and a real broader-impact statement. Both committees found that the DETR curves stop at 100k steps and that the limitations section is missing. None of these issues changed a decision.
