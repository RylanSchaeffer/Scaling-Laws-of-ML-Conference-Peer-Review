**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same weakness differently. The paper tests its contribution, a learned metric, against one direct baseline: DCM, the authors' earlier model. Beyond that, it cites the TADPOLE leaderboard. In A, Reviewer_w1hQ wrote the most thorough review and was the only reviewer to reply in the discussion. w1hQ rose only to 5, because no linear mixed-effects baseline or granular result shows what the flexibility buys. The AC adopted that critique. In B, both confidence-4 reviewers raised the same single-baseline point but still scored 7 and 6. On the experiments, the paper supports A's doubt more than B's praise. In Figure 4, GB matches DCM on ADAS-Cog13 (3.59 against 3.61). On VENTS, GB trails DCM (0.43 against 0.42) and the best challenger (0.41). The headline TADPOLE result therefore belongs to DCM, not to the learned metric. The main evidence that the learned metric improves forecasts is Table 1: GB against DCM on an AD-only subset. No one found an error. The paper is borderline, and the two chairs resolved it in opposite directions.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 7, 7, 5 at confidence 2, 3, 2, 3. The two 7s are weak evidence. Reviewer_wbfL spent 2 hours and wrote "I don't have much feedback to give". Reviewer_Wxum said they "cannot speak much to the novelty or technical soundness". After the rebuttal, Wxum kept the 7 but endorsed w1hQ's suggestions. Reviewer_nBtD's 5 is brief, but its points are correct: one baseline, and two qualitative synthetic examples. The rebuttal convinced w1hQ about the contribution, not about the evidence. The TADPOLE comparison, w1hQ wrote, "tells us very little" about gains over standard methods. The AC named w1hQ and asked for a linear mixed-model baseline and a simulation against it. The request was cheap, because the Euclidean case of the authors' own model is linear (line 118). But the stated contribution is relative to DCM, and the paper does test that.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 6 at confidence 4, 3, 2, 4. Reviewer_FMHL called the approach "isolated", with one baseline. FMHL also asked what the Riemannian form adds over a flexible model in flat space. That question is sharp: lines 138-140 show that the learned space stays flat. Reviewer_W3mj asked for comparisons with the state of the art. Both still scored above the threshold. Reviewer_kAFz correctly noted that the method wins on ADAS-Cog13 but not on VENTS. Reviewer_EBmu's 6 rests on a copy-edit list with no stated assessment. No reviewer posted after the rebuttal. The authors told w1hQ that GB "outperforms the best challenger in both outcomes", and told both committees the same in broader words. Figure 4 contradicts this for VENTS, and no reviewer in either committee objected. The AC copied kAFz's summary sentence word for word and turned the criticisms into suggestions.
