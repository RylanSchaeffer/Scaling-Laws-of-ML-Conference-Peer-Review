**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. All seven reviewers accepted the problem as new and real: deletion requests that depend on published models void SISA-style unlearning guarantees (Section 5.1, Theorem 5.1). Most also found the reduction through differential privacy and max-information a clean first fix. The same limits held every rating at 6 or 7. The theorem needs privacy of the internal random bits, and the paper shows this only for SISA-style private aggregation. The experiments run DP-SGD noise on SISA, not the proposed algorithm (l.342-344). The paper itself says its constants "will not give useful parameters" in most realistic settings (l.357-358); no reviewer cited this. Committee A looked much harder. Reviewer_cu9f (10-14 hours) and Reviewer_bBvQ raised a group-privacy baseline, the missing accuracy guarantees and the ε ≈ 1/√k regime. This did not change the outcome. The rebuttal corrected two misreadings, and nobody judged the open points fatal. In each committee a reviewer below the threshold rose to 6 after the rebuttal, and nobody went above 7. Both committees weighed the same facts the same way.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 7, 6, 6). Reviewer_bBvQ argued that SISA handles adaptive deletions at a log n runtime cost. The authors pointed to Section 5, where adaptivity voids the guarantee itself. bBvQ conceded the "misunderstanding" and rose to 6. bBvQ's other points stood: there is no accuracy guarantee, and Theorem 4.1 forces ε near 1/√k (correct). Reviewer_cu9f said that the toy example overstates retraining accuracy. This is wrong: the adversary deletes both copies of each correctly classified point (l.312-316). cu9f's group-privacy baseline got an argument, not a comparison, and cu9f stayed "not yet convinced" at 6. Reviewer_QxA8 correctly flagged the accuracy cost in Figure 1 (MNIST k=6 from 0.97 to 0.91, Fashion-MNIST k=2 from 0.82 to 0.76) and the duplicate tick labels in Figure 1(f). QxA8 rose to 7. Reviewer_bzCs gave 7 with no weakness and one question; it adds little evidence. The AC accepted on novelty and disagreed with the authors that the MNIST drop is small.

Committee B (6, 7, 6). Reviewer_jxp1 spent 2 hours, wrote generic criticism ("the baselines are weak") and never replied. Its core is right: no experiment runs the proposed algorithm. Reviewer_FL5i (7) asked whether DP is necessary and how unlearning differs from DP. The answers were sound, and FL5i did not reply. Reviewer_6QGG found the condition of Theorem 3.1 impractical and Section 5.2 unrelated to the fix. The paper supports both points. The AC asked 6QGG: "Do you still think it is a borderline reject?" 6QGG then rose to 6, said the response "resolves some of my questions" and gave novelty as the reason. The meta-review has three sentences and follows the scores.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these issues:

- the group-privacy baseline, never compared (cu9f);
- the accuracy guarantees and the ε ≈ 1/√k regime (bBvQ);
- the accuracy cost of the noise in Figure 1 (QxA8, cu9f, AC);
- independent sampling in place of SISA's disjoint shards, and the missing Ullah et al. (bBvQ);
- the toy example and the randomized initial model (cu9f).

Only B asked whether DP is necessary and how the unlearning definition relates to it (FL5i, 6QGG). Only B flagged the undefined notation Z*, Θ* and m (6QGG; correct but minor). Both committees raised the abstract opening, the reach of the reduction beyond SISA and the gap between the experiments and the method. None of these issues changed a decision.
