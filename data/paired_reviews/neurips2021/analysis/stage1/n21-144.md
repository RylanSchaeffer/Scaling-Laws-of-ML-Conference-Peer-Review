**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected a borderline paper on largely the same facts. No reviewer found an error that breaks a result. Both committees saw a mixture construction of limited novelty, small gains over the strongest baseline, ROC-AUC as the only metric, and many notation errors. The outcome turned on who reviewed and how each chair read the texts. A drew two 7s that carry little weight. Reviewer_9ST8 gave a 7 at confidence 2 and "was not able to check all the math". Reviewer_vZcB's evaluation is three sentences of praise plus notation fixes. A's chair counted "three positive reviews and one negative one", granted the novelty objection and accepted "the modeling as a whole". B drew no score above 6. Its most thorough reviewer, Reviewer_iRRM (14 hours), kept a 6 on a critical review and concluded that the method is "overly complex" for "very similar results". B's chair followed that text, not the two 6s, and closed on competition.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 5, 6, 7, 7. Reviewer_Fw2q gave the only score below the threshold and wrote the most specific review. It found real errors in Eq. 2 and Eq. 7, which the authors conceded. It also saw that GP-B2M trails at the start and on NUS-WIDE. Figure 7 confirms both: GP-B2M starts lowest on Bibtex, and CS-GP is level with or above it on NUS-WIDE from about 130 samples on. The authors blamed early exploration, but the gap exists at zero selected samples, before any sampling. Reviewer_bnVb claims confidence 5 but restates the method. Its limitations field is pasted from the checklist with the PDF line number ("are440assumed"). The authors gave bnVb AUC tables and p-values. At N=400 these show GP-B2M level with CS-GP on NUS-WIDE and Corel5K and behind MMC on Enron. No A reviewer commented on these numbers. vZcB called the ablation "convincing". The chair wrote that the experiments show "superiority".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 6. iRRM's reading of Figure 7 is right. At 500 samples a baseline is level with or ahead of GP-B2M on four of five datasets; only on Bibtex does GP-B2M still lead. The paper itself says baselines "eventually converge". Reviewer_AzUM's claim that η has no clear effect is also right. The three curves in Figure 6(a) lie within 0.005 AUC, without error bars. They already differ at zero selected samples, where η cannot act. Some of iRRM's points are wrong: line 330 defines ρ, and line 143 gives the role of X. Reviewer_dgB5 made one correct point, about AP losses on rare labels, and did not post again. The chair wrote that all reviewers agreed issues remained. In the public record only iRRM says so. The meta-review names no specific flaw.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- no analysis of the acquisition function against BALD or BatchBALD (Fw2q);
- no comparison with standard multi-label classifiers (Fw2q);
- removal of labels with fewer than 20 occurrences despite the few-shot claim (Fw2q);
- no statistical test for Figure 7 (bnVb).

Only B raised the missing sample-label query methods (AzUM), the uninformative ablation (AzUM), the likeness to a mixture of experts, which the authors conceded (iRRM), a model that uses the latent embeddings and pairwise correlations its introduction criticises (iRRM), and AP losses on tail labels (dgB5). B's extra points mostly check out, but none is fatal. They show that B read the experiments more critically. A had the same evidence, through Fw2q and its own rebuttal tables, and weighted it less.
