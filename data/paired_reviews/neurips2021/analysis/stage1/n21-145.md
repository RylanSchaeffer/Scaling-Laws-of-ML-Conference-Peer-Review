**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently. No reviewer found an error in a result. Both saw a novel bilevel view of RNNs, a stability theorem, small benchmarks, a confusing "meta-learning" label, and an Algorithm 1 that does not say how the upper level is solved. A credited the idea and the authors' promises. B asked for evidence on real data, and its chair added further objections. The case for practical gains was thin, and neither committee said how thin. No reviewer noted that Figure 5 puts the dense model behind the best baselines on all three benchmarks (98.85 against MomentumRNN's 99.08 on Pixel-MNIST), although L343 says it "can always achieve superior performance". No reviewer noted that the rebuttal's PTB run ties LSTM (117.45 against 117.41) and trails FastGRNN (116.11).

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 8, 7. All four reviewers praised the novelty. Reviewer_CDfk wanted larger experiments but found the convention "understandable". Reviewer_doUF found "the current preliminary experiments are OK". Three scores rose after the rebuttal. Reviewer_5uYD had assumed MAML-style second-order gradients. The authors explained that training is ordinary backpropagation through the cell of Eq. 11; 5uYD raised to 7. Reviewer_AbCy raised to 8 after the authors conceded the typos at L145 and L147 and promised revisions. That 8 rests on generic praise, and it accepts superiority claims that Figure 5 does not support. Reviewer_doUF raised to 6 for the LSTM numbers and promised related work, still disputing the sample-efficiency argument. CDfk flagged the assumption that each input is drawn independently given the label (L140-142) and called it minor. The chair named two doubts: a 1/T inner step size seems too small for the hidden state to adapt, and "meta learning" is the wrong frame. The chair accepted "Despite this concerns".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 5, 5, 5. All four reviewers found the benchmarks too small. Reviewer_Vvok (4) said Theorem 2 is an observation: a dominant residual connection keeps gradients stable. The theorem's condition confirms this: with η ≤ ε/(TL), each step's Jacobian stays within ε/T of the identity. The authors answered with rhetorical questions, and Vvok never replied. Reviewer_8SmP (5, confidence 5, 1 hour) misread the method as removing the recursion between hidden states. That describes the penalty relaxation of Eq. 3, not meta-RNN. Reviewer_2xM4 raised to 5 after new sparse baselines and an originality argument. Reviewer_Qm4o cited a perplexity of about 78 for regularised LSTMs on PTB and did not move. The chair's main reasons were the chair's own. The chair could not see why a gradient step on x_t optimises anything when inputs change meaning over time, and asked whether it rests on the assumption at line 142. The chair also noted that Algorithm 1 runs the inner loop with θ* but writes h_T(θ) in the upper level. Both points match the submitted PDF. These questions came only in the meta-review, after the discussion.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised:

- missing work on meta-learned objective functions (doUF);
- the name "meta-RNN" already in use (doUF, AbCy);
- sample efficiency claimed at meta-training time (doUF);
- the 1/T step size limiting adaptation (A's chair);
- a learnable h_0 (CDfk).

Only B raised Theorem 2's triviality, novelty against iRNN, sparse models against dense baselines, Transformers, the theorem conditions during training, and the PTB gap to the literature. Both raised the small benchmarks, the meta-learning label, the unclear upper-level step, the input-independence assumption, the missing low-data LSTM baseline, runtime, and sequence outputs. A called these limits of a new idea. B called them reasons to reject.
