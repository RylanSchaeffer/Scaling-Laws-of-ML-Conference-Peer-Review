## Verdict

Both committees recommended a poster with the same mean rating (6.25), for the same reasons. Each credited the first convergence theory for a stochastic Anderson mixing (AM) method and consistent test gains, small against SGD with momentum. No reviewer found a substantive error. Both committees saw the same weakness: the theory gives SGD's O(ε⁻²) complexity and no better (l.197), and each iteration costs extra memory and time. Both area chairs (ACs) accepted anyway. The committees differed in spread and activity, not in outcome. A was uniform and passive (7, 6, 6, 6). B was split and active (7, 4, 7, 7). Neither committee checked the experiments' step size against the theorems. Theorems 1 to 4 need β_k ≤ μ/(4L(1+C⁻¹)). The experiments keep Algorithm 1's default μ = 10⁻⁸ (l.230-231), and the CIFAR runs start at β_0 = 1 (l.274). So the theorems cover those runs only if L(1+C⁻¹) ≤ 2.5×10⁻⁹. The theory that both committees credited does not describe the runs that produced the gains.

Only A raised the novelty of the proofs over SdLBFGS (ZG6Y), the link to Nesterov momentum (z5fN), AdaHessian and Langevin baselines (GfPN, aC2L), and length (aC2L, ZG6Y). Only B raised these issues:

- the √N̄ batch in Theorem 4 and the missing T term in Theorem 5 (wnT6);
- no formal result for AdaSAM (wnT6);
- GPU memory for large models (bi9T);
- α_k → 0 under the theorems, so SAM tends to SGD (bi9T);
- the α_k = β_k = 1 typo in Algorithm 1 (bi9T).

The paper confirms each; the fourth holds in the theorems (l.184), and practice needs the supplement. B probed the theory's terms and the cost; A probed novelty and baselines. Neither committee treated any issue as fatal.
