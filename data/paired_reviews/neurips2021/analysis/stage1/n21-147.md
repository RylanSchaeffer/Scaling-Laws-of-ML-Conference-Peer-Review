**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons, with the same mean rating of 6.0. DMTet's results are clear: lower Chamfer distance than ConvONet, DECOR-GAN and DefTet (Tables 1 and 3), and 74% to 95% user-study wins against baselines. Its core is modest: standard Marching Tetrahedra (MT) on an SDF stored on DefTet's deformable grid. In both, the results carried the decision and the modest novelty kept it at poster. Each committee had one confidence-5 detractor whose main technical objection the paper contradicts. A's AC set that detractor aside. B's detractor withdrew the objection and rose to 5. The committees differed in tone, not in outcome. A's reviewers called the combination novel. B's reviewers and AC called it limited and the case borderline.

**Committees**

Committee A (8, 4, 6, 6). Reviewer_f9sZ (4, confidence 5, 2 hours) made four specific claims, and the paper refutes each. The main text names the GCN that predicts α_i (l.191-193), so there is no inconsistency with the supplement. ConvONet, a state-of-the-art implicit method, is the main baseline (Tables 1 and 3). Training is end-to-end through MT (l.208-209), so the pipeline is not "sequential". L_def penalises deformation size, not smoothness (l.224-225). Only the missing self-intersection guarantee holds, and the authors conceded it. f9sZ's later posts turned to "theoretical insights" and venue fit, ending "it is the call of area chairs". Reviewer_YB43 (8) wrote the most detailed review and answered f9sZ in discussion. Reviewer_hY1b found four missing citations and asked how the eight inputs of Fig. 6 were chosen. The reply admitted weaker results on non-mammals. Reviewer_Axy8 (1 hour) repeated the summary in the main review and never replied. No rating changed after the rebuttal. The short meta-review dismisses f9sZ and cites a message to the AC that is not in the record.

Committee B (6, 6, 5, 7). Reviewer_QWSU (confidence 5) called MT non-differentiable, but l.147-151 already answer this, and QWSU conceded. QWSU kept two correct points: MT is used without modification, and no ablation removes L_SDF. QWSU's claim that the deformable grid is not ablated is wrong. Table 3 ablates it, and deformation gives the largest gain inside DMTet (L1 Chamfer 0.91 to 0.81). QWSU rose to 5 for "good results and practical meaning". Reviewer_ztsv rose to 6 after running 2D toy examples. Reviewer_mF8Y asked for MC in the same learned pipeline. Reviewer_MFHh gave 7 on "results look good", asked minor questions, and posted nothing in discussion. The AC accepted "this borderline case" and asked for the L_SDF ablation and MC comparison in the camera-ready.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these issues:

- no guarantee against self-intersection or sub-grid topology errors (f9sZ);
- no SIREN or hybrid-representation baselines, and fit for an ML venue (f9sZ);
- robustness to perturbed voxel inputs (f9sZ);
- missing citations (hY1b, YB43) and the choice of Fig. 6 inputs (hY1b).

Only B raised these issues:

- MT used unmodified, so "differentiable MT" overstates the contribution (QWSU, ztsv);
- no ablation of L_SDF or L_def (QWSU, ztsv);
- no MC in the same learned pipeline (mF8Y);
- a one-to-one super-resolution task, not synthesis (mF8Y);
- no MeshSDF comparison in the main paper (ztsv).

B's issues are specific gaps that the paper confirms. A's are mostly generic limitations, preferences and citation gaps. None changed a decision. The paper also answers part of B's L_SDF question. In Fig. 8, DMTet without deformation beats MT run on the ground-truth SDF at the same resolution. A model trained only to regress SDF values at best matches that oracle. No one in B used this evidence.
