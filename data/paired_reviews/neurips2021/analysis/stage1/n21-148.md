**Verdict.** Committee B examined the evidence behind the accuracy gains, and Committee A did not. The paper confirms B's two main doubts. First, Table 1 compares SLAC-ANN trained for 800 epochs with vanilla-ANN numbers cited from [2] (L210, L215). The paper's own Figure 4(c) gives the gain at matched epochs: 0.01 points on CIFAR-100 at 400 epochs, 0.52 at 800, and 0.12 to 0.17 on CIFAR-10. Vanilla ANN at 1200 epochs (92.42%) beats the reported SLAC-ANN (92.29%). Second, no figure shows overlapping class tails in a trained vanilla ANN. Figure 1(c) is "a toy example", and Figure 3 (LeNet on MNIST) has no vanilla-ANN panel. Reviewer_9FVq raised both points, and B's chair based the rejection on them. No A reviewer raised either, and A's chair deferred to four ratings of 6 or 7. This is a difference in scrutiny, not in weighting.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 7. All four reviewers praised novelty and clarity and took the tables at face value. Reviewer_rrae (7, confidence 5) called the results "strong evidence". Its concerns were the distance in Eq. 7 and duplicate citations. Reviewer_aWB2 (confidence 5, 3 hours) reuses the paper's own sentences (L78-80, L322-325) in its summary and main review. Reviewer_AHNP wrote that "the improvement seems obvious". The reviewers asked for more comparisons: center loss, Euclidean distance, a fixed target, ResNet-50. For the CIFAR-100 variants, the rebuttal gave numbers 0.2 to 0.65 points below SLAC, without variance. Nobody asked about seed noise, and no score moved. The chair wrote that with "no attempt to reject the submission", there is "no basis to overturn the consensus".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 6, 5, 5. Reviewer_9FVq (6) asked whether the "slightly improved accuracy" came from doubled epochs. It also said the overlap is "not shown in your real trained networks". Reviewer_xfwL found no error bars, and the checklist answers "N/A" for them. The rebuttal's error-bar table listed vanilla ANN at 91.84% and 67.60%, the 400-epoch values of Figure 4(c), against SLAC-ANN at 800 epochs. It also reported "the best results" of four runs. So it repeated the mismatch, and nobody noticed. Reviewer_xfwL kept 5 because the authors' BWN rerun (64.4%) trails IR-Net's 66.5%. That figure is still below SLAC-ANN's 67.7%, so it questions baseline tuning, not the method. Reviewer_5BV4 (4) mixed valid and off-target points. Figure 1 does use CIFAR-10 and Figure 3 MNIST, ImageNet training does take 300 epochs, and BWN is missing. But its long-tail reference concerns class imbalance, and its request for hardware latency targets AdderNets in general, not this paper. Reviewer_5ZaN's 5 rests on the gap to the CNN on ImageNet, which the paper does not dispute. Only xfwL responded to the rebuttal. The chair's two reasons follow 9FVq's two weaknesses closely.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only B raised:

- the epoch mismatch against cited baselines (9FVq, 5BV4, xfwL);
- the absence of direct evidence of overlapping tails (9FVq, 5BV4);
- the missing error bars (xfwL);
- the missing binary-weight baselines and measured efficiency (5BV4, 9FVq);
- the doubled training cost and the missing code (xfwL, 9FVq).

Only A raised center loss, the parameter and FLOP increase, the adaptive target in Eq. 8, the Mahalanobis distance in Eq. 7, and citation errors. Both committees asked whether CNN features are also long-tailed, and both asked for more ImageNet architectures.
