**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently. Both saw a new result: a linear, nearly dimension-free last-iterate rate for entropy-regularized PU and OMWU. Both saw that the algorithms are close to known mirror-prox and OMWU methods. No one found an error. Committee A's area chair (AC) rejected because the positioning needed another review round. Committee B's AC accepted a 7/7/6/5 panel in five sentences. Two things moved A. Reviewer_v4uF asked twice whether PU's departure from the extra mirror descent of Mertikopoulos et al. matters. The authors conceded the departure but could not show it matters. The AC added an expert argument: strong convexity makes the QRE unique, so the paper side-steps uniqueness rather than removing it. A's support was thin: a 6 at confidence 1 after one hour, and a 7 that says "there is no algorithmic novelty". B asked an easier novelty question: whether the rate was already known. The rebuttal answered it.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 4, 7; mean 5.5. Reviewer_bKGv's 6 is a five-line review that calls unchecked math "technically sound". Reviewer_1R9o's 7 rests only on dropping the unique-NE assumption. The paper supports Reviewer_v4uF (4). Section 2.2 says OMWU is "adapted from" Rakhlin and Sridharan (2013), yet the paper says it proposes two algorithms. Algorithm 1 keeps the entropy term at the base point. In Figure 1, the only experiment, PU and OMWU overlap on every metric, and no baseline appears. Reviewer_wNLY first called the paper sound and clear. After discussion with the AC, wNLY's rating changed to 5, and wNLY asked for "another round of reviews". The meta-review rests on the AC's own reading, and its facts hold. QRE uniqueness follows from strong convexity (lines 168-169). The oracle returns exact expected payoffs (Definition 1). Leslie and Collins (2005) and Coucheney et al. (2015) are not cited. One charge is overstated: the abstract and Section 1.2 do say "approximate" and "ε-NE".

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 5, 6; mean 6.25. Reviewer_3nt9 spent 20 hours, ran the code, rechecked proofs, and raised the score after the rebuttal. Reviewer_Q1Zp correctly located the gain: one global linear phase, where prior OMWU analyses needed a sublinear phase first. Reviewer_KD2g asked whether mirror-prox already has a linear rate under strong convexity. KD2g accepted the reply that the Euclidean proof of Mokhtari et al. does not carry over to KL. Reviewer_uEKj (5) called the results "not surprising". uEKj also noted, correctly, that Algorithm 3 is value iteration in the frame of Perolat et al. (2015). uEKj did not reply to the rebuttal. The AC names no reviewer and writes that "the proposed algorithm appears to be novel". The paper itself attributes OMWU to prior work. The AC defers all comments to the camera-ready.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked why the paper needs a new variant of extra mirror descent, and why both PU and OMWU. Only A noted the missing baselines; B's Reviewer_3nt9 called the same single synthetic figure "convincing". Only A's AC called the uniqueness claim a side-step and named the missing literature on entropic learning in games. Only A asked for comparisons with recent Markov-game methods (Zhao et al., Wei et al., Daskalakis et al.). Only B flagged the misleading name of Algorithm 3. Only B noted that step 5 of Algorithm 3 needs both players' policies; Definition 2's oracle omits the opponent's entropy. Only B asked about τ = 0 and a diminishing τ. Both committees questioned the oracle's realism and whether strong convexity makes the linear rate expected. Only A's AC made these part of a rejection.
