**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently. Both flagged incremental novelty over DiGCN, an overstated "infinite views" claim, no large-scale experiments and an error in Eq. 5. Both received nearly the same rebuttal. In B, two reviewers accepted it and raised their ratings to 6. A one-hour 7 completed a mean of 6.25, and the area chair followed the consensus. In A, no rating rose. The authors conceded the views claim and admitted hardware limits on scale. The low-confidence Reviewer_WA6U turned negative after reading two peers. The only positive reviewer never replied. The area chair echoed Reviewer_fews: the paper "doesn't provide either strong theoretical results or compelling experiments". The paper supports several of A's points. In Table 1, easy-to-hard ordering beats random ordering by at most 1.01 points, inside the random-order spread. The entropy bounds of Section 2.3 feed only a heuristic difficulty score. No dataset approaches OGB scale, and the authors' model ran out of memory at 50K nodes. B's acceptance rests on the rebuttal's arguments and tables. Neither committee found a fatal flaw; Eq. 5 is a derivation slip.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 4, 5; mean 5.0. Reviewer_ZF6P (4, ten hours) called the method incremental over DiGCN and the datasets small. Both points hold; Laplacian perturbation shifts the teleport probability of DiGCN's own Laplacian. After the rebuttal, ZF6P called the move to self-supervision "somewhat trivial" and stayed at 4. Reviewer_fews found the Eq. 5 error. The error is real, but the corrected recursion is still a power iteration. fews also argued that the "infinite views" are two views per step, as in SimCLR. The authors agreed and promised to drop the phrase. Reviewer_WA6U first called the curriculum "a very effective modification". On 1 September it adopted the objections of fews and ZF6P, called the curriculum gain marginal, and edited its review. Reviewer_haDS (6, two hours) gave the only positive rating and raised conceptual questions.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 6, 6; mean 6.25. Reviewer_6X4F gave the 7 after one hour. It praises "solid theoretical analysis", misses the Eq. 5 error, and asks for a curriculum ablation that Table 1 already contains. Reviewer_bT23 also called the method an incremental combination built on DiGCN. It accepted the rebuttal's "first data augmentation method designed specifically for digraphs" and raised 5 to 6. Reviewer_FASr argued that Laplacian perturbation alone does not beat the baselines. Table 1 confirms this on three of five datasets. A supplementary table that adds Laplacian perturbation to MVGRL and GCA convinced FASr, who raised to 6. Reviewer_eZud claimed that teleportation adds edges. That is wrong: Eq. 2 keeps P̃ fixed and changes only edge weights. eZud's other points mostly hold, and it supported acceptance "while be modest". The area chair credited the rebuttal's link-prediction and ablation tables.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked whether structure preservation helps on noisy graphs. Only A found the entropy theory detached from the InfoNCE objective. Only A noted that Eq. 10's expectation does not mean "for all" views. Only A read the curriculum gain as noise; only B read Laplacian perturbation as the weak part. Only B asked for link prediction and an InfoMin comparison. Only B disputed that the structure stays fixed. The views claim shows the asymmetry best. On 10 August the authors withdrew it to fews in A and restated it to bT23 and eZud in B. No B reviewer pushed back after the rebuttal.
