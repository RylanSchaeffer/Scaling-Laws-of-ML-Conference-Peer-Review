**Verdict.** The committees judged different evidence. Committee A read the method closely and found real flaws. Committee B read mainly the results table and accepted it, although two of its own reviewers doubted that table, correctly. A's reviewers (5, 5, 4, 5; two at confidence 5) showed that the main motivation is false: the Query2Box distance is continuous, and the authors conceded this. They also found equation errors and gains with no explanation. B's ratings (7, 6, 6, 7) rest on the reported 6.2% average gain over CQD. The rebuttal gave B no new evidence. The camera-ready itself adopts the published CQD row for NELL995. With that row, CQD beats PERM on NELL995 (.375 against .328), and the average gain over CQD falls to about 2%. The paper still claims 6.2%. So A rejected for confirmed reasons, and B accepted on numbers that its own reviewers doubted correctly. A never questioned where the baseline numbers came from. B never checked the equations.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_HW1P and Reviewer_6UFN (6 hours each) wrote line-level reviews. HW1P showed that lines 38-41 misdescribe Query2Box and that Eq. 7 takes the exponential of a density. HW1P also asked why Hinton's union distance in Figure 2(c) (0.766) is below both component distances (1.347, 1.118). Under Eq. 7 the union distance is a weighted mean of the two, so it cannot be lower than both. The authors did not explain this. 6UFN suspected an error in Eq. 6. There is one: the formula for µ3 swaps the two precisions. The authors confirmed that L in Σ⁻¹ = LLᵀ is a d×1 vector, so the precision has rank one. Reviewer_TYBk objected that the mixture union puts Hinton far from "Canadians"; Figure 2(c) confirms it. Reviewer_dgsE asked why PERM loses on some query types; Section 5.2 does not say. No score moved, and 6UFN confirmed its 5 after the rebuttal. The AC summarized this consensus.

**Committee B (accept).** Reviewer_LbhD (2 hours) noted that the CQD NELL995 row is below the CQD paper (.567 against .667). LbhD still rated 7. The authors thanked LbhD for "pointing the typos" but kept the row, and LbhD did not reply. Reviewer_dcyX (24 hours) found three faults. The datasets are not the standard splits (474 relations, not 237). Baseline rows are copied from other papers. The BQE row is BetaE's published MRR, labeled HITS@3. All three are correct. The authors then posted a "standard dataset" table. For PERM, GQE, Q2B and CQD, it repeats submitted Table 1 digit for digit, including the wrong CQD row. Only BQE changed. dcyX raised the score anyway. Reviewer_MHQ9 (1 hour; it "did not see" the limitations section) and Reviewer_y6MJ (3 hours) took the results as given. The AC deferred to the "fairly unanimous consensus" and did not mention the doubts about the numbers.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the false motivation, the equation errors, the rank-one precision, the Figure 2 inconsistency, the union semantics and the untested scalability claim. Only B raised the baseline-number errors, the dataset mismatch, the fixed query structure (y6MJ) and the missing differentiable-prover work (the AC, who did not weigh it). Both committees noted the per-type losses, the missing training details, the lack of negation and the need for failure examples. On these shared facts, A gave more weight to the unexplained losses and B to the average gain.
