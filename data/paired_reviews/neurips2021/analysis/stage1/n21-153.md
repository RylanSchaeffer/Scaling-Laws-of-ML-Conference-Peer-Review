**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Reviewers in both judged HPA a sound, novel extension of Procrustes analysis to the Lorentz model, and no reviewer alleged an error. Its k-NN AUC beats every OT baseline on all six real-data rows (Table 1), although the best OT method has lower MMD on all eight rows (Table 2). No rating fell below 5 or rose above 7, so neither committee had a detractor or a champion. The committees differed in what they probed, not in outcome. A (5, 6, 6, 7) dwelt on accessibility. Its rebuttal moved no one, and its AC accepted a "very borderline" paper on condition of clarity fixes. B (7, 6, 6, 6) probed the method. New rebuttal tables and concessions lifted Reviewer_rvxG and, by the edit date, Reviewer_VwLT to 6. On clarity, the committees read the same text differently: A found it dense, B called it clear. Neither committee obtained Euclidean batch-correction baselines.

**Committees**

Committee A. Reviewer_aJ5V (5) correctly noted that the OT methods beat HPA on MMD. aJ5V also noted, correctly, that nothing shows the data suit hyperbolic space better than Euclidean space. But aJ5V asked for a comparison with PAH [46], which Fig. 2 already makes. aJ5V promised to reconsider, never replied, and stayed at 5. The reply said that Euclidean batch-correction methods need labels. That is doubtful for Harmony [23], which uses only batch membership. Reviewer_ywh5 (confidence 2) correctly found no introduction to classical PA; Section 2 covers only hyperbolic geometry. Reviewer_EAV6 called the appendix proofs "awful to follow". Reviewer_XtJE (7) accepted the MMD explanation and was the only reviewer to reply. No rating changed. The AC wrote that reviewers "greatly appreciated the enthusiastic interaction", but the public thread shows one reply.

Committee B. Reviewer_VwLT made the sharpest point. Definition 1 drops a coordinate, which preserves tangent lengths only at the origin. Elsewhere, the wrapped rotation is in general not an isometry, while a rotation about the mean always is. The authors agreed and tested an isometric variant. HPA scored higher on 4 of 6 k-NN rows, and VwLT then recommended acceptance. Reviewer_rvxG (confidence 5) correctly found that the paper omits the existence and uniqueness of the Fréchet mean. rvxG raised the score after the explanations. But rvxG wrote that the experiments cover only synthetic and breast-cancer data; Section 4.2 has three real tasks. Reviewer_Mb2Y correctly found that the choice of rotation is untested: l.170 promises a comparison that Section 4 lacks. The full ablation came only in the rebuttal. Reviewer_2HGp (7, confidence 2) copied the summary from l.17-20 and most of the review from l.52-54, l.227-229 and l.307-308. The AC wrote that the paper "rigorously compares with different baselines" and named no weakness.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised accessibility: no classical PA background (ywh5, aJ5V, XtJE, AC) and rough proofs (EAV6). Only A asked whether the data embed better in hyperbolic space (aJ5V). Only B raised these points:

- the wrapped rotation against an isometric alternative (VwLT, Mb2Y);
- the existence and uniqueness of the Fréchet mean (rvxG);
- which results hold beyond hyperbolic space (rvxG, Mb2Y);
- the missing ablation and the generative assumption of Prop. 10 (Mb2Y);
- more real-world data (2HGp, rvxG).

The paper confirms the rotation, Fréchet-mean, ablation and Prop. 10 points. Both committees questioned the worse MMD and asked for Euclidean baselines. In A, EAV6 flagged the same tangent-space passage (l.163) as VwLT, but only as wording. None of these issues changed a decision.
