**Verdict.** The two committees saw the same borderline paper and mostly the same weaknesses. They weighed them differently, and the discussion phase moved B's scores more. A ended at 5, 6, 5, 6 (mean 5.5) and rejected. B ended at 7, 6, 6, 5 (mean 6.0) and accepted. In B, Reviewer_CHLY rose to 7 after the authors corrected CHLY's misreading of Theorem 2. Reviewer_thNZ rose to 6 on promised related-work changes. In A, only Reviewer_HgrD rose (5 to 6). Reviewer_FaWn kept 5 with a two-sentence note, and Reviewer_pdrf never replied. Reviewers in both committees said the experiments do not show that α* works as a transferability measure (HgrD and pdrf in A, thNZ in B). A's area chair (AC) made this a reason to reject. B's AC found the replies clear and accepted. A's main objection points at a real gap; B's main objection was a misreading that the authors could correct. The paper supports A's concern. Section 4 reports only accuracies, without error bars. The only per-source α* values, given in A's rebuttal (0.19, 0.01, 0.09, 0.56), rank sources 2 and 3 opposite to their single-source accuracies (59.7 and 56.2, Table 1).

**Committee A (reject).** HgrD made the sharpest point: α* "were never mentioned or discussed in the experiments". This is correct in substance. Figure 2 traces α1 only to show convergence. pdrf asked for a monotonic link between α* and accuracy gain. FaWn called the method "relatively straightforward" and asked for "theoretical justification" without saying what kind. The authors did not understand the request, and they deferred any justification of the convex-combination model to "a follow-up paper". Reviewer_NvNX kept 6 but still doubted the reading of |X||Y|−1 as "learning capacity". The AC sided with the two 5s. Its theory point repeats FaWn's vague request. Its empirical point repeats pdrf and HgrD, although HgrD wrote "I am happy with the response".

**Committee B (accept).** CHLY's 2-hour review said the measure "is not related to model complexity or sample size". Eq. (6) contradicts this: α1* contains n0, n1 and V(0). After two rounds, CHLY accepted the construction, suggested the name "task complexity", and rated 7. CHLY's correct point on missing error margins got no answer. thNZ asked to compare α* with weights derived from LEEP. The authors declined: the scales differ and α* is "theoretically optimal". thNZ still raised to 6 but wanted to see the revision. Reviewer_9X81 kept 5. Its claim of small gains holds for Table 3: the method and UDDA both average 85.9. In Table 4 the method gains 1.8 to 4.2 points in four of six settings and loses two. Reviewer_HA1J called the first related-work answer "quite vague"; the second was no more specific. The AC accepted on the majority and asked for clearer writing.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for a direct test of α*: a synthetic task with a known best source (HgrD) or a monotonic link to accuracy gain (pdrf). Only A asked for theoretical justification (FaWn) and questioned the switch from log-loss to χ² (pdrf). Only B named the missing transferability literature, LEEP and NCE (thNZ), and meta-learning (HA1J). Only B flagged the missing error bars (CHLY) and the estimation of target distributions from few labels (9X81, CHLY). Neither committee noticed two facts. The multi-source experiment (Table 2) has no baseline from the transfer-learning literature, only random search over α. And the paper states α*'s dependence in two opposite ways: lines 45-47 say it rises with source samples and similarity and falls with model complexity; lines 111-114 say the reverse.
