Same facts, weighted differently. Both committees saw the first variational framework for continuous-time switching systems, rigorous and clearly written. Both also saw its faults: qualitative 1D and 2D experiments, no baseline, no scaling study, and biased parameter estimates. The paper confirms the bias. Sec. 4.1 reports learned set points 0.70 and −0.52 against ground truth ±1 and calls this "high accuracy". The best-informed reviewer on each side flagged it (Reviewer_x8VX in A, Reviewer_naZJ in B), and neither committee treated it as decisive. B's reviewers called the faults camera-ready work and scored 8, 7, 7, 7; B's AC turned the unanimous scores into a spotlight. In A, three thin reviews scored 6 against one expert 8, and the AC followed the 6.5 mean to a poster. No committee found a fact that the other missed.

Committee A (poster; about 13 reviewer-hours). Reviewer_x8VX (confidence 4, 5 h) wrote the most expert review of the eight. It places the work as an extension of Archambeau and Opper, catches the Sec. 4.1 bias and the linear-only experiments, and calls the qualitative evaluation "great". The three 6s carry less information. Reviewer_7pq7 is "not familiar with the literature" and makes the missing baselines and quantitative results the main objection. Reviewer_4Rfw spent 1.5 h on three questions about scaling and comparisons. Reviewer_5o85 calls the work "not new" and the results "somewhat inadequate", gives one specific point, and never replied. 7pq7 and 4Rfw edited their reviews after the rebuttal, and 7pq7 wrote "willing to reassess my score"; the earlier ratings are not public. 4Rfw still wanted a comparison. The two-sentence meta-review does not mention x8VX and gives no reason for poster over spotlight.

Committee B (spotlight; about 23 reviewer-hours). Reviewer_naZJ and Reviewer_czKb wrote long, specific reviews that list A's weaknesses almost exactly: qualitative validation, large parameter error, no comparison with discrete-time models, linear drift only. Both still rated 7 ("Good paper, accept"). Reviewer_d46C (confidence 2) asked for analytic solutions and a Gaussian-sum-smoother comparison and offered to raise the score. After the authors' second reply, d46C wrote "well explained" and edited the review that day. Reviewer_QSy5's 8 is the least argued score: its summary and significance repeat the paper's conclusion almost word for word, and its concrete content is two clarity items and three open questions. Without it, B's mean is still 7.0. The AC reused naZJ's words ("timely, significant, and relevant"), listed the parameter-error study as a revision, and gave no argument for spotlight beyond the scores.

Only A asked whether discrete modes exist a priori, wanted a plain GP baseline, asked about the bias of VI and VEM (Turner 2011), and asked whether per-mode posteriors revert to the prior.
