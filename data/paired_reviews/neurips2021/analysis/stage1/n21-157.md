**Verdict.** Both committees read the same submitted PDF. They found mostly the same faults: no HER baseline, a planner cost that leans on Euclidean distance, an unspecified cost model c_φ, simple domains and no limitations section. No score moved in either committee, so the decisions follow the first reviews (A: 4, 3, 4, 4; B: 6, 6, 6, 6). Each AC restated its reviewers. The difference is weight and depth. Committee A judged the method too underspecified to accept, and its rebuttal phase found new gaps. Three B reviewers called the same text well written. B accepted vaguer answers and left the faults to the camera-ready. The paper supports A's specific claims. Line 217 names a "monotonic hyperbolic model" with no definition or loss. Line 334 keeps a Euclidean term in the planner cost (α from 0.9 to 0.1), against the rebuttal's "warm start" claim. The authors' own HER table puts HER within 5 points of CO-PILOT in 2D and 3D, and ahead in 5D.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_SCqH (4, confidence 5) argued that the planner gets a dense Euclidean signal that sparse-reward baselines lack. SCqH added that without it the method falls below SoRB (appendix Fig. 6, not in the PDF). The authors did not dispute this. The rebuttal then gave a c_φ loss that increases monotonically in the prediction. It has no optimum at the true cost, and its constant b is inert; the authors agreed b can go. The rebuttal also described a sub-goal re-sampling step that Eq. 9 and the text omit. Reviewer_uZ3D (3, ten hours) listed formulation gaps: test-time execution, stochastic costs under SGT's policy gradient, and costs pooled across tree levels. uZ3D never replied. Reviewer_YPXK and Reviewer_Exeq accepted the clarifications but said the rewrite needed new review. Exeq misread the ambiguous Ant-maze sentence (lines 305-306) as removing walls. The AC wrote two sentences: unclear writing, weak baseline comparison.

**Committee B (poster).** All four gave 6, and none argued strongly. Reviewer_8yHH (confidence 5, 1.5 hours) wrote the most substantive critique: thin literature on hierarchical abstraction and wrong claims about planning. The paper says sampling-based graphs "cannot be shared across tasks" (lines 38-39), yet it cites PRM. 8yHH was "left with more questions" but stayed "predisposed" to accept. Reviewer_NJTw asked SCqH's question: how is c_φ trained, and does L2 drive the cost? The authors told NJTw that they minimise "the difference" from true costs. That answer differs from the formula given to A, and NJTw accepted it. Reviewer_qKpT kept 6, citing simple domains and missing ablations. Reviewer_Fa86 accepted a training-time answer to an execution-time question. Fa86 also accepted the claim that RRT* ignores obstacles, although Fig. 4(c) counts RRT* collision checks. The meta-review praises the "generally clear presentation" and names no weakness.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the unsound c_φ loss, the missing sub-goal validity step, test-time execution, the appendix result without the Euclidean prior, and the "planner" misnomer. Only B raised missing hierarchical-abstraction and motion-planning work, inaccurate claims about planning, limited novelty over sub-goal trees, non-stationarity, and missing error bars (Figs. 2a, 2c, 4a-c; checklist 3.3 says yes). Both raised HER, the Euclidean prior, c_φ training, simple domains and limitations. A treated them as disqualifying; B treated them as revisions. Neither noted the +10 penalty for segments that cross obstacles (lines 332-333). It gives the planner map knowledge that SAC, PPO and SoRB lack. Checks use paper_submitted.pdf, main text only; the appendix is not in the PDF.
