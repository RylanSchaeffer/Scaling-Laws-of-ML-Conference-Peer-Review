## Verdict

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Each saw a curiosity bonus that no reviewer showed to be wrong, strong results on the super-hard SMAC maps, and doubts about the episodic memory. In each, the rebuttal's new ablations turned a split into a three-to-one majority. Reviewer_XAps (to 7) and Reviewer_qZwR (to 6) rose in A; Reviewer_DyLw rose to 7 in B. In each, the only 5 did not push for rejection. Reviewer_rGKf wrote "I am not fighting for rejection", and Reviewer_hMh6 did not reply. Means are 6.0 (A) and 6.25 (B), and both area chairs (ACs) followed them. The memory, which both committees doubted, got the least scrutiny. Only A received the 17-map memory ablation. It shows EMC below EMC-wo-M at 2.0M steps on four maps, for example 46.9 against 71.9 on 5m_vs_6m. The authors still wrote "EMC significantly outperforms EMC-wo-M", and no one in A replied.

## Committees

Committee A (7, 6, 6, 5). Reviewer_vJkd (6) read Figure 8 correctly. Memory speeds early learning a little and leaves final performance unchanged, on 3 of 17 maps. vJkd asked for all 17 maps, then did not answer the reply that showed the four losses. Reviewer_rGKf (5) called the max-return memory a greedy heuristic. The paper concedes this at lines 362-364: memory "may result in local optimal policies". rGKf wanted the memory removed but accepted the rest. XAps and qZwR rose after new baselines (EMC-Decentralized; QPLEX-Global on three super-hard maps). qZwR's sparse-reward point rested partly on a misreading: the gridworld rewards only joint arrival (lines 258-260). The AC wrote that "all of the reviewers" found the idea well motivated and were "generally satisfied" on stochasticity. Neither statement fits rGKf, and vJkd never replied.

Committee B (7, 6, 5, 7). Reviewer_DyLw drove the record, and their checkable points held. MAVEN is absent from Figures 4 and 5. WQMIX is absent from the didactic example, although line 276 says "other methods failed". Figure 6 counts medians with no significance test. After three rounds and new ablations (TD-error, centralized and individual-error bonuses, a noisy-reward gridworld), DyLw rose to 7 on four conditions. Reviewer_d4Cx (7) saw correctly that Eq. 6 describes exact matching; the nearest-neighbour threshold appeared only in the rebuttal. d4Cx then accepted a WQMIX table that repeats most of the same values for two different maps. Reviewer_hMh6 (5) argued that the method lacks novelty. The cited reachability work does not combine prediction error with episodic control, and Eq. 2 does define x (lines 184-186). Reviewer_DEY8 (6) summarised the method as "sharing local observations among agents", which it is not. The AC set hMh6 aside as the reviewer "who did not respond".

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A: remove the memory (rGKf); a decentralized Q^ext baseline (XAps); why outdated maximum returns do not break learning (XAps); the hyper-parameter search (rGKf); the cost of the curiosity module (XAps).

Only B: why not use the TD error as the bonus, and a true global-curiosity ablation (DyLw); WQMIX on the didactic task and the significance of Figure 6 (DyLw); limited novelty (hMh6, DEY8); exact-match memory, weak WQMIX baselines, and a bonus that can vanish before exploration ends (d4Cx).

Both committees raised the memory's motivation, stochastic environments, missing MAVEN comparisons, the single-agent question, sparse rewards and notation. The authors sent near-identical tables to both. B's discussion went deeper but reached the same weighting. Both treated the memory doubt, the paper's weakest point, as minor.
