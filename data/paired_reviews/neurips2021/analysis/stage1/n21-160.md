## Verdict

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. In each, three of four reviewers scored 6 or 7. No reviewer found an error that holds up. Both saw the same gaps and judged them fixable. The theory combines [3], [24] and [26]. The experiments drop the theory's conditions, report no runtime, and give S-GP-TS 5,000 evaluations against 750 for the baselines. The support differed. In A, the three positive reviewers had confidence 2, 2 and 1, and none checked the proofs. A's only reviewer above confidence 2, Reviewer_eTmV (4, confidence 5), called the paper "incremental". A's area chair (AC) accepted on a novelty claim that the paper's own reference [30] contradicts. B's reviewers had confidence 3 or 4, and its AC called the paper "somewhat borderline". B's acceptance is the better supported.

## Committees

Committee A (6, 7, 6, 4). Reviewer_eTmV spent 9 hours, and seven of its eight checkable points hold. The experiments set α_t = 1 (line 295), but Theorem 1 needs a growing α_t. Figure 1 reports simple regret (lines 304-305), but the theory bounds cumulative regret. There is no wall-clock time, the budgets are 5,000 against 750, and there is no UCB baseline. One point fails: Proposition 1 at α_t = 1 is consistent, because Assumptions 3 and 4 bound the unscaled posterior. The authors replied that Figure 1 shows "average cumulative regret". Their own text says otherwise, and eTmV never replied. Reviewer_sgAN gave a 7 in six sentences and named no weakness. Reviewer_52bF called its own review an "educated guess". Reviewer_Psad (6) wrote "I was unable to check the proofs". No score moved. The AC called this "the first paper I am aware of" with a regret bound for Bayesian optimization with sparse GPs. Reference [30], Calandriello et al. (2019), is subtitled "scalable and no regret", and eTmV cited it. The AC turned eTmV's α_t point into a camera-ready request.

Committee B (5, 6, 7, 7). Reviewer_AeT2 (5) correctly noted that the experiments omit [30]. AeT2 also asked for a contrast with Bedi et al. (2020) and did not answer the rebuttal. Reviewer_MHRh (6) correctly noted no runtime plot, no inducing-feature experiment and a molecular kernel outside the theory. MHRh accepted the proof explanation and kept 6. Reviewer_Fhfj raised the score to 7 after the rebuttal explained τ and the budget comparison. Reviewer_MheU (7) praised "extensive numerical simulations" and asked one question. MheU also said the method scales "to larger dimensional problems"; the paper says it "still suffers from the curse of dimensionality". The AC summarised the reviews in five generic sentences.

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A: α_t = 1 in the experiments, which the AC adopted; simple instead of cumulative regret; a t^{2d} discretization that dominates the sampling cost; a batch bound linear in B, as for B separate runs. All four came from eTmV. Psad alone noted discrete maximisation in theory against L-BFGS-B in practice.

Only B: Bedi et al. (2020) and the regret-complexity trade-off (AeT2); k-DPP inducing points untethered from the objective (AeT2); no inducing-feature experiment or ablation (MHRh); the cost of choosing inducing points (MheU).

A's unique issues are the sharper ones, because they test whether the experiments show what the theory claims. A's AC adopted only the α_t point. No one followed up the regret metric, and neither decision depended on it.
