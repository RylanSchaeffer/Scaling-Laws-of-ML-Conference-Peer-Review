**Verdict.** Both committees saw a borderline paper and mostly the same concerns. They split on how the rebuttal landed. Reviewers on both sides saw the idea as monolingual syntax-aware pre-training (StructBERT, LIMIT-BERT) carried to the multilingual setting. Both sides noted small gains (0.5 to 1.1 points on XNLI and XQuAD) without error bars. Both doubted that the gains come from UD supervision, given extra parameters and extra UD text. The rebuttal's main new experiment, a four-language XQuAD ablation, answered what B's reviewers had asked for. All four B reviewers raised their scores to 6. A's central request, a comparison with StructBERT and LIMIT-BERT, got only a promise. No A reviewer replied, and A's area chair (AC) made that missing comparison decisive. The paper bears out the shared doubts more than B's AC's "carefully done". The checklist reports no error bars. The Table 1 baselines are the numbers published in the XLM-R paper (XNLI 66.3, 76.2, 80.9), while the UD models were pre-trained from scratch (lines 171-172). Only Table 7 and the rebuttal table hold the pre-training run fixed. A's AC also overstated one gap. Table 7's "w/o Universal Structure Integration" row already tests a multilingual parsing objective without the architectural change, on two languages.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 4, 5, 7, 6; mean 5.5. Reviewer_JRfY (4, 1.75 hours) gave a specific reason: no empirical comparison with the closest prior work. The paper confirms it; StructBERT and LIMIT-BERT appear only in the introduction and related work. Reviewer_kc6w (5) showed that the biaffine equation adds a matrix to vectors, and the authors conceded unwritten "extra processing". kc6w also named the parameter and UD-text confounds, which the paper confirms (lines 133-134, 164-166). Reviewer_ZFQx's 7 at confidence 3 is three short paragraphs; its only caveat is that the idea "is not new". It is not evidence of quality. Reviewer_uazG (6) objected only to missing random seeds. The rebuttal claimed five-seed averages that the paper never reports. uazG edited the review on 2021-08-25, after the rebuttal, but stated no change. The AC sided with the two low ratings, adopted JRfY's point, did not mention the rebuttal, and invited a resubmission.

**Committee B (poster).** All four final ratings are 6 at confidence 4. Each reviewer raised the score after the rebuttal. Reviewer_WQqW wrote "rating 5 --> 6" but still wants XNLI and XQuAD ablations. Reviewer_dbNQ said the new ablation "partially" answered the concern. Reviewer_Cgr3 raised but rejected the authors' claim of a misunderstanding. Four of Reviewer_RgYB's six questions came from missing the XLM-R large rows in Tables 1 and 2. RgYB apologized and raised. So part of B's rise corrects a reviewer error, not the paper. Every B reviewer moved on a partial answer and kept open concerns. The AC's meta-review is warmer than four 6s: "breaks new ground", "carefully done", "results are strong". It drops the open confounds and keeps only a request for the follow-up XNLI and XQuAD results.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A demanded an empirical comparison with StructBERT and LIMIT-BERT. B's Reviewer_WQqW cited both papers but asked only to state the differences. Only A questioned the separately trained evaluation parser (kc6w) and the token-level parsing choice (JRfY). Only B asked for ablations beyond SPMRL's two languages (WQqW, dbNQ, RgYB, Cgr3). Only B noted that pre-training on UD inflates the UD parsing results (Cgr3); the paper's footnote 2 concedes it. Only B asked for XTREME and for initialization from the released weights (WQqW). Neither committee noticed that the main-table baselines are published numbers, not models retrained under the authors' set-up.
