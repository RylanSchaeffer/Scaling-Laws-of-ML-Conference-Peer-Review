**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. Every reviewer liked the idea of a learned selector that inserts hard bugs for a detector. Both valued the new PyPIBugs test set and the 19 new bugs found. Both treated the false-positive rate (19 of 1000 warnings real, l.332) as a limit, not a flaw. Both had reviewers who asked for other baselines, and both ACs judged that gap non-fatal. The means are close (A 6.67, B 7.0). The committees found different weaknesses; none moved a decision. In A, Reviewer_6URn showed correctly that the learned selector's gain over a random selector is small and unreplicated, but still rated 7; B never raised it. In B, Reviewer_e6hN found a real error in the CuBERT comparison text and held a 5 until the rebuttal. B's spread (5 to 9) is wider, but its extremes did not set the tier.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 7, 6). Reviewer_6URn (confidence 5) wrote the only deep review. It argued that the experiments do not show the learned selector matters. Table 1 confirms this for RandomBugs: the gain over the random selector is 0.1 to 0.2 joint points. On PyPIBugs the gain is 2.5 (GNN) and 3.4 (GREAT) points, from one run each, and checklist 3(c) admits no error bars. 6URn also correctly called "semantics-preserving" a misnomer; l.183-184 concedes this for mirroring. The authors promised error bars and a selector-hardness test. 6URn kept its 7 and called the paper "otherwise great". Reviewer_3FwM (7) gave generic praise and good questions (aliasing, k = 5, CodeBERT); its summary calls the method "supervised". Reviewer_XqDo (6, 2.5 hours) repeats its summary word for word, then adds praise, a vague baseline request and typos. The AC called the paper "strong" and the evaluation "still fairly convincing". It did not mention 6URn's selector concern.

Committee B (7, 9, 5). Reviewer_e6hN wrote the most careful review. It found that the CuBERT example at l.293-295 omits CuBERT's in, is, and/or operators. It also found no NoBug examples in PyPIBugs and a wrong line reference for Fig. 3a. All three hold. The authors called the CuBERT text a writing error and listed their operator mask. They also added an AUPRC test with fixed code as NoBug examples. e6hN rose from 5 to 7, and the camera-ready corrects the example. Reviewer_vPhi gave 9 at confidence 5 after 1.5 hours. Its summary is accurate but gives the real-bug yield as "a TPR of 0.19%"; the paper reports 1.9% precision. vPhi never joined the discussion. Reviewer_aZJ8 (5) wanted Hoppity and DeepDebug baselines. Both are absent; DeepDebug appeared on arXiv weeks before the deadline. Its later addendum asks for comparison with DeepBugs-style generated data, which Table 1's Supervised and Random Selector rows already give. aZJ8 never replied to the authors. The AC cited the reviewers' "final feeling" and passed aZJ8's points to the revision.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A asked whether the learned selector reliably beats random selection (asynchronous training, no error bars, no hardness or diversity measure). Only A asked for an augmentation-only ablation, objected to "semantics-preserving", noted harmless "bugs" from aliased variables, and raised k = 5 and an uncited ICLR 2021 paper. Only B raised these issues:

- the wrong CuBERT operator example (e6hN, confirmed, fixed);
- no NoBug examples in PyPIBugs (e6hN, confirmed);
- Hoppity and DeepDebug baselines, and novelty against DeepBugs (aZJ8);
- training time above one week (aZJ8, confirmed);
- a precision-matched CuBERT comparison (vPhi).

A's selector concern is the most damaging point and is correct. A accepted the paper before the authors answered it, and B never asked.
