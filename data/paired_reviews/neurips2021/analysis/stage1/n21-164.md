**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. All eight reviewers rated the paper 6 or 7. It is the first study of bandits in the shuffle model and is clearly written. Its best algorithm comes within a log T factor of the central model and removes the local model's multiplicative 1/ε². It joins arm elimination with a known shuffle summation protocol; its one new idea is growing batches. It has no experiments. Seven of eight reviewers called the techniques standard or the novelty limited. Only the level differed: A averaged 6.75, B 6.0. Three A reviewers named the novelty concern and still gave 7. Three B reviewers made it their centre and gave 6. The discussion widened the gap. The authors sent the same novelty paragraph to Reviewer_Kux9 (A) and Reviewer_gRZS (B). Kux9 raised its score, although the submission already makes that argument at l.224-231. In B, Reviewer_321T lowered its score after reading the others. Both ACs followed their reviewers. No reviewer found an error in the results. The committees weighted the same facts differently, and both weightings stayed above the accept line.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 7, 7, 7). Reviewer_HQch (6, confidence 5) wrote the most detailed review. It asked for sub-Gaussian rewards, experiments and a practical batch size. It correctly found that the central (ε, δ) bound at l.220 shows no δ; the authors conceded this. Reviewer_iPAH (7, confidence 4) called the techniques "fairly standard" but the result surprising. It asked whether the log T and δ terms are tight, then never replied. Reviewer_Kux9's only weakness was few new ideas. It rose to 7 after a reply that mostly restated Section 3.2, and did not say the concern was resolved. Reviewer_dj8Y's short 7 is weak evidence. It says the algorithm has "the same regret guarantees" as the central model, but the bound is a log T factor worse. It calls the analysis "correct", but its confidence label says the math was not carefully checked. The AC's short meta-review asks the authors to add the naive-conversion argument, which the submission already contains.

Committee B (6, 6, 6, 6). Three of four final reviews turn on novelty. Reviewer_gRZS (confidence 4) called the method "a mix and match of known results". The paper partly agrees: it calls its analysis "somewhat standard" (l.192) and its privacy proof "trivial" (l.189). gRZS also correctly noted that communication cost is not analysed. Its text reads below the bar, yet it rated 6; its edit of 2021-08-31 states no change. Reviewer_3BED (confidence 2) found the results "not very surprising" given a Θ̃(1/ε) summation protocol. Reviewer_qxTz found the algorithms novel but listed gaps that the paper confirms: no shuffle lower bound, no experiments, a one-line privacy proof. Reviewer_321T, a self-declared non-expert, lowered its score to 6 on novelty after reading the others. The AC accepted on the unanimous 6s. It overstated the result as "the same results as global DP".

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the missing δ in the central bound (HQch, iPAH; confirmed), returning users (HQch) and the standard bounds in Section 2.3 (HQch). Only B raised these:

- 1/Δ factors instead of KL-based ones (qxTz);
- no comparison of the distribution-independent bounds (qxTz; confirmed);
- an unclear batch set B in the regret definition, and an unused "LB" in the caption of Table 1 (321T; confirmed);
- no communication-cost analysis (gRZS; confirmed);
- why the shuffle model matters for bandits (3BED, AC).

Both committees raised the log T gap: iPAH asked about it, and qxTz and gRZS counted it against the paper. None changed a decision.
