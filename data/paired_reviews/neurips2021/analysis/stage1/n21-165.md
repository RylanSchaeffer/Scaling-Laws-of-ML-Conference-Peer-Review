**Verdict.** The committees saw nearly the same scores and the same doubts; their area chairs weighed them differently. A scored higher (5/5/6/7, mean 5.75) than B (6/5/6/5, mean 5.5), yet A rejected. Both committees asked why meta-learning on mini-tasks from the current task alone stops forgetting. Both suspected the method needs tasks from one domain. The paper confirms this: Section 2 calls the shared domain "key", and both benchmarks are splits of one dataset. In A, Reviewer_AG6A restated the point as its reason to hold at 5, and the AC made it decisive. The camera-ready still calls the question "beyond the scope of this study". In B, Reviewer_FTM8 and Reviewer_KoD1 raised the same doubt but never answered the rebuttal, and the AC did not mention it. B also found a concrete weakness that A missed, baselines copied from papers with other architectures, and accepted anyway. AC discretion on the same facts decided both outcomes.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 6, 7 at confidence 4, 4, 4, 5. Reviewer_1x2r gave the 7 and the most detailed review. Three of its points are wrong: line 175 already cites [24], the masks are per channel (lines 117-119), and the task ID selects the head. Its limitations field asked "What would break the KB?", the AC's later reason to reject. It never joined the discussion. Reviewer_phLM's 6 names no weakness. Its "over 10%" gain holds only on MiniImageNet; on CIFAR-100, MARK-Task beats ACL by 0.23 points. Reviewer_GtkW rose from 4 to 5 but held below threshold on novelty, naming no prior method. Reviewer_AG6A asked for CTN and a dissimilar-task sequence. The authors ran SupSup and replay instead and promised the dissimilar-task test for the final version. The camera-ready does not have it. The AC called the paper borderline and its results "quite surprising", then rejected.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 5, 6, 5 at confidence 4, 5, 5, 4. All four reviews are substantive. Reviewer_ZFES found real errors: Algorithm 2 never initialises S_F, Figure 3 labels a 0-1 axis "Percentage", and the novelty claim at line 293 overlooks OML and ANML, which the paper itself cites. The authors conceded all three. Reviewer_fktr's 5 rests on baselines run under other protocols. Lines 231-232 concede that competitors use "AlexNet-based architectures", and the main text never says where baseline numbers come from. fktr rose from 4 to 5 but still found the empirical section "not as strong as it could be". Reviewer_KoD1 noted, correctly, that the masks, not the meta-learned KB, drive accuracy. Only fktr answered the rebuttal. The AC's text paraphrases ZFES and treats the open questions as improvements, not reasons to reject. It says the rebuttal moved "some reviewers"; only fktr moved, and it stayed below threshold.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for dissimilar-task experiments, CTN and HyperNetworks baselines, GPM on CIFAR-100 and the training cost. Only A held novelty against a positive score. Only B questioned the baseline protocols and asked whether the KB is needed at all; the rebuttal's random-KB ablation gave 37.86%. Only B noted that parameters grow linearly with tasks and found the Algorithm 2 errors. Both raised the current-task meta-learning doubt, the γ_k weighting that favours easy mini-tasks, and the missing limitations section. A's AC made the domain assumption decisive. B's AC ignored it and tolerated a protocol gap that A never saw.
