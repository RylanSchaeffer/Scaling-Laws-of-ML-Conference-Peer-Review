**Verdict.** The committees saw the same objections and weighted them differently. Both found the tractability assumptions strong and unmotivated, Algorithm 1 intractable, and the REINFORCE experiment beside the point. A's ratings (6/5/5/4) did not move after the rebuttal. Its AC listed the open concerns and said a fix needed another review round. B split 2-2 (5/6/7/4) for two reasons. Reviewer_jHzB gave a 7 against its own list of cons and never joined the discussion. Reviewer_NR8J moved from 5 to 6 after a correct clarification: under stochastic transitions, the logistic map changes the optimal policy. B's AC broke the tie toward accept "after having a brief look". It moved to the camera-ready the same deficits that A's AC found decisive. A also had sharper scrutiny. Reviewer_ViC9 showed that Assumption 3.4, as submitted, contradicts itself, and the authors conceded a "typo". B never learned this.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 5, 4 at confidence 3, 4, 3, 3. ViC9's catch is right. Lines 185-187 require each step's features to cover every direction and also to be orthogonal across steps. For H ≥ 2, no feature map satisfies both. The camera-ready sums the condition over steps. Read literally, even that version fails for the nonnegative tabular features that the rebuttal gave as an example. Two other ViC9 claims are wrong: orthogonality forces only d ≥ H, not d ≥ SA(H−1), and a monotone link does not let one maximise the linear score instead. ViC9 held at 5 because "the assumptions and the resulting bounds" were "a bit too weak". Reviewer_s9t9 held at 5 because a bounded logistic label can never be deterministic. That is correct; near-deterministic labels make κ grow like exp(B). Reviewer_LW3y misread Algorithm 1 and Section 3.2, then pressed on Assumption 3.4. The authors replied "This is wrong", although Eq. 13 defines the per-step reward LW3y meant. They later admitted they "aren't sure of the direct practical implications" of the assumptions. Reviewer_NZK6's 6 predates the rebuttal, and NZK6 never posted again. The meta-review follows ViC9 and s9t9 closely.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 5, 6, 7, 4 at confidence 4, 3, 3, 4. jHzB's 7 is the only accept-level score in the eight reviews. Its cons include the claim that Section 3.2 "is the same as the setting considered by Efroni et al." The claim is wrong; if true, it would erase the tractable result's novelty. Reviewer_RHC6 made the same Markovian-reduction error and claimed that explorability needs d ≥ HSA. RHC6 felt "more positive" after the rebuttal but kept its 5. NR8J asked why, in the tabular case, the logistic map would change the optimal policy. The authors answered correctly that the expectation sits outside the link, and NR8J raised its score. Reviewer_NUdX's 4 rests on generic complaints about presentation and novelty; it did not move. The authors' d ≥ H answer to RHC6 holds only for the corrected assumption, which B never saw. The AC conceded that all reviewers found the assumptions and the novelty over Efroni et al. insufficiently explained.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A found the contradiction in Assumption 3.4. Only A raised the non-deterministic label, the unstated complexity of Algorithm 2, the abstract's "computationally efficient" claim, and missing citations. Only B called the work an incremental mix of logistic bandits and UCBVI. Only B asked about folding success into the state and about L2 against entropy regularisation. Both raised Assumption 3.4, the intractable Algorithm 1, the N^(2/3) rate, knowledge of ω, the REINFORCE experiment and the motivation for the logistic label. Both got near-identical answers, including the robot-dance example.
