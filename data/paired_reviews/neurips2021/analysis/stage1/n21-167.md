**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently. No reviewer found a fatal error. Both saw the same limits: one internal action, regression on a fixed dataset, linear features, and a loose tie to the end-to-end model-based methods in the title. Committee A called these fair simplifications for a first theoretical step. Committee B held that the paper does not give the "theoretical account of the performance of end-to-end model-based methods" that lines 43-44 promise. In A, Reviewer_6XLT raised this framing gap, treated it as a clarity fix, and raised the score to 6. In B, the authors conceded to Reviewer_kLUp that their formulation and VIN "should be thought of as 'model-free' methods". kLUp and Reviewer_EkYa, B's two confidence-4 reviewers, then kept their 3s. EkYa and Reviewer_CGbD cited kLUp's objection. B also found correct weaknesses that A never raised. A's two 7s carry little weight, because neither reviewer checked the maths. Both ACs followed their reviewers.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 6 at confidence 3, 3, 3, 4. Reviewer_6RBj calls the analysis "fairly disconnected from existing empirical implicit-model methods" but accepts the simplifications. 6RBj also says it cannot "spot non-obvious mistakes". Reviewer_u8GD's 7 rests on two clarity points and says nothing about the setting's limits. Reviewer_eRAu wrote the most careful review in either committee. It found proof typos that the authors conceded, and it noted that the results cover optimization, not generalization. It also saw that both parameterizations reach the same optimum, so Figure 2 shows a fixed-budget effect. The authors confirmed this. 6XLT raised one action, the supervised setting and VIN versus model-based RL, as B did, plus unclear novelty. A point-by-point rebuttal with a 4-action sanity check moved 6XLT up. The three-sentence meta-review restates the consensus.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 6, 4, 3, 3 at confidence 3, 2, 2, 3, 4, 4. kLUp's 3 is a principled significance judgment: the method is a reparameterized model-free regressor without policy improvement. kLUp also calls the maths "sound". EkYa spent 20 hours but misread the setup. It doubted that g reaches every weight vector and asked how the data are sampled. Line 140, line 127 and lines 265-271 answer both. Reviewer_LFGm withdrew a claimed error in Corollary 3. The cancellation holds with one datapoint per state, a condition the paper does not state. LFGm rose to 5 but kept a valid objection to Theorem 4. Reviewer_AqM3's 6, at confidence 2, rests on proofs it did not check. The meta-review says "all six reviewers" struggled to see the connection. That overstates AqM3, who found "novel insight" into end-to-end model-based RL.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B raised these points, and each holds against the paper:

- θ̂ = A⁻¹w has a closed form (line 140), so implicit differentiation is unnecessary (Reviewer_g4M6, LFGm).
- Theorem 4 gives a rate for the residual's 1-component only, but lines 217-220 compare it with whole-loss rates (LFGm).
- At zero reward offset, Figure 2 shows the two parameterizations alike (kLUp).
- Corollaries 1-2 need an O(α²) ≤ α²C condition that the abstract omits (CGbD). The camera-ready restates Corollary 1 for gradient flow.

LFGm and kLUp also argued that a bias term, TD or baselines already handle a uniform offset; lines 306-310 dispute this. Only A asked about the optimizer in Theorem 1, the novelty of the explicit results, the fixed-budget experiments and how to set η. Both raised the one-action restriction, the supervised setting, the tie to VIN, the toy experiments and proof typos at (106).
