## Verdict

Committee B recommended an oral because its scores were higher, not because it found more in the paper. Both committees judged the paper a clear accept. The careful reviews in the two committees are close: 7 and 7 in A (Reviewer_3uQ8, Reviewer_yauQ), 7 and 8 in B (Reviewer_6QWr, Reviewer_9r4w). B's top score, the only 9 among the seven reviews, comes from a 2-hour, one-paragraph review (Reviewer_xycR). Reviewer_LgSM raised a generic review to 8 after the rebuttal and gave no reason. B's area chair wrote two sentences that restate the scores. A's area chair wrote a reasoned meta-review and read the paper as "another nicely done example of using a mix of existing and new tools". That reading fits a poster. The two committees weighed the same evidence differently. B's thinner, more generous reviews set its tier.

## Committees

Committee A (7, 7, 8; poster). Reviewer_yauQ said most of the theory repeats C-learning, the authors' earlier paper. This is correct: Sec. 4.1 follows that analysis, and the authors conceded it. Reviewer_yauQ also said SQIL was the only imitation baseline. This is wrong, because AIRL and DAC are in the paper. After the rebuttal, Reviewer_yauQ raised the rating, but only to 7. Reviewer_3uQ8 called the image tasks "relatively simple" and asked whether the tasks were chosen to favour the method. The paper itself calls the reaching tasks "mechanically easier". Reviewer_3uQ8 stayed at 7. Reviewer_FCFz gave 8 after 1.5 hours, with general praise and no question. The area chair limited the "rather significant impact" to "some domains".

Committee B (8, 9, 7, 8; oral). Reviewer_xycR's 9 rests on "very well presented and the work is solid" and "one minor comment". It is not evidence of exceptional quality. Reviewer_LgSM listed general strengths, and the only con is "not really a negative". Reviewer_9r4w (8 hours) asked specific questions on halting, success-example actions and data needs. The authors answered each one and cited two ablations. Reviewer_9r4w then raised the rating to 8. Reviewer_6QWr (7 hours) wrote the strongest praise ("very high quality") but gave 7. The meta-review says only that all reviewers liked the paper and that the discussion raised the scores.

## Issues one committee raised and the other did not

Only A raised the overlap with C-learning. The point is correct, but A's area chair judged the elements "nicely distinct". So the point at most shaped the "existing and new tools" wording. Only A raised the simple image tasks, possible task selection, evaluation by return instead of success rate, and the missing C-learning baseline. The paper concedes the image limitation in Sec. 6. Only B raised halting, data needs against imitation learning, the interpretability of an implicit reward, non-binary success and the off-policy choice. These were questions, not weaknesses, and the authors answered all of them. Both committees saw the same strong state-based results. A gave weight to the weaker image results, while B called the experiments "comprehensive".
