**Verdict.** Both committees saw a novel idea with small gains. They split on the reviewer draw and on how the AC weighed small gains. A drew two thin accept reviews, Reviewer_WWma (8) and Reviewer_NjUA (7). Neither named a real weakness, and neither posted after the rebuttal. With two detailed critics at 5 and 4, the mean was 6.0. A's AC conceded that the reject reasons were "far more detailed" and accepted on its own reading. B drew three engaged reviewers and one thin one, and all four ended at 5. Their main points hold against the submission. The abstract claims to "simultaneously mitigate" noise and imbalance, but no experiment combines them. GLC beats GDW by 2.04 and 10.67 points under 40% flip noise. In Table 4, GDW leads the best baseline by 0.11 to 1.04 points, within about one of its own standard deviations. B's AC adopted these points and rejected. A's AC rested its novelty case partly on the claim that no loss change can match a gradient method. The paper's Eq. 6 refutes this, as Reviewer_jnwG said.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 5, 8, 7, 4 at confidence 3, 4, 3, 4. WWma says GDW "significantly outperforms existing methods" and asks only notation questions. Tables 3 and 4 do not support that sentence. NjUA spent 2 hours, restates the paper's claims, and concludes that it "has some contributions". jnwG raised to 5 after the rebuttal's mixed-setting table but held on novelty. That point is correct: under the zero-mean constraint, the update equals the gradient of a weighted cross-entropy on logits shifted by log ω. AiLN wrote that it would raise to 5 but never entered the change. Its points on ImageNet and small margins hold. Its remark that [22-24] are arXiv papers is a misreading that the reference list invites. The AC accepts that "Improvements are indeed small" because the method costs nothing and "seems to always improve" with noise. That holds against MWNet only; GLC leads under 40% flip noise. The missing ImageNet results stay its "main concern".

**Committee B (reject).** All four rated 5. Reviewer_tepP spent 10 hours and wrote the most detailed review. tepP accepted most answers but kept 5 because the paper "needs major revision rather than minor one". tepP's claim that Eq. 3 holds only for j = t is wrong, because y_j = 0 for j ≠ t. Reviewer_B8Dp raised to 5 but rejected the authors' answer that combination baselines were "not our duty". Some of B8Dp's clarity points are answered in the text; lines 40-41 define the "three kinds of information". B8Dp's point on the inverted imbalance-factor definition and Reviewer_Khcd's flip-noise point are correct. Reviewer_iJG7 praised every criterion, gave 5 for a "somewhat marginal" technique and no theory, and never replied. The meta-review copies Khcd's post-rebuttal points almost word for word. It adds B8Dp's baselines, instance-dependent noise and Menon et al. 2015.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B said that the paper never tests its claimed joint setting. In A, the authors used the mixed-setting table only to answer the margin complaints, and no reviewer questioned the framing. Only B singled out flip noise, the unclear "target" notation, the unreported zero-mean ablation, combination baselines, and the Clothing1M ethics flag, which the ethics reviewer cleared. Only A asked for ImageNet, for RIDE and OLTR baselines, and for state-of-the-art Clothing1M accuracy. Both raised small margins, gradient versus loss reweighting, and the missing limitations section. Both ACs called the idea novel. A judged small gains "enough for adoption"; B wanted the claimed setting tested first.
