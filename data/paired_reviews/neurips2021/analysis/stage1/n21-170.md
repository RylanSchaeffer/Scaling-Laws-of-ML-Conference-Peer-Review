**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently. Both found the method a small change to PGD adversarial training and to two certification methods. Both found the Figure 1 gains small, and the figure confirms it. In Figures 1a and 1b the data-driven variants beat uniform training by at most about 3 points, with no error bars. The credit test has only 155 adversarial examples. The one large malware gain comes from a mask built from the features that the test attacks change (lines 212-218). Committee A was a security-heavy panel. It valued the evaluation on modified malware binaries. Its main doubts were factual, and the rebuttal settled them. Reviewer_8HjK also named one deciding experiment, PDF malware against EvadeML, and the authors ran it. All six A reviewers ended at 6 or 7. Committee B weighed novelty and effect size, which a rebuttal cannot change. Two B reviewers revised to 5; none crossed the threshold. B had no champion, three weak reviews and a meta-review that restates the scores. On the malware and credit results, B's reading of the effect size is the better supported one. A's accept rests on clarifications and on experiments run during the discussion.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 6, 6, 7. All six reviewers took part in the discussion. Reviewer_Dp1A (6 to 7) and Reviewer_9LNj (5 to 7) doubted that the malware attacks touched real binaries. Lines 209-213 already say that the binaries came from VirusTotal and had bytes added, so the rebuttal mostly restated the paper. Reviewer_tqQw accepted budgets that are equal only on average (lines 219-220). A strict per-sample cap cut one result from 61.75 to 47.88 percent, against 30.5 for uniform training. Reviewer_LM6b raised to 6 without naming a reason. Reviewer_8HjK correctly found that the credit and spam attacks are feature-space attacks, although the Figure 1 caption calls all three "problem-space". He then accepted one PDF table (92.9 versus 87.4 percent) without error bars. The authors admitted that it falls short of the conserved-features defense he had named. The meta-review copies sentences from tqQw and Dp1A.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 5, 5, 5. Reviewer_z45J gave the sharpest critique. The paper promises "semantically meaningful dependencies", but Ω holds only a covariance factor or per-feature weights. The authors conceded this. z45J misread the malware test attacks as norm-bounded, was corrected, and revised the score to 5. Reviewer_QUCv correctly found that lines 188-189 call Pearson and Shapley weights "expert knowledge". The Liu et al. [25] comparison (0.0617 versus 0.0601) made QUCv "less excited", though it kept 6. Reviewer_kjQY, at confidence 5, calls the defense "the proposed attack" four times and reads the certification tables as attack results. Reviewer_txFx spent 2 hours and never replied, although its "several percent" point holds. Reviewer_cq1G's 5 rests on three questions. The first B reviewer reply came 16 days after the rebuttal.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked whether the malware examples came from real binaries. Only A noted that the credit and spam attacks are feature-space, and asked for stronger problem-space attacks. Only A raised realizable-attack prior work, Tong et al.'s conserved features, per-sample budgets and discrete features treated as continuous. Only B raised correlation versus dependency, the gap between the immutable-feature motivation and the data-driven Ω, and the errors in Theorem 2.1. Only B asked about architecture, normalisation and training cost. Both raised incremental novelty, small untested Figure 1 gains, certification as a routine extension, Liu et al. [25] and fairness against uniform attacks. A accepted the rebuttal on these points; B did not.
