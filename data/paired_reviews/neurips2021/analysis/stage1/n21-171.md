**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same moderate paper and set a different bar. VigDet adds a co-activity prior graph to AMDN-HAGE [25], filtered by an unexplained cube or one 12-hour overlap rule. Only the IRA dataset has labels. Both committees named these limits. B's two detailed reviewers, P6ya and oo8n, still gave 7 for an important application and a principled model. A's reviewers wanted dense-block and tensor baselines, a graph-only ablation, and proof that the method finds coordination rather than communities. The paper confirms those gaps. Each side also had weak reviews: two A detractors made claims the paper contradicts, and B's hg1U gave a three-sentence 7. Neither flips its side: without neqB, A's mean is 5.33; without hg1U, B's is 6.33. Nobody found a technical error.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 5, 4; no review was edited. aBu9 (6) checked the derivation and asked for VigDet on the prior graph alone, and whether the method only finds communities. After the rebuttal, aBu9 said key ablations were still missing and kept 6. insa (5) made the strongest case. The baselines it names (Fraudar, temporal graph embedding, tensor factorisation) are absent, and Table 1's two prior-graph baselines score at chance (AUC 52.5, 53.5). Its hashtag point is correct: 11 of the top 30 hashtags of the "coordinated" COVID group (7k of 31k accounts) are Canadian politics, and suspension rates are 4.26% against 3.45%. neqB (4) said VigDet is compared only with methods without prior knowledge. Lines 246-255 contradict this: Co-activity, Clickstream, LPA and GNN all use prior graphs. q2wM (5), "Without having deep expertise in this area", said no ablation removes the prior. AMDN-HAGE + KMeans, VigDet's starting point, is that comparison (AP 82.0 against 87.2). Neither replied to the rebuttal. The AC counted "three knowledgeable reviewers" against one and listed every concern, including clarity, which aBu9 and q2wM had praised.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 5, 7, 7, 7. P6ya (7) knows the disinformation literature and called the work incremental over AMDN-HAGE. It still valued the new mix of data and expert knowledge. The rebuttal's account of the cubic weighting satisfied it; whether its 7 was a raise is not visible. oo8n (7) raised the cubic exponent, the single temporal rule, an unproven convergence claim (lines 197-198) and the single labelled dataset. It kept 7 and called the COVID validation "compelling". hg1U (7, four hours) wrote three sentences of praise. Its one fact is wrong: the authors did not verify the model on the data they collected. w5yn (5) called the method incremental and Section 4.2 hard to follow, then went silent. The AC's three-sentence meta-review opens with oo8n's summary almost word for word. It says "All the reviewers" saw an important application and "They found the experimental evaluation solid"; w5yn said neither.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for dense-block, tensor or temporal-graph baselines. Only A asked for a graph-only ablation, a run without AMDN-HAGE initialisation, a supervised setting and synthetic data. Only A pressed coordination against community. Only B raised adversarial evasion, other kinds of coordination (URL sharing, cross-platform), demographic bias and the convergence proof. Both raised incremental novelty, the cubic exponent, the single temporal rule, one labelled dataset and the missing harm discussion. Neither noted that the EM loop, the main technical step, adds little over one E-step: +0.6 to +2.1 AP unsupervised, -0.1 to +0.5 semi-supervised. A asked for the missing comparisons before acceptance. B accepted promised clarifications.
