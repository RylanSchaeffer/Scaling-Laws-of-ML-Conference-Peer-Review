## Verdict

Both committees saw the same facts; A's discussion lifted its scores to a spotlight, while B's reviewers kept their doubt about the XML demonstration. Every reviewer describes the same contribution: a projection step that makes HRRs trainable, shown on XML with large output-layer compression and accuracy near the baselines except at 670K labels. Before the discussion A's mean was at most 6.25, below B's final 6.67; three raises in discussion carried A to 7.0. In A, Reviewer_sKB5 received a new capacity table and rose to 8, asking for acceptance. Reviewer_m8WY and Reviewer_LW6b then raised their scores, each citing the other reviewers' views. A's AC repeated Reviewer_rafZ's case for broad interest and future work. In B, all three reviewers said that XML, with two fixed roles, never tests what HRRs are for. Only Reviewer_uoCQ moved, to 7. B's AC wrote that the results do not show usefulness in applications. A weighted the enabling step; B weighted the thin demonstration.

## Committees

Committee A (8, 7, 6, 7; spotlight). Reviewer_sKB5 is a principled champion: six hours, informed questions on capacity and loss design, two follow-up posts. The praise "very well written" is generous; the same review lists typos, a broken sentence and a placeholder. Reviewer_rafZ wrote an expert review and supplied the words the AC paraphrases ("will likely spur greater interest", "a fairly diverse audience"), but never joined the discussion. Reviewer_m8WY did not know HRRs and deferred on significance to rafZ and sKB5. Reviewer_LW6b's original review is two sentences with no specifics. The real objection came later: no comparison with TPR, NSCL or Logic Tensor Networks. The paper confirms it. LW6b still raised the score, citing "the enthusiasm of the other reviewers". The AC's "all reviewers much more confident" overstates this.

Committee B (7, 6, 7; poster). Reviewer_uoCQ and Reviewer_M8pZ show working knowledge of HRRs. Both say the task binds each label to one of two fixed roles, so it never tests compositional structure. Section 4 (eqs. 4-5) confirms this. Reviewer_rRrp, not an expert, asked what the method is for and what it replaces, and misread the results as "SOTA-exceeding". The authors answered B with promised text and a worked example, not new results. Reviewer_uoCQ rose to 7 on the "foundation" argument. Reviewer_rRrp stayed at 6, unable to see a revision. Reviewer_M8pZ never posted. The two-sentence meta-review mirrors this: novel method, unconvincing application.

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A raised capacity (sKB5, rafZ), comparisons with other neuro-symbolic systems (LW6b), and Plate's own gradient-based work with HRRs (rafZ). The capacity point helped the paper. The authors answered with a table: projected HRRs reach 384 error-free pairs at d=8100, naive HRRs 24. The champion then rose to 8. The comparison point was never answered with data. The prior-work point was never answered; the camera-ready still says no fixed-length VSA has been used in gradient-based learning. Only B said that XML underuses symbolic binding (uoCQ, M8pZ, rRrp) and that the purpose is unclear (uoCQ, rRrp). Only B noted that AttentionXML's hierarchical head became a fully connected layer (rRrp; Sections 5 and 5.2 confirm). A rebuttal cannot close these concerns, so B had no comparable upward move. Claims about omissions in the submission rest on the authors' own concessions.
