**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, and both decisions sat at the threshold. Both saw a simple extension of double oracle to extensive-form games, close to SDO, that wins mostly on games the authors built (m-Clone Leduc, the Loss Game). In each committee the rebuttal moved one or two reviewers up, but nobody above 6. A ended at 5, 6, 6, 7 (mean 6.0) and B at 5, 6, 6, 5 (mean 5.5). Each AC then accepted, on opposite readings of the same facts. A's AC was grudging: "not a particularly novel direction", "fairly weak" results on existing games, but "a pretty good reference point". Privately it had asked for "very convincing" experiments, and it accepted below that bar. B's AC was generous. It read the clone-game wins as support for the motivation, called the theory "significant and novel" (no B reviewer said so), and waived re-review of the NXDO reframing, against Reviewer_2oEE and Reviewer_7BdA. So the agreement comes from two lenient calls on a borderline paper, not from strong reviews. The paper bears out the critics. Its abstract claims gains over PSRO and XFP "in the same amount of time", but no experiment measures time. On Oshi-Zumo (Fig. 3c), XDO needs several times CFR's states to reach 10^-2 and leads only below about 2×10^-3.

**Committees**

Committee A (5, 6, 6, 7). Reviewer_ae9r (5, confidence 5) wrote the strongest critique. It found SAEF and Čermák et al. close and uncited, and said the paper shows NXDO only where it helps. The plots it asked for showed NFSP beating NXDO on Leduc, and the authors conceded. It kept its 5 through five replies. Reviewer_6vBM questioned novelty against SDO, accepted the authors' explanation, and probably rose to 6: its edit date matches its "convincing" comment. Reviewer_H8Fr (6, confidence 5) found the contribution "not particularly exciting" but a useful reference point, and read Fig. 3c correctly. Reviewer_1Gab's 7 rests on a two-hour review with no discussion, though it caught the abstract's unsupported claim. The AC took H8Fr's framing, overstated the reviewers' praise for the experiments, and dropped its own SAEF question.

Committee B (5, 6, 6, 5). Reviewer_pMFZ rose from 5 to 6 after the SDO explanation, a random tie-breaking plot and promised SDO comparisons. It kept one concern: NXDO's edge shows only on new benchmarks. Reviewer_7BdA (6, confidence 5) wrote the most careful review, and its checkable points hold: the DREAM notation is POSG notation, Algorithm 1 omits the ε schedule, and "low exploitability" is not measured. It liked the reframing but would not credit it "sight unseen". Reviewer_2oEE rose only to 5, because the reframing "requires another round of review". Reviewer_nx7U (5) asked for definitions that the paper gives (Eq. 1, l.147-148), listed ARMAC as missing although the paper cites it, and never replied. The AC accepted on its own reading and ignored nx7U.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these issues:

- the abstract's time claim (1Gab; confirmed, conceded);
- XDO's cost against CFR on Oshi-Zumo (H8Fr; confirmed);
- SAEF as near prior work, and warm starting (ae9r);
- Proposition 2 stated for PSRO, not DO (6vBM; conceded);
- Kuhn's theorem stated without perfect recall (H8Fr; confirmed).

Only B raised the POSG notation, the late statement of the two-player zero-sum setting, the ε schedule, deterministic tie-breaking, missing deep-RL baselines (DREAM, ReBeL, ARMAC) and the need for re-review. Both raised SDO, NXDO's failure in small action spaces, weak CFR baselines and Čermák et al. No one-sided issue changed a decision. The camera-ready adds SDO and CFR+ comparisons, but not the continuous-poker experiment promised to B.
