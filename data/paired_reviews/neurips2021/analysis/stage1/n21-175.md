**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. The paper adapts DETR to grounding with phrase queries and trains REC and RES jointly, as MCN [36] did. It beats prior work by large margins. Nobody found a technical error. In A, Reviewer_7Fb1 and Reviewer_e6Cm called it a combination of known parts and kept 5 after the rebuttal. In B, Reviewer_zbBR made the same objection, "framework similar to DETR, multitasking studied by MCN", but kept 6 and called the model "a new strong baseline". All four B reviewers gave 6. A's most thorough reviewer, Reviewer_u5gv (confidence 5), raised to 7. A's area chair (AC) followed the two 5s and gave novelty as the reason. The split is a different novelty bar for a strong empirical paper. In a three-reviewer panel, two borderline rejects decided it.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 7, 5. u5gv wrote the most detailed review in either committee, and its checks hold. Single-task RES already beats the best prior result on RefCOCO+ val (58.39 against 54.21, Tables 2 and 4). The paper's own tables show MAttNet [56] and NMTree [29] on both tasks, so the claim that only MCN does both (lines 60, 271-272) is false. Yang et al. (ECCV 2020) is not cited. After new ablations and a comparison with that paper, u5gv raised 6 to 7. 7Fb1 (two hours, confidence 3) raised its score after the pretraining reply. Its six-epoch claim is correct (line 244), but a short schedule can only understate the gain. Its final ground is taste: "no new conceptual thinking on REC and RES". e6Cm's update says it agrees with 7Fb1. It does not address the explicit-constraint ablation that answered its own request, and it calls the losses "performance tricks". Neither 5 posted in the discussion. The AC wrote that "the reviewers agreed" on limited novelty. u5gv agreed that the parts were known but did "not strongly share the novelty concerns". The AC called the gains "reasonable"; e6Cm had called them "huge".

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 6, and nobody edited a review. zbBR (confidence 5) made A's novelty point. It also asked for a multitask ablation that Table 4 (left) already reports. Reviewer_aAnS called text queries as anchors "quite novel" and named real limits: one box per phrase, which the conclusion concedes, and manual phrase spans. It accepted that MDETR scores higher. Reviewer_Ljjc got answers on Hungarian matching, naming and loss ablations, and kept 6. Reviewer_GFR8 wrote one paragraph of praise with no weakness in two hours and never replied. Its 6 carries no argument. Three of four B reviewers stated confidence 3. The AC wrote that the rebuttal "further strengthens their confidence", but no rating or confidence changed.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the Flickr30k setup with full captions, the missing caption-based baselines [1, 9], the missing Yang et al. comparison, the false "only other method" claim, the six-epoch pretraining and MCN's explicit constraint. The rebuttal answered these, some only with promises, and the AC mentions them only as revision advice. Only B raised MDETR, one box per phrase, manual phrase spans, the analysis of text anchors and caption bias. Both raised the DETR-plus-MCN novelty, Hungarian-free training as a direct result of one-to-one queries, the source of the gains, missing loss ablations, the unablated attention-graph layer, inconsistent names and the missing societal-impact discussion. The decisive issue, novelty, was shared. Only its weight differed.
