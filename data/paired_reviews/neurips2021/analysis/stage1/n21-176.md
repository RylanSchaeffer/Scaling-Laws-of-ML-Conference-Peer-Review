**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently. The area chairs (ACs), not the reviewers, made the difference. Both panels saw a known logit loss and a longer attack schedule that give strong, broad results. Both detractors praised the experiments. Each panel had one novelty-based 4 and three ratings of 6 or 7. Committee A's panel was more expert and more engaged. After the rebuttal, three A reviewers raised their scores, to a mean of 6.0. No B reviewer responded, and B stayed at 5.75. A's AC wrote that "Reviewers were happy with authors' responses", then rejected: "I feel the paper lacks depth in the contributions". B's AC accepted on the scores. Both committees knew the closest precedent, Zhang et al. (CVPR 2020). In A it fed an originality complaint; B's AC asked only for a citation.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 7, 4, 6, 7; confidences 5, 4, 4, 2. Reviewer_ML4S (4, "20+ hours") made the sharpest point, and the paper confirms it. Zhang et al. [19] already used a near-identical logit loss for targeted UAPs. The submission cites [19] only for proxy data and names only C&W as a similar loss (lines 40-42, 188-190). The authors' second reply called the question "not relevant". ML4S did not reply again but had asked the AC to ignore the 4 if the concerns were met. Reviewer_1r48's main points also hold. The C&W claim has no table (lines 190-192). "Training-free" is wrong for a 300-iteration UAP, and the authors conceded. 1r48's claim of no societal-impact paragraph is wrong (lines 364-369). The rebuttal added a gradient derivation, a C&W κ sweep, and ε = 8/255 and MNIST results. Reviewer_eWPQ (confidence 5) rose to 7: "I don't think this should be a reason of rejection." 1r48 rose from "marginally below" to 6. Reviewer_Q2rC, a two-sentence review at confidence 2, rose to 7. The AC overrode this panel on its own reading and echoed ML4S's "hyper-parameter tuning". It asks for "more analysis/experiments" but names none.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 4, 6; confidences 2, 3, 3, 3. No review was edited, and no reviewer replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_c8VG (confidence 2) wrote the most careful review. Its checkable points are correct: the C&W κ is not defined, the Table 2 and Table 3 settings are incomplete, and the UAP bound is missing. Reviewer_nyxN's 7 is three bullets after two hours; it carries little weight. Reviewer_yhEq's 6 asks for code that line 58 says is in the supplement. Reviewer_dgjq's 4 rests on novelty and missing theory. Its one precedent, Naseer et al. (2019), trains a generator with a relativistic cross-entropy, a weaker match than [19]. dgjq also gives no source for results "mentioned in previous works". The meta-review praises "simple but effective" and does not mention dgjq. It names Zhang et al. (CVPR 2020), which no public B review names, and asks only for a discussion.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A found the undisclosed overlap with the loss of Zhang et al. and the wrong "training-free" claim. Only A asked for ε = 8/255, the attack cost against TTP and FDA, the robust-feature argument under small bounds, and MNIST. Only B asked about the ensemble settings, a combination with FDA or TTP, and code. Both panels flagged low novelty, the bare C&W comparison, missing theory and the thin UAP section. A's rebuttal resolved most of these for its reviewers. Only A's AC treated what remained as a reason to reject. The camera-ready adds the [19] sentence, the derivation, a C&W appendix and a UAP figure.
