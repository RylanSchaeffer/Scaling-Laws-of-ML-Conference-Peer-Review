**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same core weakness differently, and B's panel found more of the paper's gaps. In both committees, at least one reviewer called the analyses incremental: Reviewer_nCmj in A, all three reviewers in B. A's area chair (AC) rejected this charge, because each problem "is quite essential" and simple proofs are "a positive thing". B's AC adopted it in one sentence. The panels also differed. A's two 7s came from reviewers with clarification questions: whether p must be known, why agents discard messages, and how the paper compares with Chawla et al. The rebuttal answered these, and both reviewers raised their scores. B's reviewers spent more time (8, 10 and 7 hours, against 5.5, 2 and 6). They raised novelty and completeness concerns that no rebuttal can fix, and the paper confirms most of them. B's reject rests on better-checked evidence than A's accept.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 5; mean 6.33. Reviewer_kyrg raised the score to 7 after the authors explained that broadcast communication needs regulation. That review took two hours. Reviewer_4XMJ raised the score after the authors called "known probability p" (line 45) a typo. RCL-LF never uses p, so the explanation holds. 4XMJ also wrote that link failure "has not been studied before", but lines 84-85 of the paper cite earlier work on stochastic communication. Reviewer_nCmj kept a 5. Two of nCmj's points hold. Section 5 needs a central choice of the exploring agents (lines 256-260), and the authors conceded this. The proof sketch of Theorem 3 applies a lemma of Joulani et al. nCmj's first concern is weak: a known bound on the reward variance is standard in this literature. The AC followed the majority and set nCmj's objection aside on the AC's own reading.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 6; mean 5.33. No score moved. Reviewer_9KQ6 checked the appendix and found a missing constant in equation (62); the authors conceded it. 9KQ6 also noted that the Theorem 1 bound is smallest with no discarding. This is correct: the factor Σ(1 − p_i·p) + χ̄(G)·p·max p_i falls as each p_i rises. But 9KQ6's claim of no empirical evidence is wrong. Figure 3(a)-(b) shows that discarding lowers regret on multi-star and tree graphs at higher p. After the rebuttal, 9KQ6 argued that forwarded messages or shared mean estimates remove the χ̄(G) factor on log T. Reviewer_huWS (10 hours) found the work incomplete. The only lower bound, Theorem 6, is a minimax bound that matches none of the upper bounds. Section 4 drops message-passing "for simplicity". Remark 2 needs the global minimum degree. Reviewer_CiVs gave a 6 but wrote that the paper is "incremental". CiVs correctly saw that the "probabilities" in Remark 1 do not sum to one. In discussion, the AC disputed one novelty claim and wrote "I am not a big fan of arm elimination".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked about the known p, the comparison with Chawla et al., and the known variance bound. Only B noted that the theory favours no discarding, and argued that the χ̄(G) factor is avoidable. Only B noted the missing or mismatched lower bounds, the dropped protocol, and many errors in notation and naming. Both committees asked whether Theorem 1 beats no communication. As stated, it does not when Σp_i < χ̄(G)·max p_i, and Remark 2's tuning permits this. The refined bound in the rebuttal settles the point. Both committees questioned the d_min tuning, the central partition in Section 5, and the missing RCL-LF pseudo-code.
