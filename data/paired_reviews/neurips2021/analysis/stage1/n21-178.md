## Verdict

Each committee had one outlier, in opposite directions, and that alone separates spotlight from poster. Without A's 9 (Reviewer_BBLZ) and B's 3 (Reviewer_wwer), both panels end at 7, 7, 8. Both cores praised the same things: a simple baseline, strong D4RL results and a clear analysis of why iterative methods fail. Both cores raised the same weakness: under-credited prior one-step work. In each committee a reject-side reviewer rose to 7 after the rebuttal (Reviewer_vwkm in A, Reviewer_yZ2w in B). Neither outlier joined the discussion. So the means (7.75 against 6.25) differ only through the outliers. A's AC reports an explicit spotlight consensus. B's AC wrote two sentences with no case for a higher tier. The committees weighted the same facts differently; neither saw evidence that the other missed.

## Committees

Committee A (7, 7, 9, 8; spotlight). Reviewer_BBLZ's 9 is careful but exuberant. The review lists typos by line and notes that the behaviour-policy training appears only in the appendix. But it rates "Top 15%" on the strengths that the other three scored 7 or 8, and it ignores the prior one-step work. It calls the claims "modest and well validated". The camera-ready Table 1 does not support this. Against Fu et al.'s per-dataset best, Rev. KL wins on 7 of 16 datasets and Exp. Weight on 6. Only Easy BCQ (10 of 16) fits the text "all of the one-step algorithms usually outperform". Reviewer_T3oZ made this point, and the authors conceded "sometimes better and sometimes worse". Reviewer_vwkm demanded 10 seeds, standard errors and credit to Gulcehre et al. and Nadjahi et al. The authors delivered, and vwkm wrote "All issues solved". Reviewer_oZUe rose to 8 on the answers to the others. The AC repeated the consensus.

Committee B (3, 7, 7, 8; poster). Reviewer_wwer's 3 is inattentive. It calls the method "one step gradients", but the paper trains Q^β to convergence; single gradient steps define the iterative baseline. It asks for TD3+BC (arXiv 2106.06860), posted in June 2021, after the deadline. It calls the paper "very hard to follow" and "not technically sound" without one example. Its fair points are limited novelty and little theory. Reviewer_yZ2w argued novelty with specific papers, and the paper concedes the missing theory in Section 8. wwer never answered the rebuttal. Reviewer_yZ2w rose to 7 after a YOEO comparison table and wrote "I didn't double-check the GN scores". The review text still says "I cast a weak vote to reject". Reviewer_fkPB rose to 7 on the same exchange. Reviewer_Uz96 (8) asked about Gulcehre et al. and YOEO and never replied. The meta-review names no reviewer and calls the topic "off-policy" RL.

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A questioned the statistics (3 seeds, standard deviations) and the architecture (1024-unit layers and a truncated normal, unlike the D4RL baselines). The authors answered both with new runs, which made A's case stronger. Only A raised mixed behaviour policies, Figure 6's use of HalfCheetah alone, and RL Unplugged. Only B raised Goo and Niekum's YOEO, missing theory, the loose "iterative" naming, a missing broader-impact statement and Table 1's merged Fu et al. column. YOEO became a point for the paper once the authors' table showed one-step ahead on 7 of 9 datasets. The theory point is true, but the paper claims no theoretical results (checklist item 2 is N/A).
