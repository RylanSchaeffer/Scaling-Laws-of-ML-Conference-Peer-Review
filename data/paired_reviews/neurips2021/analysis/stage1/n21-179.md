**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same weakness. The bounds hold for averaged and averaged-max risks under perturbation distributions that cannot depend on the learned model. They reach the worst-case adversarial risk only through a TV term that can be arbitrarily large. Before the rebuttal the scores were close: A had 4, 6, 6, 6, and B had at most 5, 6, 6. The rebuttal phase split them. No A score moved, and A's AC rejected over three marginal 6s. Two B reviewers raised their scores, to 6 and 7, and B's AC accepted. The committees weighed the same facts differently. A detailed detractor in A, and a rebuttal that met B's concrete requests, decided the outcomes.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_Jhjk (4, 6 to 8 hours) wrote the most detailed critique in either committee, and the paper confirms its checkable points. Theorem 7's TV term depends on the sampled perturbations, so the bound is not "independent from the kind of attacks" (line 46). The authors conceded, and the camera-ready abstract changed. PGD_U adds uniform noise after the attack (lines 249-251). Lines 303-305 claim "non-vacuous guarantees on the classical risks". The theorems bound only the relaxed risks, which Proposition 3 places below the classical risk. The test perturbations also come from attacks on the prior H_P, not the learned H_Q (lines 266-268). The rebuttal skipped this claim and Jhjk's request for an n-dependent link; the camera-ready keeps the claim. Reviewer_N9WH found that training never optimizes the TV term (n=1, line 252). At n=100 that term pushes every attacked Theorem 7 bound for the voter set H above 1 (Table 1). Reviewer_y2sg and N9WH kept their 6s after the rebuttal. Reviewer_KAbu's 6 rests on one question and three wording fixes. The AC set the scores aside and asked for worst-case results or a clear link to them.

**Committee B (poster).** Reviewer_mbWu (8 hours) raised the same core limitation: the bounds are "for random noise" or carry terms "hard to interpret". mbWu made acceptance conditional on a comparison with Montasser et al. The authors supplied it, and mbWu raised the score to 6. Reviewer_YUnr asked whether the averaged-max risk tends to the adversarial risk as n grows. The authors said yes, if adversarial draws have non-zero probability, and offered a TV-free form of Theorem 7. YUnr raised to 7. YUnr's last concern stayed open: an arbitrary perturbation distribution loses the link to adversarial examples. The experiments add uniform noise of ±0.01 to a PGD point, which does not cover the perturbation ball. Reviewer_pSP3 (2 hours) wrote one paragraph of praise; its only criticism asks for more baselines. The meta-review leans on the score increases. It repeats the "independent of the type of perturbations" claim that the authors withdrew in A.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the unsupported guarantee on the classical risk, the weak noise-after-attack adversary, and the missing randomized-smoothing and distributional-robustness work. Only A also raised the conflict between low TV and voter diversity, the attacks on the prior, the MNIST-family data, and the missing single-classifier baseline. Only B raised the missing Montasser et al. comparison, notation errors at lines 73 and 137, and Theorem 5 as a known fact. Both raised the relaxed-risk limitation, the opaque TV term, the link as n grows, the one-perturbation trick in Algorithm 1, and Theorem 6's hidden n. A treated these as reasons to reject; B treated them as items for the camera-ready. Only B received an answer on the link as n grows.
