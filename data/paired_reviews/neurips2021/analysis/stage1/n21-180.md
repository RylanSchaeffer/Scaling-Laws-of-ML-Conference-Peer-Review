**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently, and A's area chair overrode its own reviewers. Both saw a pure-theory paper with no experiments. Its guarantees hold only for binary features under the uniform distribution, and it is built mostly from prior results. A's reviewers rated it higher (6/5/8/8, mean 6.75) than B's (7/6/7, mean 6.67). Three of A's four were above threshold. A's AC still rejected it with a meta-review that names no flaw: the paper "was judged as not quite ready". The only objection argued as decisive in A's record is the missing experiments. Reviewer_XbsB called it the "more severe" limitation, and Reviewer_GLcW's update argued against rejecting on that ground. B's Reviewer_22UY also wanted experiments but kept a 7. B's AC praised a "fresh theoretical approach". The discrepancy sits with A's AC, not in the reviews.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 8, 8, all at confidence 4. XbsB's 5 rests on specific points that the paper confirms. There are no experiments; every experiment item of the checklist is N/A. FindCertificate also pads every certificate to the full depth k. Figure 1 checks precision only at the leaf, and the tree is complete (lines 294-295). On the paper's own parity example, the algorithm thus adds k−2 irrelevant features, the kind of failure it charges to [RSG18]. The authors conceded and promised a remark; the camera-ready has none. GLcW doubted the polynomial's powers, with reason. Lines 308-311 imply a size bound of order C(f)^6/(ε^30 δ^27). GLcW still rose to 6 and accepted experiments as follow-up work. The two 8s are weak support. Reviewer_ikim's rests on two generic sentences; the rest of it asks for experiments and lists missing formal-XAI work. Reviewer_NH6L credits the main technical results to prior papers. Neither 8 engaged after the rebuttal. The meta-review cites none of these points.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 7 at confidence 3, 3, 4. Reviewer_22UY wrote the most substantive review. It linked the uniform-distribution limit to the hardness of learning DNF (Juba and Li, uncited). It concluded there "was not much scope for the work to accomplish much more". Reviewer_YjV9 found a real overclaim. Lines 81-82 credit the escape from hardness only to the 1−δ relaxation, but the bound is relative to average certificate complexity. The authors conceded and fixed it. Reviewer_f3tU misread the paper twice. It asked whether the noise rate p should be 1/2, and said the algorithm ignores x's value at each node; step 3(c) records it. The authors answered both, and f3tU rose from 5 to 6, citing the response and the other reviews. The meta-review is the AC's own reading and names no weakness.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the depth-k padding and the size of the polynomial. Only A asked for comparisons with SHAP, LIME, anchors and prime implicants. Only A noted that Definition 1 matches Wäldchen et al. and cited formal-XAI work on decision-tree explanations. Only B raised the lines 81-82 overclaim, Juba and Li, and the notation of Definitions 4 and 9. Both raised the missing experiments, the uniform-distribution limit, why implicit learning is needed, and the average-case guarantee. XbsB treated missing experiments as severe; 22UY treated them as a possible improvement.
