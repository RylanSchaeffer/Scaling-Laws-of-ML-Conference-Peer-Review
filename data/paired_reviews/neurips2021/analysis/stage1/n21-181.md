**Verdict.** The split comes mostly from the two area chairs (ACs), who judged near-identical borderline reviews differently. Neither committee found an error in the method. A rated the paper 7/5/6 (mean 6.0); its AC accepted and called the experiments "quite thorough". B rated it 4/6/6/6 (mean 5.5); its AC rejected against three 6s. B's AC conceded that "the majority of reviewers ended up mildly positive" and cited "fidelity and practicality". Fidelity is the one substantive issue that only B raised, and the paper confirms it. Section 6.1 measures fidelity only as regression accuracy against the network's advantage values. Apart from a visual agreement with saliency maps, the only explanation-level test is one counterfactual. In Figure 5 its leaf reads 0.23 before and after the intervention; the text says 0.11, the rebuttal 0.15. The authors conceded the gap, but Reviewer_nsW5, who raised it most fully, kept a 6. The half-point score gap comes from Reviewer_RYit's 7 in A and Reviewer_zwwU's 4 in B. On the shared facts, the two ACs set different bars.

**Committee A (poster).** Reviewer_hnrw (6, 8 hours) wrote the most careful review in either committee. hnrw noted that IDOE is MONet with an IVAE conditional prior, which lines 158-161 say only in passing. hnrw noted that Proposition 1 reuses the tree code of Quinlan and Rivest. hnrw also noted that the joint objective never trains anything end-to-end. hnrw found two figure faults. In Figure 2, moving Z2,3 also grows the bottom pillars, which weakens the no-confounder claim. In Figure 5(c), the counterfactual shows no change. Still, hnrw wanted acceptance because "the results are impressive". hnrw kept the 6 after the authors agreed to rename IDOE. Reviewer_RYit's 7 calls the method "technically correct" without a visible check. But RYit asks real scaling questions and replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_NQFz's 5 says the method has a weak link to RL. NQFz calls the target a state value; it is the advantage. NQFz did not reply to the rebuttal. The AC repeated hnrw's framing points and judged them fixable.

**Committee B (reject).** Reviewer_zwwU's 4 has a one-sentence summary and took 2.5 hours. It says the paper has no comparison with saliency and attention methods. That is wrong: Section 6.2 and the human study compare them, though only qualitatively. zwwU's valid points are the single unquantified counterfactual and the missing IRB statement. zwwU did not reply to the rebuttal. Reviewer_nsW5 (6, confidence 4) argued that approximation accuracy is not explanation fidelity. nsW5 also rejected agreement with attention maps as evidence. The authors agreed and promised a new experiment step. Reviewer_SaJC raised the score to 6 after the rebuttal. Reviewer_8RF7's 6 rests on three line-level remarks. The AC's meta-review has three sentences and names no specific flaw.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised attribution: IDOE as a renamed MONet plus IVAE, and Proposition 1 from Quinlan and Rivest. Only A said the joint RAMi objective adds nothing, because the two stages train separately. Only A noted the entangled latent Z2,3. Only A asked whether the method needs RL at all, and how MCRTS differs from VIPER. Only B asked for a quantitative fidelity test against the original agent. A's reviewers accepted regression accuracy as fidelity. Only B raised the human study's ethics, a gradient-boosting baseline, and the mimic's task performance. Both committees noted one illustrated game, 12 human participants, doubts about complex games, and unclear writing. A's AC treated these as camera-ready fixes. B's AC asked for more experiments.
