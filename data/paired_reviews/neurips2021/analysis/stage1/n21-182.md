**Verdict.** The committees saw mostly the same weaknesses and weighted them differently. Both saw that the user study cannot separate the methods, and that one deterministic prediction is scored against one ground truth up to 3 s ahead. In A, supporters raised these points as minor or as future work. In B, Reviewer_7KAw (8 h, rating 4) made them the core of a detailed evaluation critique whose checkable claims the submitted paper confirms. The rebuttal told only B that each user-study comparison had 3 raters, which strengthened the objection. Reviewer_3D5S rose only to 5 and deferred to 7KAw on evaluation. B's two 6s were thin and gave the AC no counterweight. In A, two specific 7s and two 6s raised after the rebuttal outweighed one 3, which the AC set aside as "overly harsh". No reviewer found an error in a result. The method beats all three baselines in every MPJPE cell of Tables 1-3, and the aligned-pose split in B's rebuttal keeps that lead except on 3DPW. The evidence for natural motion is as weak as B said.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B asked for the user-study size, separate root and pose errors, and distribution metrics beyond 1 s. Only B reported foot skating, bone-length changes, mismatched joint definitions across datasets, identical ablation numbers and the Δx confound. Only A questioned novelty against the original Transformer, the need for the local encoder and DCT, and the undisclosed reimplementation of [2]. Only A asked for scene-context baselines and SoMoF. Both raised the close user-study scores, deterministic prediction of a stochastic future, the value of the discriminator, few baselines, grouping by proximity and missing limitations.
