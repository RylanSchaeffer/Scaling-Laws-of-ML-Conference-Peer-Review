**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently, and A's reviewers probed the results harder. Both saw a new encoder module tested only on Spider, a 3rd-place leaderboard result, and an architecture built from known parts. Committee A judged the gains unproven. Reviewer_QG5a said the gain over RAT-SQL with large pretrained encoders is within about one point and has no standard errors. Table 2 confirms it: test gains are 1.1 points with BERT-Large and 0.4 with GAP. No table has error bars, although checklist item 3(c) answers [Yes]. Three of four A reviewers asked for other datasets. The authors replied that ATIS, Advising and WikiSQL "are subsets of Spider". That is false. Spider adds questions from six older datasets, and only to its training split. The paper's claim that Spider "contains 9 traditional specific-domain datasets" (lines 170-172) matches the nine datasets that Spider's Table 1 only compares against. A's area chair (AC), citing the reviewers, made the training-split point and rejected. Committee B never raised the single-benchmark or variance questions. Two of its reviewers called the gains "marked" and "substantially improved"; the test numbers do not support this. Its criticisms were mainly about clarity, and the rebuttal promised fixes. A's panel also had more text-to-SQL depth. B's Reviewer_1Eab calls itself "a reader without too much experience on the Spider dataset".

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 5, 4; mean 5.0. No review changed, and no reviewer replied to the rebuttal in public. QG5a's 4 is the most specific review, and its checkable claims hold. Lines 38-39 say existing methods encode the question sequentially. The paper itself calls RAT-SQL "a unified encoding mechanism" (lines 267-268). QG5a proposed a simpler baseline: a RAT layer masked to two hops. The authors described their pipeline instead of running it. Reviewer_kkfK's 6, at confidence 5, lists the same weaknesses as the negative reviews: only Spider, not state of the art, known parts. Reviewer_FKN7's 5 took one hour at confidence 2. Reviewer_va3j's 5 asserts "limit innovation" with no specifics. The AC followed the consensus. It added that ATIS and GeoQuery queries are harder than Spider's in some respects.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 6; mean 6.5. Reviewer_RQfk's 7 is praise plus two questions, and it lists no weakness. Reviewer_Db2V asked for a parameter-matched control and accepted the answer: 13M versus 15M parameters. Reviewer_hu28 called clarity "the biggest weakness" and the paper "not ready yet for publication". After the rebuttal, hu28 moved from 5 to 6. The rebuttal gave clarifications and promises but no new experiment, and that sentence stays in the final review. The AC repeated hu28's point that raw SADGA beats raw RAT-SQL. That holds on the dev set only (64.7 versus 62.7); Table 2 has no test number for it. The AC also said the design "can possibly inform and be extended to other tasks". A's reviewers counted the single task as a weakness.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for other text-to-SQL datasets (kkfK, FKN7, QG5a, AC). B's 1Eab accepted Spider as "the standard benchmark". Only A asked whether one-point margins survive RAT-SQL's training variance (QG5a). Only A flagged the wrong description of prior encoders, the concurrent LGESQL, and the fit of a one-task architecture for NeurIPS (QG5a). Only B asked for enough detail to reproduce the encoding (hu28). Only B asked for evidence that SADGA helps cross-domain transfer specifically (1Eab). Both committees questioned novelty and clarity. Both asked for controls that isolate SADGA's contribution. B accepted a parameter count; A received no experiment.
