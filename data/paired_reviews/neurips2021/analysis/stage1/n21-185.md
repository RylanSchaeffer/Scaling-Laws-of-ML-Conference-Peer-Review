**Verdict.** The committees saw the same paper and mostly the same facts, and weighted them differently. Reviewers in both found the statistic close to existing work, and they are right. Eq. (6) is a scaled squared MMD between two segments, so the statistic is Vogt and Dette's (2015) statistic over an RKHS unit ball. A's panel was lukewarm (6, 6, 5, 6) and had no champion. Its two most specific reviewers kept their objections after the rebuttal. The AC rejected because the needed comparisons and experiments existed only as promises. B's panel was warmer (8, 7, 7, 7, 5). Its confidence-5 champion misread two checkable points, and its only detractor softened to "lean to accept". Its AC gave a spotlight that names no weakness. No reviewer found an error in a theorem or a result; the errors found are typos. A rejected a borderline paper on undelivered promises; B over-rewarded it.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_pviK wrote the most specific review. It found that one untuned kernel served both the method and KCpA (lines 274-275) and that no type I error is reported. Both points are correct. pviK raised 5 to 6 after the rebuttal but still called tuning "critical" and type I error "very important". Reviewer_Rm3m (5, the most confident at 4) called the statistic an adaptation of MMD and noted that no baseline runs on the real data. Both points are correct, and Rm3m held 5. Reviewer_PzmP (confidence 2) and Reviewer_MgSk gave 6s on minor points, such as the typos at lines 256 and 111. MgSk never posted again. The AC's two demands come from pviK and Rm3m: put the MMD and KCpA comparison in the paper, and test kernels and bandwidths fairly. The AC wrote that this comparison "should not happen in the authors' response".

**Committee B (spotlight).** Reviewer_W1jH (8, confidence 5, 3 hours) credits "a novel gradual anomaly detection and localization problem". The paper itself cites earlier gradual-change tests and Vogt et al. (lines 25-40). W1jH also reports "exceptional performance on detection", but the paper says power is similar for all methods (lines 284-285). Reviewer_oZvV (7, confidence 2) calls the related work "rather exhaustive", yet the paper cites no MMD work. Reviewer_ZPrG and Reviewer_5aeA correctly saw the Vogt special case and the MMD link, and still gave 7s. Reviewer_hFCk (5, 48 hours) made the sharpest checkable point. The claim of "any data type and any generating process" (lines 82-83) conflicts with the independence assumption (line 45), and both real datasets are serially dependent. After the rebuttal hFCk wrote "I now lean to accept" but kept the 5. The meta-review restates the abstract and praises "great scholarly work". It names no weakness.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A tested the experiments: untuned kernels, the KCpA comparison, type I error, baselines on real data. These were real gaps, but not fatal. KCpA's Table 1 errors equal Q's in 9 of 10 columns. On the linear ramps they sit near 1/6, the bias of an argmax estimator that lands at the ramp midpoint, as the authors argued. In the camera-ready's tuned-bandwidth table, ρ̌ still beats KCpA in 9 of 10 columns. Only B raised independence against serially dependent data, the overclaimed generality and the 20 replications. These points are correct, but B's reviewers did not count them against acceptance. Only B heard the rebuttal claim that Vogt's statistic is a special case of the new one. Its example kernel is not positive semidefinite, and no reviewer challenged it.
