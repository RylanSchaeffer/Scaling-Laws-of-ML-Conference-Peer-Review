**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Six reviewers called the method new. Five called it technically sound. No reviewer found an error. The method computes score-function gradients through the execution trace of recursive exponential-min algorithms. Both committees found the same weakness: the paper is hard to follow. Their clarity complaints match almost item by item, and the submitted paper confirms them. The main text never expands "SST", the only external baseline. "Lemma 2" at l.152 should be Lemma 1. The general statement of "stochastic invariant" comes only at l.131-132. The subroutines of Algorithm 2 get one sentence each, by role only. Both ACs judged these faults fixable for the camera-ready. The results are competitive, not dominant. Against SST, T-REINFORCE+ wins clearly on ListOps (accuracy 87.3 against 78.4). It has the lower L2X MSE in 5 of 6 settings, by 0.07 to 0.18. It recovers less structure on Graph Layout (edge precision 70 against 87). No rating is above 7, so nobody argued for more than a poster. The small gap in means (6.25 against 6.5) comes from Reviewer_npTm's 5 in A. npTm gave the 5 for clarity alone, called the work "technically sound", and did not answer the rebuttal. Neither committee resolved one rebuttal result. The gradient standard deviation of T-REINFORCE+ is 10 to 42 times that of SST. The authors called the two "not directly comparable", and Reviewer_eF5a's objection got no direct answer.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 7, 5, 7). Reviewer_ahTk could not judge significance because SST was undefined. ahTk rose to 6 after the authors explained when the score function of X is intractable. Reviewer_NYB7 (confidence 2) found SST numbers worse than in Paulus et al. (Table 2: 2.93). NYB7 accepted the explanation that the paper averages over seeds. Reviewer_SiHy called the paper "clearly presented" and kept 7. Reviewer_npTm stayed at 5 and did not reply. The AC gave two reasons of its own: the invariant has independent interest, and it permits lower-variance estimates. The AC asked for minor revisions.

Committee B (6, 7, 7, 6). Reviewer_9Dk3 spent 2 hours and took RELAX, the authors' own estimator, for the baseline. 9Dk3 also said that SST recovers structure better. That holds for Graph Layout, but Table 4 shows the reverse. Reviewer_ZnaB rose to 6 after new train-set tables and gradient statistics. ZnaB's claim that T is defined nowhere is wrong; l.119-121 define it, informally. Reviewer_eF5a rose to 7 when the authors confirmed the scope: models whose score function of X is intractable. Reviewer_Csyp kept 7. The AC relied on the unanimous support and listed many clarity fixes.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these issues:

- SST results worse than the published ones, and speed per iteration (NYB7);
- why Prim's algorithm fails, and novelty over [31] (NYB7, npTm);
- the unsupported "more stable" claim at l.321-322 (SiHy).

Only B raised these issues:

- a single external baseline (Csyp, AC);
- train-set performance and gradient variance during training (ZnaB);
- weaker structure recovery despite a better objective (9Dk3);
- the ungrammatical name and title (ZnaB, AC).

The paper confirms the l.321-322 gap and the single baseline. None of these issues changed a decision.
