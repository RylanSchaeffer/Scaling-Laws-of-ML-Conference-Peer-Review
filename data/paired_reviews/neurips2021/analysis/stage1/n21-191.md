**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. No reviewer found a real error in the theory or the algorithm. Both saw an overclaimed "common misunderstanding" paragraph, four Atari games compared with HRA only, and loose notation. Both got the same core rebuttal: a constraint-maze experiment against marginal-only baselines, six games with multiple seeds, and promised rewrites. In A, three reviewers replied and raised their scores, and the area chair (AC) accepted with camera-ready requests. In B, Reviewer_X5DH (3) and Reviewer_A2nn (5) never replied. Reviewer_TpSB (confidence 5) liked the idea but asked for another review. Reviewer_fJbd cut its score to 6 after the panel discussion. B's AC adopted that standard. The split is a different bar for how much change a camera-ready may carry. A also knew the worst empirical fact, which B never saw: MD3QN does not beat MMDQN on Atari, and the authors conceded it. Part of B's case is wrong: one of TpSB's three reasons for re-review, and A2nn's main complaint.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 6, 6. Reviewer_oRgH noted from MMDQN's figures that MD3QN does not beat it on Gopher, MsPacman and Pong. It also noted that Figure 3 shows single runs and that Algorithm 1 selects actions as RD2 does. Reviewer_ZZ5A and Reviewer_SgBk argued that the Atari runs cannot show a benefit of the joint distribution. Action selection uses only the expected total return (Algorithm 1, line 1), and the Atari sources are positively correlated or independent (lines 304-306). The constraint-maze reply answered this. ZZ5A found it "indeed convincing" and raised to 7. SgBk raised to 6 but asked for the MMDQN baseline. oRgH raised to 6. Reviewer_Soeq spent 0.8 hours, doubted that the experiments "can support the claim", rated 6 and never replied. The AC read the paper, added Freirich et al. (Bellman GAN) as missing related work and asked for more Atari games.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 3, 5. X5DH is right on its main point. The paper claims to correct a misunderstanding three times (lines 53-56, 110-114, 160-161), the first time without a citation. Its own C51 example (lines 163-166) shows that prior work already used different metrics for proof and loss. The authors conceded, and the camera-ready deletes every such sentence. But X5DH calls Theorem 2 an unbiasedness result; it is a contraction (eq. 17). It calls "exclusive" undefined; lines 270-271 define it. TpSB's metric critique is correct. Its unbiasedness critique is wrong. Lines 197-200 drop the target-only term, and the cross term is linear in the target, so one transition gives an unbiased gradient. The authors said so, and the camera-ready keeps the claim. A2nn posted two weeks after the others and says that Algorithm 1 returns the loss, but line 6 returns its gradient. fJbd wrote generic praise at confidence 2, then followed the panel. The AC's "another round of reviews" repeats TpSB.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised MMDQN's better Atari scores, RD2 as the matching baseline, single-run curves, a marginal-only baseline and scaling to many reward sources. These are the paper's real empirical weaknesses, and A accepted anyway. Only B raised MMD gradient unbiasedness (wrong), Algorithm 1 returning the loss (wrong), MARL mixing baselines, the DRDRL and Singh et al. references, missing particle counts and sweeps, and the qualitative Figure 2. Both raised the misunderstanding paragraph, the unexplained choice of four games, notation, the kernel bandwidths, limited novelty and Freirich et al. A read the misunderstanding paragraph as unclear; B read it as an overclaim. The decisive issues were shared. Only their weight differed.
