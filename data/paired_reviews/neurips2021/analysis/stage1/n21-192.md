## Verdict

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. After the rebuttal, every reviewer scored 6 or more (A: 7, 6, 8, 6; B: 6, 7, 9, 7), and no reviewer found an error. Both weighed the same trade-off. Against the paper: a direct port of NLP sparse MoE to ViT, proprietary data and code, and very large compute. For it: the first sparse vision model at this scale, 90.24% on ImageNet against 90.20% for MPL with less TPU time, and Batch Prioritized Routing. In each committee, one reviewer raised a below-threshold score to 6 after the rebuttal. B's mean is higher (7.25 against 6.75): B has a 9 and two 7s where A has an 8, a 7 and a 6. B's area chair (AC) did not use Reviewer_Zhow's 9 to argue for more than a poster. The committees were not fully independent. Reviewer_wGNF (A) and Reviewer_pW2k (B) share two near-verbatim paragraphs on ImageNet label errors and leakage into JFT-3B (character similarity 0.95 and 0.97; B's text was posted first). The main text has no dense model trained on JFT-3B with the same changes, so the 15B result does not isolate sparsity. No reviewer asked for one.

## Committees

Committee A (7, 6, 8, 6; all confidence 4). Reviewer_QFfS (7) wrote the most careful review in either committee. It asks about training details, reports appendix typos that the authors conceded, and correctly notes that line 131 calls 32 experts a "sweet spot" with no data. Reviewer_kbeB raised the score to 6 after the rebuttal. kbeB's points were novelty, random routers tested only after training (correct, lines 267-268), and parameter efficiency from appendix numbers I could not check. Reviewer_5sB5 (6, 3 hours) held 6 on novelty and called the performance "comparable"; Table 2 agrees. Reviewer_wGNF (8) listed three short pros and four cons that apply to any JFT-pretrained model. The AC accepted and named proprietary data and code as a limit on impact, as checklist 3(a) states.

Committee B (6, 7, 9, 7). Reviewer_Zhow (9, confidence 5) is a generous outlier. The praise is generic ("Strong as I can tell"), but the review makes two correct, specific points. Lines 86-88 present the softmax-before-top-k order as the difference from [38] and do not cite GShard or Switch, which already use it. In Table 2, V-MoE-H/14 Every-2 ties ViT-H/14 on fine-tuning (88.08). Reviewer_fg9p started below threshold on questions that the paper answers: lines 84-85 define x, and the softmax acts on router logits, not on expert outputs. The rebuttal pointed back to the text, and fg9p moved to 6. Reviewer_mFJK (7, confidence 3) wrote four short generic points and asked nothing. Reviewer_pW2k (7) raised only data concerns. The AC copied two of pW2k's sentences into the meta-review and accepted.

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A: code not released (QFfS, 5sB5, AC); parameter efficiency against dense ViT (kbeB); random routing during training, and MoE for CNNs (kbeB); what "few-shot" means after JFT pre-training (wGNF); loss, optimiser and schedule details (QFfS).

Only B: notation and presentation of routing (fg9p); credit to GShard and Switch (Zhow); latency at small batch sizes, and saturation at H/14 (Zhow); consent and bias in JFT, from two ethics reviewers that A did not have.

Both raised limited novelty, proprietary JFT data, the fixed 32 experts, cost, and label errors with leakage. The last point reached both committees through one text. The author replies on the expert count differed. A was pointed to ImageNet-21k ablations in Appendix E.6; B was told that only anecdotes exist. Neither committee treated any issue as disqualifying.
