**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and B rejected mainly because A's reviewers engaged with the rebuttal and B's did not. Both saw the same contribution: a learned, permutation-equivariant permuter that lets a VAE train a node-order-invariant graph vector. Both raised the same weak points: an overstated "first" claim, loose notation in Eq. 5, thin downstream baselines and O(n^3) cost. In A, Reviewer_bsmb (confidence 5) leaned to reject on three readings that the paper contradicts. It conceded each and rose to 6. Reviewer_gusa rose from 6 to 8 after three rounds on why training does not depend on input node order. In B, no reviewer posted after the rebuttal and no review changed. B's area chair (AC) set aside Reviewer_394F's 4 as a misunderstanding, which left 6, 5 and 6. The AC then rejected on its own concern that the experiments do not show "whether/when" the method beats GAE and Graphite. Table 1 already shows held-out NLL seven to eleven times lower than both. The gap is real only in the downstream tasks. The same new GAE classification result (0.65 against 0.83) satisfied bsmb in A and was "not comprehensive" for B's AC.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 8, 6, 6, 8. bsmb first read the decoder as using the encoder's messages. Lines 165-168 and 181-183 initialise it from z and position embeddings only. It then said output size must equal input size, but lines 187-190 describe a node-count MLP. It said regular graphs would fail, but Figure 2 includes them. It had admitted "I do not carefully check the experiments". It stayed borderline: Sinkhorn-style matching is standard, and its expressive-power question stayed open. It also found real prior work, Yang et al. (NeurIPS 2019), and the authors withdrew the "first" claim. gusa's last objection, that the permuter learns the input order, was wrong (lines 203-207). It raised its score once shown. Reviewer_oyaX (12 hours) kept 6 and called the interpolation demo new. Reviewer_2ehV's 8 adds little: four bullets of praise, a question that lines 165-168 answer, and no discussion. The AC repeated the consensus.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 4, 5, 6; no review was edited. 394F (confidence 5) feared that P could become the identity. The decoder sees only z and the permuter is equivariant, so an identity P cannot fit arbitrary orderings. The AC agreed. 394F also misquoted the "first" claim and cited a graph-edit-distance algorithm and two set models against it. Its point that Table 3 compares only with ECFP is correct. Reviewer_1wGW called the reconstruction results "particularly impressive". It misread m_0,0 as a scalar; line 132 makes it a vector. It correctly noted that Figure 3 samples only near encoded molecules. Reviewer_V8HM (confidence 2) wanted GAE and Graphite in the classification task, which Table 2 lacks. Its call for AIC or BIC misses that Table 1 reports held-out NLL. Reviewer_d88Y (confidence 2) asked four questions and gave no argument for its 6. The authors answered all four reviewers on 2021-08-09. Nobody replied.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised Yang et al. 2019, sequential-decoder VAEs, Li et al.'s marginalisation over node orders, Weisfeiler-Lehman power on regular graphs, Sinkhorn matching and OGB benchmarks. Only B raised an identity P, GNN baselines for molecular properties, samples from the prior, a trivial Proposition 1, posterior collapse, running times and AIC/BIC. Both raised the "first" claim, Eq. 5's treatment of P, the isomorphism argument, pooled-GAE baselines and cost. The content overlapped. The process did not: A's rebuttal reached reviewers who replied, and B's reached nobody. B's AC also weighted thin baselines more heavily.
