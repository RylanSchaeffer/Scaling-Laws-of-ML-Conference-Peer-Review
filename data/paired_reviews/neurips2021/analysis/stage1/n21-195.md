**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Both saw a novel, simple link between how fast SGD moves the first-layer bias and the network's local Lipschitz constant. Both also saw its limits: toy experiments, dense notation, and a result that rests on one parameter. A rated it 6, 7, 7, 7, 6 (mean 6.6); B rated it 8, 8, 5, 6 (mean 6.75). B's two 8s came from confidence-3 reviewers, one a self-declared non-expert (Reviewer_KizT). B's 5 came from its most confident reviewer, Reviewer_BRU5. BRU5 held that the result is fragile, because the bias gradient only mirrors the input gradient. B's AC kept that point as a caveat and accepted on the majority. In A, the rebuttal conceded theorem fixes and cleared a misreading, and two reviewers rose to 7. In B, the rebuttal moved no one. So the committees weighed the same facts alike. A's reviewers mostly checked statements; B's mostly questioned the framing. Neither saw a gap next to the σ_n debate. Since ∇ₓf = W₁ᵀ ∂f/∂b₁, the upper bound of Theorem 1 needs a first layer no wider than the input. The paper never states this. Example: f(x) = ReLU(x+1) + ReLU(1−x) is flat on (−1, 1), yet its bias gradient is (1, 1). Task 1 fixes W₁ to the identity (l.276), where the two gradients coincide. The camera-ready keeps the bound. Corollary 3 inherits the gap; Corollaries 1–2 and Theorems 2–3 use only the valid direction.

**Committees**

Committee A. Reviewer_i77r wrote the most careful review. Like Reviewer_AHNy, it found that Theorem 1 omits σ_n(W₁) > 0. It also found that Corollary 1 does not follow from Definition 1, which bounds only the sampled point's step. The authors conceded both; i77r rose to 7. Reviewer_j63f misread Assumption 1 as making the network linear, accepted the correction, and rose to 7. Reviewer_7TvM, outside its field, kept 6 for readability; its summary misstates the main result. Reviewer_juSM ("a light review") lists only problems, yet rates 6. Its novelty charge ignores l.287, which credits Arpit et al. The AC called all five reviewers "knowledgeable" and followed the scores.

Committee B. Reviewer_8FRD valued the depth-mild generalization bound. It correctly noted that Theorem 1's |T| condition can become unattainable as σ_n shrinks. Reviewer_KizT's NTK objection fails: the bound is normalised by step size and loss derivative. Its request to call a small Lipschitz constant sufficient, not necessary, became the AC's first caveat. The paper confirms Reviewer_BRU5's critique. Corollary 1 is "immediate" from the steady-learner definition (l.177), and the authors concede that the analysis fails without a first-layer bias. Reviewer_9MLt gave 6 at confidence 2 and wrote "I'm not sure that I understand this paper".

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Beyond the two theorem fixes, only A questioned whether Assumption 1 holds in practice (j63f), the identity first layer (i77r), and the opening claim about overfitting (juSM). A's discussion also asked whether an underfitting MLP can be compared with a CNN. Only B raised these:

- the circular steady-learner definition and the failure without a first-layer bias (BRU5, KizT, 9MLt, AC);
- the |T| condition, the NTK regime and the role of the optimiser (8FRD, KizT);
- the dog-versus-airplane split and the unmentioned appendix experiments (8FRD);
- the errors at l.45, l.70 and l.207, all confirmed (BRU5).

Readability split the reviewers: A's 7TvM and juSM found it hard to read; A's j63f and B's KizT found it clear. Both committees asked whether low complexity is what drives generalization; only B's AC made it a caveat.
