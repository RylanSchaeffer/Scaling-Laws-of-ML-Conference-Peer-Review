## Verdict

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Both started at the borderline. Before the rebuttal, A's scores were about 5, 5 or less, 7 and 4 or less; B's were 5, 6, 5, 5. Both read the same paper: a simple first-order method, clearly written, with many experiments. It gains 2 to 4 points over MAML, under 1 point over BOIL, and nothing over MT-net or Meta-SGD in 1-shot. The submission's few-shot results used only a 4-layer ConvNet. Both committees got a large rebuttal with new experiments, and two or three reviewers in each raised their scores. No reviewer found an error in the method or the main results, and none argued for more than a poster. Neither area chair (AC) departed from the post-rebuttal scores. A's higher mean (6.25 against 5.75) does not change the tier. The submitted PDF is not in the files, so the checks use the camera-ready.

## Committees

Committee A (7, 6, 7, 5). Reviewer_WL2f (confidence 5) asked for larger models, sparsity regularizers, meta-shrinkage and a BatchNorm control. WL2f went from 5 to 7 after the ResNet-12 results and a ten-item change list that WL2f requested. Reviewer_ah52 rose to 6 after the clarifications and the class-incremental Split-CIFAR-10 results. Its BTI point is correct: on Rotations, sparse-La-MAML forgets more than the baseline and MER (Table 5: −8.16 against −5.44 and −5.60). Its reading of Eq. 5 is wrong: the gate follows an accumulated mask, not each gradient product. Reviewer_C1hv (5) objected only to structure, and WL2f agreed. C1hv rose to 5 for "the significance and importance of the scientific findings" but still wanted "a significant amount of rewriting". Reviewer_giJs (7) restates the abstract and never joined the discussion, so that 7 is weak evidence. The AC echoed the reviewers and relied on the promised changes.

Committee B (6, 6, 5, 6). Reviewer_HSzX, a step-size specialist, correctly found that IDBD and SMD were missing. HSzX rose to 6 after the authors added them and an exponential step-size baseline. HSzX still said the baseline's weak result "contradicts their hypothesis". Reviewer_u5xJ rose from 5 to 6 after a post-hoc mask control and a mask-reversion analysis. Reviewer_bJTT (6) asked fair questions and did not reply. Reviewer_Lbaj (5, confidence 1) was out of area but read the tables correctly. In 1-shot, sparse-MAML (50.35) trails MT-net (51.70) and Meta-SGD (50.47), within their error bars. Lbaj also caught a wrong supplement value, which the authors conceded. The AC cited the positive post-rebuttal reviews and named MT-Net as prior work.

## Issues raised by one committee only

Only A: the experiments lack motivation (C1hv, WL2f); no regularizer or meta-shrinkage comparison (WL2f); parameter-count fairness (WL2f); transductive BatchNorm (WL2f, giJs); restrictiveness against GEM, forgetting on Rotations and frozen biases (ah52).

Only B: the older step-size work and an exponential baseline (HSzX); a small sparsity factor, post-hoc masks and mask reversion (u5xJ); sparsity against layer width (bJTT); weak margins over MT-net, Meta-SGD and BOIL (Lbaj).

Both committees asked about larger models, binary masks against learned step sizes, and continual learning beyond MNIST. The same rebuttal experiments answered both. Neither committee tested the new evidence. The rebuttal put sparse-MAML above ANIL on ResNet-12 (73.11 against 72.52). WL2f cited this result, and u5xJ called it "encouraging". Camera-ready Table 2 shows a tie (70.02 against 70.03). Table 4 has sparse-MAML below BOIL in 4 of 6 cross-domain cells, yet the text says it "consistently improves" on manual freezing. Only Lbaj, at confidence 1, raised the BOIL point.
