**Verdict.** The two committees saw the same paper and mostly the same faults. They weighted those faults differently. Reviewers in both committees noted the hand-built world model, thin novelty over constrained decoding and generate-and-rerank, a stretched System 1/System 2 framing, and missing neuro-symbolic citations. Both also noted that the human study pairs each dual-system sentence with a sentence that System 2 rejected. Committee A treated these as stated limits of a clear proof of concept. Committee B treated the hand-built world model as fatal to significance and generality. Three things widened the gap. A's spotlight rests on one thin 9. B's two lowest scores rest partly on misreadings that the rebuttal corrected, and neither reviewer changed the score. Nearly identical rebuttal text on framing and on the human study moved A's main critic up and got no reply from B's. No reviewer found a real error in a result.

**Committee A (spotlight).** Ratings 6, 9, 6, 7. Reviewer_oMsG raised most of B's objections: no originality over Roth et al. (2005), an evaluation that favours the dual system by design, and missing symbolic-learning work. The rebuttal gave reframing, arguments and promises, and oMsG raised the score to 6. Reviewer_wEz8 gave 9 at confidence 5 after two hours. That review is mostly summary with three minor points. It calls the results "impressive", does not address the sample selection, and never joins the discussion. Reviewer_Eow4 (6, confidence 5) lists strengths and asks for a narrower title. Reviewer_Tn5q (7) accepts the method "for non-complex generations with a relatively straightforward world model". That is B's main objection, judged acceptable. Tn5q also misstates the resampling budget as 20; the paper says 10. The meta-review has four sentences: consensus, clear writing, good human results. It gives no reason for a spotlight.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 4, 3, 5. No score moved. Reviewer_2tE8 liked the idea and Reviewer_jmVT valued the topic (both 5). Both found the hand-designed world model too limiting for larger domains. The rebuttal conceded this, and 2tE8 read the concession as confirmation. Reviewer_Ms3t (3) made two validity claims. The human-study claim describes the design correctly. But the paper's NLI arm uses the same selection and gets near-chance preference (0.51, 0.58). So selection alone does not make the effect, and nobody noted this. The gSCAN claim is wrong: gSCAN commands carry verbs and adverbs, and System 2 checks only the end location. Ms3t never replied. Reviewer_CnYk (4) thought the parser was trained on supervised data; it is few-shot GPT-3. CnYk said the rebuttal "answered my major concern", then kept 4 on new grounds: closed GPT-3 and novelty. The AC summarised all four reviews and named the hand-built world model as the main concern. The AC called the paper "close but just below the bar".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised loose logical terminology (wEz8), a title that suggests end-to-end differentiable reasoning (Eow4), and sensitivity to the sampling budget (Tn5q). Only B raised inference speed, which the main text does not report (jmVT), and dependence on closed GPT-3 (CnYk). Only B raised unmeasured parser errors in CLUTRR (2tE8). Only B questioned gSCAN validity and the lack of other baselines (Ms3t). Both points are wrong. The gSCAN error is above, and the paper has an NLI reranker and a prior gSCAN model as baselines. None of these issues is a correctness flaw. B's decisive concern, the hand-built world model, also appears in A, where reviewers accepted it as a stated limitation (Section 6). All checks use paper_submitted.pdf, main text only; the appendix is not in the PDF.
