**Verdict.** The committees did not mainly weigh the same facts differently. B saw the decisive facts, and A never saw them. Three B reviewers at confidence 4 or 5 know the equivariant-CNN and steerable-basis literature. They showed that the paper's novelty claims are false. The paper says no earlier CNN gives "a rigorous invariance to the continuous set of all possible rotation angles" (lines 6-8, 31-33, 293-295). Harmonic Networks (2017) and E(2)-CNNs (2019) already do this, and the paper cites neither. Its only equivariant baseline is a G-CNN with four rotations (line 196). The authors also agreed with Reviewer_oaoL that the Bessel radial part "could be replaced by other (trainable) radial functions". No A reviewer named these methods. A accepted on uniform scores, on liking for the idea, and on a low-data result that exists only in the rebuttal. The camera-ready drops the priority claim and cites both methods as prior work. It still compares only against the four-rotation G-CNN. B's rejection is the better-supported decision.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 7, 7. Reviewer_9Dgi (5 hours) read the paper closely but repeated its priority claim as a strength. 9Dgi also said the experiments compare "with the state of the art techniques". Reviewer_Vncf (1 hour, confidence 3) called the approach "rather primitive" and still gave 7. Vncf wrote that global invariance comes "simply by training on objects in all different rotations". That is wrong: B-CNN trained without rotation gets about 89% on rotated MNIST, against 53% for G-CNN (Figure 3a). Reviewer_iKDt wrote the most expert A review. iKDt flagged the coarse four-angle G-CNN and the missing Fourier-Bessel prior work, but called them minor and kept 7. Reviewer_nHDN found the results "not convincing" and doubted significance. After the rebuttal's low-data Outex result (about 15 points above G-CNN), nHDN raised to 6. The AC repeated that result and wrote that reviewers found the experiments "compelling". nHDN's final text says otherwise.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 8, 4, 5. Reviewer_oaoL (6 hours) named five continuous-rotation models that the paper omits. oaoL also found that line 184 says MNIST-rot has "only 8 different angles". Its angles are uniform on [0, 2π], so the error removed the standard benchmark. Reviewer_nxbX asked for Harmonic Network and E(2)-CNN baselines and noted small networks and unconverged baselines. Reviewer_dNyU (12 hours) showed an uneven set-up: B-CNN uses nu_max up to 15 against four G-CNN rotations, with fewer layers. The paper confirms each point (line 184, Section 5.2, Figure 3, reference list). The rebuttal offered the same low-data result, again against the four-rotation G-CNN. All three kept their scores. Reviewer_gUNY's 8 rests on a 68-word main review. It asks for "theoretical justification", though Section 4 derives the invariance. Its only reply is "Thanks for your response." The AC gave it no weight and rejected on the missing state-of-the-art comparisons.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B raised the false priority claim and the missing Harmonic Network and E(2)-CNN baselines. These decided the case. Only B noted that the Bessel radial basis is not needed for equivariance, and the authors accepted this. Only B raised the MNIST-rot error, the lack of batch normalization and residual connections, and possibly undertrained baselines. Only A asked how to keep orientation information, whether truncation assumes low-pass images, and about adversarial weakness. Both committees raised the four-rotation G-CNN, inexact invariance from grid sampling, the cost of nu_max+1 convolutions, and small datasets. A treated these as minor. B made them part of the case for rejection.
