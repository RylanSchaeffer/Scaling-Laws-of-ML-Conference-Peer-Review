**Verdict.** Committee B rejected because one expert found a checkable flaw in the evaluation, and its area chair (AC) adopted it. Reviewer_xEbL (3, confidence 5) noted that in Figure 2 AFD keeps around 50% accuracy on CIFAR-10 against L-inf PGD at eps 0.125 (32/255). The submitted paper confirms it: about 44%, while AT and TRADES fall to about 5%. Curves this flat mean the attacks were too weak against AFD, possibly through gradient obfuscation. No reviewer in A spotted the flat curves. Both committees got the same AutoAttack results, with AFD at or below TRADES on three of four datasets; A's AC still accepted on novelty. The evidence favours B. On CIFAR-10, AFD leads TRADES on PGD-inf AUC (0.56 against 0.32, Table 2) but not on AutoAttack AUC (0.24 against 0.25). The camera-ready Table 1 shows AFD falling from 59.38% under PGD to 37.33% under AutoAttack, below TRADES at 43.48%. The split comes from evaluation expertise present only in B, and from A's AC preferring novelty to a headline result that failed a strong attack.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 6, 5; no review edited. The positive reviews praise the framing and the writing. Reviewer_1Prj (6) warned "we should be cautious about the results" and asked for AutoAttack, then never replied to the result. Reviewer_inuZ (7, confidence 5) called AFD "a new defense with SOTA performance", which holds only under the paper's own attacks. Its Table 1 point is correct: the caption promises std, but the table shows single runs. Reviewer_R6D1 (6) asked for more architectures and cost data and never replied. Only Reviewer_nZT8 (5) engaged. After the AutoAttack numbers he wrote that AFD "does not show significant advantages" over TRADES in robustness or cost, and that the claim at lines 62-63 is inaccurate. Both points hold. The AC accepted a paper "very much on the border" as "technically sound" and novel. Its "marginal improvements over AT and TRADES" is generous: under AutoAttack AFD trailed TRADES everywhere except MNIST.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 4, 3, 6; no review edited. xEbL's review is short, but its central claim is specific and correct. After the rebuttal xEbL held firm: the curves "do not seem to converge to 0%", and MNIST robustness at eps 0.5 "can't be non-trivial". The authors conceded that their attacks had limited iterations, and that AutoAttack drives AFD to 0% near eps 0.4 on MNIST. Reviewer_WEvD (5) asked for AutoAttack and cited feature scattering, a similar defense that AutoAttack exposed. Reviewer_h6S6 (4, confidence 3) is the weakest review. It leans on the ICLR 2021 reviews, and its natural-accuracy objection fails against Table 1, where AFD matches or beats AT and TRADES on most datasets. Reviewer_SkVe (6) found all questions answered, but cited the ResNet18-only scope and would "not object to a decision of rejection". So B had no champion. The AC named xEbL and the "too good" results; "don't add up in most cases" overstates xEbL's narrower point.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B raised the decisive issue: implausible robustness at large eps as a sign of weak attacks. Only B also raised adaptive attacks through the discriminator, the feature-scattering precedent, transfer-only black-box tests, the motivation relative to the ICLR version, and gains on the seen attack that undercut the generalization claim. Only A raised the weak AFD-DCGAN variant, ImageNet scale, missing error bars and feature-sensitivity evidence. Both raised the missing AutoAttack evaluation, training cost, the single architecture and the upper-bound argument against adversarial training. nZT8 in A read the shared AutoAttack result as B did; A's AC discounted it.
