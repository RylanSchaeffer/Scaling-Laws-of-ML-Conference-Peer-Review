**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same paper and named nearly the same weaknesses. They scored them differently. The paper extends the low-rank-SVD results of [16, 17] to nonsmooth problems through a saddle-point reformulation. Its guarantee is local. Its experiments show that rank-r SVDs reproduce the full-SVD iterates, but they give no baseline, timing or convergence curve. In A, two reviewers raised these points and still gave 6, and Reviewer_M6vn gave an uncritical 9. A's area chair (AC) echoed the 9. In B, three reviewers gave 5 for the same points, and the AC followed them. The split is calibration at the borderline, 6 against 5 for the same criticisms, plus one exuberant champion in A.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 9. Reviewer_Ahfq found no baselines, no running time and unclear assumptions. It concluded that "the work is not ready for NeurIPS 2021", yet its rating is 6. It edited the review on 2021-08-17, after the rebuttal, with no note, so the 6 was most likely set then. Its supposition that Assumptions 1 and 3 are equivalent is wrong: lines 79-83 make Assumption 1 imply Assumption 3 only under Assumption 2. Reviewer_3rGU spent two hours, listed mostly wording fixes, and called the work incremental with no reason. Reviewer_M6vn's 9 rests on stock praise and names no weakness. It says the "proofs seems to be correct", but its own confidence label says the math was not carefully checked. It calls the paper "very clearly written", but "strict complementarity" appears at line 57, long before Definition 1 at line 129. No reviewer posted in the discussion. The meta-review names no weakness. Its "important hole in the literature" restates M6vn's "clear gap".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 5, 5. Reviewer_xRc5 wrote B's most detailed review. Its first con is wrong: the main result does not "only apply" to the form in Assumption 2. Remark 1 (lines 222-225) says the convergence results need only Assumption 3. Its other cons hold. The paper itself says that Lemmas 3 and 4 follow from [16] and [17] (lines 146-148, 160-162), and it calls the rate "well known" (line 246). Tables 1-3 show no convergence rate, and the authors told Reviewer_zvmw "We did not check the convergence rates at all". Remark 3 concedes the warm start and offers only empirical schemes. After the rebuttal xRc5 kept 5, because the algorithm lacks "strong guarantees of success". Reviewer_u8Jo (confidence 2) and Reviewer_MxNL (1.5 hours) wrote thin reviews and never replied. u8Jo said faster algorithms exist but named none. zvmw gave 6 at confidence 5 after two hours, with three questions. Its claim that the paper states no limitation is wrong (Remark 3). The AC followed the 5s. Its "quite restrictive" partly rests on xRc5's misreading, which the rebuttal had corrected.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A noted that lines 271-272 say "it does not matter whether the conditions of Theorem 1 hold". Only A noted that the experiments set the SVD rank to the known ground-truth rank. Only B asked how to initialize the dual variable, why extragradient, and whether faster methods exist. Only B's xRc5 made the missing global guarantee a reason to reject. Both committees raised the missing baselines and timing, the weak case for the eigengap assumption, the warm start, incrementality over [16, 17], and confusion about which assumptions Theorem 1 needs. The decisive facts were shared. Only their weight differed.
