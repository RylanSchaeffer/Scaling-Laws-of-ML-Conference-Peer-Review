**Verdict.** The committees agreed on the facts and weighted them differently. Both found the PAC versus EQ separation theorem novel and non-trivial. Both area chairs (ACs) and five of six reviewers doubted the adversarial-robustness material, about half the main text (lines 41-72 and 207-365). A's AC called that link "tenuous at best", accepted on the theorem, and told the authors to "remove all unnecessary parts". B's AC rejected the paper "in its current form". A's scores gave its AC room. Reviewer_rx52's 9 took 2 hours, names no weakness, and praises the framing that every other reviewer doubted. That is enthusiasm, not evidence. Reviewer_p28f's 6 contradicts his own text: "as is, I lean towards rejecting the paper". He offered a higher score if those sections became "more precise and less tangential". The authors promised on 2021-08-23 to "focus more on the separation result", and he edited his review that day. In B, the authors pasted one defence of the adversarial model into all three replies and offered no cut. No B score moved. B's specific criticisms hold on the submission. Corollary 1 bounds only the standard risk, so the abstract and lines 371-372 claim a robustness that the paper does not prove. Montasser, Hanneke and Srebro (2021) is missing. No fatal flaw separates the committees. A judged the paper the authors promised; B judged the paper it read.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 9, 6; mean 7.0. p28f (5 hours) wrote the most substantive review. He liked the theorem but found the attack model unrealistic: attacks must follow D on the error set and "need not even depend on the target image". The note to Definition 2 (lines 310-312) confirms this. Reviewer_Nq1S (3 hours) asked two questions, admitted "I'm not familiar with the Equivalence-Query-learning model", and did not reply to the answers. rx52's 9 adds no analysis. The AC gave an own reading: the gap is "not completely surprising", but the argument "requires significant insight". The AC also cites an earlier review process that doubted the same link. No such author statement is in either public record.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 5; mean 5.33. All three reviews were edited after the rebuttal to keep the score. Reviewer_SBEU (8 hours, confidence 2) knew the literature best. SBEU related the result to active learning and called it "likely of broad interest", but found the robustness part "hand-wavy and very confusing". Reviewer_X9Aw judged Section 5 by robust-learning-theory standards: no goal is defined, and Corollary 1 gives "at best" low standard error. Both points are correct. Reviewer_mNRE found the speed-up "not very surprising" and the adversary unclear. The AC wrote that "all the reviewers recommended rejecting", but SBEU's posted rating is still 6. That unanimity comes from the non-public discussion that mNRE mentions.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B noted the missing lower bounds and the loose log^9 factor (SBEU); Section 6 states both as open. Only B showed that Corollary 1 is not a robustness guarantee and that lines 371-372 overclaim (X9Aw). Only B named the missing Montasser et al. work. Only A asked for a clearer proof overview and less tangential text in Sections 3.3 and 5 (p28f). Only A had a reviewer who called the framing "highly relevant to modern problems in practical machine learning" (rx52). Both committees raised the same core doubts: attacks assumed to follow D on the error set, an adversary not tied to its input, and an unclear link to robust PAC learning.
