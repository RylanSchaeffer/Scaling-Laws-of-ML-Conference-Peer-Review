**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently. Reviewers on both sides named the same weaknesses. The method applies an existing semi-CRF to one task. Clear gains appear only on the two note-with-offset metrics, about 4 points F1 over Kong et al. (2020). The speed claim had no baseline, and the CRF section is hard to follow. Committee A's reviewers are transcription specialists. They valued the event-level reformulation and the offset gains, and gave 7 regardless. Committee B judged the paper as a NeurIPS machine-learning contribution and probed the semi-CRF design. Three of four B reviewers stayed below threshold. The rebuttal widened the gap. In A, bug-fixed results, a runtime comparison (95 s against 353 s) and paired t-tests moved Reviewer_pJNf from 5 to 7. In B, only Reviewer_1vpu replied, and stopped at 5 because the promised rewrite could not be seen. B's area chair (AC) called the rebuttal "good" but rejected on the size of the revision. Neither committee found an error in a key result.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 7; mean 7.0. All three reviews are specific, and all three reviewers replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_7qii, ten years in piano transcription, argued "strongly" for acceptance but doubted the algorithms' novelty "in terms of the NeurIPS standard". Reviewer_jUoX (confidence 5) called the work "an incremental combination of existing techniques" of limited reach. jUoX still gave 7 while calling clarity "clearly the area for the most improvement". pJNf's objections (no statistics, speed not shown) were correct on the submission, and the rebuttal answered both. The AC accepted on the scores and the updated results. Its claim that reviewers "had no reservation of its impact" overstates jUoX and pJNf.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 5, 6, 5; mean 5.0. Reviewer_LiUp (2 hours, confidence 3, 150 words) is the weakest review. Its claim that the results do not significantly improve the state of the art is wrong for the offset metrics. LiUp never replied, although the rebuttal answered both of its points. Reviewer_ygBT (confidence 5) read the model as "88 + 3 independent semi-CRF models with a shared feature encoder" and wanted a real extension of the semi-CRF. ygBT also called the numbers "extremely close", which misses the 4-point offset gains. Reviewer_nVTb gave 6 and objected only to scope. Reviewer_1vpu wrote the most careful review, and its checkable points hold. Theta is undefined, the autodiff claim (lines 137-139) had no evidence, and the pedal F1s trail Kong et al. 1vpu also misread the score models as separate per event type. The AC leaned on 1vpu's remaining questions (temporal correlation, slow autodiff). Yet 1vpu had called the answer on correlations "reasonable".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked why baseline figures differ from the published ones, and asked for MAESTRO v3 results (jUoX). The recomputed Kong et al. baseline is higher than its published figure, so the change favours the baseline. Only A received the paired t-tests. Only B raised the missing temporal correlations, the unexplained autodiff speedup, the absence of a maximum duration, pytorch-struct as an alternative, the missing speech work, the pedal results and the undefined theta. The authors answered each with numbers or reasons. Apart from the clarity rewrite, the revision that B's AC called "significant" was mostly material already in the rebuttal. The camera-ready adds the autodiff benchmark, the runtime comparison and a speech citation.
