**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently, and the reviewer draw set the weights. Both saw a simple, novel method, accuracy at or slightly above the listed baselines, a costly two-phase procedure, missing baselines and typos. Committee A drew two confident detractors. Reviewer_KcPD found no "tangible benefit" over existing quantizers, and Reviewer_J3PT wanted a real assessment of the overhead. Committee B drew four reviewers at 6 or 7, who treated the same gaps as fixable. The paper supports A's central doubt. The submitted abstract says CBP beats the state of the art "For all cases". Its own Table 1 puts LQ-Nets ahead on AlexNet ternary (60.5% against 58.8%), and two of four ResNet-18 margins are 0.1 points. Quantization Networks (QN), which B's Reviewer_WFRz asked for, beats the submitted CBP accuracy in 5 of 8 ResNet cells of the camera-ready table, whose abstract now says "For most cases". Both forums got the same cost answer: about 2% more FLOPs per iteration than backprop for ResNet, but 101 to 201 CBP epochs after full pre-training. That confirms KcPD's total-cost point. B's area chair (AC) called it adequate; J3PT did not, and KcPD never replied. No fatal flaw separates the committees.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 4, 4, 6; mean 4.67; no rating moved. The two 4s took 1.5 and 1-2 hours, the least of seven reviewers. Their central points hold, but each review has a miss. KcPD says the post-training setup appears only at line 225 and that limitations are absent; lines 59-61 and 275-283 state both. KcPD wrote "I am willing to adjust my ratings" but never answered the rebuttal, so the 4 stood by default. J3PT says Section 3.1 does not explain why the multiplier is a variable, but lines 101-151 give the textbook derivation. His other points are correct: WaveQ is not cited, and line 180 reads "Section ??". Reviewer_kc15's 6 rests on two one-line pros, including "SOTA results" taken at face value. The AC followed the majority and added a doubt about the baselines, which the QN row later confirmed. The AC also judged the writing "pretty rough", "based on my own quick scan"; KcPD had called it "very well-written".

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 6, 6; mean 6.25. Reviewer_BfHB moved from 5 to 6 after a new GoogLeNet run and the cost estimate, but still asked why CBP beats other methods. Reviewer_nXqG's 7 (confidence 2) names no weakness and praises experiments "on a variety of datasets"; the paper uses ImageNet only. That review is not evidence of quality. WFRz and Reviewer_ECoB wrote specific, positive reviews that flagged cost and missing baselines; neither replied to the rebuttal. BfHB and WFRz call the method theoretically sound, but Theorem 3.1 states a joint minimisation over the multiplier, while the algorithm does gradient ascent on it (line 185). B's AC never mentions the missing baselines.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked whether CBP has any practical advantage over existing quantizers (KcPD). Only A asked why the multiplier is learned and not a fixed penalty weight (J3PT). Since Eq. 8 is non-negative, the multiplier only grows, so the question has a basis. Only A counted the writing against the paper. Only B asked for more architectures; the resulting GoogLeNet table never reached A. Only B asked how many epochs other methods use. The answer gave 151 CBP epochs on ResNet-18 after pre-training, against 8 to 120 for INQ, TWN and LQ-Nets. Nobody in B followed up. Only B raised hyperparameter cost, separate optimisers, non-differentiable points and other domains (ECoB, nXqG).
