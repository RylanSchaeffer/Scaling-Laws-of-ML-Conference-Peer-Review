**Verdict.** Committee A rejected because two of its reviewers knew the closest prior work, and its area chair (AC) would not judge the rebuttal's comparison without a second review. Committee B never saw that work. Reviewer_wktj and Reviewer_LkFg named Daniely (NeurIPS 2020) and Bubeck et al. (2020). Both claim memorization at width about n/d with training guarantees. The submission cites neither (references [1] to [37]). The rebuttal argued that Daniely's Õ(n/d) hides super-polynomial factors. A's ratings stayed at 6, 6, 6, 6, 5, and the AC still rejected: such claims "must go through the scrutiny of peer-review", and NeurIPS takes no revisions. The caution was sound. By the rebuttal's own account, Daniely fixes n = d^c, so for constant c the hidden factor is polylogarithmic; the camera-ready concedes this. Daniely also analyses SGD, while this paper proves no convergence, so the paper is not better on every axis. None of B's four reviewers mentioned Daniely or Bubeck. B's AC accepted on 6, 8, 7, 6 and called the regime "far from existing work". The decisive fact reached only A; this is not the same facts weighted differently. The program chairs made acceptance conditional on a re-check of the Daniely claims.
