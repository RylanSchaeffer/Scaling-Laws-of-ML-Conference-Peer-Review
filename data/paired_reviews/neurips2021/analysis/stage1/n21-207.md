**Verdict.** The committees saw the same paper and weighed one weakness differently. Both found it clear but technically incremental, and the paper confirms this. Algorithm 1 is LSVI-UCB with batch updates; lines 221-222 call the batches "the main difference". Algorithm 2 uses the determinant rule of rarely switching OFUL (lines 253-256). Lines 239-241 call the lower-bound result "similar to its counterpart for batched linear bandits". The main text names no new proof technique. All four A reviewers and three of four B reviewers named limited novelty. In A, this gave two 5s; in B, four 6s. A's area chair (AC) broke a 5.5 tie on novelty. B's AC copied a review summary and cited "consensus". No reviewer on either side found an error. The split is a one-point calibration difference on two reviewers, plus the AC's framing.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 6, 5. Reviewer_ZiZV (4 hours, confidence 4) wrote the most careful review. ZiZV briefly checked the proofs, found the high-level ideas correct, and rated 5 for novelty. ZiZV traced the batch algorithm to Jin et al. 2020 and to the uniform grids of SBUCB (Han et al. 2020), and the switch rule to Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011. ZiZV also wanted a rare-switch lower bound and results under misspecification. This is a principled 5, and the paper supports its premises. Reviewer_w5L3 (2 hours, confidence 2) rated 5 for novelty and for the √(dH) gap between Theorems 4.1 and 4.2. The gap is real but comes from LSVI-UCB; the paper claims tightness only in B. Reviewer_BH6Q's 6 is seven short, generic sentences at confidence 2, and it calls the result "not that surprising". Reviewer_LtAC (12 hours) was the only real supporter: "The method is new in the function approximation setting". The authors answered ZiZV with misspecification tables and a novelty argument. No A reviewer replied. The AC restated ZiZV: the paper "seems to combine known techniques", and the results are "what one would expect".

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 6. Reviewer_FRs1 wrote that Algorithm 2 "is the same as" rarely switching OFUL except for the setting. Reviewer_Xhcq called the novelty "relatively limited", and Reviewer_3Uwm found the results "a bit incremental". All three still rated 6 and kept 6 after the rebuttal. They valued clean algorithms and a complete study; FRs1 added that it covers the concurrent Gao et al. 2021. Reviewer_ZCnH (1.5 hours) raised the score to 6 on 2021-09-01, after an author follow-up asked to "reconsider the rating". ZCnH's question had the direction wrong: the bounds match for T = Ω(d³H³), not O(d³H³). FRs1's checkable point holds. In Fig. 1(a), the B = 50 curve stays above the fully adaptive curve until about episode 1,500, so line 312 overstates the result. The authors conceded this. The meta-review is Xhcq's summary, word for word, plus "There is a consensus that the paper should be accepted". It never mentions novelty.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for a rare-switch lower bound and for robustness to misspecification (ZiZV). Only A noted the √(dH) gap in the leading term (w5L3) and the poor S and A dependence in the tabular case (LtAC). Only B flagged the Fig. 1(a) overstatement and the missing baseline (FRs1), the "B = T" typo and the undefined [·]⁺ (Xhcq), and the B ≥ (d−1)H/2 condition (ZCnH). None of these drove either decision. Both committees shared the novelty concern and the request for a comparison with Gao et al. 2021. B's reviewers put the complete study above the novelty concern; A's ZiZV and A's AC did not.
