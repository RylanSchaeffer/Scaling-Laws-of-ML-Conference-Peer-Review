**Verdict.** Two things split the committees: the reviewer draw and the area chairs' (ACs') weighting of one shared doubt. A's ratings (6, 7, 7; mean 6.67) made acceptance routine. B's ratings (4, 6, 6; mean 5.33) left the decision to the AC. Both ACs doubted how much the analysis adds to earlier fixed-confidence work. A's AC asked the authors to lay out "what additional complexity in analysis stems from misspecification" in the final version. B's AC rejected for "not much technical depth beyond (carefully) combining techniques". No public B review says this. The authors told Reviewer_1wnn that Equation 2 is the Garivier-Kaufmann bound with a new set of alternatives, which supports the AC for the lower bound only. Nobody in B discussed the paper's claimed key analysis device, the orthogonal parametrization (lines 206-214). 1wnn, the most confident reviewer in either committee, called it a "probable typo" and still rated 6. No reviewer found an error.

**Committee A (poster).** No review was edited after posting. Reviewer_vF9d wrote the most specific review. vF9d saw that LinGapE beats MisLid on synthetic data. Figure 1 confirms it: at ε = 2, LinGapE needs several times fewer samples, with an error frequency of 0.036, still under δ = 0.05. vF9d also found no bound on the initialization length t_0, and the authors conceded this. The rebuttal added an ε = 5 run in which LinGapE erred in 94.6% of runs; vF9d called the rebuttal "cogent". Reviewer_dLDo stayed "not convinced" by the experimental protocol but kept 6. That dispute is not a defect: a comparison at a fixed empirical error measures a fixed-budget property, not the stopping time. Reviewer_Q9sG's 7 is two sentences and "I lack the domain knowledge"; it is not evidence. The AC followed the scores, saw "a good story to tell", and made the t_0 bound and the relation to Kaufmann (2016) revision requests.

**Committee B (reject).** Reviewer_TZ2B's only edit predates the rebuttal. TZ2B asked why δ = 1 was set for LinGapE in Figure 2. The red label is δ̂ = 1.0, the error frequency, and lines 286-288 set δ = 5% for all algorithms. So TZ2B read the paper's main evidence for MisLid as a tuning choice. TZ2B also proposed to absorb the misspecification into the noise. That fails, because η is a fixed per-arm offset that can change the top-m set. TZ2B never answered the rebuttal. 1wnn raised the points A's reviewers also raised (LinGapE's lower sample count, an implicit Theorem 2) and withdrew a proposed (d+1)-feature reduction. 1wnn also asked for unstructured baselines, but LUCB is one. Reviewer_sjPk's 6 is two sentences from a self-declared non-expert. The AC granted that "The end results are novel" and rejected on its own reading.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for a bound on t_0, a reason for the heuristic stopping threshold, an unstructured baseline in Experiment B, and proof that m(K−m) half-space problems are cheaper than the (K choose m) sets (all vF9d). These are fixable; A's AC named only the t_0 bound. Only B raised the noise argument and the δ̂ misreading (TZ2B); the paper refutes both. Only B asked how the δ → 0 argument handles the t-dependence of β^uns (1wnn). That is a presentation gap, because the t term is lower order. TZ2B's complaint of thin proof intuition is fair but minor. The decisive "technical depth" doubt is not B's alone. A's AC raised it too, as a request for the camera-ready version.
