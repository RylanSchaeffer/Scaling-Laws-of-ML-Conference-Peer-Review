**Verdict.** Both committees recommended a poster for the same reason. Reviewers on both sides saw the same paper: a sound, new importance-sampling derivation of DisARM, extended to categorical variables, with small gains over a strong RLOO baseline. No reviewer found an error beyond notation slips and one overclaim. Before the rebuttal the committees stood level. By the reviewers' own notes, A started at a mean of 5.25 or lower; B's first four reviews averaged 5.25. The final gap (6.5 against 5.4) came from the discussion, not from different facts. All four A reviewers engaged and raised their scores. B received much the same rebuttal, and no B reviewer changed a score. B's area chair (AC) settled a 6/4/6/5 split with a late expert review. Both committees accepted over the same open weaknesses: RLOO as the only baseline, VAE-only tests and unclear exposition. These weaknesses were real. In the paper's two-sample runs, Omniglot shows no reliable gain. The camera-ready adds RELAX, which has the best train ELBO on all three datasets.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 6, 7, all raised after the rebuttal. Reviewer_tJ9n (2 hours) started at 4 or lower. In the discussion it argued that the "hard regioning" of Eq. 4-5 harms inference and needs Lagrange softening. That misreads case definitions over discrete samples as constraints. tJ9n moved to 5 after the first replies and to 6 after the correction, citing "a consensus between reviewers". Reviewer_WpYg compared Table 1 with another paper "from a quick reading". It also said stick-breaking needs a power-of-2 cardinality, but line 157 limits only the tree variant. Reviewer_Zj7z rose from 6 to 7 because DisARM-* still beat RLOO with 10 and 20 samples. Reviewer_kQGn rose to 7 after the t-tests and longer runs. kQGn did not remark that the Omniglot t-statistics are 0.3 to 0.9. The AC's three-sentence meta-review restates the scores.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 4, 6, 5, 6; none changed. Reviewer_AbN2 kept 6. Reviewer_ozdD (5, "a bit out of area") made the sharpest checkable point. Lines 239-240 claim better performance than RLOO "across all datasets", but Omniglot does not support it. Table 1 confirms this: RLOO scores -122.70 ± 0.80 and DisARM-Tree -123.08. The authors conceded. Reviewer_u8r7 (4) rejected for a sole baseline, a VAE-only task and a gap "too marginal" for the added complexity. The paper confirms all three, but u8r7 never answered the rebuttal. B never saw the t-tests, and its multi-sample tables came on 2021-08-27, in reply to the late review. The AC sought that review from Reviewer_KiRx (6): "definitely a valuable contribution, without being a huge leap forward." The AC followed it. The AC also wrote that promised results were not posted. That holds for the RELAX runs promised in both forums.

Only A raised Joint Stochastic Approximation (WpYg) and the near-identity of the SB and Tree estimators (tJ9n); the authors agreed on the latter. Only B flagged the Omniglot overclaim and the missing cost and wall-clock comparison (Msvo, u8r7, KiRx). Only B questioned the place of the ARS+/ARSM+ section (AbN2, Msvo, ozdD, KiRx) and asked for a variance analysis (KiRx). Only B (AbN2) said line 127 should read "≥ 0.5". That is right, but the feared support violation cannot occur: the ascending order caps the ratio at 1/3 wherever it matters (line 134). Both committees found the line-133 index typo and the missing limitations section. None of these issues changed a decision.
