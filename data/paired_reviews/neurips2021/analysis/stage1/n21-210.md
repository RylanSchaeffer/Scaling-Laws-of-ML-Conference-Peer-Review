**Verdict.** Committee A's reviewers tested the paper's central claims and found them weaker than stated; Committee B's reviewers took the claims as stated. FknY and npqJ (A) showed that the classical forward (VAW) bound, with a high-probability bound on max|y_t|, already gives regret d log²T against θ*. Theorem 3.3 gives d² log²T, so the new bound is worse by a factor of order d. The paper's own Section 2.3 and line 249 support this. FknY also showed that the lower bound, Corollary 3.3.1, bounds a worst case, not the stochastic regret, and got no answer. The camera-ready deletes the corollary and now claims upper bounds of order d log T log log T, which a valid Ω(log²T) stochastic lower bound would forbid. B's reviewers listed the "better bounds" and the lower bound as strengths. B's reviews were also lighter: 1, 2, 3 and 7 hours, against 5 to 6 in A. Both ACs followed their scores. The committees also weighted shared facts differently: npqJ (5) and 4n5Q (8) both saw that ridge and forward perform alike most of the time.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 5, 5. FknY (confidence 4) wrote the most searching review in either committee. Its "factor d" and "made-up" regularization points both hold. The authors conceded that a textbook exercise already removes the bounded-reward assumption for linear bandits, and that two references were missing. FknY rose from 4 to 5 after the authors explained an appendix step. npqJ accepted that λ = 1/T serves a best-of-both-worlds goal. It still called the title "somewhat misleading", because ridge with a constant λ suffices when the noise is stochastic. jxuz (6) doubted the paper's explanation of the extra d, and the authors agreed it was wrong. jxuz kept 6 but backed a "major revision". um9E's 5 rests on a generic "moderate increment" judgment. The AC cited limited significance, the d² gap and the unclear motivation for λ.

**Committee B (spotlight).** Ratings 7, 7, 7, 8. WQsZ (7 hours) checked the proofs. Its one soundness objection, on Lemma C.1, was its own sign error; it retracted and rose to 7. XAWf (2 hours) correctly noted that Fig. 3 uses only λ = 10^-5. YH9r (1 hour, confidence 2) called the forward algorithm "a new algorithm"; line 13 credits it to Vovk and to Azoury and Warmuth. 4n5Q (8), an expert, noted that the ridge bound matches the lower bound only for large λ, and that the two algorithms "are not that different most of the time". The authors told A that their explanation of the extra d was wrong; they did not tell B. The AC's four sentences cite consensus and give no reason for a spotlight. They also say the paper removes a boundedness assumption "on the inputs"; it removes it on observations.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A compared the new bounds with the classical bound plus a high-probability bound on |y_t|. Only A asked whether the lower bound covers the stochastic setting. Only A found the textbook exercise, the small-loss work and Abbasi-Yadkori, Pál and Szepesvári (2012). Only A noted that Theorem 4.1 still needs X and S. Only B raised Vovk's 2001 lower bound, the λ condition for matching bounds and the Lemma C.1 false alarm. Both saw that the bandit experiment uses an "aberrant" λ = 1/T, and both heard the authors concede parity at moderate λ. A's findings hold against the paper and go to its central claims; B's were minor or, for Lemma C.1, wrong. Checks use b/paper_submitted.pdf; A's line references match it.
