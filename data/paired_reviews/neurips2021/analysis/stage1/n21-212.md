**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. Three reviewers, UycQ in A and XqTW and qDh3 in B, called ACE a standard gradient attack at small eps. Algorithm 1 confirms it: one FGSM-style step, with eps halved until the label stays the same. XqTW and qDh3 put the novelty in the problem and said that was enough. Both raised their scores after the rebuttal. UycQ held technical depth as the bar. The rebuttal gave UycQ no PGD baseline and no cross-entropy ablation, so the 5 stayed. A's other 5, from omcp, rests on a conceded side claim and a misread, and omcp kept it without a reason. A's AC followed the two 5s. B's Spotlight does not follow from B's reviews. B scored 7, 6 and 7, one 7 is generic, and the three-sentence meta-review rests on the AC's own view.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_UycQ (5) is a principled detractor. UycQ asked for FGSM and PGD baselines at comparable eps, a cross-entropy ablation and a discussion of Ovadia et al. (2019). The submission has none of these. The rebuttal added a lending scenario and AdvProp results, but no PGD numbers and no ablation. UycQ kept the 5 and called ACE "a rescaled version of PGD" with a line-searched eps. Reviewer_omcp (5) praised the experiments and gave two cons. The first is correct: the paper claims an easy extension to regression and runs no regression experiment. The second is wrong. omcp says no experiment supports the claim that the attack "severely harms some of the most popular techniques". Sections 5.1 to 5.4 test softmax, Deep Ensembles, MC-Dropout and SelectiveNet. After the rebuttal said so, omcp kept the 5 and gave no reason. Reviewer_PoYC raised to 7 after new cross-architecture and adversarially trained results. The meta-review says omcp "supported the concern" and that all reviewers want more evaluations. The public record shows neither. The AC also asks for a DeepFool baseline that no reviewer raised.

**Committee B (spotlight).** Reviewer_qDh3 made the same point as UycQ: weak FGSM attacks, untargeted on correct images and targeted on incorrect ones. But qDh3 called concept-level novelty "completely fine" and asked for real-world relevance instead. The lending example and the AdvProp table each moved qDh3 up one point, probably from 4 to 6. Table 1 already answered qDh3's question about transfer across architectures. Reviewer_XqTW raised to 7 and called the paper a "paradigm shift". XqTW's AURC mismatch between Table 1 and Figure 4 is a misread: the captions say black-box and white-box. Reviewer_bohc (7) wrote four generic lines, listed no weakness and did not join the discussion. That review is not evidence of quality. The AC called the method "elegant" and recommended a Spotlight. No reviewer scored above 7, and qDh3 still wanted real-world evidence and a main-text AURC definition.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for baseline attacks (FGSM, PGD, DeepFool) and a cross-entropy ablation. The rebuttal left this open, and A's meta-review rests on it. Only A raised dataset shift (Ovadia et al.), the regression claim and the missing societal-impact statement. Only B raised ground-truth labels at scale, epistemic uncertainty, detection by monitoring AURC instead of accuracy, the appendix-only AURC definition and the missing CW and MMA attacks. The authors answered each B point with new results, arguments or promises. So B's requests suited the rebuttal, and A's main request did not. Both committees raised the simple method, the weak threat model, adversarially trained targets and transfer.
