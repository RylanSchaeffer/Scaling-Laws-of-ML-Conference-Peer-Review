**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same borderline paper and nearly the same weaknesses. The evaluation uses three binary datasets and averages three seeds without error bars. Every debiasing baseline needs sensitive-attribute labels. The proxy-annotation step rests on an unverified assumption, and the multi-attribute case is only a sketch. The authors gave both committees nearly the same answers: small new tables and promises to revise. Committee A ended at 6, 6, 5, 7, and its area chair (AC) rejected because the fixes were only promises. Committee B ended at 6, 7, 7, 7. There, Reviewer_Pdht went from reject to 7 "based on these commitments", and Reviewer_Aa1C gave an uncritical 7. B's AC named no weakness. The split is mainly the weight each committee gave to rebuttal promises at the borderline. A's panel also raised the attribute-free literature; B's did not.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_5yfA began at 4 or lower and rose only to 5. Its main point holds: lines 225-231 compare only Vanilla, Adversarial and EOR, and the last two need attribute labels. It discounted the JTT and CivilComments results as "only promised to be added upon acceptance". The JTT comparison is one operating point on MEPS. Its "typo" in Eq. (1) is a misreading: the equation applies c to the averaged representation. Reviewer_s8dC asked for a synthetic test of each component. It got a four-point hand example, yet said its concerns were clarified. It also called the bias-amplified model the "teacher", which the paper never does. Reviewer_DyXC (7) correctly found Theorem 1's bound, ε_p(λ_z ε_c + ε_L), possibly loose. It also found that the paper omits Nam et al. (2020), whose GCE-trained biased model Section 2.3 also uses. Reviewer_Ehds (6) asked for harder datasets and never replied. The AC overrode a 6.0 mean. Most of its reasons match the reviews. Its "unclarity about the neutralization scheme" has no public support after the rebuttal.

**Committee B (accept).** Reviewer_Pdht wrote the most careful review, and the paper confirms it. Lines 241-242 average three seeds, and the checklist reports no error bars. In Figure 3, EOR beats RNF on equalized odds for Adult and MEPS, yet lines 252-253 say RNF "outperforms" the baselines. A five-seed table for RNF at one Adult point and three promises moved Pdht to 7. Reviewer_Ho2y correctly noted that Hardt et al. [27] and Fair Mixup [40] are cited but not compared. Lines 370-376 even claim "comparable or better performance" than [40] without an experiment. Ho2y accepted a DP-regularized baseline at one point instead. Reviewer_FmVc (7) accepted the paper's framing that existing methods need attribute labels. Reviewer_Aa1C's 7 is not evidence. Its summary copies the abstract, including "our RNF framework". Its review has four generic sentences, no weakness and no discussion. The AC called the paper "soundly evaluated", which Pdht had disputed until the promises.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for attribute-free baselines, harder datasets, and the missing related work (Nam et al., Kang et al., group DRO). Only A questioned Theorem 1 and hyperparameter sensitivity. Only B tested statistical robustness and the "outperforms" claim. Only B asked for post-processing and Fair Mixup baselines. Both raised the proxy-annotation assumption, the multi-attribute extension, limitations hidden in the appendix, and how γ is tuned. Neither noticed that γ is chosen by validation-set fairness metrics (lines 196-197), which need attribute labels.
