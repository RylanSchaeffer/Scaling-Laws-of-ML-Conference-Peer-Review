**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same gap and weighed it differently. The paper shows that its PMd-like result needs at least three areas and Dale's law. It does not explain why. The paper confirms the gap. Area 1 already has a direction axis nearly orthogonal to color (overlap 0.14, lines 173-176). Yet the last area of a 2-area network keeps color (Fig 3e), and the main text gives no reason. Committee A drew two confident detractors and held a long private discussion. In it, the top scorer, Reviewer_L9GC (8), agreed that the paper lacks the mechanism and that the abstract's "Dale's Law is critical" claims too much. The authors' own reply in A calls Dale's law "a sufficient constraint". A's area chair (AC) then rejected at a mean of 5.75. In B, Reviewer_SBJB named the same three-area gap but gave 6. Nobody in B questioned Dale's law. B's AC wrote three sentences that repeat the abstract's "critical" claim. Neither committee found an error. A asked for a causal account that the paper lacks; B accepted the empirical result as enough.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 4, 4, 8, 7, all at confidence 4. Reviewer_EDPH wrote the decisive critique: direction and color already separate in Area 1, so why three areas, and why Dale's law? The paper bears this out. Reviewer_jJXH (7) asked what Area 2 computes; the paper analyses Areas 1 and 3 but not Area 2. Reviewer_vT5V's 4 rests mainly on venue fit and a preference for data-constrained RNNs. It suggests that a color-free Area 3 is expected a priori. Fig 3a refutes this: without Dale's law, Area 3 decodes color at about 0.8 to 1.0. L9GC's 8 rests on general praise, and Fig 3a, which L9GC cites, already answers its question about Dale's law. The rebuttal gave new 2-area analyses in text only. vT5V found them "somewhat more convincing" but kept 4; EDPH kept 4. jJXH edited its review on 2021-08-30, when it asked for more analysis; the direction is unknown. The meta-review restates EDPH and the sufficient-versus-necessary concession, and ignores venue fit. It credits the PMd result to "the Mante et al paper"; the data come from Chandrasekaran et al. 2017.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 7, at confidence 4, 3, 3, after 2 to 3 hours each. SBJB raised the three-area gap (line 147, Fig 3e) and correct presentation faults: a missing "Methods" section and a non-existent Fig 4e. It never replied. Reviewer_eHSi asked for the promised testable hypotheses; the submission has none. Reviewer_LjFK asked for related work, an area mapping and a BPTT caveat. It accepted that the sweeps found "the necessary ingredients". eHSi and LjFK approved the rebuttal in one line each and kept their scores. The meta-review follows the scores. Its "All reviewers agreed on the significance and the novelty" is too strong. SBJB could not tell whether similar work exists, and LjFK found related work missing. The submission does not cite Michaels et al. 2020, a close prior multi-area RNN model, which the authors named in their rebuttal.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked why Dale's law is needed and whether "critical" exceeds the evidence. Only A raised ML relevance, venue fit, and task-trained versus data-constrained RNNs. Only B raised related work, BPTT plausibility, and the mapping of Areas 1 and 2 to brain regions. Both raised the unexplained three-area result, the missing testable predictions, and method details left to the appendix. The decisive fact was shared; only its weight differed.
