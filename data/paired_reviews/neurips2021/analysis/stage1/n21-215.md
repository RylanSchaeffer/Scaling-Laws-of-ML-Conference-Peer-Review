**Verdict.** Both committees read the paper the same way. It is clear. Its algorithm is Thompson Sampling (TS) plus a per-arm doubling rule that ends each batch (Algorithm 1). Its proofs adapt Agrawal and Goyal. Both committees called it incremental. Committee A had no champion. Two of its reviewers and its area chair (AC) also pressed a point that the paper confirms. B-TS needs O(N log T) batches (Theorem 4.1), but the paper's own related work credits batched elimination with O(log T) (lines 92-93). Committee B's mean rests on a well-founded 8 and an uninformed 7. Its AC wrote "Õ(log T) batches" and never weighed N. The split is reviewer draw plus the same novelty facts weighted differently.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 3, 6 (mean 4.75). All four reviewers named limited novelty. The 6, Reviewer_9U7C, called it "the main weakness". Reviewer_DzXv's 3 rests on checkable points that hold. B-TS differs from Beta-Bernoulli TS only in the if/else rule. Theorem 4.6 uses O(N log T) batches, but O(log log T) suffice for that rate (Gao et al. 2019). No theorem gives regret for a given batch budget. Reviewer_2bFv found the extra factor N. Remark 4.5 and the abstract do drop it. Reviewer_9U7C correctly noted that Remark 4.5 applies a finite-time lower bound to asymptotic optimality. The rebuttal answered DzXv's minimax point with the problem-dependent bound. No reviewer replied. Reviewer_JP6Y added "okay to publish" on 2021-09-10 but kept 5. The AC rejected for the factor N and said the authors try to "minimize the importance of N". It also called the combination of the doubling method and TS "straightforward".

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 8, 4, 6, 7 (mean 6.25). Reviewer_tYDL checked the main proof, and the authors accepted each of its corrections. But its own text calls the work a "natural expansion" of batched UCB with little practical impact, so the 8 is generous. Reviewer_Qcd6 gave 7 at confidence 2 and is "not very familiar with this research area". Its significance section repeats its summary. Reviewer_2HGA called the contribution incremental and gave 6. Reviewer_E33d (4, confidence 5) said that the stage-based update of Zhang et al. (2020), with one state and H = 1, is the batching rule of Algorithm 1. That is correct; only the sampling within a batch differs. The authors first described Zhang et al. as switching on observed rewards. They then credited E33d with a one-action-per-batch design that it never proposed. E33d kept 4. The AC set E33d aside with another reviewer's points on contextual bandits and on the merit of TS. These points do not address the multi-armed rule. The AC called the experiments "thorough and convincing". But tYDL noted that the static baselines use only equal-size batches (lines 314-316).

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A treated the factor N as a weakness. In B, tYDL stated it and did not object. Only A raised the O(log log T) minimax batch count, the missing regret-versus-budget trade-off, and the lower bound misapplied in Remark 4.5. Only B named Zhang et al. (2020), the missing fixed geometric-schedule baseline, the proof typos and the misleading "parallelizing". Both raised the doubling trick, proofs that follow Agrawal and Goyal, no better rates than batched UCB, and weak motivation for batch sizes that the algorithm chooses. On those shared facts, A said reject and B said weak accept.
