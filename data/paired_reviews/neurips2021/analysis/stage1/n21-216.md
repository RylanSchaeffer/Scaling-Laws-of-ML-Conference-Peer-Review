**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts. Local IF relaxes individual fairness, and Laplacian smoothing for IF has uncited precedents (Lahoti et al.; Kang et al., InFoRM). The committees weighted these facts differently, and only A's reviewers engaged after the rebuttal. In A, two reviewers rose to 6 after discussion, all four ended at 6 or 7, and the area chair (AC) followed the scores. In B, no reviewer posted in the discussion. Reviewer_bTqy held at 5 because local IF "seems to be a significant relaxation of IF". The AC rejected for that reason alone, over a 7, 6, 5, 4 split. The submission supports B's concern. Definition 4.1 asks only that an expected local Lipschitz ratio be finite; it has no constant L. Under Assumption 4.4, every hypothesis already meets it. So the content of Theorem 4.6 is a limit: the regularizer converges to a mean squared gradient in the fair metric. The camera-ready added "≤ L" and an (ε, L) form. No A reviewer probed the definition.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 6. Reviewer_q7wC (8 hours) checked the notation and called local IF "well motivated". Reviewer_3BRp found the inductive extension weak but the transductive method worthwhile. Reviewer_yCJc asked what "local" means and why it beats global IF. The rebuttal pointed to Figure 2: GLIF violates only far-pair constraints and keeps accuracy. yCJc then went from 5 to 6. Reviewer_YBcz said the method before Section 3.2.2 is "almost identical" to InFoRM. The submission cites neither InFoRM nor Lahoti et al. YBcz argued for three rounds and then raised its score "a bit". The authors conceded that dropping dissimilar pairs equals zeroing adjacency entries. They also said that without this zeroing the Laplacian "does not enforce local IF". But Theorem 4.6 is proved with τ = ∞, with no entries zeroed. The AC called the novelty concern "partially (but not completely) addressed" and accepted on the scores.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 7, 5, 6, 4, all at confidence 3. The two accept-side reviews are thin. Reviewer_63Qx spent 1.5 hours, wrote an accurate summary, asked three questions and never replied. Reviewer_9cRG's main review reads "See above." It calls the gains in Tables 1-2 "trivial", but prediction consistency rises from 0.614 to 0.844 on Toxicity. Reviewer_LaWU gave 4 for uncited prior work and few baselines. The prior-work point is correct. But LaWU misread the inductive update (3.7), an exact closed-form step with no step length, and it never replied. Reviewer_bTqy wrote B's most specific review. It called Theorem 4.6 unsurprising given eq. (3.2); lines 133-136 give the same intuition. It asked for global IF with a truncated metric. Section 5.1 has no such baseline, although IF-constraints runs in 5 minutes there. The authors declined it as too costly. The AC wrote that the relaxed notion "was proposed since it is satisfied by the Laplacian method". Lines 202-204 support this: they introduce the definition as what solving (3.1) achieves.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B made the relaxed definition a reason to reject. A's yCJc asked the same question and accepted the answer. Only B asked for a truncated-metric baseline and called Theorem 4.6 unsurprising. Only A asked about memory in the inductive setting, BERT running time, and the notation in (3.8). Both raised the overlap with Lahoti et al. and InFoRM. A discussed it until YBcz raised its score. In B, LaWU never engaged, and the AC did not cite it. The facts were shared. Their weight and the engagement differed.
