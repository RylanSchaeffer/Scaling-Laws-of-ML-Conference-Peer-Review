**Verdict.** The panels were nearly the same. Committee B's area chair (AC) made the split. A rated 7, 6, 5, 6 (mean 6.0). B rated 6, 6, 5, 6 (mean 5.75), after three B reviewers raised their scores. B's AC rejected against that panel, and its lead reason is wrong. It says that with no constraint and f_1 = 0, step (iii) of Algorithm 1 adds nothing to step (i). In that case step (ii) returns u = z − γF(z), and step (iii) gives z⁺ = z − γF(u). That is the extragradient method. Without step (iii) the method is gradient descent-ascent, which can diverge on bilinear problems. The authors told both committees that step (iii) is the extragradient step. The AC's other cons repeat Reviewer_HYkt: Theorems 1-2 are stated for 1-smooth functions with μ, δ < 1. Rescaling f by L leaves δ/μ, and so the bound, unchanged. The authors said so, and the camera-ready restates the theorems for any L. The AC called this a proof change that needs "substantial revision". It is a normalization remark. A's AC followed a thinner panel and named no weakness. Both committees saw the missing intuition for Algorithm 1. A treated it as minor; B's AC made it decisive.

**Committee A (poster).** Reviewer_PG9W (7) wrote a short positive review. One point is wrong: it says no method is compared, but Figures 1-3 compare Algorithm 1 with extragradient. It also asked why Algorithm 1 has three steps, the point that B's AC later made decisive. Reviewer_Crdz (6) spent 1.5 hours at confidence 4. It says the algorithms contain "time-varying communication topologies"; the paper uses a fixed graph. It raised its score with one line. Reviewer_6jmq (6, confidence 2) repeated its summary as its main review and gave two one-line cons. Reviewer_ngA2 (5, confidence 2) wrote the most technical review. Its points hold: the bounds involve only ‖y*‖², and Theorems 1-2 use Z = R^{2d} although Assumption 1 requires a compact Z. The meta-review cites "the majority of reviewers" and names no weakness.

**Committee B (reject).** B's reviews are more detailed and mostly accurate. Reviewer_VL91 found that line 261 says 10 and 15 workers while the figures show 10 and 20. It also found that the meshed-network experiments promised in the supplement were missing; the authors replied "We added". Reviewer_J36K noted correctly that Algorithm 3 needs λ2(W). Reviewer_HYkt (5) doubted the lower bound because Arjevani and Shamir prove √(δ/μ). That doubt is wrong. Their bound is for minimization, and the paper itself contrasts linear saddle-point rates with square-root minimization rates (lines 74-87). HYkt accepted the answer. It also asked whether F_1 and f_1 are typos; line 222 defines them. It rose only to 5, still disputed the normalization, and did not answer the rescaling reply. J36K said its concerns were addressed. The AC still rejected.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B raised the normalized constants (HYkt, AC), the claimed conflict with Arjevani and Shamir (HYkt), per-step full gradients and subproblem solves (HYkt), the need for λ2(W) (J36K), the log²(1/ε) decentralized rate (TSma), and the claim that the algorithm is not designed for similarity (TSma, AC). Only A raised the ‖y*‖-only bound, the compactness conflict and novelty over prior lower bounds (ngA2), and the multi-consensus design of Algorithm 2 (PG9W). Both raised the missing intuition for Algorithm 1, the weak motivation for δ-similarity, and experiments without a decentralized run. The one B-only issue that the AC used is a presentation point that the authors resolved.
