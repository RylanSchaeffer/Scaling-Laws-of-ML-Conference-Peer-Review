**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Six of seven reviewers called the removable-variable recursion new or surprising, and no reviewer found an error in a proof. Table 1 supports the empirical claim: RLCD uses the fewest CI tests and has the best adjacency F1 on all seven benchmarks. Both committees found the same main weakness. A removable vertex exists only if the undirected-edge subgraph is chordal, and only the appendix says so. Reviewer_TRky in A and all four B reviewers raised it. A two-case proof in the rebuttal and a promise to move the condition satisfied all of them. Both committees also saw that FCI+ is cited but not compared; neither treated that as decisive. No rating is above 7, so nobody argued for more than a poster. The gap in means (6.33 against 7.0) is not a different judgment. Reviewer_kohp gave A's 5 for Table 1 reporting gaps. kohp then wrote "I am happy with acceptance for this paper" but did not edit the score. The committees differed in what they caught. TRky found that Figure 1, the motivating example, is wrong. B's longer reviews (1,928 words against 919) examined definitions, citations and the bound.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 7, 5; 5 reviewer-hours). TRky wrote the most useful review. In Figure 1a, the path Z, T, W, U, Y is open given W, so T is not removable as drawn. The authors conceded, and the camera-ready reverses an edge. TRky also saw that l.257 needs a removable vertex in every set. TRky kept 7 but still wanted an FCI+ experiment, not a bound argument. Reviewer_r1m9 spent one hour, said it could not judge the theory or its novelty, and listed no weakness. Its 7 carries little weight. kohp asked what Table 1 measures, against which graph, and with which code. The paper states none of these. The authors confirmed adjacency-only scores against the true PAG. The AC wrote two sentences on novelty and consensus and named no weakness.

Committee B (7, 7, 7, 7; 21 reviewer-hours). Reviewer_Wz2i partly checked the proofs. Wz2i found the hidden chordality condition not "completely above board". Wz2i also found a non-standard m-connecting path definition, harmless to m-separation. Reviewer_SAUv found the same condition, a citation that ignores selection variables, and a bound without the Markov boundary cost. Reviewer_pWDU and Reviewer_QNSv asked whether a removable vertex always exists. QNSv (confidence 2) rose to 7 after the rebuttal, although its concern about errors in the Markov boundaries got no answer. The AC's text is generic. It calls the concept a "removal variable" and does not mention the condition that all four reviewers raised.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these issues:

- the wrong motivating example in Figure 1 (TRky);
- adjacency-only metrics, an unstated reference graph and no orientation scores (kohp);
- unnamed baseline implementations and the coefficient range (kohp).

Only B raised these issues:

- the m-connecting definition and the l.133 citation (Wz2i, SAUv);
- the Markov boundary cost missing from Proposition 3 (SAUv);
- reliance on accurate Markov boundaries and a one-sentence limitations discussion (QNSv, SAUv, pWDU);
- no sparse against dense comparison (Wz2i).

The paper confirms the reporting and presentation gaps, although l.265-267 already mention the Markov boundary cost. No reviewer noted that in the densest synthetic setting (Figure 2e) RLCD runs as many CI tests as RFCI; its lead there is F1 only. None of these issues changed a decision.
