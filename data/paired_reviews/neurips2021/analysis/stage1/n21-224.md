**Verdict.** The usual pattern is inverted. A's reviewers gave 7, 8 and 7, and A rejected. B's reviewers gave 5, 4, 7 and 8, and B accepted. The area chairs (ACs) made the difference, in opposite directions. A's AC made over-claiming the decisive flaw, from an own reading of the method. The paper confirms the premise. Equation 4 has no term that ties a decoding to the label, and the Figure 3 probes come from networks trained with it. Yet lines 138-140 offer the decodings as a step towards seeing "what features a neural network is attending to". Lines 275-276 say a classifier trained with Eq. 8 "must have" ignored the protected attribute. A's reviewers flagged the overselling too, but as a rewrite. The AC judged that rewrite too large to accept without new review. B's AC broke a 2-2 split toward novelty. B's negative reviewers objected to scale and cost, not to the claims. Nobody in B weighed the over-claiming. The facts overlapped; the ACs weighed them differently. The program chairs ruled A's concern not fatal and made A's AC's edits conditions of acceptance.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 7, 8, 7; all reviewers engaged. Reviewer_3B1Z made specific, correct points. Line 122 implies probing is cheap and omits the training cost. Line 275 ignores that the model could fool the attribute classifier. Reviewer_gKMX called the introduction "a bit of an oversell". Its summary calls the models "convolutional", but the paper uses an 8-layer MLP (line 129). Reviewer_9qhL called the software analogy "strained" and raised its score to 7 for a new Figure 1. gKMX's edit date matches its "even more so now" comment, so its 8 likely rose then. The AC noted "the high scores" and rejected anyway. The reviewers supplied the overselling and the line-275 point. The Eq. 4 handbag argument and the Eq. 8 hyperparameter argument are the AC's own.

**Committee B (accept).** Ratings 5, 4, 7, 8. Reviewer_csH8 and Reviewer_v4fL objected that the paper tests only an 8-layer MLP on small datasets, at high compute. That is correct (lines 129, 344). csH8 also noted no comparison with standard OOD and calibration methods [20, 22-24]. That is correct too. The max-softmax baseline [22] needs no extra computation, so the rebuttal's scoping argument does not cover it. Two csH8 points misread the paper: Table 5 varies the pretrained teacher, not the main network, and the Eq. 8 term needs the decoder. Both kept their scores after the CNN and runtime results. Those results are missing from the record. Reviewer_f5Sy (7) wrote that decodings "retain only the task specific information"; Figure 3 shows they keep face, pose and background. Reviewer_7fvR's 8 rests on a 2-hour review and no reply to the rebuttal. The AC wrote that the reviewers found the idea novel, though v4fL called it "old". It said scalability "still remains a challenge" and accepted.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked what the decodings show (the AC) and whether Eq. 8 merely fools the attribute classifier (3B1Z). Only A had ethics reviews. They led to dropping the attractiveness task, which the AC called "such a bad example". Only B objected to the MLP-only scope, the small datasets and the missing standard baselines. Only B noted that MC-Dropout beats ReDecNN on MNIST and FashionMNIST (Table 2). Both noted the strained software analogy, the training cost and the narrow sense of "composition". A's AC made the analogy the core flaw; B's reviewers treated it as minor. B's negative reviewers made cost a reason to reject; A's reviewers treated it as minor.
