**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. All six reviewers rated the paper at the threshold or one point above it (A: 6, 6, 6; B: 6, 6, 7). All six saw new, clean theory for partition recovery on d-dimensional lattices. They also saw the same limits: a simple merging step on a known algorithm, thin motivation, and a k_dyad(θ*) gap to the lower bound that the paper admits (l.249-250, l.344-346). In each committee, one confidence-2 reviewer started below the threshold and rose to 6 after the rebuttal. Neither committee had a champion or a detractor, and both ACs restated the scores. So the agreement shows a uniformly lukewarm reception, not a strong paper. The committees differed in what they probed, not in what they concluded. A probed the theorems; B probed the algorithm and the motivation. Each found real errors, and both treated them as fixable presentation. No reviewer noticed that the experiments run the η-free variant of Section S4 (l.317) at one lattice size. So the simulations test neither the estimator of Theorem 2 nor its rate.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 6, 6). Reviewer_SRUt wrote the longest review in either committee, at confidence 2, without a detailed check of the proofs. SRUt asked why Assumption 1 has the same scaling as the Theorem 2 bound. The authors' explanations moved SRUt from 5 to 6. In the same comment, SRUt found that the P_N condition in Proposition 3 (l.287) is wrong, and the authors conceded. Reviewer_mM4N made the sharpest correct point: the bound (15) does not give the consistency (3) that the paper claims (l.142-143, l.256-257). The authors denied the claim but gave the missing condition, and the camera-ready adds it. mM4N did not reply. Reviewer_jHsk called the merging fix "a bit straightforward", kept 6 because the k* gap remained, and "would not push for it". The AC wrote "slightly above the acceptance threshold".

Committee B (6, 6, 7). Reviewer_ZZUp (15 hours) liked the technical contribution but held back over motivation and over k_dyad, which can grow fast with d. ZZUp said optimality needs a constant k_dyad. This overlooks Corollary 4, although (13) keeps the factor. After the rebuttal, ZZUp still saw no case where signal recovery fails and kept "a weak accept". Reviewer_2a7D (confidence 2, 5 hours) rose to 6 on a motivation paragraph and one unsupported sentence about region size. The paper contradicts two of 2a7D's experimental objections. Figure 3 already has regions of unequal size, and the TV-based competitor is a denoise-then-prune baseline. Reviewer_qw8i gave the only 7, with generic praise and clarification questions, and did not reply. One question was well founded: the paper calls DCART greedy (l.58, l.129), but (4) is an exact minimization. The camera-ready replaces "greedy" with dynamic programming. The AC's "address the concerns of the reviewers satisfactorily" overstates ZZUp's last comment.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these issues:

- Theorem 2 does not give consistency (3) (mM4N; confirmed).
- The scaling of Assumption 1 and the Proposition 3 condition (SRUt; the typo is confirmed).
- The simple merging fix, and the k* gap for the NP-hard estimator (jHsk).
- No rate check over N (mM4N, SRUt), and the cost of the merging step (mM4N).

Only B raised the greedy-or-exact ambiguity and the merging of non-adjacent rectangles (qw8i). Only B raised non-axis-aligned partitions and more experimental cases (2a7D), and the growth of k_dyad with d (ZZUp). Both committees raised the k_dyad gap, thin motivation, dense notation and the λ1/λ2 typo at l.252. None of these issues changed a decision.
