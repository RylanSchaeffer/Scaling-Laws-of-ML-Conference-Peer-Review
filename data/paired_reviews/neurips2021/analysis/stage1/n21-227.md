**Verdict.** Both committees found the same central limitation and weighted it differently. Theorem 3, the upper bound, reaches Theorem 1's M^(1/4)/n^(1/2) rate only when the analyst already holds the support of X and a mean function mu with a small error bound err_mu that holds almost surely (lines 212-216). How to get these from data appears only in a parenthetical (lines 199-201). So the bounds match per distribution, not uniformly: when M > n, no mu is good for every distribution. In A, Reviewer_Rqsq saw exactly this, accepted the authors' "wrapper" framing, and moved to 7. In B, Reviewer_UaZ8 (confidence 5) pressed the same gap. B's area chair (AC) drew the concession that the almost-sure assumption "is of course too strong". The AC then rejected because the promised rewrite could not be checked. The split is reviewer draw plus AC tolerance for a conceded, fixable gap. The camera-ready made the L2 change and added a data-splitting paragraph.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 8, 5, 7, 7, 7. Rqsq (10 hours) wrote that the upper bound "takes away the core of the estimation problem" and called the paper "a somewhat borderline case". Its claim that a per-atom sample-mean interval is "optimally adaptive" is wrong for near-uniform P_X with n < M < n^2: its own bound is then near 1, while Theorem 3's bound vanishes with a good mu. The rebuttal said so, and Rqsq moved to accept. It still judged the estimation analysis "perhaps best left out of this version". Reviewer_tn9d's 8 is substantive. Reviewer_uYq5 correctly notes that Theorem 3 needs a finite support. It raised its rating to 5 after "having read the other reviews". Two 7s carry little weight. Reviewer_dAue wrote one accurate paragraph in two hours and never joined the discussion. Reviewer_WAr3 (confidence 2) gave generic praise and said it could not check correctness. The AC posted nothing and wrote a two-sentence "consensus" note.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 7, 4, 8. UaZ8 read the proofs and found real defects. The proof of Theorem 1 (lines 267-273) does not cover M_gamma = infinity. Theorems 1 and 3 state Y in R against Definition 1's [0,1]. It also asked where the err_mu assumption could be met. Its later claim that the bounds do not match overreached: with a good mu and a known support, Theorem 3 has Theorem 1's rate in M and n, which is all the paper claims (lines 222-224). That challenge still made Reviewer_teiB consider dropping from 7 to 6. Reviewer_6nGY's careful 8 answered the challenge only with a short note siding with the authors. Reviewer_idBd's 5 (confidence 2) misreads the target as Y_{n+1}, and it later admitted confusion. The AC asked the authors for a reference for the err_mu assumption. The meta-review's two reasons come from that exchange and from UaZ8's request for an end-to-end analysis. No rating moved after the rebuttal.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B probed the almost-sure err_mu assumption, whether the bounds match, the comparison with Barber 2020 and Gupta et al. 2020, the infinite-M_gamma gap in the proof, and the Y in R typo. Only A raised the misleading title, the per-atom alternative, the gamma for 1-gamma slip at line 183, and the thin literature review. Both raised the oracle inputs to Theorem 3, the missing experiments, the role of the dimension d, and an infinite estimated support size. The decisive fact was shared. A accepted it on the authors' promise of caveats. B, after extracting a concession, would not accept on a promise.
