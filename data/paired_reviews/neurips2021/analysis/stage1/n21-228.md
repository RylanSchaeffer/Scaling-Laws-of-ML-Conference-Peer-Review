**Verdict.** The split comes from who reviewed the paper, not from different facts. Both committees named the same main weaknesses. Robustness and noise are vaguely defined. CF-GNNExplainer is missing from the graph-classification experiments. The "human intuition" claim has no user study behind it. The decision-boundary machinery is not specific to GNNs. Committee A drew Reviewer_ZnuS (3, confidence 5, 10 hours), the only expert-level critic in either committee. Most of ZnuS's checkable points hold against the submission. ZnuS stayed unconvinced after the rebuttal, the other two A reviewers never replied, and the mean was 4.67. Committee B's four reviewers spent 3 to 4 hours each, at confidence 2 to 4. Its two critics, Reviewer_gQ2w and Reviewer_Vbh5, said the rebuttal addressed most of their concerns, and the mean was 6.0. Each area chair (AC) wrote a generic note that followed its mean. No issue is a fatal flaw, and the camera-ready fixed several of them.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 3, 6. ZnuS is right that the paper calls CF-GNNExplainer an adversarial attack (lines 109-113) and omits it from graph classification (lines 326-328). It is right that the abstract claims human intuition with no user study, and that Definition 1 rests on undefined "significantly" and "slight changes". It is right that lines 101-102 say PGExplainer optimises each graph independently, while lines 367-368 say its network "implicitly considers all the graphs". Figure 2a shows no robustness gain over PGExplainer on Mutagenicity. ZnuS overstated two points. Minimality is not "totally absent", because Eq. 8 has an L1 sparsity term (lines 288-290). And the baselines do support graph classification. Reviewer_SgPD (5) correctly found that one vague sentence defines the noise (lines 350-352). It also doubted the robustness metric when 30% of a molecule changes. Reviewer_uoze gave 6 after one hour, with stock praise and three short questions. No score changed. The meta-review names no specific concern.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 6, 5. Reviewer_gQ2w called the "source code ... not available" reason for omitting CF-GNNExplainer "a ridiculous excuse", and the paper does give that reason (lines 326-328). Vbh5 added that the code was already public. The authors extended CF-GNNExplainer to graph classification and reported it worse than their method. gQ2w then said most concerns were addressed, and the authors agreed to drop the human-intuition claim. Vbh5 received new adversarial-noise experiments and ended at 5. Both edited their reviews after the rebuttal; any score change is not visible. Reviewer_BMo4 gave 6 at confidence 2, said it could not judge originality, and asked the AC to check it. The AC did not. Reviewer_1Uri's 7 mostly restates the abstract, and its label says the math was not carefully checked. The meta-review says the reviewers "agree" the work is "well evaluated and explained". That overstates a panel with a 5 and three complaints about the writing.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A (ZnuS) flagged the PGExplainer contradiction, the "adversarial attack" label for CF-GNNExplainer, the non-counterfactual baselines, the dependence on sampled boundaries and the restriction to piecewise-linear activations. Only A (SgPD) raised distribution shift from edge deletion and asked for an ablation of the counterfactual term. Only B asked about prediction-changing perturbations (Vbh5), guarantees for the approximations (BMo4), the explainer architecture (gQ2w) and graph-generating models (1Uri). The decisive facts were shared. A's confident expert weighted them heavily and rejected the rebuttal. B's less confident reviewers accepted it.
