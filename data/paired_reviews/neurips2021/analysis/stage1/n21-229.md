**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected on the same facts. Both committees saw three weaknesses. The method is tabular and needs known dynamics and fixed features. The policy-quality baselines date from 2004 to 2009. No experiment includes MaxEnt IRL, although Theorem 1 makes MaxEnt a special case. A's area chair (AC) named the first two weaknesses, called the scope "severely limiting", and accepted anyway: "impact is subjective" and half the reviewers liked the ideas. B's AC rejected on scalability and the missing MaxEnt comparison. The paper supports B's main objection. The highway demonstrations are "sampled from the stochastic MaxEnt policy" (lines 339-340), so MaxEnt IRL is the natural baseline. The reviewer pools also sat one point apart: means 6.0 and 5.0, pooled 5.5. A had Reviewer_w9mr's 7; w9mr judged the limitations not major but "did not check" the derivations. B had Reviewer_4tjM's 4, which made the MaxEnt omission decisive. Neither committee found a fatal error. The split comes from how much each AC tolerated a correct but modest tabular contribution.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 6, 5; mean 6.0. Reviewer_xMat noted that Eq. 2 holds only for deterministic dynamics. The paper allows stochastic transitions (lines 68, 284-285), and the authors did not answer this point. xMat also noted that Figure 1 omits the log loss and that the baselines' decline with more data is unexplained. Reviewer_w9mr saw that Eq. 4 still needs an inner RL step; the authors agreed "loosely". Reviewer_8Tiy (2 hours) "enjoyed reading this paper" and caught two real slips: p0(s) in Eq. 5, and "exponential in size of action space" (lines 172-173). Reviewer_iosT raised the score from 4 to 5 after detailed answers on notation, but still called the theory "minor". iosT asked how Double Oracle performs, but Table 2 already reports its cost. The AC called the paper "correct". Three of four reviewers say they did not check the math carefully. The AC did not mention the missing MaxEnt comparison.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 5, 5, 6; mean 5.0. Reviewer_4tjM gave a principled 4. Both of 4tjM's math slips hold: line 251 drops 1/λ, and Algorithm 1 cites an undefined f. Reviewer_KGfa (confidence 2) asked how Theorem 2 differs from Syed et al.'s LP method. The paper cites that work only for the occupancy-to-policy mapping (line 238). KGfa also challenged lines 363-369, which say the baselines' decline "matches theory". The paper gives no theory. The authors gave KGfa and A's xMat two different explanations. Reviewer_4wnP wrote a generic review. Its only concerns, scalability and simple domains, are limits that Section 7 already states. Reviewer_rEEu spent 12 hours, three times any other reviewer, and called the reformulation "a significant contribution". The AC's "consensus" ignores rEEu's 6. No B score moved. The rebuttal promised baselines, gave no results, and called the MaxEnt comparison "out of the scope".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A questioned Eq. 2 under stochastic dynamics (xMat) and the inner RL step in Eq. 4 (w9mr). Only A asked for modern distribution-matching baselines and flagged the name clash with disagreement-regularized imitation learning (8Tiy). Only B found the missing 1/λ and the undefined f (4tjM). Only B asked about Syed et al., BROIL and Bayesian IRL (KGfa). Only B noted that Chen et al. 2016 already accepts general losses, which the authors conceded (rEEu). Both raised the tabular scope, the old baselines, the missing MaxEnt comparison, unclear notation in Eq. 1 and Lemma 1, and the unexplained decline in Figure 3.
