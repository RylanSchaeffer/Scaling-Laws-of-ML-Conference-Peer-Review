**Verdict.** The committees split on the premise of the paper, not on an error. Both found the paper hard to read. Both heard the same defence: the average treatment effect (ATE) is a level-2 quantity, equal under every mechanism, so one may pick the mechanism with the lowest variance. Committee A took this as a clarification, and three reviewers raised their scores to 7. In B, Reviewer_ybLo (3) called the choice an unfalsifiable assumption made for convenience. B's area chair (AC) turned the defence against the authors: ATE estimation needs no counterfactuals. A's support is thin. Its confidences are 3, 3, 2 and 2, two reviewers said the core was beyond their expertise, and no rating is above 7. A's meta-review repeats the authors' rebuttal and gives no reason for a spotlight. The paper supports B. The "CRN" panels of Fig. 3 show Gadget 2's variance gains with shared noise alone, without counterfactual inference. In Table 1, the plain inverse CDF equals the LP optimum and beats Gadget 2 for every monotone reward (0.58 vs 2.12). The gadgets beat it only for the non-monotone reward. There, an inverse CDF over outcomes sorted by reward would reach the optimum (0.09) and beat both gadgets. The paper's own lines 272-277 imply this, and I confirmed it numerically. Neither committee noticed.

**Committee A (spotlight).** Ratings 7, 7, 7, 5. Reviewer_g3MG and Reviewer_4cdP went from 6 to 7. The authors had restated the problem formally and given parameter settings that reduce the gadgets to Gumbel-max. Reviewer_cSRr raised to 7 after the ATE answer and a running example. All three wanted a clearer problem statement and accepted the promised rewrite. Reviewer_eGhr gave 5 after one hour, in four short paragraphs that praise the theory and ask for a comparison with Pearl's methods. eGhr never replied. The AC wrote that the replies "cleared up these concerns". Four of its six sentences repeat the authors' reply to g3MG almost word for word.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 3, 5, 5. Reviewer_ybLo's objection is principled, and the authors conceded that the choice "cannot be falsified". But ybLo spent 1.5 hours and summarised the goal as a maximal coupling, which misreads Eq. 5. Reviewer_gci3 made the same misreading, then raised specific points. Corollary 2's bound, 2α, is vacuous for α ≥ 0.5; gci3 wrote 1.6 for α = 0.6, but it is 1.2. The symbol z (line 167) is defined only implicitly, and TruncGumbel not at all. The rebuttal answered neither point. Reviewer_XBKm (confidence 2) did "not quite get the details" and later added only that the writing "needs much improvement". No reviewer replied in the discussion. The AC added its own view: monotonicity is "much easier to defend".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B challenged the premise: ybLo on falsifiability, the AC on the ATE. Only B raised Corollary 2, the undefined notation and the missing Lu et al. (2020). Only A noted three gaps, and the submission confirms all three. It claims the gadgets contain Gumbel-max but never shows it (4cdP). It never defines "CRN" (4cdP). It never cites Fig. 2 (eGhr). Both committees asked about latent confounders and got different answers. A heard "the same set of cases" as Oberst & Sontag; B heard "Markovian models". The camera-ready moves toward B. It calls g and D "a-priori choices", like monotonicity, and assumes "no latent confounders". It also drops the submitted claim that Gadget 2 beats the inverse CDF.
