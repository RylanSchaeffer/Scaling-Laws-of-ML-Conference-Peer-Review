**Verdict.** The two area chairs (ACs) weighted the same facts differently. Reviewers leaned positive in both committees: A rated 5, 8, 6, 5, 6 (mean 6.0); B rated 3, 7, 6 (mean 5.33). Both committees said that "alignment" is never measured, that the finding is intuitive, and that the theory covers only a simplified noise case. The submission concedes the first point: it uses alignment "more as a qualitative rather than quantitative measure" (lines 126-127). A's AC accepted on the reviewers' shared interest in the question. B's AC overrode two positive reviewers "Based on my reading of the paper" and sided with the lone 3. B's AC added one point that no A reviewer made: the probe is fit on clean labels, so its gain over the trained model reflects those labels. Table 1 supports this. Under 80% flipped noise on CIFAR-100, DivideMix scores 0.2% and its probe 76.1% (ResNet). That noise shifts each label to the next class, and a clean linear probe undoes the shift. No fatal error separates the committees.

**Committee A (poster).** Reviewer_AauR's 8 is the outlier: confidence 2, 4 hours, no discussion. AauR calls the core claim "a very intuitive statement" and still rates it a clear accept. Reviewer_mMWd's 6 credits "a practical framework to compute the alignment", which the paper disclaims. Reviewer_8N9C (confidence 5) found the main theorem "a bit weak" and the writing "not very logical", yet rated 6. 8N9C edited the review on 2021-09-24 and posted nothing; the record does not show what changed. Reviewer_RRS5 offered to raise a 5 and never replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_NWJ7 rejected the rebuttal, and the authors answered by citing the reviewer guidelines. The AC took RRS5's "potentially very useful" and dropped its condition: "if the paper can provide more clear theoretical and empirical justifications". The AC also called NWJ7's objections theoretical, but they were mostly empirical. The AC did not address 8N9C.

**Committee B (reject).** Reviewer_Qmfd's 3 is principled but silent after the rebuttal. Its line citations hold: lines 126-127, and the "human perception system" argument for CNN alignment (lines 262-265). It overstates one point. Figure 6 does measure the sample complexity of MLPs and CNNs on CIFAR-Easy, and Qmfd never answered the rebuttal that pointed to it. Reviewer_G3rQ's 7 is careful, but after the rebuttal G3rQ still wrote that the decomposition behind alignment lacks "strong experimental evidence". Reviewer_oX51 (6) wrote "I will stick with my score". The AC's first reason restates Qmfd, and lines 285-287 confirm it: the paper uses predictive power "to test alignment" but argues only the other direction.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A found the missing clean-set size condition in Theorem 2 (RRS5). Only A found the erratic DivideMix entries in Table 2: 10.00% at 50% uniform noise and 99.99% at 80% (8N9C). Only A contested novelty against Zhang et al. (NWJ7). Only B questioned the clean labels in the probe (AC, oX51) and the decomposition in the alignment definition (G3rQ). A raised more specific flaws than B and still accepted. AC weighting decided the outcome, not new evidence.
