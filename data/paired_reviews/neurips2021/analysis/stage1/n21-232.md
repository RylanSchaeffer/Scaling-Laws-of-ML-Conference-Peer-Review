**Verdict.** Both committees recommended a poster, from the same final ratings: 7, 7, 7 and 6. Both got there through the rebuttal. Before it, each had borderline critics: Reviewer_anCt in A, and Reviewer_SBkb (below the threshold) and Reviewer_SrTJ in B. The authors answered both forums with new experiments, and all three critics raised their scores. The committees raised mostly different concerns, and none was decisive. The checkable ones were either correct and answered, or misreadings that did not survive the rebuttal. No reviewer rated above 7, so neither committee had a case for a spotlight. The committees did not weigh the same facts differently. They saw different facts and reached the same level.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 7, 6; confidences 3, 4, 3, 2. Reviewer_anCt (8 hours) wrote the most critical review. It disputed that the accuracy drop is "surprising" (lines 28-29) and said the main text ignores calibration. That is true: Figures 2, 3 and 5 plot accuracy only. anCt also read appendix Figure 6 as showing no log-likelihood or ECE gain for EmpCov, and later retracted that. anCt's point that lines 326-328 hold only for regression is correct, and the authors conceded it. anCt raised to 7 ("likely too harsh") but found the NNGP reply off target. Reviewer_YvmA's 7 is competent; its note that Propositions 2-3 need Gaussian priors is correct. Reviewer_uotf's 7 is thin: praise, a question about MNIST that the CIFAR-10 results already answer, and no reply. Reviewer_2XLh's 6 at confidence 2 names no weakness. The chair wrote four generic sentences that lean on "the most active reviewers ... championing this manuscript". The chair placed the paper in "model evaluation", which misdescribes it.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 7, 7; confidences 4, 4, 3, 3. Reviewer_SBkb (3 hours) wrote the most careful review in either committee, and its checkable points hold. The BMA uses 90 HMC samples (lines 101-103). Lines 310-313 cite an EmpCov result on the MNIST CNN that the submission does not show; the authors "forgot to include" it. SBkb was wrong that Section 4 adds only MNIST and smaller networks over Izmailov et al. It also tests their cold-posterior conjecture, a Laplace prior and domain shift. A sample-convergence table and the missing numbers moved SBkb to 6. Reviewer_SrTJ asked why one would predict on OOD data at all, and doubted that small prior draws matter. Figure 4(b) answers the second doubt directly. SrTJ raised to 7. Reviewer_rUts gave a solid 7, and its Figure 4(a) observation is correct. Reviewer_8WYY reports 15 hours but twice calls the EmpCov prior an "initialization method", and did not reply. The chair's first paragraph copies rUts's summary almost word for word, and the second reuses rUts's main review. The third lists the concerns of SBkb and SrTJ as resolved.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised these:

- framing on accuracy instead of calibration (anCt);
- the linear-model claim and GP classification (anCt);
- Gaussian-only propositions and heavy-tailed priors (YvmA);
- misspecification (2XLh) and the cost of the covariance (uotf).

Only B raised these:

- Monte Carlo error and the missing MNIST-CNN figure (SBkb);
- novelty over Izmailov et al. 2021 (SBkb, SrTJ);
- alternative explanations and the term "covariate shift" (SBkb);
- prediction versus detection on OOD inputs (SrTJ);
- full-rank data, small data and over-parameterization (rUts).

Both committees asked about variational inference, and the authors sent both forums the same MFVI analysis. Both noted the small scale and accepted the mechanism as a partial explanation.
