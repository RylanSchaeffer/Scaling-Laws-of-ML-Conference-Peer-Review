**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same weaknesses and weighted them differently. Reviewers on both sides called the NTK-MMD link new. Both sides also found that the power theorems are generic kernel-MMD results, and that the experiments show no gain over a median-bandwidth Gaussian MMD. A treated these facts as decisive (3, 4, 4, 7). B treated them as fixable (7, 6, 6, 7), and its AC wrote "could be improved, but generally acceptable". The rebuttal widened the gap. In B, three reviewers raised their scores after the authors explained the role of the NTK and added baselines. None of them said what the new results showed. In A, Reviewer_Kznb read the new tables, found no gain over the linear-time ME test, and kept a 3. The camera-ready Table 1 agrees with Kznb: NTK-MMD reaches 7-18% power where ME reaches about 10-75%. The record holds no author responses or discussion for either committee.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 3, 4, 4, 7, all at confidence 4. Reviewer_Kznb (24 hours) wrote the most specific review, and most of its checkable points hold. MNIST has no baseline. No linear-time test is compared. Theorem 3.2 analyses a concentration threshold, but the experiments use a permutation threshold. One point is wrong: Theorem 3.3 covers the full bootstrap. Reviewer_e8cj (1 hour) rejected on motivation and asked for citations that lines 20-28 already give. Reviewer_aee6 asked why the NTK matters when the bounds hold for any bounded kernel; lines 172-174 confirm that they do. The only champion, Reviewer_tRT5, wrote a short review. It says the method "solves the computational and memory difficulties", which the paper never measures, and it does not discuss the experiments. The AC's meta-review opens with e8cj's summary and says "the reviewers agree", but tRT5's 7 was never edited.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 6, 7. Three reviewers chose confidence 3, which states that they did not check the maths carefully. Reviewer_QV5c wrote the most thorough review. It found that the experiments "do not completely manage to show the advantages" and that the power results are not specific to the NTK. It still judged the progress significant. Reviewer_jdVg's summary and first two pros copy the abstract and the contribution list. Its major objection was weak baselines, and it moved to accept after the rebuttal without saying what the new results showed. Reviewer_AatE (3 hours) wrote that the experiments use ReLU; line 274 says soft-plus. It raised its score after the rebuttal explained how the NTK links the trained network to the exact kernel MMD. Reviewer_VLkd gave 7 although its own summary reports "similar test power" to the two baselines. The AC accepted on the novelty of the connection.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A questioned the motivation for network-based tests and for the Eq. (3) loss (e8cj, aee6). The paper agrees that this loss diverges under long training (lines 92-93). Only A challenged the validity of the test (Kznb). The theoretical threshold needs constants that a user does not know. Theorem 3.1 assumes, and does not prove, that the NTK separates the two distributions. The change-point study plots statistics without a threshold. All three points hold. Only A raised unequal sample sizes (tRT5). Only B asked for the HDGM benchmark with C2ST and optimised-kernel baselines (jdVg), and for a measured speed or memory gain (QV5c). The submitted paper reports no runtime or memory result. Only B asked about ReLU smoothness, batch size 1 and the biased estimator (AatE, QV5c).
