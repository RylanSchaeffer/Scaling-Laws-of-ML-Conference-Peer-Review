**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reason. Every reviewer valued the core point: few-shot benchmarks test out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, while much meta-learning theory assumes in-distribution (ID) tasks. Both committees also saw the same weaknesses. The gaps between PN, Ridge and SVM are 1 to 2 points, the model-selection study has no take-away, and the paper is crowded. Committee B weighed them more and scrutinised more (3,543 review words against 2,184). Its two most detailed reviewers stayed at 5. B's mean of 6.0 rests on a thin 8; without it the mean is 5.3. B's AC broke the split by reading the paper. Committee A's only detractor had misread in-distribution as in-domain and moved to 6 once corrected. No reviewer found a real error. Both committees got the same rebuttal on effect sizes, and nobody disputed it. Its new result is genuine: with 50 Zappos-ID training tasks, PN beats SVM by 3.6 points. But it also claims that SVM beats PN OOD by at least 1 point on all four modified benchmarks. Figure 1(d) shows PN ahead on cifar-M (about 83.7 against 82.3). Nobody checked.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 6, 7, 6). Reviewer_QWos (8-10 hours) asked whether per-task variance explains the flips, but each estimate already averages 20,000 tasks (l.384-386). Reviewer_hGy7 began on the reject side. hGy7 called the Table 1 gaps insignificant given the confidence intervals. That holds only on FEMNIST; elsewhere gaps up to 1.5 points exceed intervals of about ±0.15. hGy7 also read the BaseGen selection rule as "lowest loss on the meta-train set". After the in-domain correction, hGy7 apologised and moved to 6. The review text still says the claims are not "sufficiently backed". Reviewer_ficQ's 7 is generic. Reviewer_Scao's 6 calls the work "purely theoretical" and rebuts a claim the paper does not make, that the OOD protocol is invalid. Scao never replied. A's AC cites unanimity and names no concern.

Committee B (5, 5, 8, 6). Reviewer_Qp31 (1,714 words, two follow-ups) accepted the 50-task result but still called the paper "borderline". Qp31's reasons: model selection has no take-away, small-test-set unreliability is known, and the OOD flips rest on three models. Reviewer_mSBf misread Figure 1(d) and took the ranking claim as an accuracy claim. After the corrections, mSBf kept 5 and asked for more datasets. Reviewer_rpPm moved to 6 but found the presentation "below bar". Reviewer_qFSe gave 8 after two hours in 263 words. qFSe praised "strong supporting experiments" and said the best ID method "can in fact be the worst" OOD; FO-MAML is lower. qFSe never replied. The AC listed the critics' objections, read the paper, found that it "raises interesting questions", and added that no reviewer was "indifferent". Any contested paper passes that second test.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A asked why PN flips and FO-MAML does not (QWos), for a Task2Vec measure of the task gap (hGy7), and for non-vision tasks and more theory (ficQ). Only B raised these:

- Table 1 compares ID and OOD ranks across different datasets (mSBf). The one same-source pair, Zappos-ID against Zappos-OOD (Figure 3(c)), ranks the methods in the same order. The authors conceded.
- BaseGen on mini tests no new classes and may exaggerate the gap (mSBf, Qp31).
- Zappos-OOD is a different problem, not just another benchmark (Qp31).
- The proof's assumption on the number of training tasks is unrealistic (Qp31). This is wrong: l.135-141 need only N = Ω(√|C_L|), with N > 10^5.
- Hyperparameter search and more methods and datasets (rpPm, mSBf).

The first point is real and weakens the headline claim. None changed a decision.
