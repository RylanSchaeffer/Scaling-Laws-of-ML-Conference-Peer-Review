**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same paper, the same gaps and the same rebuttal experiment. Their area chairs (ACs) used the scores differently. A's AC accepted on three 7s: "all marks match to a clear accept, that I will follow". B's AC rejected on 6, 7, 6, 6. Three B reviewers had raised their ratings after the rebuttal, and no reviewer argued for rejection in the discussion. B's AC gave three reasons: the work is "somewhat incremental" over [19, 27, 29, 44], the setting and assumptions are poorly motivated, and its significance is unclear without experiments. Only Reviewer_WFKv called the work incremental, and named [29] as the base; the paper generalises [19] (line 66). Both forums got the same Algorithm 1 experiment, and B also got a Jennrich run; B's meta-review mentions neither. No reviewer found an error in a theorem or proof, and all seven say they did not check the mathematics carefully. A's 7s are weak evidence: two came from reviewers outside their area, at confidence 1 and 2, after 2 hours each. B's softer critiques have a basis in the paper. It cites applications of passive mixture models only, not of the active-query setting it studies (lines 34-36, 50-52). Its MLR bounds are at least cubic in k, while Reviewer_8hHY notes that [29] is linear in k for ℓ = 2. These are the same facts weighted differently. B's AC gave them more weight than B's own reviewers did.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 7; confidences 1, 3, 2. The two outsiders are Reviewer_kx4J and Reviewer_MfJp; MfJp's evaluation is one sentence. Both misread the text. Corollary 1, not Theorem 4, carries the quasi-polynomial claim. Definitions 1, 2 and 4 already remove duplicate supports. Reviewer_a6qx (10 hours) did the only close reading. a6qx found a real error in the intuition paragraph (lines 194-198), which the authors conceded; the proof of Theorem 1 is correct. a6qx moved to 7 after the correction, the cover-free-family answer and the experiment, and wrote "I agree with the evaluation of the other reviewers". The AC followed the scores and asked for the experiments in an appendix.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 7, 6, 6; confidences 3, 3, 3, 2. Reviewer_8hHY (6 hours) wrote B's most detailed review. 8hHY asked why a passive Gaussian design would not do, asked for Diakonikolas and Kane (2021), which the paper does not cite, and questioned flip-independence. 8hHY raised to 7 but still doubted the MLR results. Reviewer_riqJ kept 6, found real typos (Eq. 1 minimises over ℓ) and questioned applications. WFKv and Reviewer_LAr2 raised to 6, citing the other reviews and the responses. Their final texts still say the paper "does not meet the standards for NeurIPS publication" and "seems incomplete". LAr2 (1 hour) criticised a "low CP-rank assumption" that the paper does not make. The AC adopted the critiques and set aside the ratings. The meta-review's key sentence is garbled: "It is currently clear if this results are of significance interest".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A found the error at lines 194-198. Only A asked whether the cover-free families can be built, and which results need distinct supports. Only B asked what the active-query setting is for. Only B compared the MLR bounds with passive designs and [29], and asked for Diakonikolas and Kane. Only B called the work incremental. Both noted the missing experiments. A's AC wanted them in an appendix; B's AC counted their absence against the paper. Both asked about lower bounds; the authors told B the question is open and never answered A.
