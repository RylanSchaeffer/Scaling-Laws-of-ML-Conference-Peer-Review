**Verdict.** Committee A rejected because Reviewer_W1WL showed that the main result is a corollary of MMR19; Committee B never tested this. Theorem 4.1 says that a JL map to O(log n) dimensions preserves the cost of every barycenter solution with n support points. W1WL wrote the barycenter problem as an n-clustering of the pooled input points, with extra coupling constraints. The theorem of Makarychev, Makarychev and Razenshteyn [MMR19] preserves the cost of every n-clustering, so it also preserves every constrained one. The submission supports this. Definition 2.2 defines the cost as a weighted clustering cost with optimal centres, and line 174 calls each term "just a weighted k-means problem". The authors first called the two problems "fundamentally different". Then they conceded and promised to add "the elegant connection". The camera-ready deletes the paragraph (submission lines 261-278) that claimed four new difficulties beyond MMR. A's area chair (AC) made this point decisive against ratings of 7 and 6. In B, all four reviewers took the dimensionality reduction as the main strength ("quite strong", "impressive"). They criticised only experiments and presentation, and the AC followed the scores. The decisive fact reached only A. The program chairs accepted on B's recommendation.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 7, 6, 3; mean 5.33. W1WL's 3 is the outlier, and its basis is principled: a specific reduction, written out in full and conceded by the authors. W1WL called the coresets, the hardness result and the lower bounds "interesting". W1WL kept 3 because the paper presents a corollary as its main contribution. Reviewer_gFL6 (7) asked why MNIST runs at 73% of the full-dimension time, against 9% for FACES. gFL6 asked twice for the MNIST time breakdown. The authors gave a sparsity guess, then FACES numbers, and the AC noticed. Reviewer_e4YX (6) wrote a short review: the coreset follows a standard route, and the experiments need graphs and variance. Neither positive reviewer addressed MMR, so their ratings do not answer W1WL. No rating moved after the rebuttal.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 7, 6; mean 6.75. Reviewer_y9H3 wrote the most careful review, and its checks hold. The coreset experiment uses single point masses with p = 2 (lines 359-362), so the barycenter is the Euclidean mean. y9H3 kept 7 for the theory. Reviewer_1Zyc (6, confidence 4) noted that the paper "builds upon" MMR19 but did not ask what is new. 1Zyc's concern was the thin experiments. Reviewer_GF2E misread Theorem 4.3 as a Gaussian-only guarantee on the barycenter and admitted it. GF2E then raised the rating on a promised comparison with Huang et al. The camera-ready main text has no such comparison. Reviewer_SYrR (7) read closely (the "corset" typo, Figure 3 is a table) but judged only the presentation. The AC set the weak experiments aside and followed the scores.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A argued that Theorem 4.1 is a corollary of MMR19. Only A questioned the assumption that each input has poly(n) support. Only A asked for a per-step time breakdown and for coreset error against runtime. Only B found that the coreset experiment never involves Wasserstein geometry. Only B questioned coresets that need an approximate barycenter first. Only B asked for comparisons with projection-robust barycenters and Subspace Detours. Both committees found the experiments thin and the MNIST speed-up small, and neither treated that as fatal. The paper's own account of its novelty over MMR (lines 251-278) satisfied B. A tested that account, and it did not hold.
