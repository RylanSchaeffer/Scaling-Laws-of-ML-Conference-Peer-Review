**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same paper and the same weaknesses. The theory needs full actuation, which the pendulum experiment itself violates. The evaluation is one 2-D pendulum with one ablation baseline. Theorems 5 and 6 need the true context c^(i) after each episode. The committees split on one judgment: is a clean but easy assembly of known tools enough? In B, Reviewer_xzK3 knew both precursors, Boffi et al. [12] and Agarwal et al. [24]. It showed at proof level that the theory is "a simple extension" of them. The paper supports this. Theorem 5 states the same bound as Theorem 3. With c^(i) observed, its proof needs only the regret split of Lemma 2. B's area chair (AC) engaged and adopted xzK3's view. A's reviewers saw the same fact and did not hold it against the paper. ZMnJ called it "a straightforward application" of online convex optimization. UjXF counted it as a contribution, with "clean and intuitive theorem statements and proofs". A's AC accepted on the scores and on promised changes. The facts were shared; only their weight differed.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 7. Reviewer_UjXF wrote the strongest review. It found that the pendulum is not fully actuated and that e-ISS is not "standard". It also found that the experiment never resets the state, although Lemma 1 assumes x_1 = 0. It asked whether N > T holds. The authors' answer (delta = 0.01 s, so T = 200 steps against N = 30) shows that it does not. Corollary 4 states OMAC's advantage for N > T. Nobody followed up. Reviewer_ZMnJ found that the experimental baseline adapts only c-hat, while Corollary 4's baseline adapts both parameters (lines 182, 253-254). It said the promised experiments would "sufficiently improve the paper"; only the drone table came with numbers. Its 6 most likely dates from its edit that day. Reviewer_H6i6 spent two hours. Its summary says the paper proves convergence for every scheme, but no guarantee covers the neural network. It then lowered its score "to be more in line" with the others. Reviewer_BB7T praised the theory without detail, listed only experimental weaknesses, and never posted. The four-sentence meta-review calls the approach "sufficiently evaluated", which ZMnJ and BB7T had questioned.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 6, 6, 4, mean 5.0. Reviewer_xzK3 spent two hours, but its case checks out. Theorem 6 adds only a ridge-regression concentration step. The bound converges to an upper bound on the omniscient error, not to that error; the authors conceded this. xzK3 first declined to credit experiments that were not in the paper. When the AC pointed to the drone table, it kept 4 for its theory concerns. Reviewer_vw5a (confidence 3, "not an expert" in meta-learning) said a greedy pseudo-inverse controller does not advance adaptive control. That is an objection to scope, not an error in the paper. On novelty, vw5a deferred to xzK3. Reviewer_ic62 rose from 5 to 6 on the "first end-to-end" argument, but its review still calls the analysis "a rather simple application". Reviewer_7eB6 kept 6, unconvinced by the matched-uncertainty extension. The AC named xzK3 as the source of "the main weakness".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the baseline mismatch with Corollary 4, the reset and N > T questions, and a supervised-learning ablation. Only B made novelty over [12] and [24] a reason to reject. Only B raised the gap between the bound and the omniscient controller, the environment-independent B(x), and certainty-equivalence control as such. Both raised full actuation, the thin experiments, the observed-context assumption, and realizability.
