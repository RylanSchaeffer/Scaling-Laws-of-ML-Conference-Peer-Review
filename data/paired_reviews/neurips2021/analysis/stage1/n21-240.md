**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Each saw a clean, correct theory paper that improves the best known efficient bound for tracking experts with memory, by a small amount, on a niche problem. Each committee had one early detractor whose main objection was a misreading. In A, Reviewer_i3gM (4) said Figure 1 compares a tuned bound with an untuned [26]. Bound (9) is [26]'s tuned bound, and i3gM conceded. In B, Reviewer_gqjS ("do not accept", confidence 5) built a Theorem 3 counterexample on θ = O(k/T). Theorem 3 sets θ = (k−m+1)/((m−1)(T−2)), and gqjS admitted "my oversight". Both rose: i3gM to 6, gqjS to 7. No reviewer went above 7, and both ACs named the narrow scope and the small gain. The committees weighted the same facts the same way. A's mean of 6.7 overstates its evidence: Reviewer_ETi1's 7 rests on two short paragraphs, and its only question ("who is H") is answered at line 49.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only B raised these points, and the checkable ones hold:

- The Fixed-Share claim (line 134, "under mild assumptions on n") needs a large n. At Figure 1's T = 4000 and k = 40, the bounds favour PoDS-θ for all m in [2, k] only if n exceeds about 424,000. The figure uses 500,000; the margin at m = 40 is 0.2c (gqjS; conceded; the camera-ready says "for sufficiently large n").
- The need to know T, k and m in advance (dt3a, AJRz, gqjS, B meta-review). A raised tuning only in the withdrawn Figure 1 complaint.
- No experiments (dt3a, AJRz, t33Y).
- Theorem 4 compares one step from equal weights, so it gives no guarantee over a run. Its strict inequality fails when v = w (gqjS; conceded).
- Missing information-theory work on switching sources (gqjS; added in the camera-ready).

Only A questioned the novelty of Algorithm 3 over [23] and the link of Algorithms 1&2 to [26] (i3gM). Both committees called the gain small when m ≪ k. At Figure 1's settings with m = 2, the bound drops from 474c to 470c. No one-sided issue changed a decision.
