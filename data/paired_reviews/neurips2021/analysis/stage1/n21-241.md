**Verdict.** The split comes from how the committees scored the same facts, not from different facts. Both found narrow experiments (one ResNet-50/CIFAR-10 setting, one 1-bit compressor, no wall-clock data), over-claims ("the best compression method", a "faster rate"), a gain only at small α, and errors in the theory and the citations. In each committee the two critics raised their scores after a rebuttal made mostly of promises. A's critics stopped at 5. B's critics stopped at 6, and Reviewer_7b4p said its 6 was not a recommendation to accept. B's margin came from Reviewer_56o9 and Reviewer_HwbF, two 7s at confidence 3 with the math unchecked. A's only 6 came from a one-hour review. A's area chair (AC) rejected because the experiments show no practical value and no one showed why ECX needs two error buffers when single-buffer error feedback on the momentum exists. B's AC repeated 56o9's praise and left every weakness to the camera-ready. The forum version differs from the submission only in author names, header, checklist and acknowledgements, so none of the promised fixes appeared.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 6, 5. Reviewer_UoCD (14 hours) made the strongest checkable points, and the submission confirms them. Corollary 6 sets α_t = 8L²γ²/n, below the 8(L²+L_F²)γ²/n that Theorem 5 requires (lines 199-203). Assumption 2 bounds the compression error by an absolute ε (line 180). Common compressors meet such a bound only for bounded inputs, and Assumption 1 bounds the variance, not the gradient norm. Table 1 multiplies STORM's A_t by 1/α_t, so the bound sets aside how the error scales exactly where ECX claims its gain. UoCD overstated one point: the T^(1/3) batch in Corollary 6 is only the initial batch B_0. UoCD and Reviewer_Qn5b asked how ECX compares with error feedback on the momentum, the method of Zheng et al. (2019a). The paper defines Single Compensation on v_t (lines 107-110) but analyses it on A (lines 117-122). The authors said they had "tried" it and gave no data. Reviewer_2AUC wanted wall-clock evidence that the extra model-size buffers pay off. Reviewer_dVax got a one-line reply. Qn5b's 6 came after one hour; it calls the theory "sound and convincing" but cannot follow Eq. (3).

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 7, 7. Reviewer_7b4p (14 hours) was as critical as UoCD. It found ε in the denominator of Corollary 4 (line 196), unsupported "best" and "faster rate" claims (lines 32-38), and an IGT rate worse than SGD's (lines 217-218). It asked for an ablation of the low-pass filter against the second error term. After the rebuttal it moved to 6. Reviewer_LLeQ moved to 6 "in the hopes that exposition and notation will be streamlined". Reviewer_56o9 wrote five sentences, almost all praise, and never replied. Its claim that the work is "well-motivated with respect to previous work" conflicts with the mis-cited STORM paper and the conceded over-claims. HwbF listed the missing timing data and the single compressor, the gaps A held against the paper, and still gave 7. The meta-review copies 56o9's praise for "balancing quantitative results and qualitative explanations".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A questioned the strength of Assumption 2, the Corollary 6 condition, the T-dependent step sizes and the ∇f in the variance bound (UoCD). Only A asked for a comparison with error feedback on the momentum buffer (UoCD, Qn5b, AC), and only A raised Adam and PowerSGD. Only B found the ε error in Corollary 4, the weak IGT result and the missing low-pass-filter ablation (7b4p). The other weaknesses were common to both. A scored them at 5 and B at 6.
