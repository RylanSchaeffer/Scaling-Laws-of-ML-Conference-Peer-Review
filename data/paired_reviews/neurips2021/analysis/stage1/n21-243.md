**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same weaknesses and weighted them differently. Both area chairs (ACs) named the same main weakness: Assumptions 4.3 and 4.4 are restrictive or not justified. A's AC rejected on it. B's AC accepted on "generality and unification", although it called the assumptions "fairly restrictive" and the work mainly a generalization of prior ideas. The rebuttal moved scores only in B. There, it removed Reviewer_vCrF's claim that the paper implies P = NP, and vCrF and Reviewer_YN8z raised their scores to 6 and 7, for a mean of 6.2. In A, no reviewer replied to the rebuttal. A's mean of 5.75 depended on a one-hour 8 from Reviewer_VttL; without it, the mean is 5.0. A's Reviewer_HdRs also found defects that the paper confirms. No issue is fatal. The camera-ready fixed Algorithm 1 and kept the assumptions.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 8, 4, all at confidence 3. HdRs (6, 6 hours) is right on three points. Algorithm 1 (page 4) never updates W, and at the first step every candidate scores S(∅). Section 3.1 shows only Chen et al. [5] as a special case, but line 50 claims "several". Lines 237-238 say Assumption 4.3 "will hold in most settings" with no support. Reviewer_3sfT (5) asked why 4.3 and 4.4 should hold. Reviewer_m1Q2 (4) is right that the main experiments stop at d = 5 (lines 342-343) and give no running times. It is wrong that the paper gives no conditions under which the algorithm is optimal: Corollary 3.2 gives one, and Theorem 4.6 gives exact recovery. VttL's 8 restates the abstract. The AC cites a reviewer view that is not in the public record: Assumption 4.3 is stronger than causal minimality. That view holds outside linear models. For φ(x) = x², 4.3 needs every parent to shift the conditional mean. A parent that changes only the noise scale satisfies minimality and violates 4.3.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 5, 6, 6, 7, 7. vCrF first argued that the paper proves P = NP. Algorithm 1's output line does read "arg min S(W)", but lines 116-117 say "We do not prove that this algorithm exactly solves (1)". After the authors cited that line, vCrF raised its score to 6. YN8z (9 hours) raised its score to 7 because the rebuttal "addressed some of my concerns". Reviewer_tJvN (7) found the same missing W update as HdRs and noted that Lemma 4.2 is known. Reviewer_xMq4 (5) called the results "not surprising" next to Chen et al. Reviewer_d421 gave 6 at confidence 2 with five lines of praise and called the assumptions "mild". No B reviewer called the assumptions restrictive. The AC raised them itself and accepted anyway.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A found the first-step degeneracy of Algorithm 1, the overstated "several special cases" claim, the unsupported "most settings" sentence and the thin experiments (d = 5, synthetic data, no timings, no exact-recovery curves). Only A asked why 4.3 and 4.4 should hold (3sfT) and how 4.3 compares with faithfulness (HdRs). The rebuttal said 4.3 implies minimality in linear models, which concedes that 4.3 is at least as strong. The camera-ready leaves the comparison as future work. Only B raised the P = NP reading, the unknown gap Δ that sets the pruning threshold (xMq4, tJvN), and complexity comparisons with the special cases (YN8z). Both committees found the missing W update and the closeness to Chen et al.
