**Verdict**

Both committees read the paper the same way. The framing is new: choose an identification formula with best-arm identification. The bandit algorithms and their analysis are standard. Theorem 1 is the one technical result, and its proof is short. A accepted because the rebuttal converted one reviewer and the AC accepted below the mean. B rejected because no reviewer championed the paper and almost none answered the rebuttal. The two ACs drew opposite conclusions from the same facts. A wrote "the techniques are not new … an important first step". B wrote that the techniques are "standard and the contribution is somewhat limited". Neither committee found a fatal flaw.

**Committees**

Committee A (5, 4, 7; poster). Reviewer_5RfN (10 h) first wrote that the work "seems to be lack of technical and algorithmic contribution". That is B's objection. It then challenged the claim that an uncentered influence function is needed for the second-order rate. The paper asserts this (lines 119-121) but proves only sufficiency. After a Taylor-expansion reply, 5RfN raised to 7. Reviewer_QgMM lists four strengths and two mild requests, yet gives 5. It never answered the rebuttal's new runs with up to 65 formulas. Reviewer_NBmz's 4 is weak evidence. It admitted it "misread the content of the paper" and "could not read the paper carefully before". It raised its score, then reverted after the discussion closed. Its last argument, that the best asymptotic variance should come from theory, is wrong: σ²_k = var_p[ψ_k] depends on the unknown distribution. The AC accepted at a mean of 5.33 and did not address NBmz.

Committee B (3, 4, 5, 5; reject). No reviewer rose above 5. Reviewer_ysqL (15 h, confidence 1) found that Algorithm 1 ignores costs and that its stopping test compares the wrong bounds. The PDF confirms both. It offered to raise its score for a simple account of significance. The authors sent the argument that moved 5RfN, and ysqL never replied. Reviewer_t43v found real typos in Theorems 2-3 and Corollary 2 and asked for lower bounds. Reviewer_bePw called the setting "just an application of BAI" and kept 4 after the rebuttal. It asked for a main-text citation of Chernozhukov et al., which is already there as reference [2] (lines 45, 117). Reviewer_sw9W (2 h, confidence 4) gave 3. It was right that λ_x is undefined and that the PAC event below line 132 is deterministic. It was wrong that the formulas at lines 81-82 need a do-operator; they are the standard backdoor and frontdoor formulas. It never answered the rebuttal. The meta-review summarises the scores and does not mention the rebuttal.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the inefficiency of sample splitting, scaling beyond 9 formulas, the unproven necessity of the uncentered influence function, and the lack of baselines other than uniform sampling. Only B found the main-text errors: Algorithm 1's costs and stopping test, the undefined λ_x, the deterministic PAC event, the ν_θ and Corollary 2 typos, and "Casual" in the title. These errors are real but easy to fix, and the camera-ready fixes them. Only B's reviewers raised the missing causal-bandit work, though A's AC asked for it too. Both committees raised standard techniques, no optimality result or lower bound, weak motivation, adaptive-experiment work by Kato and co-authors, and best-arm versus regret framing. A counted these as limits of a first step; B counted them as reasons to reject. Claims were checked against paper_submitted.pdf.
