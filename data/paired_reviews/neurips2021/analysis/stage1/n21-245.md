**Verdict**

The committees weighed the same modest results differently, and only B's reviewers engaged with the rebuttal. The submitted Fig. 3 supports both readings. r2v-config leads on three of six link-prediction graphs and, by small margins, in community detection. Glove with its bias term matches or beats it on Airport, AstroPh and most of HepTh. A's AC called this a "lack of clear superiority"; B's reviewers called it as good or better. A also judged the paper against degree-debiasing work that the submission ignored. The submission had no related-work section and cited none of the methods that HWNH and q94Z named. B never raised that literature and valued the insight that SGNS cancels degree bias. Both committees got long replies with new baselines on 11 August. No A reviewer answered. In B, RJD5 went from 4 to 6 and yRN6 rose to 7. On the record, B's mean went from at most 6.0 to 6.75, while A's stayed at 4.75. B found more concrete faults than A, and the fixes weakened two results, but B credited the fixes. B's spotlight rests on one 8 from a reviewer who left originality to the others. Neither committee found a fatal flaw.

**Committees**

Committee A (4, 5, 4, 6; reject). Reviewer_HWNH (9 h, confidence 5) said approximate personalized PageRank already penalises hubs, and Lasagne uses it for node embeddings. The omission is real. "Solved" is a judgment, because that work regularises a diffusion and does not change the SGNS null model. Five of HWNH's eight references share one author. Reviewer_q94Z named Fairwalk, role2vec, DEMO-Net and GAT, and said r2v loses to Glove on 3/6 graphs. Fig. 3 confirms this for most K. Reviewer_2mkY doubted novelty without a reason and wrongly said Glove is "consistently performing better". Reviewer_MjL3's 6 is generic praise with no weakness. The authors added Fairwalk and GAT and asked reviewers not to judge by state-of-the-art scores. Nobody replied. The two-sentence meta-review lists novelty, missing comparisons and no clear superiority.

Committee B (6, 6, 8, 7; spotlight). Reviewer_RJD5 wrote the most exact review on either side. It found that Section 2.2 calls NCE "the unbiased variant of negative sampling". It found that the kNN case study lets one journal sit in both train and test. It asked how well the degree offset alone predicts links. The authors acted on all three. After the fix, node2vec beat r2v-dcSBM on impact factor (0.284 vs 0.277). The offset alone reached 96% of r2v's AUC. RJD5 still raised from 4 to 6 "based on your edits". Reviewer_yRN6 got NetMF added and rose to 7. Reviewer_eKFw (6) asked specific questions and called the paper borderline. Reviewer_yRQF's 8 is five sentences of praise from a reviewer who will "rely on other reviewers to judge the originality". The meta-review calls the concerns "mostly regarding the presentation". That understates a corrected result.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the prior debiasing literature (APPR, Lasagne, Fairwalk, role2vec, DEMO-Net, MONET), multi-label classification, GAT, the SVD cost, and why debiasing should help downstream tasks. Only B raised the NCE misstatements, the train-test leak, the offset-only baseline, the soft configuration model, NetMF, and undefined notation. Both saw hard-to-read results in Fig. 3, an unconvincing Fig. 4 projection, and thin baselines. A counted these against the paper. B accepted the authors' answer that understanding, not accuracy, is the contribution. Claims were checked against paper_submitted.pdf.
