**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. The gap opened in the discussion, not the first reviews. On the reviewers' own statements, A's mean before the rebuttal was at most 5.5 and B's at least 5.67. In A, the rebuttal moved Reviewer_nyW6 from a "weak" accept to 7 and Reviewer_Nzqf up to 5. In B, Reviewer_tEY1 read the other reviews and lowered its score to 5. Both committees knew the weak points: representation selection costs a polynomial factor in the number N of representations, the proofs follow Papini et al. (2021), the optimal action must be unique, and the paper says little about Modi et al. (MOFFLE). A judged the paper by its core result, that UNISOFT is necessary and sufficient for constant regret (Theorems 5, 8, 9). B judged it by the selection result (Theorem 10), the only place where N appears. Neither committee found an error in a key result.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 5, 7, 5, 7. Reviewer_UvSm (7, 10 hours) wrote an accurate summary and named one fair limit: every candidate representation must be a low-rank MDP (line 270). It did not reply after the rebuttal. nyW6 found a real error: the Bellman optimality equation puts the max outside the expectation (line 81). It misread the claim after Theorem 5; Bellman closure implies linear rewards, so lines 171-172 hold. The explicit bound on the critical time κ̄ satisfied it. Reviewer_rJnu (5) correctly noted that the √N factor in β_k (line 286) becomes a linear N factor in the gap-dependent bound, and the authors agreed. Nzqf's five-sentence review says the result saves log T next to a √T term. Theorems 8 and 9 have no √T term. The area chair (AC) called the paper "a natural generalization" of the bandit work but "noteworthy", judged the MOFFLE setting "quite different", and did not mention the N cost.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 6, all at confidence 4 after 7 to 10 hours. Reviewer_aaaf (5) made two points decisive: the polynomial N cost and the missing comparison with Modi et al. The first is correct, and the paper says so (lines 293-294). The second rests partly on a misreading: lines 37-38 credit Modi et al. with sample-complexity results, and aaaf read the passage as denying them. The authors said this February 2021 preprint was under review at the same conference. aaaf's select-then-run alternative needs each representation's λ+, which depends on the unknown optimal policy. tEY1 lowered its score for three reasons: the unique optimal action (correct, Assumption 4), a constant that may be exponentially large, and the N cost. Reviewer_WqxU (6) called its concerns presentation issues. Two of its points are wrong: Theorem 5 states finite states (line 162), and one-hot tabular features fail UNISOFT whenever a reachable state has two or more actions. WqxU said a novelty summary would help "a better final rating"; the authors posted one, and WqxU stayed at 6. The AC's three-sentence meta-review repeats aaaf's two points and puts the N cost "in the main results". N appears only in Section 4.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for experiments (rJnu, Nzqf) and received the authors' simulation. Only A raised the Bellman-equation error, the K-independence of κ̄ and the realizability of every candidate. Only B raised the size of the constant through λ+, the computational cost of LSVI-LEADER, the misspecified case and the finite-state restriction. None of these decided the outcome. The shared facts did: A had two reviewers at 7 who valued the core result, and B had none above 6.
