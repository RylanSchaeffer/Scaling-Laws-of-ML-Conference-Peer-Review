**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, for the same reasons. The paper extends the mean-field fluctuation (CLT) analysis from shallow to multilayer networks, and all seven reviewers called this novel or interesting. No reviewer reports checking the proofs. The submission has no experiments, and its main text has no proofs ("All proofs are deferred to the appendix", l.159). Six of seven reviewers asked for a numerical check. The authors posted one MNIST illustration in both forums: a 3-layer tanh network, 100 training images, widths 50 to 400. It decided the borderline votes. Reviewer_SSUu (A) rose from 5 to 6. Reviewer_PZpb (B) had promised to accept on such evidence, and rose to 6. Reviewer_Zo8z (A) tied its acceptance to it. After the rebuttal, six of seven ratings were 6. The only 5 (Reviewer_ErCU, confidence 1) came from a self-declared non-expert. No rating exceeded 6, so neither committee had a champion for a higher tier. Both ACs followed the scores. B's AC named the weakness ("hesitant to accept an almost purely theoretical work where no reviewer had time to go over the proofs") and accepted anyway. The agreement rests on shared taste and one rebuttal figure, not on verified proofs. The committees weighed the same facts the same way.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 6, 6). Reviewer_Zo8z (confidence 4) wrote the most technical review. It noted that Theorems 9 and 10 take N→∞ before t→∞, while training fixes N; Section 4 confirms this order. The authors conceded that they cannot exchange the limits, and Zo8z accepted the plots as evidence. Reviewer_dTFu (confidence 2) could not check the proofs and misstated the main result as a "large depth" limit. It pressed on the 1/N per-layer scaling against standard and Yang–Hu parametrizations, which the submission does not discuss, and kept 6. Reviewer_SSUu (confidence 2) asked how the argument relates to the shallow CLT of Chen et al.; the plots raised it to 6. The three-sentence meta-review restates the reviews and keeps only the parametrization concern.

Committee B (6, 6, 5, 6). Reviewer_D7BN called the work "technically sound" while declaring the math unchecked, and doubted its significance over the shallow result. Reviewer_EQhg wrote the most careful review: the main result is unwieldy and fully stated only in the appendix (l.181 confirms). After the plots, EQhg kept 6 and still found the paper "a little too theoretical" for NeurIPS. Reviewer_PZpb (2 hours) made acceptance conditional on evidence for Theorem 5 and Section 4. Reviewer_ErCU wrote "I did not understand it". Two of its four "careless mistakes" are wrong: Definition 1 and Theorem 2 share one numbering sequence, and the bibliography dashes repeat the authors above. ErCU did not reply to the rebuttal. The AC gave ErCU's 5 no weight and called the model "standard feed forward networks", although the paper uses mean-field 1/N scaling.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A questioned the order of limits behind the variance-reduction result (Zo8z). Only A pressed the realism of the 1/N parametrization (dTFu), and A's meta-review keeps that concern. Only B raised notation gaps and supposed errors (ErCU), asked for a toy example and plainer meanings of "sampled neurons" and "second-order MF limit" (EQhg), and doubted the significance over Chen et al. (D7BN). Only B's AC made unchecked proofs a stated reason to hesitate, although A's dTFu said the same. None of these issues changed a recommendation. The decisive issue, missing experiments, was common to both committees, and one rebuttal answered it in both. The camera-ready adds the figure as Section 5 and a paragraph on the scaling limitation.
