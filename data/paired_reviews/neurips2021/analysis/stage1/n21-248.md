**Verdict.** The committees weighted the same facts differently. SENTINEL's guarantee holds only when the interval predictions are correct. Robustness comes from one sentence: run ZCL on part of the capacity (lines 87–89). Both committees saw this. A's two confidence-4 reviewers held the paper to the consistency–robustness standard of learning-augmented algorithms, which its introduction invokes (lines 18–23, 33–35). Both gave 4, and the AC followed them. B's reviewers took the interval predictions as the contribution. Reviewer_PbZr called robustness "trivial"; Reviewer_YbwL and B's AC made it a revision request. B's Reviewer_ZGPi shared Reviewer_K1Av's venue and motivation doubt but gave 6, not 4. No reviewer found an error in a result. No A reviewer posted or edited after the rebuttal. B's spotlight is generous: its two confidence-4 reviewers gave 6, and the meta-review gives no reason for the tier. The pooled mean of all seven ratings is 6.0, which fits the final poster.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 7, 4, 4 (upZi, xbrn, K1Av). Reviewer_K1Av's case is a judgement of framing: selling the paper as learning-augmented is "misleading", and NeurIPS may be the wrong venue. Its claim that robustness is not "discussed" is wrong, because lines 87–89 state a worst-case bound. Its claim that SENTINEL alone can fail arbitrarily is right: Step 3 sets the budget to zero below v*. Reviewer_xbrn's requests for a robustness trade-off, a closed-form ratio and a prediction-aware baseline all hold against the paper. Its boundary counterexample does not hold. It puts all items at v_min with u = ∞, which the continuous model excludes (lines 137–144). In that model, Eq. (1) holds at v* = v_min when ℓ ≡ 0. Reviewer_upZi (7, five hours, 13 line-level comments) liked the additive loss and the weak predictions. It also asked for "a revamp for clarity's sake". The rebuttal's new (1+ε) sketch for slightly wrong predictions got no answer. The AC restated two points: doubt that this is a learning-augmented algorithm, and limited experiments with no source of predictions.

**Committee B (spotlight).** Ratings 6, 8, 6, 7 (YbwL, PbZr, ZGPi, et4L). Reviewer_PbZr read closely and caught the "smallest" for "largest" slip at line 170. It valued the proof that threshold algorithms cannot be optimal here (Section 3.2). Reviewer_YbwL raised to 6 after the authors cleared a misreading: max_i s_i runs over arriving items, not over the k discretized values. The paper's reuse of the index (line 59) invited that misreading. Reviewer_et4L's 7 rests on one paragraph of praise at confidence 3, with no later engagement. Reviewer_ZGPi doubted the venue, the motivation and the novelty of the techniques. It did not "fully understand" why interval predictions help, yet gave 6 at confidence 4. The AC wrote three sentences and named no reason for a spotlight over a poster.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A alleged a boundary error, and it does not hold. Only A asked for a closed-form ratio and a prediction-aware baseline. Figure 2 supports that request. On its random-order synthetic instances, ZCL already reaches about 0.78–0.96, against about 0.90–1.00 for SENTINEL. Only A called the extensions straightforward. Section 4 is a half-page reduction, and the two-stage algorithm with predictions is only in the supplement. Only B asked about the size of the additive loss, α* = 1 when ℓ = u, the arrival protocol, and error accumulation across intervals. None of these threatens a result. The decisive facts were shared; only their weight differed.
