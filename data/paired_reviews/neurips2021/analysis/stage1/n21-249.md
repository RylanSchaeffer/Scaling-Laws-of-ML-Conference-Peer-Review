**Verdict.** The committees split because they judged the same density problem differently, and because only Committee B had an expert critic. Both committees and both area chairs (ACs) called the guarantees new and significant. Both also found the paper very hard to read. Committee A's AC asked for "a revision for clarity". Committee B's AC said proof sketches would not fit in nine pages, so the paper needed a major revision and a new review. The push came from Reviewer_q5vX, a late reviewer (2021-08-01) at confidence 4, the highest in either committee. Two B reviewers then lowered their scores, and one named q5vX. The authors then refused "anything close to a rewrite". The AC rejected at mean 6.0, with three of five ratings at 6 or above. No reviewer found an error in a theorem.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 8, 6, and no confidence above 3. Reviewer_7ciX's 8 is earned: 8 hours, line-level notes on the supplement proof, and a correct catch of a stray epsilon in the Theorem 1 bound. Reviewer_BwZs's 7 rests on one question about Freedman's inequality. Reviewer_G8uS had "trouble seeing the forest for the trees", and Reviewer_beVp said the paper is "pretty much outside of my area of expertise". Both still gave 6. beVp's main limitation is wrong: the ISWERM objective (line 55) needs only the logged propensities, not the full data density. beVp correctly noted that the paper proves no lower bound for its "right dependence" claim. The authors apologised, and beVp stayed at 6. Only BwZs replied in the discussion, beVp added a short note to its review, and no score moved. The two-sentence meta-review follows the unanimous scores.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 7, 7, 6, 5. Reviewer_5gHo (2 hours, confidence 2) and Reviewer_P7T1 (confidence 2, "unable to judge the depth") add little, and their scores cancel. q5vX made three checkable points. First, Remarks 2-4 fill about 63 of 352 numbered main-text lines; this is correct. Second, the claimed match to Zhan et al.'s lower bound mixes a Natarajan-dimension setting with bracketing entropy; this is also correct. The authors conceded the slip. The camera-ready drops "rate-optimal" from the abstract and says "seemingly closing the gap". Third, q5vX said bracketing cannot give parametric rates. This is overstated, because lines 209-210 cover Lipschitz-parametric classes for any p > 0. Reviewer_ZSG9 lowered its score to 7 and cited the Zhan et al. point. Reviewer_kdLu first kept 7, then asked for "a fairly thorough rewrite" and went to 6. ZSG9 also saw correctly that unweighted ERM beats ISWERM in Fig. 1(c). Most dots there are red, but the text says "all ERM methods perform similarly" (line 324). The stated confidences do not support the AC's "absolute experts". The AC's "no space" point holds for the submission, but the camera-ready used a tenth page. The camera-ready still has no proof sketch.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B challenged the Zhan et al. comparison, the space given to Remarks 2-4, the venue fit and the bracketing restriction. Only B noted the Fig. 1(c) result and asked for doubly robust variants. Only A asked whether Theorem 1 holds beyond bandit data, whether Freedman's inequality would do, and whether ISWERM needs the full density. Both committees saw the density, the bare assumptions, the undefined norm at line 146 and the modest experiments. 7ciX called Theorems 2 and 3 a "direct corollary" of Theorem 1, and q5vX called the applications "very standard". The same fact was neutral in A and counted against the paper in B.
