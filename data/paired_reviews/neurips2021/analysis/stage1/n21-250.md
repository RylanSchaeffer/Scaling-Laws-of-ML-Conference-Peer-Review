**Verdict.** The committees saw the same evidence and weighted it differently. Reviewers in both asked whether LADDER's gains come from the structured kernel alone (A: Reviewer_czoi, Reviewer_pjM7; B: Reviewer_KoaP, Reviewer_mmhZ). All four got the same rebuttal table. LADDER leads early, but BO with the fingerprint kernel alone ends ahead (3.36 versus 3.25 logP after 100 evaluations). No string-kernel-only run exists for arithmetic expressions, where LADDER's large gain lies. In B, KoaP (4, confidence 5) had named this baseline as a key reason to reject, and kept 4. Reviewer_iwAZ added a weak 4. The area chair (AC) adopted KoaP's reading. In A, Reviewer_nmQh and czoi reached the same conclusion ("the performance hinges on the used structured kernel"), but nmQh stayed at 6 and czoi rose to 5. The only clear accept was a thin 7. The AC accepted on its own view that the idea is simple and new, and did not discuss the kernel-only result. Nobody found a fatal flaw. The split lies in B's confident detractor and the ACs' weights.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 5, 7, 6. Reviewer_czoi found an error. Line 188 calls v_i "the eigenvector" of L, so Eq. 3 does not give Eq. 4. The authors conceded a typo; Eq. 4 itself is a valid kernel. Czoi later rose to 5 "based on discussions with other reviewers", while "still not convinced". Reviewer_nmQh got a random-search baseline (3.09 versus LADDER's 3.25) and was ready to raise, but kept 6 after the discussion. Reviewer_pjM7 kept 6 after the kernel-only table. Reviewer_G6A5 gave 7 after 2 hours at confidence 3, with a one-line assessment. It asked for a model-fit study that Figure 2 already shows, and never replied. The AC's rationale is close to the paper's Challenge #1, which KoaP in B called misleading and the authors agreed to rephrase.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 7, 4, 6. KoaP wrote the most detailed review in either committee, and most of its checkable points hold. The paper has no structured-kernel-only baseline, uses a dot product instead of Tanimoto, omits the fingerprint radius and the number of initial points, and reports MAE only. In discussion, KoaP broke the authors' reason for rejecting a sum or product kernel. LADDER's own acquisition function is not differentiable and runs on CMA-ES (lines 256-257), and the authors conceded this. The 4 from iwAZ is weak. It asks why no BO baseline exists, although Naive LSBO and LSBO with retraining are BO methods. Its novelty objection cites work in other settings, and iwAZ never replied. Reviewer_JPaw (7) never replied either. Reviewer_mmhZ took the BO training sets (up to 100 points) for the VAE's data; the paper says 100K and 250K (lines 210, 219). The AC called the missing baselines a "consensus". All four reviewers did name some gap, but two scored 6 and 7. The AC also added a reference that no reviewer raised (Jenatton et al. 2017).

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B tested the Nyström construction against sum or product kernels; nmQh in A accepted the same flawed answer. Only B challenged the motivating claims (lines 112-125), the Figure 2 protocol and the fingerprint details. Only iwAZ (B) cited Moriconi et al., Iwata and Otsuka, and BOCS, COMBO and BOSS. Only A raised the Eq. 4 and footnote errors and pre-training sensitivity. Both asked for random search, but only A got the result. Neither committee noted that Naive LSBO in Figure 3b ends at about 2.77, below the authors' own random search. The camera-ready adds the kernel-only table, but not the promised string-kernel-only or random-search results.
