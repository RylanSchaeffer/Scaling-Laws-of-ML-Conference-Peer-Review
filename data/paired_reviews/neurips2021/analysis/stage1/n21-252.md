**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Each read the paper as a solid empirical study of student fidelity with a narrow scope: CIFAR-100 and logit distillation only. No reviewer found an error in a result. Both got the same rebuttal, with new ImageNet, IMDB and teacher-initialization results, and scores rose in both. Each committee ended with one 5 and no rating above 7 (means 6.4 and 6.25). The two dissents differ in quality. A's Reviewer_4uZD kept 5 on misreadings. B's Reviewer_oZSf kept 5 for the "clickbait" title, a view that Reviewer_1LbM and B's AC shared. Neither committee had a champion, so both stopped at poster. The sharpest technical point came only from A's Reviewer_n5i1. With baseline augmentation, the ResNet-56 student matches the teacher on its training data almost perfectly (Fig. 5, l.268-270). So for ResNet-56 the optimization failure appears only when the distillation data go beyond the teacher's training set. This narrows the abstract's main claim, but it changed no score.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 7, 7, 7, 5). Reviewer_n5i1 wrote the most careful review (12 hours). It found the train-agreement point and noted that the epochs evidence is not conclusive, which l.302-303 concede. The replies and the teacher-initialization run moved it from 6 to 7. Reviewer_E993 rose to 6 on the new experiments but still saw no practical remedy. Reviewer_CMC1 gave 7 on a nine-word main review; its first question wrongly ties the KD approximation to a correct teacher. Reviewer_xYbr gave 7 after one hour and called limitations missing, but the checklist points to Appendix D. Reviewer_4uZD asked for ImageNet, got it, and kept 5. It read the accuracy drop in Fig. 1(a) as overfitting, but Fig. 1(b) shows accuracy rising. It said the ImageNet and IMDB tables disagree, but both show accuracy and agreement rising together. The one-sentence meta-review names no weakness.

Committee B (5, 7, 7, 6). Reviewer_G2ra read closely: it listed typos by line and asked for a definition of self-distillation. Reviewer_KHGR questioned the claim at l.335-336 that the highest-fidelity student "is always the best calibrated". Fig. 4 supports it only if fidelity means test KL: the D0∪D1 student has the highest agreement, but the D1 student has the lowest ECE. Reviewer_1LbM (confidence 5) credited only the new CKA numbers and wrote "it is also ok for rejecting it". Reviewer_oZSf rose from 4 to 5. Its technical points missed α = 0 (l.71-73) and the held-out split of Section 5.2. Its title objection stands: l.34-36 say KD transfers "very limited knowledge". The AC praised the design and asked for a toned-down title. The camera-ready keeps the title and l.34-36 unchanged.

- overfitting to the teacher (4uZD; a misreading);
- the KD approximation and teacher correctness (CMC1; incorrect);
- missing limitations (xYbr; incorrect).

Only B raised these points:

- an over-selling title and framing (oZSf, 1LbM, AC);
- undefined "distillation data quality" at l.339 (KHGR);
- GAN confounds, the lottery ticket hypothesis and earlier uses of "fidelity" (oZSf);
- undefined self-distillation and unexplained Fig. 6(c) axes (G2ra);
- feature-level similarity such as CKA (1LbM).

Both committees raised the narrow scope, the lack of a remedy, the calibration claim, and why fidelity matters when accuracy is the goal. No one-sided issue changed a decision.
