**Verdict.** Committee B found a checkable flaw that Committee A missed. SiaSamRea starts from ClipBERT, yet Table 5 (TGIF-QA) leaves ClipBERT out. ClipBERT's own paper reports 82.8, 87.8 and 60.3 on Action, Transition and FrameQA. The submission reports 79.7, 85.3 and 60.2. So the abstract's "+4.3% (action) on TGIF-QA" claim is false. Table 3 does list ClipBERT, on MSRVTT-QA, where it loses. Three of four B reviewers raised this; no A reviewer did. B also asked how much of the gain comes from the extra TGIF-QA pretraining. By the authors' own rebuttal numbers, the margins over CoMVT fall from +2.1 and +2.9 to +0.2 and +0.4 without it. In A, both scores above threshold came from thin reviews. The area chair (AC) then accepted on "strong results ... on 5 datasets". Both panels were borderline (means 5.75 in A, 5.25 in B), so these differences were enough to split them.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 5, 7, 5, 6. Reviewer_dFuU gave 7 at confidence 5 after 3 hours. Its review is three bullets of praise with no weakness. Reviewer_iH3u gave 6 with four sentences of praise and one question about W1 and W2. Reviewer_THcD (5) asked the best question: does the gain come from new clip content or from ensembling? The rebuttal answered it. Eight copies of the anchor clip give 41.3 on MSVD-QA, against 39.4 for the baseline and 45.5 for the full method. THcD never replied. Reviewer_JZcw (5) kept its score because the method is "not specially designed for the task". That reason is weak, and the AC rightly set it aside. The AC asked for the "missing comparison to [22] on TGIF" but did not note that ClipBERT scores higher. The AC also says a reviewer raised its score. No A review was edited after posting, and both reviewers who replied kept their ratings.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 5, 5. These reviews check the experiments. Reviewer_R2rx correctly notes that Table 5 lacks ClipBERT while Table 3 has it. It also notes that the pretraining benefit claimed at line 335 has no ablation, and that tuning is dataset-specific (lines 275-278). Its guess that "the main improvement" comes from pretraining goes too far. Table 1 shows +5.0 and +6.1 points from the new modules. The rebuttal revealed a fact the submission omits. The authors dropped 56% of TGIF-QA videos, those under 40 frames. They then compared against a ClipBERT retrained on the rest (75.1 on Action). That does not answer a comparison with published results. No score moved. Reviewer_QHNZ and Reviewer_a2XV added notes that kept 6 and 5. The meta-review cites an "intensive discussion" that is not in the record. Two of its three stated reasons match the reviews.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B raised the omitted ClipBERT results on TGIF-QA (R2rx, Reviewer_o6HV, a2XV). Only B raised the unablated TGIF-QA pretraining, which the baselines did not use (R2rx, a2XV). Only B raised dataset-specific tuning, missing error bars (R2rx) and training cost (QHNZ, o6HV). The rebuttal put it at about 5 times ClipBERT's time and 8 times its memory. Only A asked for the anchor-copies control (THcD) and a W1/W2 ablation (iH3u). Only A suggested that plain soft labels explain much of the gain (JZcw). Table 1 supports this: averaging alone adds 3.9 and 5.3 points, and the similarity module adds 1.1 and 0.8. Both committees raised vague clip sampling, missing limitations and the restriction to VideoQA. A's AC treated the missing ClipBERT row as a camera-ready fix. The camera-ready added only the retrained ClipBERT and kept the "+4.3%" claim.
