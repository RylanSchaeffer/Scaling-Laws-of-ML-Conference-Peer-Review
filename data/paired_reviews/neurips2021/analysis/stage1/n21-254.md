**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently, and Committee A's senior area chair (SAC) decided the split. Both committees saw the first learned AMG coarsening method. Both saw that it is tested only on 2D Poisson, against one greedy baseline from 2007, with no solver timing. A's AC wrote a positive meta-review with a borderline recommendation. The SAC then read the paper and judged it not ready. Its three reasons are true of the submission. Lloyd aggregation is never described, and there is no appendix. Related work omits RL for combinatorial optimization. Lines 147-148 invoke simulated annealing (SA), but no experiment compares with it. B's AC listed similar limits and accepted on novelty. B's two 6s came from self-declared non-experts at confidence 2. The strongest checkable weakness came from B and moved neither committee. In Figure 3 (right), RL's effective convergence factor is worse than greedy's for most Structured, Different Size and Wide Valence grids. The Aspect Ratio family is missing from that panel.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 3, 6, 7, 4; mean 5.0; no review edited. Reviewer_nCB6 (3) is right that the abstract drops the 2D-Poisson scope and that no timing comparison exists. The authors conceded both, and they conceded broken code. Its O(n^2) claim is wrong. The argmax in Algorithm 2 runs over one Lloyd subdomain, whose size Theorem 2 bounds. Its point on accuracy after coarsening misreads AMG: coarsening changes solver speed, not the converged solution. Reviewer_984W (4) wrote five generic sentences and asked for "strong theories" without naming a gap. It reports 25 hours and did not answer the AC's question. Reviewer_hVW8's 7 rests on "sufficient empirical validation". It asks for θ, which line 141 states. Reviewer_oM5M (6) wrote the most specific review: more PDEs, 3D, stronger baselines, a Lloyd description. The AC set aside the theory complaint and called 2D-only "a minus". The SAC's reasons come from oM5M, hVW8 and its own reading, not from the two detractors.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 4, 6, 6; mean 5.33; no review edited, and no reviewer replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_5a9n (6, two hours) is "not familiar with AMG". It calls the results superior "both theoretically and empirically", but no theorem compares RL with greedy. Reviewer_xtZu (6) found the convergence-factor problem. The authors conceded that RL's convergence factors are worse than greedy's, and xtZu's score stayed at 6. Reviewer_qGDk (4) is the only reviewer who claims AMG experience. It judged the ML part standard and the venue wrong, and it noted the lone 2007 baseline. The AC listed the same limits: a two-grid bound as objective, simple examples, no wall-clock time, no ablations. It still accepted for "a refreshingly new direction". Its view that the paper "does not attempt to exaggerate" is weak. The abstract claims "better coarse graphs than existing algorithms" with one baseline.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the missing Lloyd description (oM5M, hVW8, SAC) and the missing combinatorial-optimization literature (SAC). Only A flagged the overbroad abstract, the broken code and the disputed O(n) bound (nCB6), and the missing Aspect Ratio points (hVW8). Only A asked for an SA comparison (SAC); B's qGDk asked only for a newer baseline. Only B raised the worse convergence factor (xtZu), venue fit and the small network (qGDk), and the missing architecture ablation (xtZu, AC). Only B noticed that footnote 1 links a non-anonymized GitHub repository (qGDk).
