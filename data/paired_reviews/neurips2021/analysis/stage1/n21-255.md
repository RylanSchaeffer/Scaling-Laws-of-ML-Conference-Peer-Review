**Verdict**

The split comes from what each committee asked of the paper, not from different facts. Committee A read it as a tool for behavioural neuroscience. DBE beats BehaveNet by 26 and DLC+VAME by 22 accuracy points (Table 2), and A rated it 8, 7, 7, 7. Committee B read it as a methods paper and asked what the new model adds. The submission confirms B's answers. The full stochastic model trails its own deterministic part: DisAE+VAME scores 81.25/67.31/67.33 (accuracy/NMI/ARI) against DBE's 79.53/64.85/66.94, and lines 293-295 say so. The disentanglement follows Yingzhen and Mandt [31] (lines 71-72), and the paper has no [31] baseline. The paper claims better interpretability four times, but no experiment tests it, and the authors conceded the overclaim. Most results come from one unreleased dataset, each from a single run. A's reviewers saw some of these facts and called them minor. None mentioned that DBE trails DisAE+VAME. B's rebuttal lifted Reviewer_U58y to 5, but its new single-view table again put DisAE+VAME ahead of DBE. B's AC rejected on that point and on interpretability. A's AC gave a spotlight with no stated reason, and A's only 8 came from a one-hour review. B's objections are correct, but they target the added stochastic model and the framing; the disentanglement gains that A rewarded are real.

**Committees**

Committee A (8, 7, 7, 7; spotlight). Reviewer_88jd gave the 8 after one hour. It called the model "thoroughly tested on two head-fixed mouse datasets", but WFCI appears in the main text only in Table 1. It called the comparison rigorous, yet noted no comparison to general video models. Reviewer_Yzq9 said the paper "lacks ablation studies" and accepted a promise of future work. Reviewer_bsYQ found the generated videos "relatively poor" and still gave 7. Reviewer_d5ns (7 h) found a code bug that the authors confirmed, plus clarity faults; its summary swaps pose and context. The rebuttal added no experiment, and only Yzq9 replied. The meta-review praises "the ablations performed", which Yzq9 said were missing, and names no weakness.

Committee B (4, 6, 5; reject). Reviewer_U58y wrote the most detailed review. It found that the best motif scores come without the variational component, that multi-view input was not ablated, and that Equation 2 assigns c_t twice. All three points check out. After new experiments it rose to 5 but still leaned to reject: it could not see how DBE is "more interpretable than a K-means approach". Reviewer_c1eX (4) objected to one dataset, single runs, no [31] baseline and unsupported interpretability; all four points are correct. Reviewer_poVh (6) saw the gains come "from using the DisAE", which Table 2 confirms, and agreed with the criticism in private. The AC adopted U58y's advice to reframe the work "as a study of different representations".

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only B raised that the full model trails DisAE+VAME, that interpretability is unproven, that the results rest on one dataset with single runs, and that the baselines may be untuned. B also asked for context-embedding results and a trajectory-decoding baseline, and noted the unused neural-activity motivation. Only A raised the context-frame code bug, time-varying nuisance such as the moving pellet disk, undefined acronyms, and freely moving animals. Both committees noted the missing ablations, the shaky generated videos, the uncited Figure 1, and the debt to [31]. A treated these as minor or as related-work wording; B counted them against the paper. The camera-ready still shows DBE+VAME (the renamed DisAE+VAME) ahead of the full model and still calls the method "more interpretable".
