**Verdict.** The two area chairs (ACs) weighted the same weaknesses differently. Both review pools were borderline-positive. A's AC followed its reviewers (7, 5, 7). B's AC rejected against its reviewers (7, 7, 6 and a late 4). B's AC found the impact unclear. As biology, the astrocyte model is not shown realistic or better than spike-rate adaptation. As engineering, the paper lacks comparisons with other stabilizing feedback mechanisms. A's record holds the same facts: Reviewer_eTQM's astrocyte "inspired by what the model needs", Reviewer_SaLC's small gains, and MNIST-only tests. A's AC listed all three as minor weaknesses and accepted. B's support was also softer than its scores. Reviewer_879c showed, correctly, that the paper equates a branching factor above 1 with chaos. 879c raised to 7 but kept the objection and deferred to the AC. B's meta-review mentions neither that dispute nor any score. No fatal flaw separates the committees.

**Committee A (poster).** Mean 6.33. Reviewer_4Eeo (5 h) liked the "simple, clean" route to the edge of chaos. It rose from 6 to 7 after a new Fashion-MNIST result (85.61%). Reviewer_eTQM gave 7 after 2 hours. It assessed the experiments in one line ("It works.") and spent the review on biology and on an overclaim in the discussion. Reviewer_SaLC gave 5 for a reason the paper confirms. With 1,000 neurons, NALSM beats the LSM baseline by 0.66 and 0.88 points in mean accuracy, and LSM+AP-STDP by 0.47 and 0.67 (Section 3.4). SaLC raised its score but kept the gain "not expressive enough". The AC wrote that earlier approaches were not "convincing" and that this one "seems to work very well". It did not mention the Fashion-MNIST result.

**Committee B (reject).** Mean 6.0. Reviewer_879c (6 h) gave the deepest review. The paper labels σBF > 1 "super-critical (chaotic)" (lines 179-180) and reports no Lyapunov exponent. 879c rejected the authors' E/I-balance and autocorrelation evidence. It still raised to 7, because the results "do not depend on the transition to chaos", and wrote "I trust the area chair's judgment". Reviewer_rDJf's 7 is three short points at confidence 3, with no reply. Reviewer_qHbj rose to 6. It found that STDP stays on during spike collection for NALSM but not for LSM+STDP (lines 138-143). The authors explained this but did not remove the confound. Reviewer_r3sw posted a 4 on 3 September, seven weeks after the others. Its chaos point is correct. But Section 2.3 does describe baseline tuning, and Table 1 does compare with backprop-trained spiking networks. r3sw never replied. The AC named no reviewer and no score. Its call for comparisons is fair: the paper names two criticality regulators for LSMs ([15], [16]) and tests neither.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B disputed that critical branching is chaos (879c, r3sw). Only B noted that Eqs. 7-8 ignore population sizes and that C in Eq. 3 is undefined (879c, qHbj). Only B asked for comparisons with other criticality or feedback mechanisms (rDJf, AC) and for theory (r3sw). Only A called the gain too small (SaLC). Only A flagged the discussion's overclaims: dismantling the "neuron doctrine" and "LSMs are the most brain-like" (eTQM, SaLC). Both committees raised thin biology, easy benchmarks, missing limitations, the astrocyte's untested role during spike collection, and absent Lyapunov exponents. A treated the last as a curiosity; B treated it as a flaw.
