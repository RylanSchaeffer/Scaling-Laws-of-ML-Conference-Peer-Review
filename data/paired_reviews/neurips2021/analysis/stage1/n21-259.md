**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, with the same mean rating of 5.75. The two area chairs (ACs) weighed the same facts the same way. Reviewers in both committees valued the approach: a learned CCG lexicon of neuro-symbolic programs, trained by expected execution (CKY-E2). On the hard splits the results hold: CLEVR count 96.76 and depth 98.49 against at most 81.81, and SCAN length 1.00 against at most 0.18. Both committees also saw the same weaknesses: a dense results section that does not show what lexicalism adds, a closed DSL with no lexical ambiguity, and an unclear CKY-E2 section. No reviewer found an error that undermines a result, so both ACs made the weaknesses revision items. The routes differ. A split 2–2 and no score moved, so its AC broke the tie on its own reading. In B, Reviewer_sQTY rose to 6 after the rebuttal, and the AC set aside one thin 3. Claims were checked against paper.pdf, the camera-ready; no paper_submitted.pdf exists.

**Committees**

Committee A (5, 6, 5, 7). Reviewer_JDyB (7, seven hours) wrote the most careful review; its points hold. Section 2.1 assumes no lexical ambiguity. A baseline leads G2L2 in four of six CLEVR columns, by at most 1.21 points. Section 2.4 writes the loss as an expected loss over derivations, but the authors say they apply the loss to the expected execution. Reviewer_Wq9E gave 6 on one sentence of assessment. Reviewer_MH2M (5, two hours) said the parser is given to G2L2 but not to the baselines. This misstates the setup: G2L2 gets three combinatory rules and learns its lexicon, while NS-VQA trains its parser on ground-truth programs. Reviewer_45Px (5) found the paper hard to read and asked how lexicalism explains the count and depth gains. No score changed; only JDyB replied, with thanks. The AC accepted the authors' answer to MH2M and made 45Px's points revision requests. It also wrote that "several reviewers praised the paper's clarity", but only JDyB did.

Committee B (6, 7, 3, 7). Reviewer_mHT4 (7) read closely and got the Transformer baseline and split statistics it asked for. Reviewer_wcV1 (7) noted that expected execution is exact only for linear operations; the camera-ready concedes that quantifiers break this. Reviewer_sQTY engaged most: it asked how CKY-E2 differs from uncited latent-tree CKY work and from sum-product CKY. It raised its score to 6 and called its remaining doubts "nit-picking". Reviewer_kovo gave 3 in a two-hour review about presentation. Its claim that the paper has no societal-impact analysis is false: Section 5 has one, and mHT4 and A's JDyB refer to it. kovo never replied. The AC counted three accept votes and called kovo's concerns "addressed adequately".

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these points:

- the built-in grammar as an unfair prior (MH2M; partly incorrect);
- the effect of the curriculum (MH2M; answered by an ablation);
- background on lexicalism, and possibly convenient author-made splits (45Px);
- other methods already solve SCAN (45Px). This is correct: NeSS and LANE (NeurIPS 2020) reach 100% on SCAN length, and Table 2 omits them;
- a loss that does not match the expected execution (JDyB).

Only B raised these points:

- CKY-E2 novelty against latent-tree and sum-product CKY (sQTY);
- linearity and quantifiers (wcV1);
- the missing Transformer baseline and split statistics (mHT4);
- Table 2's perfect SCAN scores (kovo). The authors then disclosed that runs restart until training accuracy reaches 100%, which 74 of 100 runs do.

Both committees raised the shared weaknesses named above. No one-sided issue changed a recommendation. Neither AC weighed the SCAN omission or the restarts.
