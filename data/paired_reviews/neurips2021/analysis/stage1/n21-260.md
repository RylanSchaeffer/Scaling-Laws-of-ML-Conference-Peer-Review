**Verdict.** Both committees saw a borderline paper, and the reviewer draw set the outcome. Every A rating (5, 5, 5, 6) is at or below every B rating (7, 7, 6, 6). Neither committee has an outlier, and both area chairs (ACs) followed the mean. The central weakness was shared but weighted differently. The method needs a held-out rare-class set and a user who knows which scenario applies, and every scenario is simulated on standard benchmarks. Reviewer_enP1 (A) rested a 5 on this. Reviewer_hRHU (B) rejected the authors' answer on the same point and kept a 7. A also drew a novelty critic, and B did not. Reviewer_ryua judged SIMILAR an application of existing SIM functions. It also noted that SMI and SCMI lack the greedy guarantee of submodular functions. The paper confirms both points. No fatal flaw separates the committees.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 5, 6; mean 5.25. Reviewer_ryua praised the writing and experiments but scored 5 on novelty. The paper takes every function from [18] and [20] (lines 78-82, 126-128). It cites the 1 − 1/e greedy guarantee (lines 102-104) but never shows that its objectives are submodular. The authors conceded both points in part. Reviewer_FgsV wanted clearer SIM instantiations and scenario-specific baselines. The baseline claim is wrong for the rare-class experiment, which gives FISHER and GLISTER the same held-out set. Reviewer_enP1 (10 hours) argued that a user cannot know in advance that rare classes or OOD data exist. Reviewer_yRLn (6) caught a real typo at line 295 that makes the "rare" classes the majority; the camera-ready reverses it. Three reviewers edited their reviews to say that they kept their scores. None acknowledged that the method collects OOD points on the fly (lines 352-354). No reviewer replied to the chest X-ray experiment that the authors posted on 1 September. The AC listed the reviewers' concerns, including "accessibility to OoD data", which the method does not need upfront.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 6; mean 6.5. Reviewer_Pxy3 (7, confidence 4) found the contribution "simple and elegant". It called the unrealistic use-cases its "only real complaint" and later accepted that OOD data can be gathered on the fly. Its summary credits the method with "almost linear time", which Section 3.5 contradicts for every function except FLQMI. Reviewer_hRHU (7, two hours) replied that "you can NOT expect the seed labels to cover all rare/OOD classes" and still kept the 7. Reviewer_gPM8 (6, confidence 2) is thin and calls the simulated benchmarks "real-world". Reviewer_YdBA raised to 6 after new experiments. The combined-scenario table has no error bars, and LOGDETCMI beats LOGDETMI by only 0.4 points. The AC's short meta-review notes that the reviewers "well appreciated" the paper. It does not mention the realism limit.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A questioned novelty against Iyer et al. (2021) and conditional-gain batch AL, and the missing submodularity guarantee (ryua). Only A flagged weak standard-AL results (enP1, yRLn), the rare-class typo (yRLn), and missing ImageNet error bars, hyperparameter studies and rebalancing baselines (FgsV). Only B asked for co-occurring scenarios and a relevance-diversity trade-off parameter (gPM8, YdBA). Only B asked for a learning-theoretic derivation, non-image data and the Shui et al. citation (Pxy3). Both committees saw the realism limit, the synthetic data, the O(n²) cost and the unexplained similarity kernel.
