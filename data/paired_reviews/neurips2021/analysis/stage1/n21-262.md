**Verdict.** The committees weighted much the same facts differently, and B's spotlight has no basis in B's reviews. Both saw a simple, clearly written auxiliary loss with a gain in every table, large mainly where Swin or T2T train badly from scratch. Both asked for ImageNet-1K, a ResNet baseline, longer training and a λ ablation, and both got the same core rebuttal experiments. No score moved in either committee. A started lower: 5, 6, 7, 4, 5 (mean 5.4) against 6, 6, 5, 6 (mean 5.75). A's reviewers also probed the evidence harder, and the paper confirms their points. These points shrink the gains but do not refute them. A's AC called the paper "generally solid" and followed the sub-threshold majority. B's AC claimed a consensus on novelty and interest that B's reviews do not show, and gave no reason for a spotlight.

**Committee A (reject).** Reviewer_jUVG (7, confidence 5) flagged one run per loss variant, scored on the test set. Section 5.1 confirms this, and m was also picked on CIFAR-100 test accuracy (Table 2a). The five-seed IN-100 rerun in the rebuttal answered it for one dataset: gains of 0.47 to 1.29. Reviewer_44YH doubted the default VT hyperparameters. Swin and T2T reach only 8.20 and 6.89 on Infograph (Table 3). The authors' own learning-rate sweep lifted those baselines by 4.8 and 6.7 points and cut the gains to 0.60 and 1.34. Reviewer_8uyZ's convergence point holds: at 300 epochs on IN-100 the baselines gain 4.5 to 6.9 points, and the gains fall by half or more (Table 2b). Reviewer_ndSd's 4 rests on a judgment ("expected") and a true point: the cited pretext tasks appear only in related work. Reviewer_Tk2H (20 hours) kept 5 over the Gaussian-prior variant, which the method does not use. Three reviewers never answered the rebuttal. The AC deferred to the scores.

**Committee B (spotlight).** Reviewer_K16j (6, confidence 5) wrote the strongest review. It doubted the from-scratch setting without a CNN comparison. It found two wrong deltas in Table 4, and the paper confirms them: T2T ClipArt is +3.63, printed +0.37. After the rebuttal it judged the optimization "likely poor throughout", the same doubt as 44YH, yet called the rebuttal "somewhat positive" and kept 6. Reviewer_cpF3 found its concerns "addressed adequately" and kept 6. Reviewer_G9ps gave a thin 6: it calls the setting fine-tuning, adds hyphenation notes, and answered the rebuttal with one line of thanks. Reviewer_Z3Px (5) said the gains are large only for poor baselines; Table 3 broadly confirms this. Its final comment set 100-epoch transformers against 200-epoch ResNets; at equal budget CvT is close to ResNet-50. The meta-review says the reviewers "were in agreement" on novelty. Z3Px called the contribution "not enough for a NeurIPS paper", and K16j "not groundbreaking".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised single runs, test-set selection, per-dataset hyperparameters, comparison with other pretext tasks, and the loss variants. These go to how large and reliable the gains are. Only B raised the positional-embedding shortcut, where Section 4.1's argument is indirect, plus model size and the Table 4 deltas. The authors answered those with experiments or typo fixes. The shared requests (ImageNet-1K, ResNet, longer training, λ) got the same answers in both committees. In A they moved no one; in B they were called adequate.
