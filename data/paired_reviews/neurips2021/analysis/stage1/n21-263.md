**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same borderline facts differently, and B's area chairs (ACs) made the difference. After the rebuttal, A's reviewers stood at 8, 6, 6 and B's at 6, 5, 6, 6. Both committees saw the same two weaknesses. The momentum estimator is STORM-type variance reduction adapted to FL. The Õ(ε^-1) communication rate needs b·I to grow as ε^-1/2. Two of A's reviewers raised these points and still moved up. A's AC accepted on the scores. B had no reviewer above 6, and two ACs with the senior AC rejected on their own reading. That reading is accurate. MIME and FedGLOMO already reach O(ε^-3/2) samples (Table 1), and with b = I = O(1) STEM's communication falls back to theirs (Remark 3). But Table 1 also shows that FedAvg, FedPD and FedProx need ε-dependent local work for their best communication rates. B counted a standard feature as a weakness. B's other reasons, full participation and a second vector per round, are true of Algorithm 1. Both came from Reviewer_4cGF's review, posted after the rebuttal, and the AC repeated one four days before the decision. The authors never answered either. No fatal flaw separates the committees.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 8, 6, 6; mean 6.67. Reviewer_siNV gave the 8 after 2.5 hours. The review restates the paper, names no weakness, and admits the technique is "known". siNV never joined the discussion, so the 8 is weak evidence. It did not decide the outcome, because the two 6s clear the threshold. Reviewer_RPhx (9 hours) called the contribution "relatively weak". RPhx also claimed that the output step needs communication at every iteration. That claim is wrong: Step 14 needs the average at one random time only. RPhx accepted the "virtual variable" answer and moved from 5 to 6. Reviewer_GjiB (6 hours) flagged the ε-dependent batch, strong assumptions and a two-baseline evaluation. Clarifications and new tables with FedProx, FedDyn, MIME, FedGLOMO and Shakespeare moved GjiB up to 6. The AC cited "better communication complexities in theory" and "reasonable experimental performance". It does not mention the novelty or batch-size doubts.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 6, 6; mean 5.75. Reviewer_9RXX (10 hours) started below 5. 9RXX conceded Remark 3 ("I apologize for missing this important point") and rose in two steps to 6. 9RXX credited the new experiments and still judged the theory "not significant enough ... to guarantee acceptance on its own". Reviewer_2QZ9 (5, no reply) showed that the "server-side momentum step" is plain averaging after local steps. The authors agreed, which weakens the "two-sided" claim in lines 142-144. Reviewer_pfmU spent one hour at confidence 3. The review asks why the estimator uses two iterates, which is the whole variance-reduction mechanism. Reviewer_4cGF posted on 2021-08-23, after the rebuttal, and got no author reply. The AC panel took 9RXX's view of the theory, 4cGF's two cons, and its own comparison with MIME and FedGLOMO. It gave no weight to the experiments that moved 9RXX.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B raised full participation (4cGF, AC) and the second vector with its privacy exposure (4cGF). Only B showed that the server-side step is plain averaging (2QZ9). Only B asked for error bars (9RXX); checklist 3(c) confirms single runs. Only B's AC argued that MIME and FedGLOMO already reach the sample rate. Only A questioned the output step and the σ-dependent stepsize (RPhx). Only A raised the 4096 constant and the missing compression baselines (GjiB). RPhx also claimed that the initial batch B never enters the analysis, but Theorem 3.1 sets B = bI.
