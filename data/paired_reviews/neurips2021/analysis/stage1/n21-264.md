# n21-264 (FREED): Reject (A) vs Accept Poster (B)

Both committees listed almost the same defects: rough writing, only two RL baselines, a hand-filtered
fragment library of 66-91 members, "chemically realistic" asserted from pictures, Figure 4 panels
unlabelled against large variances. They split on two things. First, what the curated library means.
Committee A read it as the thing doing the work, so the algorithm contributed little: Reviewer_ADzq
wrote that the main advantage "is the library of fragments ... and less the algorithmic contributions",
Reviewer_5BYY that "the novelty on the machine learning side is very limited". Committee B's reviewers,
who were further inside molecular generative modelling (Reviewer_sXy7 cites Guacamol, HierVAE, Maziarz,
the failure-modes paper), read the same constraint as the right inductive bias, "yet another useful
place where expert knowledge can be plugged in". Second, what a promise is worth. A judged the
manuscript as submitted; B credited the camera-ready. The discussions diverged with it: A had 12 posts,
one reply per reviewer and done; B had 26, six rounds with sXy7 and four with Reviewer_dE1i, which
produced a HierVAE active-learning baseline that sXy7 designed, a DeepFMPO comparison, Guacamol runs,
significance tests and pose profiles.

Committee A: 4, 4, 5, 4, 5, mean 4.4, no outlier and no champion. Its best reviews were specific and
right. Reviewer_TmJ6 found that Table 1 reports fa7 alone though three targets were trained (the
caption confirms it), that the graph input carries no bond types (lines 149-151; the authors conceded),
and that the scaffold results match random fragment attachment, which the paper itself concedes at
lines 286-288. Reviewer_bYsg named Kajino (ICML-19) and Xu et al. (NeurIPS-20) as uncited prior work;
neither is in the 45 references. Two reviews were weak: 5BYY spent three hours, wrote line notes
on the abstract and four citations from a different task, called the results impressive, voted 4 and
never returned; USDx declared confidence 2 and non-chemist, offered a 6 if the writing were fixed,
then left the 5. The rebuttal moved only TmJ6, 4 to 5, still rejecting. The area chair wrote
three sentences that restate the review headings and claim unanimity.

Committee B: 7, 5, 6, 6, mean 6.0, all confidence 4. Reviewer_BZA3's 7 is earned, not exuberant: it
names missing fragment baselines, an unclear PER description and six technical queries, and he returned
twice. Reviewer_dE1i caught the false claim at line 85 that this is the first RL plus fragment
generation, and that Table 1's filtered library makes the quality comparison unfair. Reviewer_sXy7
spent five rounds on vocabulary construction and coverage, got concessions (fragments with
non-isomorphic attachment configurations are discarded; some filters fire on a fragment but not on its
supergraph), and raised 5 to 6 and confidence 3 to 4 on the engagement, with his coverage worry still
standing. Reviewer_EZqQ never moved: the reward predictor's R-squared, unreported in the paper and
0.13 to 0.35 in rebuttal, and the small library stayed broken for him. The area chair's sentence "all
reviewers were happy to accept this paper" is false on the record, and the reason given is the
authors' engagement and promised changes.

Issues only A raised: ML novelty as a ground for rejection; Kajino and Xu as prior art; the demand
that lines 93-107 be shown empirically, where bYsg refused "no public code" as an excuse; novelty
rate of generated molecules. Issues only B raised: the false "first application" sentence, which A
never noticed; the docking-score predictor's accuracy; how the fragment vocabulary is built and how
much of ZINC it spans; Guacamol; whether enlarged scaffold molecules still fit the pocket.
