**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. The paper learns one schedule of primal heuristics per problem class with a simple greedy rule and one data-collection run per instance. It cuts the primal integral against default SCIP on two problem classes (49% on DIMACS GISP, 9% on FCMNF). The most substantive reviews (Reviewer_D5iZ and Reviewer_JnKT in A, Reviewer_UfX4 in B) judged it sound, and no rating exceeds 7. The rebuttal lifted borderline scores in both committees. B's lower mean (6.0 against 6.75) comes from one reviewer. Reviewer_zjih (4) read the same simple Streeter-style greedy that A praised as too little contribution. zjih is right that the gain over SMAC is small: no Table 1 cell differs by more than one standard deviation. But the main claim is against default SCIP, and it holds. B's AC outvoted zjih by headcount. No reviewer noted that on DIMACS GISP a one-parameter tune of SCIP already cuts the primal integral by 41%. The schedule beats that tune by 15%, not 49%.

**Committees**

Committee A (7, 7, 7, 6). D5iZ wrote a careful review and caught an overclaim: the FCMNF text says the schedule dominates "in all metrics", but default SCIP finds the best incumbent faster. The authors conceded. JnKT (1.5 hours) raised the sharpest points: default SCIP as a strawman, a vague node-index definition (l.73-74), and an unqualified claim that MIPs are harder than decision problems (l.161). JnKT also asked for a primal-dual integral comparison that Table 2 already contains. After the rebuttal, JnKT and 8aHm (confidence 2) rose to 7. Nehp's main review repeats its summary word for word. Nehp kept 6, noting only that "all reviewers think positively". The meta-review cites consensus. It calls the subject "branching heuristics" and the schedule "problem/instance dependent"; the paper schedules primal heuristics, one schedule per problem class.

Committee B (7, 6, 7, 4). UfX4 read the SCIP source and found a real error. SCIP stops the heuristic loop only when a solution cuts off the node, not at any new incumbent, as l.100 and the cost model assume. The authors conceded. The camera-ready fixes the sentence but keeps the cost model. UfX4 still championed a simple method that matches SMAC. UfX4 accepted the authors' training-cost argument, but the paper gave both methods 100 hours; the saving is wall-clock parallelism. QJyG misread the node index as a learned feature and rose to 6 once corrected. Ad3G asked for theory and kept 7. zjih spent 16 hours on 13 points. Some hold: the small SMAC margin, and shadow-mode data that differ from real runs, which the authors conceded. Others misread the paper: c and A are defined at l.205, and instance generation is in Appendix D. zjih did not answer the rebuttal. The three-sentence meta-review counts three against one and names no concern.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these issues:

- default SCIP as a strawman, with no primal-emphasis baseline (JnKT);
- the l.161 claim and the FCMNF dominance claim (JnKT, D5iZ);
- the effect on overall solve time, and portability beyond SCIP (D5iZ, Nehp).

Only B raised these issues:

- the wrong loop-termination statement behind the cost model (UfX4);
- the small SMAC margin and the limited novelty (zjih);
- the shadow-mode bias and the vague Section 5 (zjih, UfX4);
- missing theory (Ad3G), random-schedule baselines and ablations (QJyG, UfX4).

Both committees found the node-index definition confusing (JnKT, QJyG). The paper confirms the loop-termination error, the FCMNF overclaim and the small SMAC margin. None of them changed a decision.
