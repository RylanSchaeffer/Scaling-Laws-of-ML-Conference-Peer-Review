**Verdict.** Committee B accepted and Committee A rejected mostly on the same facts. Both found the method technically sound. Both found that no one could say what statistical robustness the min-max over doubly non-negative matrices gives. Reviewer_uDuc in A and Reviewer_cyV5 in B pressed this over several rounds and got the same concessions. uDuc lowered 7 to 6 and called the method "a heuristic". A's area chair (AC) then rejected in two sentences that restate uDuc. cyV5 read the same open question as "upside" and moved to 8. Before the rebuttal the pools were level: A 6.25, B at most 6.2. After it, A fell to 6.0 and B rose to 7.0. B's AC agreed with the concerns and accepted on "the benefit of the doubt". The paper supports A's doubt more than B's praise. On clean data the method ties plain NW on Abalone and Bank and loses to NW and NW-Metric on CPU and KIN (Figure 1). No reviewer noticed that on KIN its error falls as label corruption grows: about 0.78 clean, 0.65 at κ≈2, 0.59 at κ≈3 (Figures 1 and 5). The split comes from opposite score drift in the discussion and from how much each AC tolerated an open motivation gap.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 6, 5, 6, 7; mean 6.0. uDuc asked what problem the method solves and why it should work. The authors defined a robust estimator as any min-max solution, offered uDuc "hand-waving arguments", and had no insight into how the choice of Ω̂ changes the estimate. uDuc concluded that only experiments support the method, and they are too narrow. Reviewer_teR3 (5) asked which real corruption the y→κy test models, offered to raise the score, and never answered the reply. Reviewer_PZV3 (6) found the baselines weak. Reviewer_MTm9's 7 rests on a five-sentence, two-hour review with no discussion. The AC posted nothing in the discussion. Its "small sample situations" point holds: the training size is fixed at 1200, and Figure 4 varies N only for the two proposed estimators.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 7, 8, 7; mean 7.0. Three scores rose by one point. cyV5 "changed my mind" to 8, partly because "I don't believe any other reviewers have brought up any major concerns", though Reviewer_sDqY had. cyV5's own review calls the paper a clear accept only "if it had" an analysis relating the method to the regression problem. The authors never supplied it: "we do not have a concrete answer to this open problem". Reviewer_rsLw (confidence 2) rose to 7 partly because other reviews showed no "crucial weaknesses". Reviewer_DQfm (confidence 2) rose to 7 after replies that pointed back to lines 56-64. sDqY rose to 6, still calling the work incremental. sDqY's claim that the methods do not beat NW holds for Figure 1 only. In Figure 2 they beat NW under label noise (KIN about 0.63 against 1.25). The AC pushed the authors to answer cyV5.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for kernel ridge regression and Huber-loss baselines (PZV3). Only A questioned the y→κy model and asked for covariate, adversarial or natural-shift tests (teR3, uDuc, MTm9). Only A noted that the abstract claims covariate robustness while the tests perturb responses (uDuc). Only B called the work incremental and compared it with NW and NW-Metric on clean data (sDqY, rsLw). Only B asked where the method sits in the distributionally robust optimization literature (sDqY, cyV5). Both raised the unclear motivation, the unspecified entries of Ω̂ (uDuc, cyV5), the choice between the two divergences (teR3, rsLw) and the missing limitations section.
