# n21-268 (Low-Rank Constraints for Fast Inference in Structured Models): Reject (A) vs Accept Poster (B)

Both committees ended close: A 7, 4, 6, 5 (mean 5.5), B 6, 6, 7, 5 (mean 6.0).
Both saw the same gap: the submission times only the HMM experiment (Figure 1). They split on which
objection led and on what the rebuttal did to it. A's detractors, Reviewer_scrw and Reviewer_1AvQ, made
practical speed the test. The rebuttal's PCFG and HSMM timings gave 0.53x to 1.59x, and at |N|=100 both
faster LPCFGs lost accuracy (203.5 and 194.3 vs 191.1 PPL). Both held, and the
area chair rejected on exactly that: "very modest gains (and in some cases, none at all)". B's lead
objection was expressivity: Reviewer_HMa8 and Reviewer_Ys2d asked whether an L-state, rank-N HMM is just
an N-state HMM. It is not, and the authors' three-state counterexample is correct. Ys2d rose to 6 and HMa8
to 5. B got the identical timing tables, and no B reviewer commented on their size. So the same modest speed
evidence was weighed differently, and the rebuttal refuted B's main objection while confirming A's.

Committee A had no real champion. Reviewer_Kr3H's 7 (2 hours, confidence 3) calls the paper "mainly a
theoretical paper", assumes Eqs. 5-6 are approximations, and gives much of its length to PTB <unk>
practice. On 2021-08-20 he wrote "I'm not prepared to be the advocate". Reviewer_6Rb7's 6 has confidence
2, and he raised it only "under the expectation" that timings would be added. The detractors were
specific and right. Only Figure 1 times anything, while line 55 promises to "greatly increase the speed
of inference". The LHMM+band variant is untimed, and the authors conceded it is "quite a bit slower".
Even Figure 1 is modest: read off the plot, 3,400 vs 1,300 s/epoch at L=2^14 is about 2.6x, not
the text's "more than 3x". The chair weighed the concern, not the 2-2 split of ratings.

Committee B was more confident (mean confidence 4.25 vs 3.0) and argued about the model class, not the
clock. HMa8 wrote the sharpest review: the abstract's "reduce the complexity of inference
by a factor of the hidden state size" is unsupported; the authors conceded and promised "empirical
speedups". His equivalence claim fell to the proof, and he moved from 4 to 5 but would not credit the
revisions "sight unseen". Ys2d (confidence 5 after one hour) asked for an accuracy-speed analysis and
found no meaningful accuracy gain, then rose to 6 citing chiefly the expressivity answer. Reviewer_Yt4e's 7
engages with the linear algebra, but on the experiments says only "the improvements are compelling", and
he raised it before any timing tables were posted. Reviewer_51Nb held 6 with an originality
doubt and misread the 8:1 ratio as an eightfold speedup. The chair wrote that the reviewers "agree ...
the experiments are good", which neither Ys2d nor HMa8 said, and credited the promised title, lemma and
semiring changes.

Only A raised: the size of the rebuttal speedups as a reason to reject; the untimed band variant;
experiments where the vanilla model cannot train; capacity lost to the rank constraint; ratios
beyond 8:1 (Table 1 in fact has 16:1 and 32:1); PTB preprocessing; error bars. Only B
raised: whether a low-rank HMM is a smaller HMM; the abstract's overclaim; originality against
Choromanski et al.; how the factored distributions are normalised; the misleading title; semirings;
related work by Thai, McAuley and Belanger. Both noted that speed is measured only for HMMs, that the
parameterization and training are under-described, and that the kernel language obscures a plain
low-rank constraint.
