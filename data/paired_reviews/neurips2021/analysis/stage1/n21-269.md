**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same strengths and the same gaps, and weighted them differently. Every reviewer liked the core idea: Proposition 1 gives the exact Bayes error of a flow model, and temperature tunes it. Both committees also found four gaps. No other Bayes error estimator is compared. Nothing shows that the flow's Bayes error tracks the real one. The flow is selected on the test split. A convolutional generator may favour convolutional classifiers. The scores split in the discussion phase. From the stated score moves, the pre-rebuttal means were close: A at most 6.0, B about 5.5. In A, two reviewers rose to 7 after replies that mostly promised future work, and the one detractor never replied. In B, Reviewer_Q5Ht stayed unconvinced, and two colleagues cited Q5Ht when they lowered or held their scores. A's area chair (AC) wrote four generic bullets. B's AC wanted the promised fixes reviewed again. The paper supports B's doubt about the real-data claims. In Table 1, SOTA error is below the estimated Bayes error in 6 of 11 rows; for CIFAR-10 it is 8 times lower. The camera-ready adds none of the promised comparisons.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 5, 7, 7. Reviewer_B5Qe (7, confidence 5, 1 hour) listed specific, correct weaknesses: Section 4.2 has two datasets and one sample size, and no flow-architecture ablation exists. B5Qe accepted anyway because "the idea is interesting". Reviewer_jysn (5) made the strongest checkable points. Table 1 has rows where SOTA beats the estimated Bayes error. Eq. 16 needs labels, and the model is chosen on test NLL. The limitations omit the equal-covariance assumption. jysn did not reply after the rebuttal. Reviewer_QpRs rose to 7 but still asked for the missing estimator comparison. Reviewer_tfr3 (details "not carefully checked") rose to 7 with no specific reason. The AC wrote "the experiments are well executed" and named no concern.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 6, 6, 5. Reviewer_Q5Ht (5) wrote the most specific review. Line 99 calls HDR integration "highly efficient, even in high dimensions"; lines 166-169 report a memory limit at 64x64x3. Line 102 claims high accuracy from a plot without numbers. Q5Ht's counterexample is valid. Distribution Q fits P better but has Bayes error 0.067; R fits worse but matches P's 0.159. The complexity answer moved Q5Ht up slightly; the continuity argument did not. Reviewer_8UYK (6) asked for non-flow synthetic tests and uncertainty-quantification baselines. Reviewer_TyQJ lowered to 6 because of Q5Ht and 8UYK. Reviewer_Vaq4 held 5 and agreed with Q5Ht. The AC's "claims need more precision" echoes Q5Ht.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked to reframe the work as a synthetic-benchmark generator (jysn). Only A noted that the limitations omit the equal-covariance assumption (jysn). Only A cited work that scores generative models with classifiers (B5Qe). Only B attacked the wording of the efficiency and accuracy claims, and asked how NLL handles discrete pixels (Q5Ht). Only B raised uncertainty quantification and the aleatoric/epistemic split (8UYK). Two B claims are wrong. Q5Ht could not find how the class means are set; lines 59-60 and 117 say they are learned. 8UYK called Proposition 1 a result for linear maps; line 127 allows any invertible map.
