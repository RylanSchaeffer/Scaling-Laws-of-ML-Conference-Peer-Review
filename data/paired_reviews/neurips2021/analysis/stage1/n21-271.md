**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. Each read the paper as a sound theory contribution. The booster needs O(log k) samples. A weak learner used as a black box needs poly(k) samples, and this lower bound holds for any booster. No reviewer found an error that threatens a main result. Reviewer_DyJ5 (B, 13 hours) checked the proofs and found only local slips. The clearest is in Theorem 3: it sets εw = γ/k, where Lemma 2 guarantees zero edge; the proof uses γ/(2k). Both committees criticised exposition, practical relevance and the lack of experiments, and both ACs left these to the camera-ready. Neither committee had an expert champion, so both stopped at poster. The committees differ in shape, not outcome. A's ratings are flat (6, 7, 6). B's are higher (7, 5, 7, 7), but two of its 7s come from self-declared non-experts, and its 5 rests on a misreading.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 7, 6; mean 6.33). Reviewer_jfEb (confidence 4) wrote the sharpest review in two hours. It saw that the lower bound depends on black-box access to the weak learner. Section 5.1 (l.292-296) states this, but the abstract and introduction do not. Reviewer_U7yb (confidence 2) hit the same gap. L.256-261 say any ERM learner needs only log k samples, but not what forces the weak learner's k². Reviewer_13Ym asked practical questions about gradient boosting, noise and experiments. It also asked the abstract to name the resources studied, which l.11-22 already do. No reviewer reported a score change. The meta-review restates the abstract and calls the results "surprising".

Committee B (7, 5, 7, 7; mean 6.5). Reviewer_DyJ5 gave the only detailed 7. Reviewer_i7mp summarised the results correctly but disclaimed expertise. Reviewer_66dG gave 7 on two sentences, admitted it is "not an expert", and lowered its confidence after the rebuttal. Reviewer_6X9w (confidence 4) gave 5. Its "main gripe" was that the theory may be "custom tailored for Algorithm 1". Section 5.1 and Theorem 7 cover any booster, so the claim is wrong for the main result. It also objected to "plurality vote", which is the right term for k > 2. Its points on repetition (l.82-85 and l.105-108 nearly match) and typos (l.179, l.248) hold. After the rebuttal it conceded "a better understanding" but kept 5 on organisation. That point has some support: l.70-71 call Algorithm 1 the "first contribution". The AC asked for a scope clarification. The forum PDF still shows γ/k in Theorem 3 and has no new scope text.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised:

- the black-box assumption behind Theorem 7 (jfEb; confirmed);
- what forces the weak learner's k² when ERM needs log k (U7yb; confirmed);
- k > 2 versus k ≫ 2 (U7yb; the theorems need only k > 2/γ);
- Daniely et al.'s lower bounds (jfEb);
- gradient boosting, label noise and weak-learner design (13Ym, jfEb).

Only B raised:

- the theory as tailored to Algorithm 1 (6X9w; incorrect);
- repetition, typos and terminology (6X9w; partly correct);
- Theorem 3's γ/k and the δB step in Theorem 7's proof (DyJ5; the first confirmed);
- the undefined name "σ-gain" (DyJ5; confirmed);
- whether both T and the sample size must exceed √k (i7mp; Corollary 8 says no).

Both committees raised the lack of experiments, the missing comparison with Mukherjee and Schapire's algorithms, and unclear practical relevance. Three reviewers (jfEb and U7yb in A, 6X9w in B) tripped on one gap. The paper does not say early that the poly(k) cost comes from black-box access. No one-sided issue changed a decision.
