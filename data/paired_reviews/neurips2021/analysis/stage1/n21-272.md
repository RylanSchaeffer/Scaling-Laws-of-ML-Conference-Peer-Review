# n21-272 (Open Rule Induction): Accept Poster (A) vs Reject (B)

Both committees saw the same weaknesses, and neither found a fatal flaw. Text-overlap metrics on a
self-built 155-premise set do not show whether rules are correct. The manual accuracy table (Table 2)
has no criteria. Orion's gains depend on continued training over 93.63 million NER-tagged sentences
that the Prompt baseline never gets: without it, BLEU-1 falls from 45.41 to 15.85 or 19.17, against
Prompt's 17.77 (Table 1). The "even outperformed the manually annotated rules" result sits inside the
error bars (Table 4: 76.05±0.52 vs 76.04±0.47). The discussion split the committees, not the facts.
Before it, B's ratings were higher: 8, at least 7, 5, 5 against A's 6, 7, 5, 6. After it, A stood at
6, 7, 6, 6 and B at 6, 5, 5, 7. In A the lone detractor moved up; in B both supporters moved down and
the critics held. B's AC then broke a 2-2 split toward reject without naming a flaw.

Committee A: Reviewer_VhJS and Reviewer_Sn8S wrote accurate, moderate 6s; VhJS correctly noted that
Orion trails AMIE+ (57.0 vs 49.2 to 51.3). Reviewer_7tur gave 7 while raising the sharpest doubt in
either committee, whether the method "simply relies on these magic" 93.63 million samples, and never
replied to the rebuttal. Reviewer_ycgL started at 5 with the strongest evaluation critique: generation
metrics do not measure rule accuracy, Table 3 shows hallucinated rules, COMET is an unsuitable
baseline, and Table 4 diverges from the ExpBERT paper. All four points hold on the paper. The authors
reran ExpBERT in its original setup (inducted 63.8/52.6 vs annotated 63.5/52.4) and stated rule
criteria. ycgL raised to 6, "to be in line with the other reviewers", and kept the metric objection in
the final review. Sn8S ran three follow-ups on existential rules and error identification and found
the answers "more convincing". The AC's five sentences repeat the praise, "The empirical results are
good on different tasks", and pass over ycgL's objection.

Committee B: Reviewer_mUXe (5, confidence 4) made four specific claims, all correct on the paper:
Table 4's inducted-vs-annotated gap is inside the error bars, rules have one body atom, BLEU is
unjustified on a small set with incomparable baselines, and Table 2 accuracies are close and
subjective. The rebuttal redefined "significant" as relative to BERT; mUXe never replied.
Reviewer_cSoa (5, 12 hours) asked for a Prompt baseline with continued training. The authors ran it,
and Orion still won 45.41 to 20.95 BLEU-1. cSoa did not move, recast the concern as "the critical
points for the proposed method is continue training", and agreed with "the negative comments from
other reviewers". The supporters were thin. Reviewer_Vt5F spent one hour, gave 8, and cut it to 7 for
a weakness "pointed out by other reviewers". Reviewer_hJMv "did not find major flaws", wrote that
limitations were adequately discussed although the paper's checklist answers [No], and lowered to 6
after talking with another reviewer. The AC called the paper "divisive" and the decision
"borderline", and rejected.

Issues only A raised: existential rules and the boundary of learnable rules; whether a wrong rule
reflects the LM or the decoder; COMET's unsuitability and the hallucinations in Table 3; the marginal
effect of supported beam search; the ExpBERT setup discrepancy. Issues only B raised: the
"significant" and "even outperformed" overclaim (ycgL saw the same within-variance numbers and called
them "an interesting result"); the independence assumption behind Eq. (3); the unsupported "revisit"
claim in the abstract; nucleus sampling; Figure 1 clarity. The facts were shared; the herding and
the tie-break were not.
