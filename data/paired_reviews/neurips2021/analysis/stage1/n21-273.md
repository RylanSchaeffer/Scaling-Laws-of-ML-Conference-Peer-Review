# n21-273 (COPS): Accept Poster (A) vs Reject (B)

Both committees saw the same weaknesses. COPS trails the state of the art, most on COCO. The AMWC
solver needs about 2 s per Cityscapes image. The "end-to-end" stage fine-tunes only the last layers
after cross-entropy pre-training (lines 275-288). Committee A called these limitations and accepted;
Committee B called them substantial and rejected. Two things made the difference. First, the reviewer
draw. A got four mild positives (6, 6, 6, 7), three at confidence 3, and nobody attacked the claims.
B got two detractors; without Reviewer_aCGb's 3, B's mean is 6.25, the same as A's. Second, the area
chair. A's AC restated the reviews. B's AC read the paper, added objections, and rejected against a
3-to-2 positive majority because there was "no strong champion". A had no strong champion either: it
had one 7, B had two.

Committee A. Reviewer_Zjn9 (7, confidence 4) wrote the only substantial review. It found the method
correct, said plainly that PQ is below the state of the art, and asked for evidence on solver and
gradient quality that the authors could not give. Reviewer_XpFH correctly saw that COCO PQ_stuff
falls after fine-tuning (test 32.8 to 31.4) and that only the classification layer is trained. But it
called Panoptic-DeepLab and SSAP "SOTA" and said COPS beats them; COPS beats Panoptic-DeepLab only at
ResNet-50. Reviewer_vDMJ's summary is the paper's conclusion sentence, copied. Reviewer_5rdz (2 hours)
said running time is "not discussed", though lines 318-320 give it, and raised to 6 citing promised
revisions. Nobody questioned the notation or the title. The AC accepted "notwithstanding" the non-SOTA
results.

Committee B. Reviewer_aCGb gave 3 at confidence 5 with a 45-word main review and never answered the
rebuttal. Its overclaim point is right. Its "no ablation study" is overstated: Table 1 isolates the
fine-tuning stage, Figure 4 compares N = 1 with N = 5, and lines 241-242 cite an appendix ablation.
Reviewer_8yaS (5, 8 hours) is a principled detractor, and its claims check out. L names two things
(lines 173 and 193). Table 1 reports validation numbers for a model cross-validated on validation
(line 249). 4000 COCO iterations at batch 24 is under one epoch; the camera-ready trains 10000
iterations, unfreezes more layers, and reports 38.5 instead of 36.6. Reviewer_G2zw and Reviewer_upv8
raised to 7 after new experiments. The AC's own findings are correct: COCO test PQ 36.6 against
Axial-DeepLab's 42.2, and fixed area thresholds at line 256 despite "no ad-hoc downstream refinements"
(line 101). Its summary that the model "is pre-trained using the proxy metric" is wrong; pre-training
uses cross-entropy (line 277). The authors later retitled the paper "A Fully Differentiable Approach",
which concedes the overclaim. That is a framing defect, not a flaw in the results.

Issues only B raised: the "end-to-end" overclaim; undefined and reused notation; validation-split
comparisons; surrogate versus true PQ; short COCO fine-tuning; boundary quality; no code at
submission; the Axial-DeepLab gap; residual thresholds; ablations confined to the appendix. Issues
only A raised: quality of the heuristic AMWC solutions and of the perturbation gradients (Zjn9);
gradient accuracy when more parameters are trained (XpFH); universality of the loss (vDMJ); the
PQ_stuff drop on COCO (XpFH).
