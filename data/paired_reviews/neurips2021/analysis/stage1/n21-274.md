**Verdict.** Committee B's reviewers knew the statistical-robustness literature and found real, checkable gaps. No Committee A reviewer raised them. Reviewer_YGbw (3, confidence 4, 12 hours) made four points, and the paper confirms each. The guarantees hold only for the idealized sampler: lines 195-203 say they hold "asymptotically for large t", yet the checklist claims non-asymptotic guarantees. No experiment compares the method with Webb et al.'s AMLS, the closest prior method. Line 47 wrongly calls PGD incomplete. Sections 3.3 and 5.2 score a p < pc test against exact certification. The authors conceded the guarantee gap, the PGD error and the framing. Committee A's two 7s came from self-declared non-experts. Their main complaints were misreadings and the 5-image ImageNet test, and the rebuttal answered both. Both committees saw the same incremental advance over Guyader et al. and Webb et al., and both scored it 5. The split comes from who reviewed, not from different weights on the same facts. B's gaps concern framing and evaluation, not wrong results, and neither committee found a fatal flaw.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 5. Reviewer_Lats called the empirical study "a significant flaw", but two of its facts were wrong. Figure 2 plots the ACAS Xu runtimes that Lats said were missing. MNIST did not use 5 images: Table 3's 99, 97 and 93 percent cannot come from 5 images on 4 networks. Lats admitted "I've missed several details", wrote "I'm not an expert in the field", and raised the rating (edit 2021-08-30). Reviewer_Aovq gave 7 after 2 hours, was "not too familiar with this area", and did not check the math. That is weak evidence of quality. Reviewer_K7DT gave 5 for incremental novelty. The point is right: line 113 says the paper copies Guyader's theorem, and Propositions 1-2 follow in a few lines. But the review is one paragraph, and K7DT never replied. The authors posted a 100-image ImageNet table on 2021-09-03, after the last reviewer post. The area chair (AC) praised the "impressive effort" and an "emerging consensus", and ignored K7DT's point.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 3, 5, all confidence 4. YGbw kept 3 after the rebuttal. The authors then tested Alg. 2 on a linear score, and YGbw replied: "A NN is of course very different from a linear function". The paper does not rule out sampler error: it blames ACAS Xu's 9 false positives on pc but reports no run with lower pc or larger t. A 3 is severe for gaps of this kind, but its basis is sound. Reviewer_kvKQ (5) argued that the method has neither formal soundness nor a shown edge over smoothing or concentration bounds. kvKQ also misread the paper as claiming soundness, then conceded. Reviewer_AB2m (5) read N = 2 particles as "TWO datapoints!!". AB2m also said no non-Gaussian experiments exist, but Sections 5.3-5.4 use uniform L∞ noise. After the corrections, AB2m wrote only "I do not see my main concerns alleviated". The AC's "practicality" reason rests partly on these misreadings. YGbw's and kvKQ's grounds stand without them.

Only B raised smoothing and concentration bounds as alternatives (kvKQ), and unguided per-experiment choices of pc and t (AB2m).
