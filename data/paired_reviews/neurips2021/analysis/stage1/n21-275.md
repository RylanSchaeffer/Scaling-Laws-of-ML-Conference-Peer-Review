**Verdict.** The area chairs (ACs), not the reviewers, split this paper. Both reviewer pools rated it borderline: A 5, 7, 5 (mean 5.67), B 6, 6, 6, 5 (mean 5.75). The committee that rejected had the higher mean. No reviewer found a fatal error. A's AC accepted against two "marginally below" ratings. It valued a new problem with a lower bound and an upper bound that matches it as the false alarm rate α goes to zero. It accepted the gap between the bounds if the revision states it. B's AC rejected against three "marginally above" ratings, one raised after the rebuttal. Its concerns are "my own read". The main one, that the contribution is incremental over classical detection ([Lai98]), contradicts r9YP, FMqm and P4K6, who all called the work new. Three AC questions misread the submission. Step 13 of Algorithm 1 uses exploration data, not "the whole history". Lines 292-299 show that only the aligned sensor is informative, which answers "why bother exploring?". Every observation updates every candidate's statistic, which answers the correlation question. A's reviewers found more real defects than B's, and A still accepted. The two ACs weighed the same borderline record in opposite directions.

**Committee A (poster).** Reviewer_p3ra (5, confidence 4, 9 hours) wrote the strongest review. The upper bound carries log|Θ^(1)| through log β, and the lower bound does not. So the claim at lines 289-291, that only a factor 1/(1−ε) separates the bounds, holds only for a fixed instance as α goes to zero. The authors conceded a gap in the dimension d. Their worked example for R5bK used d = 2, so it does not test this. p3ra also saw that the experiments run an unanalysed variant (lines 364-365), against uniform sampling and an Oracle only. p3ra kept 5. Reviewer_Qkr9's 7 is short but correct. Its query about a mean of 236 with a standard deviation of 1 exposed a code bug that the authors admitted. Its line-269 point, that a nearby parameter with a different best action inflates the bound, was conceded. Qkr9 never joined the discussion. Reviewer_R5bK (5, 3 hours, new to the literature) found "most issues were clarified" but still wanted stronger baselines. No rating moved. The authors were combative ("fundamental misunderstanding", "unrealistic and unfair expectation"). The AC took p3ra's fallback, disclosure of the gap, as enough.

**Committee B (reject).** Reviewer_FMqm (6) found that Algorithm 1 line 4 compares a log-likelihood statistic with β, while Theorem 2's 8 log β term needs log β. The authors called it "a good catch". Reviewer_P4K6 saw correctly that the abstract (lines 5-6) promises to balance sensing cost with delay, but the objective is delay alone. The rebuttal convinced P4K6, who raised the rating to 6. Reviewer_r9YP checked the appendix proof of Theorem 1. r9YP found the replies "convincing" but kept 6, because the presentation changes were too many for a clear accept. Reviewer_Fs8Y (5) posted two weeks late, said optimal methods already exist without naming one, and did not reply to the rebuttal.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the gap in d between the bounds, the unanalysed experimental variant, the standard-deviation bug, the line-269 brittleness and the vague second term of Theorem 2. Only B named the β versus log β mismatch as an error; p3ra's worry about slow stopping had the same cause. Only B raised the sensing-cost framing, trade-off curves, [Lai98], and the AC's questions on indexability, transients and non-parametric baselines. Both raised clarity, the assumption of known parameters, the missing ZM20 comparison, regret versus delay, and broken references.
