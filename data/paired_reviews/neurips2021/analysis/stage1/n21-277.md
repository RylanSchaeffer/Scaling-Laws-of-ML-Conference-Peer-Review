**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same evidence differently. Both saw a simple nearest-memory router that adds 0.8 to 4.8 points over plain VGG, ResNet and EfficientNet at nearly equal FLOPs. It costs 1.7 to 7.9 times the parameters (Tables 2-4). Both saw that the main paper compares with no other routing method; lines 241-242 defer that to the supplement. Committee A valued the simplicity and the steady gains. Its concrete doubts, a batch-size confound and single seeds, were fixable, and the rebuttal fixed them. Committee B required routing baselines. Reviewer_Qanu, the most detailed critic in either committee, found the supplementary comparison thin. The camera-ready confirms it: one reproduced method (RNR, 78.96% against 78.42%), and DeepMoE compared only by gains over separate baselines. A's Reviewer_oRgf read the same comparison as "superior results". Qanu also rejected the "easy to train" claim. The submission itself reports about 40% dead PUs at batch 128 (lines 305-307). B's AC adopted Qanu's reasons; A's AC restated the consensus. No fatal flaw separates the committees.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 8; mean 7.0. Reviewer_Svzq first judged that the paper "does not meet the bar yet". It rose to 7 after batch-1024 baselines and ten-seed tables. The camera-ready prints the same means and labels them "skip when occurring PU death"; the rebuttal did not say so. Reviewer_pRRP asked how a per-PU attention module helps, but line 197 says these modules share parameters. It rose to 7 on that clarification and on promised related-work text. Reviewer_oRgf (8, 15 hours) called the RNR and DeepMoE results "superior" and the parameter growth "acceptable for MoE methods". It never posted again. Reviewer_vPSw (6, 2 hours) correctly noted that the EfficientNet-B0 baseline, 76.5%, is below the public 77.1%. An AutoAugment rerun answered it. The AC wrote three sentences: "simple and elegant", and all reviewers agreed.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 6, 3, 4; mean 4.25. Qanu's checkable points hold: no routing baseline in the main tables, batch 1024 against 256 (lines 278-283), no GPU timing. CondConv deltas and a baseline batch sweep raised Qanu only to 4. Those deltas favour RMN, but CondConv's absolute accuracy is higher (78.6% against 78.3% on ResNet-50). Reviewer_fdXd's 7.6-fold parameter count is right (Table 3). The rebuttal deferred its Gumbel-softmax and DARTS questions, and fdXd kept 4. Reviewer_cGyg (3) wrote that the authors compared with no conditional-computation baseline, but the supplement has two. Its MEGAN and HydraNets precedents route with learned gates, not stored features. Its post-rebuttal reply engages no specific point. Reviewer_bRNA (6) found the supplementary feature separation "not obvious" and never replied. The AC copied Qanu's phrases and called bRNA's review "less informative". It kept "experiments are not controlled", though Qanu had accepted the batch sweep.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised single seeds (checklist 3(c) answers No), the weak EfficientNet baseline, the stem-epoch count and NLP mixture-of-experts work. Only B made missing routing baselines a ground for rejection. Only B raised Gumbel-softmax routing, DARTS, CondConv, hard single-path routing and GPU timing. Only B challenged "easy to train" and the four-stage training; A's Svzq and oRgf praised the same ease of training. Only B questioned novelty against MEGAN and HydraNets, and the word "memory".
