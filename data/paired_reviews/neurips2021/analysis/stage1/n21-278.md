**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster, and they read the paper the same way. Each saw a sound but incremental extension of Wu et al. [10]. Reviewers in both noted that POMO comes close to the best DACT gaps in minutes, where DACT needs hours (Table 1). Both ACs called the paper borderline. A's AC accepted it for one result: cyclic positional encoding (CPE) generalizes across problem sizes. B's AC gave no specific reason, but B's two supporters credited CPE. The result holds: a TSP50 model tested on TSP100 gets 2.98% with CPE and 7.93% with standard PE (Table 3). The rebuttal's "16.37% vs 3.87%" pairs Wu et al. with OR-Tools instead, and no reviewer noticed. No reviewer found a technical error. The committees differed in the reviewers they drew, not in how they weighed the facts. B's accept rests on thinner support than A's.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 6, 5, 7). Reviewer_YbAL (confidence 5) wrote the most detailed review and gave 7. Reviewer_wmXs gave 6. It correctly noted that DACT is slower than POMO "as they have almost the same performance". Its worry that CPE enlarges the input is wrong: each PFE has 64 dimensions (l.156). Reviewer_rrox rose from 5 to 6 after the authors defined "aspect" and "mixed correlations". It still judged the results "competitive; although, not SOTA". Reviewer_9zdA gave the only 5 after 2 hours. Its text praises a "new state-of-the-art" and gives no clear reason for the score. It asks for "an ablation study", but Section 5.2 reports three. It never replied. The AC set it aside and accepted on its own reading of the generalization results.

Committee B (5, 4, 7, 7). Reviewer_25wv made the strongest case against. It called the work "too incremental", named POMO as the stronger baseline, and wanted larger instances. After the rebuttal it argued that time windows break the cyclic equivalence, so benefit beyond TSP and CVRP is unproven. It kept 5. Reviewer_pCD7 gave 4 after 1 hour. It describes CPE as "cyclic Gray codes instead of sinusoidal positional embeddings", but Eq. (1) is sinusoidal. Its point that DACT loses to POMO at matched time is correct. It never replied. The two 7s are weak. Reviewer_VmAG cites no result and later claims "good potential for scalability" without evidence. Reviewer_vqcX wrote a 102-word main review and later said "I will slightly lower my scores". The record shows no edit, so the 7 stayed. The four-point meta-review says that the rebuttal "addresses the main concerns". Reviewer_25wv's reply says otherwise.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised these points:

- the missing concurrent DPDP paper, with better gaps (rrox; the camera-ready adds it);
- undefined terms such as "aspect" and "correlation noise" (rrox);
- L2I missing from the baselines (YbAL, wmXs);
- CVRP details: depot, route count, feasibility (YbAL, wmXs);
- CPE input size (wmXs; a misreading).

Only B raised these points:

- similarity to United Positional Encoding (25wv; l.64-66 cite it);
- non-cyclic delivery orders under time windows (25wv);
- learnable positional embeddings as a simpler option (pCD7; untested);
- performance-versus-time curves against POMO (25wv, vqcX);
- code release and missing references (VmAG).

Both committees raised the slowness against POMO, the scope beyond TSP and CVRP, instance size, and incrementality over [10]. No one-sided issue changed a decision.
