**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. All six reviewers liked the idea: grade an interactive student program by classifying the MDP that it defines. Both committees found the writing unclear and the evaluation hard to judge. No reviewer found an error that breaks a result. In each committee, one reviewer was below the threshold before the rebuttal and rose to 6 (A: Reviewer_Z8Zo; B: Reviewer_CvhS). In each, a two-hour review gave the top rating (A: Reviewer_Zkxa, 9; B: Reviewer_TaJm, 8). Both top ratings rest on the problem and the framing, not on the results. Both means are 7.0, but A's call was closer: its median is 6, and its AC called two reviews borderline. B tested the evaluation harder, and the new tables it got weaken the paper. With three teacher bug examples, the method labels every program buggy. With all ten, on the natural class mix, it beats "always buggy" by only 4.2 points (body) and 1.6 points (tail). By the same numbers, over 40% of the programs that it calls correct are buggy. No reviewer noted this, and A never saw the tables.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 6, 9). Reviewer_Z8Zo wrote the most careful review (5 hours). It found the related work thin, the domains undescribed and the supplement only notebooks. Its points on l.264 ("three toy environments"; there are two) and the uncited Algorithm 1 are correct. It rose to 6 on promises: a written appendix, a model figure, a worked example. Reviewer_3Rdw ran the code, asked for an error analysis and a described Code-as-text baseline, and kept 6. Reviewer_Zkxa's 9 is weak evidence. In two hours it praised a "creative synthesis", suggested safe-RL papers and listed 17 typos. It did not assess the experiments, and its post-response note is one line. No reviewer discussion took place, and the AC did not post. The meta-review accepted "after calibrating across a range of papers" and added UI-testing literature.

Committee B (7, 6, 8). Reviewer_JNms wrote from an education viewpoint. It asked about creative variation, class imbalance and the number of bug examples. It correctly found that l.288-289 overstate Table 1: the GMM baseline has 25.3% accuracy. Reviewer_CvhS (9 hours) first opposed acceptance on clarity. The replies, the other reviews and TaJm's defence of bug-state feedback moved it to 6. Reviewer_TaJm asked whether exploration alone, without collaborative DQN training, explains the results. The paper supports the doubt. The Code.org result uses only a play-to-win agent (l.331-333). The authors told JNms that collaboration did not substantially improve it, and they did not answer TaJm. The AC obtained a written promise, and a Code.org email, to release the dataset.

Only B raised these points:

- class imbalance and the number of teacher bug examples (JNms);
- the GMM overstatement, and Table 2 with three runs and no intervals (JNms, CvhS; correct);
- whether collaborative training matters (TaJm, JNms);
- the shared state space, one assignment only, and the ill-defined p(D > ν) at l.136 (TaJm, CvhS);

Both committees raised unclear notation, δ tuning, thin related work, an undescribed Code-as-text baseline and the need for a worked example. No one-sided issue changed a decision.
