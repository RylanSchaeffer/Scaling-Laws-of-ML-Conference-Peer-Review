**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. Reviewers in both called the idea new: reuse tight sets and active sets from earlier projections. Both also met the same weaknesses: no worst-case speed-up, an incremental cardinality-based result, and small synthetic experiments. The submission confirms B's specific critiques. The runtime experiment is one permutahedron at n=25 (line 336). In Fig. 4, REUSE has no effect: OMD-ASAFW overlaps OMD-UAFW. OFW and OMD-PAV are absent from the plots, yet lines 350-351 put them 6 to 7 orders of magnitude ahead of OMD-UAFW. That is four to five orders ahead of the best toolkit variant. A's own discussion reached the same weaknesses: Reviewer_j61r lowered to 7 because "the experiments are not convincing enough and the contribution is limited". A accepted because three of four ratings were 6 or 7, and its area chair (AC) counted the majority. B's more confident panel (confidences 5, 4, 4, 4 against 3, 2, 4, 3) made the experiments decisive. The split comes from reviewer draw and AC weighting, not from a factual disagreement.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 5, mean 6.25. Both 7s come with low confidence, 3 and 2. Reviewer_HxTZ rated at confidence 2 after 2 hours and wrote "I am not an expert in this area". Its case is conditional: "If everything is correct and novel". It did check proofs and caught the Lemma 5 error. Reviewer_j61r did not check the math and came down to 7 from a higher score. Reviewer_fArf (6) wrote that the toolkit is unlikely to improve worst-case runtime, the fact Reviewer_urFe held against the paper in B. Reviewer_AdYT (5) named the n=25 runs and the Frank-Wolfe-only comparison. The rebuttal promised larger runs but showed none, and AdYT did not reply. The meta-review credits "a novel convergence rate". The authors call the A²FW rate the "same as vanilla away-step Frank-Wolfe in the worst-case". The meta-review also omits the weaknesses j61r says the panel agreed on.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 4, 5, 6, mean 5.25. Reviewer_2XnM (confidence 5, 6-7 hours) wrote the strongest review in either committee. It checked the proofs and noted that Theorem 2 is a special case of Bach's Proposition 8.1; the authors agreed. It found that REUSE does nothing in Fig. 4. It asked for OFW in the plots and for OMD with the minimum-norm-point (MNP) algorithm. Its after-rebuttal note says the authors answered a different MNP question. Reviewer_urFe (5) correctly noted that Theorem 6 needs a smooth, strongly convex map, which excludes KL projections on 0/1 polytopes. Reviewer_LoQq (4) correctly found OFW missing from Fig. 4; its complaint about motivation is taste. Reviewer_EP3B (6) is short and wants real-world data. The rebuttal's numbers conflict. It calls OFW "3 to 5 orders of magnitude" faster than OMD-A²FW to 2XnM and "3-5 times" faster to urFe. Its regret gap of "3-10 times" contradicts the 8-25 on line 353. The meta-review restates the reviewers' critiques and wants the experiments the rebuttal only promised.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B named concrete missing baselines: MNP, Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader, and OFW in the plots. Only B showed that REUSE has no effect and flagged that the linear rate excludes KL projections. Only A noted the outdated O(T^{3/4}) regret for online Frank-Wolfe; reference [8] gives O(T^{2/3}). Only A found the Lemma 5 proof error. Both raised the small experiments, the underspecified first experiment, the undefined (P1) and (P2), and the incremental cardinality result. Neither noted that the runtime demonstration uses a cardinality-based polytope, where the paper's own exact PAV projection is far faster.
