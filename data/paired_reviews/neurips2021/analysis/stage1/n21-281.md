**Verdict.** Both committees accepted; they split only on spotlight versus poster, on nearly equal support. A rated 7/6/7 (mean 6.67). B rated 3/6/6/7, and its area chair (AC) rightly discarded Reviewer_PpQM's 3, leaving 6/6/7 (mean 6.33). No reviewer on either side rated above 7 or argued for a higher tier. Both committees saw the same core facts: large CIFAR-10 throughput gains without retraining, and robustness inherited from the base model, not improved. A's spotlight has no stated basis. Its three-sentence meta-review calls the method "clever", a word no reviewer used, and calls robustness "a nice bonus benefit", although two of A's three reviewers named the robustness claim as a main weakness. B's AC spent the meta-review defending acceptance against the outlier and never discussed tier. B's reviewers also found correct limitations that A missed. The committees weighted the same facts differently, and the record supports B's poster more than A's spotlight. paper_submitted.pdf is missing, so I checked claims against paper.pdf, the camera-ready.

**Committee A (spotlight).** Reviewers spent 3.0 hours on average. Reviewer_7tWD gave 7 after 2 hours at confidence 3, named no weakness, and suggested other tasks and CUDA versions; that 7 is not evidence of quality. Reviewer_t8ZX (6) called the robustness claim "weak" but also said GDWS is less robust than ADMM; Table 3 shows the reverse (46.28 against at most 43.80 for VGG-16). Reviewer_uhXk (7) called robustness "a by-product" and the DWS comparison unfair. The authors conceded the robustness point and cited a supplementary MobileNet table. No reviewer replied. uhXk edited on 2021-08-30 without a note, so any score change is unknown. The AC's summary passes over these doubts.

**Committee B (poster).** Reviewers spent 4.0 hours on average. PpQM gave 3 at confidence 5. Its two specific objections are misreadings: that GDWS has no hidden-layer nonlinearities, and that vectorization "breaks the structures of images". The paper replaces each convolution in place and calls the reshaping "computation-free". PpQM answered the rebuttal with "My rating is kept." Reviewer_c4Yo (6) wrote the most detailed review in either committee, engaged after the rebuttal, and kept 6, still doubting the dense treatment of sparse baselines. Reviewer_n5ng (6) and Reviewer_Sfha (7, 2 hours) wrote short reviews with specific, correct points. The AC ignored PpQM, named c4Yo "most informative", and recommended acceptance "in this instance".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B raised these limitations, and the paper confirms each:

- ADMM at p = 75% reaches 174 FPS against GDWS's 119 (Table 3). The authors' own sweep drops GDWS to 36.06% robust accuracy at 135 FPS, below ADMM's 41.07% (n5ng).
- GDWS on HYDRA-pruned models multiplies model size, from 5.9 to 31.5 MB for VGG-16 (Table 4; n5ng).
- All FPS figures come from one Jetson board, and HYDRA runs dense, so 90 to 95% sparsity gives no speedup (c4Yo).
- All attacks are PGD variants (Sfha).

Only A asked for MobileNet and DWS-from-scratch baselines and for dynamic-network citations. Camera-ready Table 2 answers with a trade-off: ResNet-18+GDWS is 1.9 to 4.0 points more robust than the lightweight networks, but slower than three of them and 2.4 to 5.3 times larger. Both committees asked about training from scratch and about RobNet fairness. Neither noticed that the ImageNet gain is small: ResNet-50 goes from 15 to 19 FPS and loses 2.2 points of top-1 accuracy (Table 5). Neither judged the depth of the theory: Theorems 1 and 2 reduce to per-channel truncated SVD with greedy rank allocation.
