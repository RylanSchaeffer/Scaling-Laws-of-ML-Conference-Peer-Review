**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected on mostly the same facts. Both pools credited the feature-space partition as the one new idea and saw the rest as FALKON plus the greedy rule of [8]. Both found the experiments thin. A rated this 7, 7, 6, 6; B rated it 5, 5, 5, 7. A's area chair (AC) restated the paper's claims and read its minimax rate as unconditional, but the paper gets that rate only when cos θ = 0. B's AC rejected on its own framing: novelty of each component, credit assignment, random features. On novelty the paper supports B. For the Gaussian kernel the authors used, ‖φ(x)−φ(c)‖² = 2 − 2K(x,c) grows with ‖x−c‖. So each ParK cell is the input-space Voronoi cell of its centre, contrary to lines 93-95. The only feature-space step is the greedy centre rule, and Table 1 shows that it adds little accuracy over random centres at about 2.5 times the time. Also, K > 0 for every pair and adjacent cells hold near-identical points, so cos θ is near 1 on these data. Propositions 2 and 3 then give no useful control. No reviewer in either committee saw either point. The split comes from reviewer calibration and AC stance, not from different facts.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 6, 6, all at confidence 4; mean 6.5. Reviewer_J64c (7, seven hours) read the proofs and listed eleven unclear points, and the authors answered each. J64c credits the partition as novel but twice says "I am not an expert on partitioning techniques". Reviewer_jHTA (7) called the method "not particularly original" but solid. It misread the TAXI times as seconds; Table 1 gives minutes. Reviewer_iZMk (6) listed four weaknesses: discontinuity, unbalanced cells, m_q and t_q set from unknown quantities, and no guarantee for the centre rule. The paper confirms all four. After the rebuttal Reviewer_o4bz still called the paper "borderline" but moved to accept by "aligning my score with other reviewers". The AC posted nothing in the discussion and named no weakness.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 5, 7; mean 5.5. Reviewer_gQRE gave the most specific review. It found that the bound at line 208 is false, and the authors conceded that κ² must replace λmax(Tq). It also saw that random centres nearly match ParK, which Table 1 confirms (AIRLINE-CLS 31.6±0.3 % against 31.5±0.2 %). Its complexity and f* ∈ H_n objections do not hold. gQRE rose only from 4 to 5 and still found the experiments "lacking". Reviewer_J1kM gave 5 on two sentences ("just a wrapper") and claims 48 hours. Reviewer_STtd's main review is three sentences. Reviewer_QUZt (7) praised the speedup on 10^9 points, which Table 1 confirms, and never returned. No B reviewer commented on the divide-and-conquer numbers in the rebuttal. Table 1 already answers part of the AC's credit-assignment request: FALKON isolates the partition, and ParK-Uni isolates the centre rule.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B found the line 208 error, the unnamed kernel and the small gain over random centres (gQRE). Only B asked for dataset sources, GPU details and why speedups are not 1/32 (QUZt), and about random features and credit assignment (AC). Only A raised unbalanced cells and unknown m_q and t_q (iZMk), the input dimension (o4bz), the fixed design (jHTA, J64c), and how little Theorem 1 adds to FALKON's analysis (J64c, o4bz). Both raised limited novelty, thin experiments, cell boundaries, the cost of centre selection, and the missing link between the centre rule and θ.
