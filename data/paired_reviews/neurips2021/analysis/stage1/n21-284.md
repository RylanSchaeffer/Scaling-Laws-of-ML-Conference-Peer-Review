**Verdict**

The split has two parts. A's spotlight was the AC's choice, not the scores'. Of 44 copies in this dataset with a mean of exactly 7.0, 32 got a poster and 11 a spotlight. A's AC cited only "potential ... broad impact". B's poster was close to forced: none of 89 copies with a mean of 6.0 got a spotlight. B's mean was a point lower for two reasons. First, Reviewer_eiQW (4, 23 h) judged the experiments inadequate: no other MI estimators, and only proof-of-concept MNIST feature extraction. A saw the same gap and still gave 6 to 7, so this part is the same facts weighted differently. Second, B read the theory more closely. It found a misstated condition in Proposition 1, dimension dependence hidden behind the "no curse of dimensionality" claim, and a confusing argument that violating the DPI is an advantage. The authors conceded all three. None is fatal, and nobody in A raised them.

**Committees**

Committee A (6, 7, 7, 8; spotlight). Reviewer_9G8T (15 h) wrote the most careful review and found the Section 3.2 typo. Reviewer_dcfo (2.5 h) said SMI helps mainly with low-dimensional structure. The authors denied this, but camera-ready Section 4.2 says SMI "performs best in structured (specifically, low rank) settings". dcfo's other point, that SMI cannot exceed log n, misapplies McAllester and Stratos, whose limit is on lower-bound estimators of MI. Reviewer_C7hP (3 h, math not checked) called SMI "a new estimator of the mutual information", which the paper does not claim. The champion, Reviewer_imZu (8, 3.5 h), called the theory "sound" without specifics. The AC adopted the rebuttal's framing of SMI as a new dependence measure.

Committee B (4, 7, 6, 7; poster). Reviewer_GPCD (3 h) found that tensorization needs mutual, not pairwise, independence, and that Corollary 2's constant depends on dimension. Reviewer_CzLh (8 h) made the same point for the Monte Carlo term, and kept 6 although "satisfied". The authors conceded that dimension enters through C and M. Camera-ready Remark 6 admits this, but Section 3.1 still says "without suffering from the curse of dimensionality". GPCD and eiQW objected to the DPI argument. After the rebuttal, CzLh and Reviewer_6PJ4 asked for the same fix, and the AC made it the condition of acceptance. eiQW's 4 mixes correct points with misreads. It treats the DV estimator as the main computation, but DV appears only in Section 4.3. It asks for results across dimensions that Figures 1 and 2 already give. eiQW did not answer the rebuttal. The AC called the reviews "overwhelmingly positive" and the idea "interesting and exciting", but a 6.0 mean left no room for a spotlight.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A asked:
- whether max-sliced SMI is preferable (9G8T);
- whether SMI helps without low-dimensional structure, or when MI is large (dcfo);
- whether SMI can approach zero while MI stays positive (imZu);
- for more dependence types and an RMSE definition (C7hP).

Only B found the Property 5 error and the hidden dimension dependence (GPCD, CzLh). Only B objected to the DPI argument (GPCD, eiQW), asked why two slicing directions are used (CzLh, 6PJ4), and asked how SMI relates to sliced divergences (CzLh). These, more than the experiments, set B's reading apart.
