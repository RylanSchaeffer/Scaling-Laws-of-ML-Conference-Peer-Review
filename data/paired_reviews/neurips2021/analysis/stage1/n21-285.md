**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected on the same facts. Both found the paper clear and the problem important. Both found the parts known, the user study small (9 users per arm, one company) and Lale-Auto slightly behind auto-sklearn. No reviewer found an error. The split is over whether a well-built library is a NeurIPS contribution, and the reviewer draw decided it. Novelty judgments ran opposite to the scores. Reviewer_6TXR (A) rated originality 2/5 and gave 7, because "success stories" are research contributions. Reviewer_cjD3 (B) called the translation scheme the first such work in AutoML and gave 4, judging it engineering rather than science. Each committee had one weak outlier, and both area chairs (ACs) followed their majorities. B held better evidence on the user study: in B's forum, the authors' own tests found only the lines-of-code difference significant. A never saw this, and its AC cited the user study as a reason for confidence. The paper supports B on empirical rigor. The scope question is taste; the call for papers lists libraries under "Infrastructure", which favors A.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 7, 4; mean 6.0. 6TXR wrote the most careful review: the translation steps are standard, but a usable library in industrial use counts (lines 43-44). Reviewer_2UcV rose to 6 after the discussion, agreeing that Lale "does not need to show superior performance". Reviewer_sNtW (7, confidence 5, 8 hours) assessed the design in one line ("the design appears to be sound") and asked that the small sample be "mentioned somewhere"; line 347 already does. Reviewer_m9sr (4, confidence 5) called the work "incremental" without argument and asked for AutoGluon and OBOE baselines. The omission is real: AutoGluon appears in one related-work sentence and OBOE is not cited, yet the rebuttal called that sentence "a comparison". The AC called the missing baselines secondary and accepted "after calibrating across my pile".

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 4, 5, 8, 3; mean 5.0. No reviewer reports a score change. The AC quotes cjD3 in all three reasons. cjD3's checkable points hold. Every translation pass is "semantics-preserving" and ends in an existing optimizer format (lines 221-222, 237-238), so Lale adds concision, not expressiveness. Lale-Auto trails auto-sklearn on 10 of 14 datasets; shuttle alone gives it the better mean. In discussion, cjD3 also judged impact by 225 GitHub stars, a test A never applied; A read industrial use as success. Reviewer_H7JW (5) asked for significance tests. The authors found one of four comparisons significant (p=0.0039) and proposed bootstrapping 9 users to 100, which adds no evidence. Reviewer_D3TC (3, two hours) gave three one-line weaknesses and never edited. Reviewer_G6BM (8) called the or combinator "particularly novel" and gradual AutoML impossible in existing systems. The paper says choice is encodable in GridSearchCV and Hyperopt (lines 69-70), and that auto-sklearn and hyperopt-sklearn "support some gradual automation" (lines 330-331). G6BM allowed that the engineering-as-contribution premise "could be wrong" and kept 8.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for baselines outside pipeline AutoML and for computation times (m9sr), asked why experienced sklearn users misread the pipeline (sNtW), and wanted the "gradual" benefit tested against libraries other than sklearn (2UcV). Only B demanded a scientific contribution separate from engineering (cjD3, D3TC, H7JW), judged impact by GitHub adoption and suggested JMLR MLOSS (cjD3), asked for significance tests and an account of why the combinators help the optimizer (H7JW), and called the datasets small (D3TC). Both raised known components, a small and possibly biased user study, the deficit against auto-sklearn, and a software-engineering-heavy presentation.
