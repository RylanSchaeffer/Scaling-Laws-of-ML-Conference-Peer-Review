**Verdict.** Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. The paper sketches the ReLU NTK and CNTK with PolySketch and proves error bounds. It reports a 150x speedup over exact CNTK on CIFAR-10. All nine final ratings are 6 or 7. No reviewer found an error beyond typos. Both committees raised the same minor issues. These are the reversed inequality in Eq. (12), thin definitions in the main text, and the limited value of (C)NTK, which trails trained networks. The rebuttal gave arguments and promises, not new results. It moved one score: Reviewer_Mq45 in B rose to 6. B's higher mean (6.6 against 6.25) comes from different reviewers, not different facts. One fact pulled in opposite directions. Reviewer_tQEn (A) put the main insight in PolySketch [1]; Reviewer_hkr8 (B) called the same combination an important contribution. That moved single scores, not a decision. Each committee saw half of one caveat on the headline, and neither AC weighed it. Reviewer_tQEn noted that the sketch's cost is worse in depth than exact CNTK: L^11 against L (Theorem 4). Reviewer_M4ry (B) noted that the CIFAR-10 comparison stops at depth 3, the shallowest depth in Arora et al. At depth 4, exact CNTK reaches 75.93%, above the sketch's 72.06%. The authors' reply links the two: the L^11 cost is why the paper stops at depth 3. These caveats narrow the claims; they do not threaten acceptance.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 6, 6; no review was edited. Reviewer_kQ2k had reviewed an earlier version ("This submission merged two papers") and gave 7 at confidence 5. But its text says "I don't check the proof carefully". It asked to replace the MSE with a relative kernel error, but Table 2's MSE is test regression error (l.293-294). Reviewer_tQEn wrote the longest review in either committee (about 700 words). It correctly found that the spectral guarantee covers only L = 1 (Theorem 3), which the abstract omits. It kept 6 because the reply did not change its view on novelty or depth scaling. Reviewer_agp1 correctly noted that no experiment measures kernel approximation error. Reviewer_CT76 (2 hours, confidence 2) wrote four sentences and did not reply. The meta-review restates the consensus. It asks the authors to use "the updated reviews", but no A review changed.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 7, 6, 7. Reviewer_Mq45 doubted the significance and the ReLU-only scope. It rose to 6 after the authors argued that the method could extend to other activations and models. They gave no experiment. Reviewer_98MV wrote the most careful B review. It correctly noted that the MNIST experiment uses L = 1 (l.268), while Theorems 1 and 2 need L ≥ 2. Reviewer_M4ry raised the depth-3 point and got a promise of depth-4 results; the camera-ready main text has none. Reviewer_hkr8 reports 15 hours but writes mostly summary. It calls the second algorithm a "random Fourier feature method"; Eq. (11) uses arc-cosine features. Reviewer_Rtas (confidence 1) did not check all the equations. It later wrote "we all agree that this is a good paper". The three-sentence meta-review praises practicality and repeats Mq45's neural-tangents suggestion.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A raised the depth-one spectral bound, the L^11 cost against the exact dynamic program, guidance on sketch sizes and the missing kernel-error experiment. Only B raised the depth-3 CIFAR-10 comparison, the L = 1 experiments outside the theorems, the absent random-feature CNTK, the ReLU-only and FC/CNN-only scope, and risk bounds. The submitted paper confirms every one of these points. None changed a decision.
