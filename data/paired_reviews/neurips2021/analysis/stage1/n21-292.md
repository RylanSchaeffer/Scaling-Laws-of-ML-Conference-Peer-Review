**Verdict.** Both committees rejected the paper for the same reason. The method puts a known tool, MCMC sampling inside stochastic-approximation EM, into a new setting, and only experiments support the new setting. The paper's own related work confirms the first half. MCMC-SAEM [31] already samples M < K components from a proposal, and minibatch versions exist [27, 32] (lines 174-181). The stated contribution is only dropping the exponential-family assumption (line 184). Both rebuttals cited Thm 1 of [31] for convergence and then conceded: "We did not validated these conditions" (A), "Indeed, we do not have any guarantees" (B). The experiments also leave that theorem's setting. The deep-model runs use ADAM (lines 280-283). The GMM runs fix the step at 0.05 (line 235), which breaks the Robbins-Monro conditions of lines 133-134. A rejected with four low ratings. B rejected over a 6 and a 7, but those ratings rest on weaker reviews, not on different facts. Both ACs weighed the same facts the same way.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 3, 5, 4, 4. This is the deeper record. Reviewer_iZc8 and Reviewer_nhhz pressed the persistent chain over three rounds. nhhz asked how a chain can track a posterior that moves with θ, worst for points rarely drawn into a minibatch. iZc8 asked whether the conditions of [31] hold. It also objected to timing runs to 95% of their eventual maximum, which the authors called "oracle-like". On 2021-09-01 iZc8 moved from borderline (6, inferred) to 5. It expected a revision to "result in a strong accept", but found the changes too large to accept unseen. Reviewer_XENJ correctly noted that the TF proposal never runs on real data and that Table 2 has no standard deviations. Its reading of M = 1 as "choosing a single component at random" ignores lines 118-120. Reviewer_n8QC (148 words) misread the chain the same way, wrongly called limitations absent, and never replied. The meta-review restates iZc8's update.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 7, 3. Reviewer_weQ2's 7 calls the paper "technically sound" but never asks whether one MH step per iteration gives a valid E-step. It asks only for a mini-batch EM baseline and a missing Einsum-networks citation, and it never replied. Reviewer_4qCw's 6 praises "the impressive 100x average speed-up". Table 2 gives means of 14× (SPTN) and 9× (real NVP) and medians near 4×; one dataset reaches 100×. Its own text calls the significance "not clear". Reviewer_UbDq spent one hour on a 67-word main review, but both of its points hold: the idea is known, and the chain's bias is unanalysed. It alone engaged after the rebuttal and drew the "no guarantees" concession. The AC set the scores aside and adopted the doubts of UbDq and 4qCw. It added its own reading: no one shows that assumption SAEM3' of [31] holds.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised the oracle timing rule, the missing error bars, the unused TF proposal, the large SGD likelihood gaps in Table 2, stale chains under minibatching, and absent VI or Gibbs baselines. Only B raised the abstract's claim to the EM-MH combination (UbDq), the mini-batch EM baseline (weQ2), and the informal SPN introduction (4qCw). Both raised the Monte Carlo EM literature (iZc8 and UbDq both cite Levine and Casella 2001), the chain on a moving target, the absent theory, and the practical value of large-K speed-ups. A's extra issues added weight to the same outcome; they did not change it.
