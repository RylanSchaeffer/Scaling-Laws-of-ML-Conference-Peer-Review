**Verdict.** Committee A accepted and Committee B rejected on the same facts. Both saw a combination of known parts: CHNN/CLNN, a MuJoCo-like convex contact model and CvxpyLayers. Both saw simple simulated systems, only learned baselines (MLP, Interaction Network) in the learning experiments, and no real data. Both saw DiffTaichi win the one head-to-head task. Reviewers mirror each other. Reviewer_GQFZ (A, 7) called the work "incremental"; Reviewer_hJGq (B, 6) named significance as its main limitation. Reviewer_adW2 (A, 6) and Reviewer_edmT (B, 4) both wanted data that the model did not generate. Reviewer_rSNF (A, 8) and Reviewer_Kv95 (B, 4) both noted the DiffTaichi result and asked for simulator comparisons. A credited joint learning of dynamics and contact parameters in energy-conserving networks as new. B judged the paper as a robotics simulator and asked for robot systems, simulator baselines and real data. Engagement widened the gap. A's reviewers asked follow-ups, the authors ran new experiments, and two scores rose to 7. B's detractors never replied and never saw those experiments. The split is the acceptance bar each reviewer draw applied, not a dispute about facts.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 7, 8, 7, mean 7.0. Reviewer_aKSy rose from 5 to 7 after new rope experiments. In them the authors corrected their own claim from "an order of magnitude" to "around 2-fold" at D=400. Reviewer_GQFZ rose to 7 after answers on target velocity and code. adW2 made the sharpest point: every trajectory error uses data from the proposed model, so there is "zero model mismatch". The paper confirms it (lines 212-218, 297-305). In the authors' LCP-data test, the BP5-e error rose from 2.34e-5 to 7.35e-4. adW2 rejected the authors' overfitting reading of the baselines and stayed at 6. rSNF gave 8 at confidence 5 on a list of open objections: no Signorini condition, SCS on a singular A, no gradSim comparison. rSNF never replied, so the 8 is weak evidence. The meta-review restates the consensus.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 6, 4, 6, 4, 4, mean 4.8. No reviewer reported a score change. Only Reviewer_i24Y replied, keeping 6 for lack of planning and control problems. Kv95 edited on 2021-09-01 without comment. edmT (1 hour, confidence 5) wrote one paragraph that misdescribes the experiments. It says the authors "primarily compare" against an LCP simulator. LCP is only a failed baseline and a robustness data source; the main comparisons are learned baselines on the model's own data. Reviewer_BwHT calls the model soft because it "is regularized", but the main model CM is not; only the CMr variant is. BwHT's 3D doubt ignores the paper's Cartesian coordinates and gyroscope tasks. These errors did not decide the case; the decisive facts are true. The rebuttal conceded that real data is "not the main focus" and robot tasks are untested. The meta-review copies BwHT's "serial-link manipulators, quadrupeds, and humanoids". Its "data from other simulators" echoes edmT, but most data come from the paper's own model.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A found the zero model mismatch, the small rope gain (about 2-fold in Figure 2), the missing Signorini condition, the target-velocity heuristic, the missing code and the missing discussion of [35] and IPC. Only B demanded robot systems and Nimble or spring-based baselines. Only B challenged line 74, which says that the contact-property learning of [26-30] "has yet to be tested". Only B asked whether SCS subgradients can find new contact sequences. Both raised novelty by combination, missing simulator comparisons, no real data, the DiffTaichi result, no control inputs and the overstated energy claim at line 83.
