**Verdict.** Both committees recommended a spotlight, and the records explain why. All eight reviewers rated the paper 7 or 8 (A: 7, 8, 8, 8; B: 7, 7, 8, 8). None found an error. Both slates read the contribution the same way. It is the first lower bound for list-decodable linear regression, and it qualitatively matches the d^poly(1/α) upper bounds. Its LP-duality moment matching is a non-trivial extension of DKS17/DKS19. It separates the problem from mixtures of linear regressions. Each committee found the one substantive caveat on its own: the SQ bound does not formally cover the SoS algorithms whose performance it claims to match (Reviewer_9Tob in A, Reviewer_iqKa in B). The authors sent both the same text. They conceded the point and said they "believe" those algorithms can be simulated in SQ. Both treated this as framing, not a flaw. Neither meta-review gives a reason for spotlight over poster, so the tier rests on the uniform scores. Each slate holds one thin 8 (Reviewer_8XsW, Reviewer_LQDD). The substantive reviews alone are still all-accept (A: 7, 8, 8; B: 7, 7, 8).

**Committee A (spotlight).** Ratings 7, 8, 8, 8, all at confidence 4. Reviewer_A5qv doubted the conceptual gain: a d^2 bound "follows from DKS17", and the separation from mixtures was unsurprising. The paper credits that Ω(d^2) bound to DKS19, for a minority of outliers (lines 168-170). The authors said it needs an unknown covariance and does not transfer, and that d^2 and d^poly(1/α) differ super-polynomially. A5qv then raised to 7. Reviewer_9D9p made the fullest case for significance and proposed "possibly as a spotlight", but did not check the proofs carefully. Reviewer_9Tob engaged with the construction and raised the SoS caveat. The authors did not understand 9Tob's second question, on thresholding y, and nobody followed up. Reviewer_8XsW's 8 rests on three sentences, including "I read most of the proofs and everything seems clean". It reports 72 hours but names no lemma, step or question. The meta-review addresses both concerns and asks for a formal proof that the KKK19/RY20 algorithms run in SQ. The camera-ready main text adds only a disclaimer (Remark 1.6), with no such proof.

**Committee B (spotlight).** Ratings 7, 7, 8, 8; confidences 3, 4, 4, 4. No score moved. Reviewer_iqKa (6 hours) raised the same SoS caveat, the unexplained reference variance (lines 319-321 only assert its later use) and related LP-duality work. iqKa kept 7 and called it "a great paper". Reviewer_twpW spent time on the main proofs. It asked where the "list" enters the proof and noted, correctly, that the list size has no α dependence. Reviewer_NeJG (confidence 3, details "not carefully checked") gave general praise and said limitations are not addressed. That holds for the submission's main text. Reviewer_LQDD's 8 took 2 hours. It calls the SQ bound "information-theoretic" and paraphrases the paper's own overview (lines 142-151, 186-187). The three-sentence meta-review repeats the reviews' praise. Its quotation marks around "matches" are the only trace of the SoS caveat.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A asked whether prior fixed-polynomial SQ bounds and the practical role of α reduce the contribution (A5qv), why thresholding y does not help (9Tob), and for a formal proof that the SoS algorithms run in SQ (A's area chair). Only B asked about the list aspect and the α-free list size (twpW), the reference variance and LP-duality prior work (iqKa), non-Gaussian covariates (NeJG), and appendix numbering and a typo. None of these lowered a score. Both raised the SoS caveat, the reliance on DKS17/DKS19, and dense exposition. The committees saw the same facts and weighed them the same way.
