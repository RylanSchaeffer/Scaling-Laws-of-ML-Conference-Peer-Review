**Verdict.** The two area chairs (ACs) made this split, not the reviewers. The panels nearly agree: A rated 6, 7, 5, 6 and B rated 7, 6, 7. All three B reviewers criticized the writing on their own; in A only Reviewer_rPEM did at first, and two others agreed after reading it. One reviewer per committee called the method incremental: rPEM in A, Reviewer_L4t3 in B. Both rebuttals carried the same Kinetics table. B's reviewers called these points addressed. A's AC called them decisive. It copied rPEM's late list, down to "kind of incremental", set Kinetics aside, and rejected against a 3-to-1 positive panel. No A score moved. B's AC wrote three sentences on unanimous positive ratings and a "through and excellent" rebuttal, and gave a spotlight with no rating above 7. Each AC moved away from its panel, in opposite directions. The paper supports the shared concerns. It calls itself "an extension of these techniques" (L67-68), and its relational context builds on self-correlation work [21, 22, 47], two papers by the same authors. Its single-run gains over the best ImageNet-pretrained rivals are 1.5 points on SS-v1 and 0.9 on SS-v2; on Diving-48 and FineGym they are 3 to 5 points. The final poster matches the pooled panel (mean 6.3).

**Committee A (reject).** rPEM's main review lists only strengths, including "the idea is interesting" and SOTA results. Its 5 rests on the limitations box: presentation, no Kinetics, and missing TRN, TPN and MoViNet. On 2021-08-29, surprised by the positive scores, rPEM reread the paper and kept 5. It added "improvement is not that significant" and "kind of incremental", and named no prior work. Its checkable points hold. "Content-to-content" means target-to-context at L170 and context-to-context at L187, and L173 calls the key "the value". The rebuttal's Kinetics baselines are single-layer variants at 63.8 to 66.3% top-1. Reviewer_iQAh echoed the writing and novelty points but kept 6. Reviewer_KG6i (7) defended the SOTA results; the AC used its words only to dismiss Kinetics. Reviewer_1xyW (4 hours) listed no weakness and asked about a CUDA implementation the paper does not have.

**Committee B (spotlight).** Reviewer_L4t3 (12 hours) wrote the most careful review in either committee. It found real slips, such as a simplified involution kernel (Eq. 3) and an undefined L. It noted that κ^R with X^R alone comes within 0.5 points of the full model (Table 4a). It called the idea "a bit incremental and convoluted", still rated 7, and accepted an untested conjecture about the 0.5 points. Reviewer_1j5f's main concern was a missing non-local baseline. The rebuttal supplied one, and 1j5f edited its review the day it wrote "I vote for 6". Reviewer_hB4H (2 hours) misread Eq. 5 as a plain matrix product and kept 7. The meta-review names no strength and gives no reason for a spotlight.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for image-domain results, memory use, and a full-kernel LSA without softmax. Only A judged the Something-Something margins small and the Kinetics baselines weak. Only B asked for non-local baselines, parameter counts, per-class evidence against SlowFast, and whether all four sub-transforms are needed. Only B checked whether Section 3 describes involution and lambda convolution correctly. Neither committee could see one rebuttal flaw. The row "32.0 G, 23.3 M, 43.2% top-1" is "Involution" in A's rebuttal and "NL w/ R.P.E" in B's. The prose fits neither table: A's text implies 43.4, and B's text says 43.0. At least one row is a copy error. The conclusions stand, because every version sits about 4 points below the full model.
