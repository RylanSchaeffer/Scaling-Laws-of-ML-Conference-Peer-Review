**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. No reviewer found an error in the derivations. Both sides raised the same three objections. The work is an incremental extension of MOCU and SMOCU, the authors' own 2021 methods. Figures 2 and 3 compare only random, MES and BALD, with no EER/MOCU/SMOCU baseline. The efficiency evidence is one 1-D set-up (Table 1), where IS+JDI is 40% slower than naive at n=10 and 18% faster at n=100. Both committees then got the same new evidence: timing to n=1000 (846 s against 0.643 s) and one checkerboard run with OR-MOCU, OR-SMOCU and ADF-MOCU. Reviewer_C1HE (A) and Reviewer_zuL2 (B) had asked for these baselines and runtimes. C1HE had promised a higher score "irrespective of the outcome". It called all concerns addressed and gave 8, although AER [3] and the GP-regression baseline never came. zuL2 found one setting insufficient and kept 5. A's 6.0 mean rests on that 8; without it, A averages 5.33. A's AC wrote that the concerns were "mitigated", but only C1HE said so. B's AC followed three 5s and zuL2's framing. zuL2's objection is the better-founded one. In the pool-based runs, JDI matches retraining to about 1e-5 (Section 4.1), so Figure 3's gains over MES and BALD belong to MOCU and SMOCU themselves. The camera-ready, posted after the decisions, concedes this and shows ADF-MOCU performing "similar" to NR-MOCU-RO.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 8, 5, 5, 6. C1HE wrote a long, specific review. It reads Figure 2d correctly: at flip rate 0.2, NR-SMOCU-RO is worse than NR-MOCU-RO, against the explanation at lines 289-292. But C1HE did not check the math, and judged the paper "so elegant" that it "should definitely be accepted" even if outperformed. Reviewer_ii33 (6, 10 hours) kept 6 but noted that MES and BALD "are quite good in terms of sample efficiency". Reviewer_gsQi (5) called the timing evidence a 1-D toy, which Section 4.1 confirms, and stayed "not quite convinced". Reviewer_U1Z1 (5) is weak. It says the pool-based study uses one dataset; Figure 3 has three. It calls the gradient assumption of Section 3.3 "unrealistic", but lines 209-210 note that it follows from the kernel gradient. It never replied. The meta-review says the reviewers "generally liked the novel ideas", yet two of four called the novelty insufficient or incremental.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 5, all at confidence 4. zuL2 is the substantive critic. It praised "creative mathematical manipulations" and called the work "a significant advancement", apart from the missing EER baseline and runtimes. Both gaps are real. zuL2 asked for the promised results on 2021-08-28 and judged one setting not enough. Reviewer_anoP's 5 rests on novelty, and its post-rebuttal update is one sentence. Reviewer_6XPv (12 hours) asked questions that the paper answers: lines 139-142 define random optimization, and lines 261-263 and 306-310 explain the IS error gap and the Vehicle result. 6XPv never replied. The AC's two concerns paraphrase zuL2's last comment.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A caught the Figure 2d contradiction. Only A asked for a GP-regression baseline, Freytag et al., EI/UCB, the SMOCU parameter k, and a sampling ablation. Only B raised sparse GP approximations, re-learned hyperparameters, deep-model baselines, and reproducibility details. Only B noted that BALD matches the proposed methods in Figure 2b without comment in the text. Neither committee noticed two weaknesses in Figure 3. BALD is no better than random on WDBC and worse on Ionosphere. On Vehicle, MES ends below both proposed methods, not merely "closely" as the text and 6XPv say.
