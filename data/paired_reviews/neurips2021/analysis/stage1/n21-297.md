**Verdict**

Both committees recommended a poster for the same reasons. fgen extends the authors' own SsNAL elastic-net solver (Boschi et al., 2020, which the submission cites) from a scalar to a k-dimensional response. Table 1 shows it faster than glmnet and sklearn in most cells. In each committee, one reviewer called the extension too small for NeurIPS (A: Reviewer_nVk8; B: Reviewer_zxwr). Both got the same answer: the group penalty couples each row, so π*, the prox and the Hessian must be derived again. zxwr accepted it; nVk8 rose only to 4. The seven ratings are 4, 6, 6, 6, 6, 7, 7, and each 7 is the shortest review in its committee. Both accepts rest on lukewarm 6s; the committees weighed the same facts the same way. Neither committee checked the speed claims. Table 1 contradicts l.266-267: fgen is also slower than glmnet in three sparse n = 500 cells. Outside the r = 486 rows, its gain over glmnet is under 3x in 28 of 52 cells, against "at least 3 times" at l.64-65, and never above 8x.

**Committees**

Committee A (4, 6, 6, 7). Reviewer_nVk8 ("not an expert in optimization") was the only detractor, on a principled basis: the paper says its convergence analysis "follows directly" from Boschi et al. (l.180-181). After two replies, nVk8 rose from at most 3 to 4 and kept the doubt. Reviewer_FbcU asked useful questions (multitask learning, X̂_J). Reviewer_2b9Y (confidence 2) judged mainly the GWAS: one SNP at cλ = 0.99 with n = 210. 2b9Y also wrote that fgen is "an order of magnitude faster" than glmnet; Table 1 shows at most 8x. Reviewer_ZQkA gave the 7 in the shortest review. It calls the semi-smooth Newton dual approach "quite novel"; l.52-55 credit it to earlier work. The AC counted three accepts and set nVk8 aside because "[1] is basically an unpublished pre-print of this paper". That is wrong: [1] is a separate, earlier paper on the scalar case, cited as prior work.

Committee B (6, 6, 7). Reviewer_AsoJ (10 hours, confidence 4) made two central objections; both were wrong. The objective does not separate by column, because λ1Σ‖B_i‖₂ couples each row. π* in Eq. (2) is a sum of squared positive parts, so it is continuously differentiable. AsoJ also read "one Newton step" (l.143-144) as the whole inner solve; Algorithm 1 iterates to a KKT tolerance. After the rebuttal, AsoJ conceded both points and called the work "an efficient implementation". The final 6 probably reflects a rise, but nobody states it. Reviewer_kboM found real errors. Eq. (D) minimises −(h* + π*), Eq. (16) sums from "i−1", and ∂²ψ is a matrix in Eq. (12) but "a tensor of order 3" at l.154. The authors called the list unreadable; after kboM's edit of 2021-08-22, nobody answered it. kboM's ADMM request got a complexity argument, not an experiment. Reviewer_zxwr gave a 7 after 2 hours, with novelty as the first concern, and accepted the reply in one line. The AC copied kboM's summary and asked only for better presentation.

**Issues raised by one committee only**

Only A raised the GWAS statistics (identifiability, power), sparse multitask learning, and the missing convergence theorem (l.179-183 cite prior work only). Only B raised:

- the sign error in Eq. (D) and other notation errors (kboM; correct);
- no ADMM baseline (kboM; l.61-64 argue only indirectly);
- separability and differentiability (AsoJ; both wrong, withdrawn);
- the choice of k in FPCA and the fairness of the timing set-up (AsoJ);
- no accuracy comparison with other function-on-scalar methods (zxwr).

None of these issues changed a decision.
