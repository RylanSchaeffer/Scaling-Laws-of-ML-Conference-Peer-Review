**Verdict.** Both committees accepted the paper and agreed on the facts. The paper makes the [HKLM20] existence result algorithmic; the new step is Proposition 2.3. With it, the [DGT19] learner for Massart halfspaces needs a sample size free of bit complexity. The committees split on how much this matters to a NeurIPS audience. Reviewer_AuTG (A) and Reviewer_48Ey (B) both called the main theorem a synthesis of existing results. AuTG gave 6 and 48Ey gave 8. In A, three of four reviewers stopped at 6 for significance, not correctness, and the AC chose a poster for "a handful of experts". In B, no reviewer raised breadth, the rebuttal closed every concrete question, and the AC called the paper "a foundational advance". The committees weighted the same facts differently. A drew reviewers who judged the payoff narrow; B drew none. Discounting thin reviews keeps the gap: A's substantive reviews gave 6 and 6, B's gave 8, 7 and 6. The paper does not settle the breadth question. It shows no application beyond Massart halfspaces, and the rebuttals only assert others. The PDF on file is the camera-ready, and several slips that reviewers flagged survive in it.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 6, 6, 6 (mean 6.25); no score changed. Reviewer_3GRx called the paper "probably among the top theory papers" but proposed STOC/FOCS/COLT. Reviewer_rK8U saw "a lot of work to achieve a relatively minor improvement". rK8U gave no argument for this and did not answer the rebuttal. Reviewer_AuTG wrote the most expert review. AuTG traced Theorem 1.4 to [HKLM20] and asked whether Proposition 2.2 was needed. The authors agreed it was "not a major contribution". AuTG kept 6 and, after reading the other reviews, called the result "somewhat niche". Reviewer_UuHj's 7 is weak support. UuHj did not verify the arguments and knew of no other use of Forster transforms, though the paper cites several. The authors' reply on fit moved nobody. The AC restated 3GRx's audience concern.

**Committee B (spotlight).** Ratings 7, 6, 8, 8 (mean 7.25). Reviewer_48Ey gave a reasoned 8. 48Ey judged the execution non-trivial and the paper "very clearly written". Reviewer_91N9's 8 is not evidence of quality. Its summary paraphrases the abstract, and its assessment is three sentences of praise with no weakness. Reviewer_px8j found the presentation "very unclear" and named concrete defects. The paper confirms each one; for example, Theorem 2.1 never mentions the transformation A. The answers lifted px8j to 6. Reviewer_Nd9v asked about [AKS20], runtime and other applications, and accepted the answers. The AC followed 48Ey's framing and added "foundational advance". The AC also misplaced the source of the sample dependence. The paper traces it to outlier removal, not to the LP step.

**Issues raised by one committee only.** Only A raised audience breadth and venue fit (Reviewer_3GRx, then Reviewer_AuTG and the AC). Only A called the gain small for the effort (Reviewer_rK8U). Only A flagged sigma for standard deviations, the radial-position definition and the wrong pointer to Theorem 3.4 (Reviewer_3GRx). Only B raised the late integer-coordinate assumption, the unexplained homogeneity step and the source of Theorem 3.3 (Reviewer_px8j). Both committees asked about novelty against [AKS20] and [HM13], unclear technical writing, and other applications. The authors gave both the same answers on novelty and applications. A read these points as limits on significance; B read them as answered questions.
