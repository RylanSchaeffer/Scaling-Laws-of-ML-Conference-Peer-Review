**Verdict.** Committee B accepted and Committee A rejected on the same facts and the same rebuttal. Both forums got the same summary of changes, word for word, and the same one-paragraph delayed-discrimination result on 2021-08-26. Both committees found the same faults, and the submitted PDF confirms them. The paper studies one neuroscience task, Ready-Set-Go. Section 4.4 gives no procedure for its six topology classes, which the authors' own summary calls "manual". Figures 2D-G and 5 have no axis labels. Before the rebuttal both panels leaned to rejection. The split came from one question: do revisions that exist only as forum text count? Committee A said no, and its AC wrote that "the changes are too substantial to be vetted". In Committee B, three reviewers raised their scores after the same rebuttal. Its AC conceded "borderline" scores but accepted because the topic is "very timely". B's case is the weaker. Its most confident reviewer stayed below the bar for A's reason. The camera-ready rests on a graph-reduction method and two tasks that no reviewer saw written up.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 4, 4, 5, mean 4.33, at 5 to 7 hours per review. No review was edited and no score moved. Reviewer_M2vD correctly noted that the paper "only studies a single neuroscience task"; Discussion lines 219-221 concede it. Reviewer_PRAa correctly said the six topologies are not shown to be canonical or exhaustive, and that the two-neuron result rests on inspecting Figure 1. PRAa's reference-8 complaint is wrong: [8] is Mehrer et al., a study of vision-network representations, and line 53 describes it correctly. Reviewer_VAVV's figure complaints hold, but lines 148-150 already answer its question on Figure 3E (LSTM units). After the rebuttal all three called the changes good and declined to score them. PRAa wrote: "I can't justify changing the score of what's been submitted". M2vD and PRAa replied after the late result. The AC restated this consensus and added nothing of its own.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 6, 5, mean 5.75. Three reviews were edited after the rebuttal, all upward. Reviewer_teJ3's review still records a first "recommendation of rejection"; it moved to 6, citing "especially the delayed discrimination task". Reviewer_H1DK (confidence 5) changed to 5 and welcomed the automatic algorithm. It still judged the paper "just below the bar, even with the promised corrections". Reviewer_vfv5 kept 6 but could not "fully assess the new automated topology classification". Reviewer_Q7WG is weak evidence. It spent 2 hours and called the paper "clearly written" with enough detail to reproduce every step, which the paper does not support. It raised its score with a generic note and never joined the discussion. The AC credited the late result with removing "a potential confound of timing", though that result was one forum paragraph. The AC also wrote that all reviewers found the work "well presented". In fact vfv5 found the paper "difficult to understand" and H1DK called the figures "poor".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only Committee A refused, as a group, to credit unreviewed revisions. In B, vfv5 and H1DK voiced the same doubt, but the AC set it aside. Only A asked whether the six topologies are exhaustive, for a fixed-point analysis, and for a narrower title. Only A judged the eigenvalue analysis too basic to be novel. Only B asked for links to biological data and across-animal variability (teJ3, H1DK), and for a machine-learning task (Q7WG). Both raised the one-task scope, the hand-made classes, the unlabelled figures, the simulation-only two-neuron example and thin related work. Both also said underspecification is not surprising.
