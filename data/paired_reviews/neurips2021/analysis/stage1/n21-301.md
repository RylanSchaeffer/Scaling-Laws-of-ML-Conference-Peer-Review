## Verdict

Only Committee B had a specialist detractor, and his partly correct prior-work critique kept B at a poster. Reviewer_VGyX (3, confidence 4) showed that faster-than-τm responses of neuron populations were known (Knight 1972; van Vreeswijk and Sompolinsky 1998) and that the submission did not cite them. He also showed that the noise filter uses synaptic time constants of 0.1 to 2 ms. The camera-ready concedes both points: Section 3 now cites both papers, and Section 6 puts τs "on and even below biological time scales". B's AC accepted on the 9 and the 8 and made these points required revisions. The AC also suggested changing the title, which promises "arbitrarily fast computation with arbitrarily slow neurons", to stress credit assignment. Without VGyX, B's ratings (9, 8) are higher than A's mean of 8.0. VGyX's "clear rejection" overreached. The prior work has no learning, and the paper's main result is phase-free credit assignment, which he concedes is an open question. Committee A never saw this critique.

## Committees

Committee A (8, 7, 8, 9) had no detractor, and no reviewer raised the population-dynamics literature. Reviewer_DcW4 gave a 9 after two hours, with generic praise, no questions and no discussion. DcW4 says prospective coding has "no precedence in the literature". The paper's own citation of Köndgen et al. and two co-reviewers' pointer to Brea et al. 2016 contradict this. Reviewer_ob4X (8, two hours) was brief but accurate. Reviewer_ztYa (8) misread the "second form of catastrophic forgetting" claim, partly because of the paper's wording. The claim concerns unlearning during relaxation (Fig. 1c), not forgetting across tasks. Reviewer_BSPV raised the strongest point: rate-based transmission may bring back the lag. BSPV moved to 7 on the promise of a limitations section, and the lag question got no technical answer. The AC's one-sentence meta-review restates the consensus and ignores BSPV's caveat.

Committee B (9, 8, 3) had two champions and one detractor. Reviewer_iVNT gave a 9 at confidence 4 while admitting confusion about Eq. 2 and Fig. 1, and wrote "CIFAR100" for CIFAR-10. iVNT read the Knight critique and kept the 9 without argument. Reviewer_gRxq (8) asked good questions but thought Section 5 used real data from cortical microcircuits; it uses a synthetic 8-image dataset. VGyX ran the code and answered the rebuttal with specific literature. His seed complaint is weak: different experiments used different documented seeds. The authors closed the exchange with thanks only. The AC took VGyX's novelty and time-constant points and stopped at a poster.

## Issues one committee raised and the other did not

Only B raised the population-level prior work, the synaptic time constants, the plausibility of the prospective time constant τr, the missing frequency-response theory, and the code seeds. These points are the cause of the gap. Only A raised Brea et al. 2016, a transfer-learning test of the forgetting claim, the narrow robustness analysis, and the lack of larger architectures. These points produced promises of revisions, not low final ratings. Both committees asked for a limitations section, a clearer energy function in Eq. 2, and testable biological predictions. The biological realism of fast rate transmission came up in both (BSPV in A, VGyX in B), but only B's AC acted on it. The PDF in the pair folder is the camera-ready version, so it already shows the concessions to both committees.
