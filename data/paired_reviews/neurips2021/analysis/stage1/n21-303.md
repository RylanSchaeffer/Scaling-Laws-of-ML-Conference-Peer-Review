Committee B rejected because its panel raised objections that Committee A's never did, and most of
them hold on the paper. Reviewer_XaVR (confidence 5) argued that λ̂min+ is already the known driver
of double descent and that the bound fits the risk only late in training. Reviewer_g8ya asked for a
lower bound, and after the rebuttal cut the score to 5, citing XaVR. The AC restated both, plus
Reviewer_rFdx's complaint about the experiments. No lower bound exists. In Figure 1 at T = 100 the
measured excess risk has no visible peak at d = n, and at d = n the bound sits 20 to 200 times
above it. The paper's own related work credits Derezinski, Rangamani and Hastie with linking the
spectrum or λ̂min+ to double descent. One step fails: XaVR and the AC say that late in training GD
is near the Moore-Penrose solution. At d ≈ n it is not (Figure 1, third row), and the paper's
noiseless claim sits exactly there. Both committees saw that the theory covers only least squares
and that the neural-network evidence is weak; A called that scope, B a defect. The decisive
difference is expertise: B had a reviewer who knew the minimum-norm literature, A had none.

Committee A (8, 7, 7; confidence 4, 3, 2) had one careful reviewer. Reviewer_uq7X (10 hours) found a
real error: Theorems 2 and 3 hold "with probability 1 − e^{−x}", but x appears in neither bound, and
both are expectations (lines 155-156, 174-175). The authors called it a typo, and uq7X raised the
score to 8. uq7X also found the empirical argument weak. Reviewer_bWET (2 hours, details "not
carefully checked") wrote that the experiments "confirm the theoretical claims"; the paper itself
calls the neural-network link a conjecture (line 309) and its test-error peak "relatively small"

enough", though the paper proves no tightness. bMDY was right that the bounds cannot show double
descent in T, and raised the score after a reply with nothing new. The AC wrote two sentences that
follow the scores.

Committee B (4, 4, 5; confidence 4, 5, 4) read the theory against prior work. rFdx (7 hours) is
right that the term E‖w*‖²(I−M) vanishes only as n grows (lines 161-167); that argument needs
eigenvalue decay, while Section 2.3 assumes isotropic inputs. rFdx is also right that the
experiments use 1000 MNIST or FashionMNIST examples and small MLPs, and track a trained layer's
eigenvalue rather than the inputs'. But rFdx asked to drop the panels at different iteration counts
because "the point of DD is looking at a trained model". Finite T is the paper's thesis. g8ya, "not
an expert in DD", asked for a lower bound. The rebuttal pointed to Figure 1, which gives none, and
said DD happens "partly because of optimization". It also told XaVR that the λ̂min+ connection had
not been "made explicit in any work we are aware of", against its own related work. XaVR never
replied; rFdx kept the 4.
