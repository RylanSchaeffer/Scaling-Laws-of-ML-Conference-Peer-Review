**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. The paper is borderline. Before the rebuttal the ratings were nearly the same: A 4, 5, 6, 7 and B 4, 5, 5, 7. Both committees found the vanishing-gradient argument informal. Both saw that TempMatch equals a temperature change plus logit rescaling (Reviewer_RKc5 in A, Reviewer_Uy2p in B). Both asked for ablations beyond ListOps and for score-function baselines. A had a confident champion and a thin 6, and RKc5 raised 5 to 6 on promised revisions. B's three confident reviewers did not credit promises, and B's only 7 had confidence 2. On the disputed facts B is better grounded. With DropRes in place, removing TempMatch moves each Table 2 metric by less than the ablated row's spread. The knowledge-graph gains have no same-scorer continuous baseline, so their source is not isolated. A's case rests on extrapolation and knowledge-graph numbers that no reviewer disputed. Neither committee found an error that breaks the results. The split comes from reviewer draw and from whether promises counted.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 6, 7, 4, 6. Reviewer_nNqs (7, confidence 4) wrote "a clear accept given its positive finding and the amount of substance". Its reading of Table 3 is right: Freebase H@10 is 89.6 against CoKE's 77.7. Reviewer_b78f's 6 rests on four short paragraphs. b78f called the work "rather incremental", admitted unfamiliarity with the application domains, and never posted. RKc5 wrote the most detailed review and discussion in either committee. RKc5 raised 5 to 6 on 2021-08-26 for a promised formalization, clarity fixes and more ablations. On 2021-08-30 RKc5 showed that the authors' sketch conflated two meanings of "more uniform". RKc5 called the analysis incomplete, and the 6 stayed. Reviewer_Xmig (4) asked for α and β ablations, score-function baselines and confidence intervals; Tables 3-4 indeed report no intervals. Xmig called the work "very novel" but kept 4. The area chair (AC) wrote that the reviewers "agreed that the techniques are novel" and listed the gaps as improvements.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 7, 5, 4. Reviewer_Uy2p (4, confidence 4) made the sharpest points. TempMatch reduces to logit scaling, and the authors conceded this. The Figure 3 toy uses a degenerate target (1, 0). Table 2's spreads hide any TempMatch gain. That last point holds for the full model; without DropRes, TempMatch adds 18.6 points of task accuracy. Uy2p missed Figure 5, which compares τ annealing with β annealing on one task. Reviewer_Thd9 (5) said the custom-model comparisons do not show where the gains come from. The paper bears this out: lines 290-292 credit the knowledge-graph gains "solely" to discrete components, with no control in the main text. Reviewer_UQHi (5) asked for related work (Shayer et al., stochastic depth) and shown interpretability. UQHi also said Jang et al. is missing, but it is reference [11]. Reviewer_DtA3 gave 7 with confidence 2 after 1.25 hours. Its own weaknesses match the detractors' points, and it joined the reject consensus. No B reviewer posted in the forum. The authors answered once and deferred the score-function comparison to "future work".

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A said the following. The "arborescence" prior is a directed pseudoforest. REBAR and RELAX already treat chains of stochastic nodes. The MNIST extrapolation claim is unsupported, and DropRes has a narrow scope. Sections 2 and 4 are hard to follow; B called the paper well written. Only B said the following. TempMatch's gain is within noise once DropRes is used. Both tricks add bias. A Gumbel-Tree-LSTM would test ListOps better. Shayer et al. and stochastic depth are missing. Interpretability is not shown.
