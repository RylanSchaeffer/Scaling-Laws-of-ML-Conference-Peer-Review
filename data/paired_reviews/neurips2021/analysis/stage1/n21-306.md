**Verdict.** The committees disagreed about significance and venue, not correctness. No reviewer reports checking the proofs, which are all in the supplement. Nobody claimed an error in a main result. Committee A gave four 7s at confidences 2, 1, 3 and 4. Only Reviewer_oGv3 engaged with the technique, and two other 7s rest on misreadings. Nobody in A compared the model with earlier online-learning models. Committee B split 8, 3, 6, and its AC then made its own analysis. The AC argued that the protocol is full-information online multiclass classification in the single-vector model. It also showed that, with a per-round margin γ, a naive center-of-gravity method gets O(d log 1/γ) regret. The authors conceded the margin bound. The paper discusses neither point. The AC still called the bounds new and the contributions "nontrivial". It rejected on significance and fit ("more appropriate for ... SODA"). A's AC saw the same fit problem ("slightly niche for the NeurIPS community") and accepted. Both committees saw no experiments and a thin motivation. A treated them as suggestions; B counted them against the paper.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 7, 7, 7, 7; mean 7.0. Reviewer_oGv3 put the contribution in the low-regret cutting-plane formalization and the curvature path. It called the first two algorithms "more standard". It also doubted the O(d log d) sum at lines 291-295. The sum holds, because step 2 sets r near w/10d, which is O(1/d). Reviewer_77iV (confidence 1) asked why the weak oracle gives worse regret. A weak oracle is a restricted strong oracle, so Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 still apply to it. Reviewer_1hLE (confidence 4) assumed only O(2^d) action sets, but X_t can be any subset of the ball (lines 33-35). It praised "near-optimal bounds", but the main text claims near-tightness only for the local variant. Reviewer_7k7P reports 25 hours for a 127-word main review. It asked whether the algorithms are efficient; lines 52-54 and 158 say they are. Only 1hLE replied after the rebuttal, and it kept its 7. The AC restated oGv3's summary in four sentences.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 8, 3, 6; mean 5.67. Reviewer_Wg9U (8) read the main text closely and found real errors, such as w* for v_t at lines 130-134. It did not verify the proofs and never posted again. Reviewer_51YE (6) asked for experiments and noise robustness and never replied. Reviewer_7jBU (3) read the paper as a bandit paper, as the abstract invites. It called the adversarial, noiseless model "pathological" and rejected the authors' doctor-and-lawyer example. Two of its technical claims are wrong. The results are not "just an application" of contextual search (lines 137-141). An adversarial upper bound also holds in a stochastic setting. Its related-work point holds for the main text: two bandit citations, both stochastic. Two of three ratings were above the bar. The AC's own thread with the authors decided the outcome.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B compared the model with online multiclass classification. Only B raised the margin baseline. Only B asked about noisy feedback and about missing bandit and learning-to-rank work. Only B said that the action-independent feedback makes the "contextual bandits" label misleading. Only A asked about the local variant's linear dependence on |X_t|/(L−1). Only A asked about efficient implementation. Both committees saw the overlap with contextual search. Reviewer_oGv3 hedged it and gave 7; Reviewer_7jBU asserted it and gave 3.
