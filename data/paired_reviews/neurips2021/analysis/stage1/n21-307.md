**Verdict.** Both committees saw the same facts and weighted them differently. No reviewer found an error. Theorem 1 shows that support recovery without the separability assumption of GMP'20 (Gandikota et al., NeurIPS 2020) is possible, robust to sign flips. But it is an existence result with no stated algorithm, and it needs O(kℓ³ max(2eℓ, 2ek)^min(ℓ,k) log² n) queries. Committee A had a champion, Reviewer_uLNB (8, confidence 5), and Reviewer_zJ8u (7). Both read the paper as solving an open problem. Committee B had no champion. Reviewer_DGsh set its bar: pure theory needs "a first proof for outstanding problems" or tight bounds. A treated the open problem as outstanding; B did not. B's main claims hold against the submission. A's support is thinner than its 6.0 mean: without uLNB the mean is 5.5. The camera-ready, posted after the decisions, concedes exponential running time in k and ℓ. It also cites a concurrent paper, flagged by uLNB, that solves the problem efficiently with quasi-polynomial queries.

**Committee A (poster).** Ratings 4, 6, 5, 7, 8. uLNB engaged with the mathematics. It asked whether Theorem 1's root α0 exists; it does, at p'/2, as the authors showed. But it never addressed the exponential term or the existence-only form. It called the paper "quite well-written", while six of the other nine reviewers found parts hard to follow. zJ8u's 7 is accurate but took 2 hours. Reviewer_jx9h's 6 discusses a "Figure 5" that does not exist; jx9h later called it "a mixup between reviews". It also asks how τ > 0 affects Theorems 2 and 3, which fix τ = 0. Reviewer_WBt8's 4 (confidence 2) is vague, and it says limitations are not discussed, which lines 79-85, Remark 1 and Section 6 contradict. Reviewer_xjw9's 5 is the best review in A. It says no algorithm is "spelt out", decoding enumerates hypergraphs, and Remark 1 is "misleading". All three points are correct. After the rebuttal, xjw9 kept 5 but deferred: "Other reviewers seem to be viewing the paper positively". No score moved. The AC's generic meta-review followed the mean.

**Committee B (reject).** Ratings 5, 4, 4, 5, 5. Its reviewers spent 42 hours; A's spent about 24. Reviewer_mc9P showed that, for fixed ℓ and large k, the bound is no worse than GMP'20 only at ℓ = 2, where GMP'20 needs no separability assumption. This is correct: the bound grows as k^(ℓ+1), and camera-ready Table 1 gives GMP'20 as O(ℓ⁶k³ log² n). mc9P and Reviewer_dmUb found Theorem 3 worse than Theorem 2. True: its only gain is one non-adaptive stage. dmUb and Reviewer_aD1b found no algorithm outlined, although line 48 claims "a new support recovery algorithm". Both are correct. Reviewer_QFJA (confidence 1) adds little beyond the exponential bound. The rebuttal called exponential cost expected and cited a lower bound for edge-detecting hypergraph queries. That is a different query model, and the authors told aD1b their dependence is probably not optimal. Nobody moved; mc9P cited "the reviewers' common concern". The AC granted the lower-bound argument and turned it against the paper. Its "no motivation is given" overstates: lines 82-85 cite GMP'20 on recommendation systems.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only B compared the bound with GMP'20 regime by regime. Only B called Theorem 3 pointless, questioned the practical motivation, argued that the techniques are standard, and asked for a constructive proof. Only A asked for a comparison with the concurrent paper, a characterisation of α0, and a fuller proof of the piercing-number estimator. Only A's AC asked that the abstract credit GMP'20 with the problems. Only A flagged missing 1-bit CS and mixed-regression references.
