**Verdict.** Both committees saw a borderline paper and weighted the same two facts differently. The submission compares only with DF (2013) and DMHP (2017), and it uses one real dataset. Before the rebuttal the committees were close: A at 5, 5, 7, 4 and B at 5, 5 or less, 7, 6. The same rebuttal then split them. In B it raised Reviewer_qLjo (confidence 1) from 5 to 6 and Reviewer_3ktE to 6. Their questions on convergence, initialisation, day segmentation and bandwidth could be answered in text. In A it moved nobody. Reviewer_aevy and Reviewer_jQiC wanted new baselines and datasets in the paper, which a rebuttal cannot supply. A's area chair (AC) rejected on exactly those two points. B's reviewers saw the single dataset too but called it a "slight disappointment", and none asked about newer baselines. B's acceptance rests on thin reviews: 2 to 3 hours each, mean confidence 2.5, two self-declared non-experts. A's mean confidence was 3.5, and aevy spent 24 hours.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 5, 7, 4. The baseline objection holds. The paper's own reference [25], Wu et al. (TKDE 2020), is a newer event-sequence clustering method that it cites but does not compare. jQiC named that paper and two others only on 2021-09-02, three weeks after the authors asked for pointers. The record holds no reply. The AC then wrote that the authors "simply did not even mention" plenty of such baselines, which overstates the case. Reviewer_aevy correctly found the GPU and EM-versus-ES speed claims untested. It also found an error that the authors conceded: an undefined G and a wrong O(nCQR^2) claim (lines 219-225). Two detractors misread the paper. Reviewer_SZaC saw "only one weak baseline" and asked for [27], which is the DMHP baseline in Tables 1-2. SZaC never replied. jQiC read "multi-level" as event types and said the model ignores temporal dependence. Equation (1) defines account and day levels, and lines 94-96 model temporal covariance. Reviewer_767p gave a short, accurate review and kept its 7, the lone accept vote.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 6, 6, 7, 6. qLjo asked about convergence and random initialisation and raised its score after the first response. Reviewer_3ktE's main review is two sentences. It credits the paper with "introducing the ES algorithm", which comes from reference [6]. Reviewer_J9LW gave a 7 at confidence 2 in four sentences and disclaimed expertise. Reviewer_DpLt wrote the most specific review: no code, one dataset, missing latent-GP literature. It still called the inference "technically impressive". The AC's meta-review is generic and hedged: "the contribution is maybe" a structure-aware model. It follows the unanimous scores and does not mention the baselines or the single-dataset limitation.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for newer baselines and questioned the untested efficiency claims. Only A found the complexity error, challenged the dismissal of Hawkes processes, and asked how equation (7) relates to the likelihood. Only B asked about initialisation, error bars in Tables 1-2, bandwidth cost, code and latent-GP literature. Both raised the single dataset, convergence and the "Exponential" typo. The camera-ready adds two datasets and a runtime table but keeps the same two baselines. So it fixes A's dataset objection but not its baseline objection.
