**Verdict.** The committees weighed the same facts differently. Both saw that SBTT is a small change to AutoLFADS: zero-fill the missing inputs and drop their loss terms (submission lines 118-119). Both saw a clear paper, strong results on three datasets and few external baselines. All four Committee A reviewers made the simplicity decisive and asked for more baselines. None replied to the rebuttal. A's AC did not fully agree but would not overrule a unanimous panel. In Committee B, Reviewer_SHsa shared A's view of the novelty but scored 6 for the experiments. Reviewer_b2vn championed the problem framing, and B's AC read the 2p sub-frame result as the contribution. A's novelty objection was sound: the camera-ready cites a 2019 paper that used the same masking trick without time. But B saw the simplicity too, so no fatal flaw separates the committees. The split is a different bar for technical novelty against application value, with a champion only in B and an AC in A who deferred to the scores.

**Committee A (reject).** Ratings 5, 4, 4, 5; mean 4.5. Reviewer_XQrZ (5) found the work sound but the technique "somewhat apparent", with no citation. Reviewer_7Nfr (4, confidence 5, 2 hours) wrote a three-sentence main review. It called coordinated dropout, masked language modelling and DeepInterpolation "competitive works" left out of the experiments. Lines 116-118 say those methods split fully observed data, and line 164 puts coordinated dropout inside every AutoLFADS model. Reviewer_bwjY (4) called it "the main limitation" that the mask is unknown in real spike data. Lines 33-35 say sampling times are known, and Figure 2 separates masked data from observed zeroes. Reviewer_EKdk (5) was the most careful. It correctly found the LFADS factor count missing and LFADS at 10 ms bins against GPFA at 20 ms. It offered to raise its score. The rebuttal added an NDT comparison and answered each point. No reviewer replied, and no rating changed. The AC cited missing interpolation comparisons. That is half right: the calcium baselines are interpolated to 100 Hz (line 232), but no input-imputation baseline exists.

**Committee B (poster).** Ratings 7, 8, 6; mean 7.0. Reviewer_SHsa (6) described the method exactly and called the zero-filling arbitrary; the authors agreed. Its post-rebuttal note says the lack of technical innovation "would normally lead to a clear reject in my mind". The experiments and the framing kept it at 6. Reviewer_b2vn (8, 7 hours) called the problem new and had no major concerns. It doubted the Neuropixels case but expected uptake for calcium imaging. Reviewer_ZS9v (7, confidence 3, 2 hours) wrote that the draft needs improvement "before further consideration". It asked for transformer and neural-ODE comparisons and never replied. Its text does not support its 7. No rating moved after the rebuttal. The AC wrote its own positive reading. It called the idea "straightforward" and praised the analyses, the results and the clarity.

**Issues one committee raised and the other did not.** Only A asked for input imputation or interpolation baselines and for other missing-data methods (GAIN, Gunnarsson 2021, Comas-Massague 2020). Only A called GPFA a straw man and asked for Poisson-GPFA, matched bins, the factor count and compute cost. Only A asked for a Kalman-filter baseline and claimed the mask is unknowable. Only B questioned the zero-filling, asked how GPFA itself copes with missing data, and asked for SBTT on other architectures. Only B flagged the single described KL penalty and doubted the Neuropixels use case.
